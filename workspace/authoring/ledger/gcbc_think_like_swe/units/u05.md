## Notes

All ten patterns are new foundations under the existing `readability` topic,
each cross-linked (rel: related_to) to the Ch.1 foundation PAT_make_code_readable
which named descriptive-names, structure, and information-locality as readability
defects. Per PASS_SCHEMA §1, these stay foundation/general/none rather than
specializations because their rules need no language/tool/etc constraint (streams,
named arguments, and TypeScript destructuring are illustrations, not constraints
on the rule).

No AP: chapter 5 is a collection of independent readability techniques, not a
staged workflow. The Ch.1 readability drill plus these patterns and three new
drills cover the practice.

Merges / rejections (no separate rows):
- 5.2.1-5.2.4 (redundant comments, comments-as-substitute, why-comments, high-level
  summaries) folded into one PAT_comment_why_not_what rather than four thin objects.
- 5.5.1-5.5.4 (nesting hard to read, restructure, nesting=doing-too-much, break into
  functions) folded into PAT_minimize_nesting_with_early_returns (early returns +
  extraction are the two composed methods).
- 5.6.1/5.6.2/5.6.4/5.6.5 (unreadable args, named args, no-great-solution, IDE)
  folded into PAT_use_named_arguments_for_readable_calls; 5.6.3 (descriptive types)
  split out as its own pattern since it is a distinct durable decision.
- 5.8.1-5.8.5 (anon good for small, anon hard to read, use named, large anon
  problematic, break up) folded into PAT_use_anonymous_functions_only_when_small.
- The "functional programming", "linters", and "IDE" sidebars are context, captured
  in the relevant patterns' Do/Notes, not standalone objects.

Cross-links resolve against Ch.1 (PAT_make_code_readable, PAT_make_code_hard_to_misuse)
and Ch.2 (PAT_write_functions_as_single_sentences) plus the new objects. Chapter 7
(dedicated types / avoid overly general types) should link back to
PAT_replace_primitives_with_descriptive_types.
