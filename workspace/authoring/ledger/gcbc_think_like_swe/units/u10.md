## Notes

All nine patterns are new foundations under the existing `testing` topic (which
already holds the Ch.1 PAT_design_for_testability), each cross-linked (rel:
related_to) to that foundation or a related earlier pattern. Per PASS_SCHEMA §1
they stay foundation/general/none because their rules need no language/tool
constraint (mocking tool syntax, TDD/BDD/ATDD are illustrations, not constraints).

Merges (no separate rows):
- The five good-test properties (10.2.1-10.2.5) became four patterns: fail-iff-broken
  (accuracy + no flakiness), agnostic-to-implementation, self-explaining (well-explained
  failures + understandable test code merged), and fast-to-run.
- Test doubles (10.4): the reasons-for-a-double became PAT_use_test_double_only_when_needed;
  mocks, stubs, fakes, their two problems (unrealistic tests, tight coupling), and the
  mockist-vs-classicist schools of thought all folded into PAT_prefer_fakes_over_mocks_and_stubs,
  which carries the real > fake > mock/stub preference.
- "Don't mix a functional change with a refactoring" folded into the agnostic pattern's Do.

No AP: chapter 10 is principles; chapter 11 covers the procedural practices and is the
better home for any staged testing workflow.

Cross-links resolve against Ch.1 (design-for-testability), Ch.2 (clean-api), Ch.3
(fail-compile-or-test), Ch.8 (dependency-injection) and the new objects. Chapter 11's
practices (test behaviors not functions, avoid making things visible for testing, test
one behavior at a time, shared setup, assertion matchers) will build on and cross-link
to these principles.
