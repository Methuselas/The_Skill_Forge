## Notes

The nullable-return-as-error-signal material was absorbed as a variant
(VAR_error_signal) of the Ch.2 foundation PAT_prefer_null_safety_or_optionals rather
than a new object: same underlying construct (nullable/optional under null safety),
applied to the context of signaling an error, with the tradeoff that null carries
no failure reason. Recorded per the decision-versus-method recovery check.

Rejected/deferred (no separate rows):
- Magic value / returning -1 as an error signal (listing 4.27): the author
  explicitly defers the detailed treatment to chapter 6 ("we won't discuss them in
  detail here"). Its "don't hide errors" aspect is captured in PAT_dont_hide_errors;
  the standalone magic-value pattern is left for Ch.6 to own, where it is grounded.
- Checked vs unchecked exception as separate techniques: folded into
  PAT_prefer_explicit_error_signaling_for_recoverable_errors (the explicit/implicit
  families) rather than two language-flavored objects; checked exceptions are the
  Java mechanism illustrating "explicit."
- "Recap: Exceptions" (call-stack unwinding) and the asynchronous sidebar are
  definitions; captured in Notes where relevant, not as objects.
- Standard-exception-types tactic (p. 96) folded into the explicit-signaling
  pattern's Notes.

Cross-links resolve against Ch1 (mental-model), Ch2 (null-safety), Ch3
(checks, compile-time enforcement, unmistakable-over-small-print) and new Ch4
objects. Chapter 6 will specialize magic values and the null object pattern —
