## Notes

Placement: all seven new objects went to `foundations/readability`, which already
holds the naming material from gcbc. Nothing here belonged in
`code-comprehension` — the chapter is about writing names, and it opens Part 3 of
the book, which turns from reading and thinking about code to writing it.

**I expected a `replace` here and there isn't one.** This chapter was flagged in
advance as the likeliest place for one, since it lands a full chapter on
`PAT_use_descriptive_names`, a foundation already carrying two variants. On the
evidence it does not supersede it. Feitelson's step 1 reaches Long's
comment-belongs-in-the-name claim independently, which is corroboration rather
than improvement, and the three-step model is a procedure rather than a competing
statement of the same rule — so it became an AP sitting beside the pattern. The
honest disposition is two variants and a new AP, not a replacement, and forcing
one would have destroyed a foundation that four other objects cross-link to.

The two variants divide cleanly by which existing decision they constrain.
`PAT_use_descriptive_names` decides how much to put in a name, so the recall-cost
evidence attaches there. `PAT_follow_a_consistent_coding_style` decides which
convention to follow, so the camel-versus-snake evidence attaches there. Both are
`constraint` basis because neither offers a different method — each narrows when
the foundation's advice applies.

`PAT_treat_bad_names_as_a_defect_search_heuristic` carries `confidence: medium`,
the first object in this source to do so. The association Butler found is
statistically significant, but the chapter spends more space on why causation is
unavailable than on the finding, and three alternative explanations are left open.
A card claiming more than that would misrepresent the source.
