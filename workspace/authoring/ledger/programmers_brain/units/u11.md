## Notes

Placement: all four new objects opened `foundations/working-practice`. None of the
existing topics fit — these are not about comprehending code
(`code-comprehension`), representing a problem (`problem-solving`), building skill
(`deliberate-practice`), or the code itself (`readability`, `code-quality`). They
are about how a working session is organised and how it survives contact with
other people. This is the second new topic in this source, after u06's
`problem-solving`, and it is likely to take u12 and u13 material too.

Subgoal labelling was the closest call. It has independent evidence from
Margulieux, and uses beyond interruption recovery — the labels survive as
documentation, and a senior programmer can design subgoals for others to
implement. That is nearly enough for its own object. It was kept inside the
interruption pattern because the chapter presents it as one of three techniques
for one decision, and splitting it would have left that pattern with a hole where
its third leg was. The additional uses are recorded in the pattern's Notes so a
later unit can promote it if more evidence arrives.

The interruption material deliberately became two patterns rather than one. The
decisions have different owners: preparing is done by the person who will be
interrupted, timing is chosen by the person interrupting. Most teams have habits
about the first and nothing about the second, which is the wrong way round given
that the timing variable belongs entirely to the interrupter.

The variant onto `PAT_comment_why_not_what` is the third variant this source has
placed on a gcbc readability foundation. It survives the self-documenting-code
objection on a narrow ground worth preserving — thought process is not expressible
as code, so no amount of clear code substitutes for recording why an alternative
was rejected.
