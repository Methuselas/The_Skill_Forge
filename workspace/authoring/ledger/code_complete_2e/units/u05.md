Two passages were considered and rejected, both on the u04 test — a Teaching-lane
candidate needs an instructional decision in the source, not a learning phenomenon
the source observes or a practice that merely involves other people.

**Collaborative design (pp. 115-116)** lists seven ways to get another mind onto a
design, and does make a real decision: structured inspection when the goal is
finding defects, looser forms when the goal is generating alternatives. But the
decision is about *review purpose*, not about causing anyone to learn. Nobody in it
is a learner; the co-worker is a second designer. It is collaboration, not
instruction, and its proper home is ch. 21, which this chapter defers to.

**The decomposition stopping rule (p. 112)** — stop when the design seems obvious,
because anything slightly tricky for you now will be a bear for whoever works on it
later — reasons about a future reader's comprehension. That is an audience
consideration inside a design decision, not an instructional one: McConnell says
nothing about what that later person should be taught, shown, or practised on.

This chapter is McConnell teaching design. It is not McConnell teaching how to
teach design, and the distinction held under a deliberate second look.

## Notes

Nineteen candidates, seven kept — six new objects and one variant. The highest
absolute yield so far, but the *rate* is the lowest of the five units, and for a
reason worth recording: this chapter's core material is the same material Good
Code, Bad Code already supplied at greater operational depth. Abstraction,
encapsulation, cohesion, inheritance, contracts, testability, and single-point-of-
control are all covered by existing topics, and McConnell's treatment of them is
explanatory where Long's is procedural. Nine of the twelve rejects fall into that
band. What survived is what Long does not have: the complexity frame the heuristics
serve, the iteration discipline, prototyping, information hiding as a generative
question, design depth, and semantic coupling.

**The one real disagreement between sources so far** is recorded as
`VAR_isolate_the_categories_that_are_historically_volatile` rather than resolved.
Long says do not predict which change will arrive; McConnell says predict the
category. Both are grounded, both are defensible, and a practitioner facing a
component that sits squarely in one of McConnell's seven volatile categories should
see both routes. Preserving it is doctrine — treat each source as one route worth
learning — and collapsing it would have been the easier and worse call.

**Forward pointers.** Binding time (deferred from u02) is still deferred: p. 107
mentions it in passing and §10.6 is its real treatment, so it belongs to u10.
Table-driven methods likewise remain u18. Inheritance depth goes to u06 (ch. 6,
Working Classes), which McConnell explicitly defers to here.
