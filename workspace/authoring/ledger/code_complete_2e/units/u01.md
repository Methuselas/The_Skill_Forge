## Notes

Yield is low — two objects from six candidates — and that is the honest result for
this chapter rather than a shortfall in the read. Chapter 1 is six printed pages of
framing: it defines construction, places it among the other development activities,
argues why it deserves a handbook, and explains how to navigate the book. Three of
the four rejects are motivation or terminology that support the two surviving
decisions; the fourth is table-of-contents guidance. Do not read this unit's count
as a calibration signal for the source. Chapters 5-9 and 20-32 are where the
technique density lives.

One thing to carry forward: this chapter is where McConnell fixes the vocabulary
the rest of the book uses. "Construction" and "programming" are interchangeable
throughout; "coding" is used only when the mechanical sense is meant. Later units
should read his activity names in that fixed sense rather than in the reader's.

### Extraction stance for this source (set 2026-08-14, applies from u01 onward)

Both cards were rewritten after a first draft landed in the wrong register. The
drafts explained and argued the chapter's position — how to justify construction
investment, how to frame the boundary for someone else — rather than stating what
to do when the situation arrives. The rewrite moved both to the execution register:
`lane_fit: skill` rather than `both`, an IF that names a moment in the work rather
than a discussion, and Do items that are actions taken on code.

For the rest of this source:

- Extract for application, not instruction. This lane has no human learner to
  teach; the reader is the executor. `lane_fit: teach` and
  `routing_class: teaching` should be rare to absent here.
- Prefer an IF that fires while building — picking up a request, naming a
  variable, choosing a loop form, reading a failure — over one that fires during
  a discussion about building.
- Reject persuasion material. McConnell writes for a 2004 reader who had to be
  convinced construction mattered; the arguments, the study citations, and the
  industry-practice framing are motivation, not moves. Keep a number only where
  it changes a decision, and keep it inside a card rather than as one.
- The book's checklists and Key Points boxes are the densest execution material
  in each chapter. Mine those before the surrounding prose.
