# Cone-of-Rays Numeric Rule Hold

## Source claim
White's printed p. 70 treats a 60-degree cone as a practical maximum for the flat picture-plane drawing and converts this into a rule that the spectator should be at least about one-and-a-half times the subject height away from a tall building. The same section advises moving the Centre of Vision when the viewer turns through a panorama and models head/picture-plane tilt with oblique perspective.

## PASS disposition
**Concept retained; numeric threshold held for later reconciliation.**

The cumulative perspective state already contains distortion control and Norling's reframe-the-view proposal. White strongly reinforces the idea that a single flat picture plane has a limited comfortable field and that panoramas involve changing view direction. However, the fixed 60-degree / 1.5x-height statement is a source-specific practical rule, not promoted here as a universal camera or lens law.

Reconcile the numeric boundary against Robertson's modern cone-of-vision treatment and the later *Viewpoints* mathematical audit.
