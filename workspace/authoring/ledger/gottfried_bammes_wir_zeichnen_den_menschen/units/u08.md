## Delta map

- VARIANT — **make support contacts deform the whole pose**: a brace, seat, back, floor, elbow, or other weight-bearing contact must change the load path, skeletal relationships, and where appropriate the soft-form response; contact without consequence is decorative.
- VARIANT — **articulate the seated torso as a fold chain**: rib cage/upper torso → abdomen intermediate mass → seat-affected pelvis → thighs, rather than one generic C-curve.
- VARIANT — **reorient reclining figures with longitudinal and transverse axes**: place a projected body mid-axis and selected cross-axis stations, let their spacing show depth, then insert the head/rib-cage/pelvis cores and limbs.
- REINFORCES — Hampton center-of-gravity design, Loomis seated-brace and reclining-measurement variants, Bammes u05 torso deformation, Vilppu total-action analysis, existing overlap/foreshortening/cross-contour architecture, and Stage 4 controlled amplification.
- NON-PROMOTION — Bammes's specific mappings from seated gestures to inner psychological states remain observational/expressive prompts, not factual inference rules about a person's mental state.

## Book reconciliation

This final chapter does what Bammes says it should do: it stops re-teaching isolated anatomy and asks the artist to experience the **interplay of the parts as one unit**. Its strongest surviving deltas are therefore not more anatomy facts, but three whole-figure consequences that were still under-specified in the library: support contacts must visibly carry weight, seated torsos need an articulated fold sequence, and reclining figures benefit from a body-axis/cross-axis depth scaffold before local form.
