# Generate Novel Options by Combining Distant Concepts

## Pattern Rule
**IF** ordinary search is repeating familiar answers or the problem needs a fresh direction
**THEN** deliberately combine the active problem with a sufficiently distant concept, mechanism, structure, image, or context and inspect what new option the interaction produces
**ELSE** keep the current search focused when novelty would distract from a solution that already satisfies the objective.

## Do
- Name the active problem or idea before introducing the distant concept.
- Choose a second concept that contributes a genuinely different structure, behavior, function, mood, or context rather than a superficial decoration.
- Ask what changes when both ideas must coexist in one solution.
- Generate several consequences of the combination instead of stopping at the first literal mash-up.
- Return the result to the domain's normal Control process: constraints, tests, audience, structure, physics, readability, or other acceptance criteria still apply.
- Keep provenance when the borrowed idea comes from a specific source, prior project, or memory record.

## Don't
- Treat novelty as proof of quality.
- Combine two things only because their names sound amusing when the active problem gains nothing from the interaction.
- Preserve every feature of both inputs; extract the relationships that actually generate a useful third idea.
- Skip verification after a surprising result appears.

## Checklist
- The two inputs are meaningfully different.
- The combination changes the solution space rather than merely decorating it.
- At least one new relationship, mechanism, or direction emerged.
- The resulting option has been subjected to domain-specific Control before acceptance.

## Notes
Dodson's "joining two bags" principle is presented as a general creativity mechanism rather than a drawing-only trick. His examples deliberately reach beyond visual art, which is why PASS owns the decision as a metaskill. It is a Search operation: the combination creates candidates; it does not exempt them from later Control.
