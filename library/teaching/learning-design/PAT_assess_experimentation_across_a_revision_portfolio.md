# Assess Experimentation Across a Revision Portfolio

## Pattern Rule
**IF** judging every early attempt as a final product would discourage learners from taking relevant risks or revising after discovery
**THEN** collect work across the learning period and make the main judgment from a curated, revised portfolio that shows development
**ELSE** assess the individual performance directly when it is already the intended terminal demonstration.

## Do
- Make clear which exploratory attempts can change substantially before summative judgment.
- Provide formative response during the period so deferring the main judgment does not mean withholding guidance.
- Require learners to revisit, select, and revise work rather than submitting an untouched chronological pile.
- State criteria that recognize craft growth and purposeful experimentation while still judging the quality of the resulting work.
- Inspect the relationship between early attempts, feedback, decisions, and revised outcomes.

## Don't
- Do not grade every experiment as if its purpose were immediate polish.
- Do not equate risk with success; experimentation must still produce evidence that can be examined and revised.
- Do not let the portfolio become a volume count detached from development.
- Do not postpone criteria until the final submission or rely on personal trust in place of transparent expectations.

## Checklist
- Can learners try a relevant uncertain approach without one weak attempt deciding the final judgment?
- Do they receive usable formative response before portfolio submission?
- Must they select and revise rather than merely collect?
- Do criteria cover both development and present quality?
- Can the evaluator trace meaningful decisions across versions or pieces?

## Notes
Starkey presents a term portfolio as a way for creative-writing learners to take chances and experiment before the instructor evaluates the accumulated work. `VAR_starkey_assess_creative_writing_through_term_portfolio` retains that course-specific application. The portable decision is temporal: when experimentation and revision are learning goals, place the decisive assessment after learners have had enough attempts and response to demonstrate development, without weakening criteria or formative guidance.
