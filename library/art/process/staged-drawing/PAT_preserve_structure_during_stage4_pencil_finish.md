# Preserve Structure During Stage 4 Pencil Finish

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an approved Stage 3 drawing is being completed as Stage 4
**THEN** use the exact approved Stage 3 artifact as the immediate visual anchor, preserve its approved Drawing decisions, and resolve its exploratory mark expression into intentional Finished Pencils
**ELSE** return to the earliest unresolved Drawing stage or fail closed when the exact predecessor cannot be accessed

## Do
- Carry `ROOT = approved Stage 0` and `IMMEDIATE = approved Stage 3`; Stage 4 owns Drawing closure, not a new composition, structure, or design solution.
- Treat canonical identity and tool accessibility as separate checks. If a registered-successor edit requires the predecessor and the exact accepted image is not exposed to the native image tool, stop and recover/re-upload that exact artifact. A re-upload restores access to the same canonical predecessor and inherited lockset; it does not create a new composition or require new approval.
- **Preserve approved decisions, not exploratory marks.** Protect camera, crop, perspective, pose, geometry, major proportions, silhouette decisions, object relationships, anatomy/design solutions, contacts, overlap order, scene inventory, and other ratified Stage 0–3 decisions. Do not preserve a search line, duplicate contour, construction remnant, hatch field, or tonal treatment merely because it exists in Stage 3.
- Resolve the mark field as needed: **delete, consolidate, replace, suppress, clarify, add, or re-express** marks so that each retained mark class has an intentional Drawing job. A large visible mark change is allowed only while the approved predecessor solution remains recoverable.
- Treat edge resolution as a decision, not as a universal hard-outline rule. Final boundaries may be hard, soft, broken, lost, implied, value-defined, hatch-defined, or mass-defined when that treatment is intentional and appropriate to the drawing.
- Establish an intentional information hierarchy. Control line weight, edge sharpness, value, mark density, texture, and omission so information does not compete accidentally. Do not require the focal subject to have the highest visible mark density or distant/subordinate regions to be intrinsically less resolved.
- Inspect articulated chains and functional endpoints after finishing: parent mass → joint → member → endpoint. Check hands, feet, head/neck, prop contacts, weapons, wings, tails, straps, attachments, and other relevant endpoints. These areas require sufficient certainty for Drawing closure, not a mandatory visual treatment.
- Compare the Finished Pencils against both anchors at reduced information. Stage 4 → Stage 3 must recover the accepted Stage 3 solution; further reduction must recover the Stage 0 picture. Distinguish global drift from local mark defects; global drift returns upstream.

## Don't
- Do not enlarge/reposition subjects, change camera/crop, alter major negative spaces, modify perspective/path geometry, change approved anatomy/design, reconnect objects, or add major inventory for a more dramatic drawing.
- Do not interpret “preserve structure” as “work on top of every Stage 3 mark.” Stage 4 may erase or replace exploratory marks when the approved decision underneath them is preserved.
- Do not use darkness, texture, hatch, smooth tone, clean linework, or micro-detail to camouflage unresolved Drawing decisions.
- Do not force one pencil aesthetic: low grayscale, high grayscale, hard contour, soft tonal rendering, sparse marks, and dense marks can all be valid when intentional.
- Do not absorb separately owned Rendering, Ink, Color, Paint, Manga/B&W, or other downstream appearance work merely because the physical medium remains pencil or graphite.
- Do not regenerate from the original prose when an accepted visual predecessor exists.
- Do not substitute a rejected artifact or a visually similar reconstruction when exact-source access is unavailable.
- Do not claim edit lineage or parentage the host did not actually expose.

## Checklist
- Exact approved Stage 3 predecessor identified and available to the native image tool when edit/reference continuity requires it.
- The Finished Pencils still read as the same approved picture before local mark treatment is examined.
- Camera, crop, subject scale/placement, major negative spaces, inventory, path geometry, overlaps, contacts, geometry, anatomy/design decisions, and major proportions remain recoverable.
- Exploratory Stage 3 marks were allowed to change; approved Stage 3 decisions were not silently redesigned.
- Important edges and functional relationships are intentional, whether expressed by line, value, hatch, mass, softness, loss, or implication.
- Information hierarchy is deliberate without assuming “focal = most resolved” or “distant = quieter.”
- The intended successor would not need to finish a Drawing decision before doing its own job.
- No separately owned downstream appearance/medium workflow was absorbed into Drawing Stage 4.
- No tool-level lineage claim exceeds what the host exposes.

## Notes
Stage 4 is the terminal Drawing operation: **Finished Pencils**. It is not a single look. The hard guardrail on substantial mark-level change is successor reducibility: the approved Stage 3 solution must remain recoverable beneath the resolved pencil expression.

The Broken Gate tightening failure still establishes a separate hard rule: **loss of edit-target access does not authorize visual reinterpretation.**

The current Broken Gate positive evidence intentionally separates quality and retention roles: the warm Hooded Warrior is the stronger current Stage 4 quality example, while the clean Spear Runner is fresh-chat retention evidence. Neither reference changes the hard continuity invariant or creates a universal pencil style.
