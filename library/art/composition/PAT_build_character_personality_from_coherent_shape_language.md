# Build Character Personality From Coherent Shape Language

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a character design needs personality to remain coherent across head, body, hair, costume, and accessories
**THEN** Coordinate head, feature, body, hair, costume, and accessory shape tendencies so they reinforce a coherent character attitude

## Do
- Choose a dominant shape tendency or controlled contrast that suits the character.
- Repeat that logic across major design regions without making every part identical.
- Use opposing shape language intentionally when it represents a real character contradiction.

## Don't
- Do not mix unrelated curves, spikes, flats, and diagonals until the character loses a coherent read.

## Checklist
- Major design regions feel related and support the intended personality.

## Notes

**Blair coordinated-proportion variant — `VAR_blair_build_cartoon_type_from_coordinated_proportion_ensemble`.** Build a strong cartoon type by making several large proportional relationships support the same fictional design premise before local detail: head/body ratio, cranial and facial mass, feature placement, neck, torso and pelvis distribution, limb proportions, hands/feet, stance, and silhouette. Test the read in simple masses first, then individualize it; Blair's historical type labels are examples of stylized construction, not rules for inferring personality from real body types.
