# Use Big Medium Small For Hierarchy Rhythm And Depth

## Pattern Rule
**IF** major masses or spaces feel equalized, repetitive, or unclear in hierarchy
**THEN** Organize major masses, spaces, or value groups into unequal large/medium/small relationships to avoid dead repetition and clarify hierarchy

## Do
- Apply the relation to subject masses, negative spaces, value groups, or environmental divisions.
- Let the largest establish dominance, the medium support rhythm, and the smallest punctuate or accent.
- Use scale variation to reinforce depth when appropriate.

## Don't
- Do not force a literal three-object formula.
- Do not distribute dominant masses at nearly equal size by habit.

## Checklist
- The hierarchy survives a thumbnail read.
- Repeated elements vary enough to avoid mechanical equality.

## Notes

The relation is comparative rather than a demand for exactly three groups, and it can recur at nested scales. Check that the smallest accent is still subordinate to the largest structure; multiplying tiny high-contrast marks can collectively overturn the intended hierarchy.
