# Design Vignette as Open Composition with Page Space

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an illustration should live openly on a page rather than inside a fully closed rectangular picture field
**THEN** compose the printed subject mass and the surrounding unprinted/page space as one figure-ground design: decide where the image dissolves, where it anchors to page edges, how open space penetrates the subject, and how detached spots, copy, or subsidiary elements connect back to the primary mass.

## Do
- Decide whether the vignette floats freely, touches one edge, anchors to several edges, or combines an open mass with a more solid rectangular element.
- Design the silhouette of the entire printed mass before polishing internal detail.
- Let page space cut into and around the subject deliberately; treat those openings as active negative shapes, not leftover blank paper.
- Connect small detached spots to the main vignette through direction, rhythm, value, spacing, or repeated shape so they do not feel accidentally scattered.
- Consider copy, product, border, caption, or other page elements when they are part of the actual layout.
- Test the vignette at reproduction size, where weak connecting shapes or over-fragmented edges may disappear.

## Don't
- Do not confuse a vignette with simply fading every edge uniformly.
- Do not crop as if a complete rectangular frame exists when the intended effect depends on open page space.
- Do not scatter isolated details into the margins without a visual relationship to the primary mass.
- Do not make every boundary equally soft; open composition still needs decisive shape design and focal control.
- Do not let decorative edge treatment destroy subject readability or reproduction clarity.

## Checklist
- The combined silhouette of image plus page space reads as an intentional design.
- The viewer can tell which edges dissolve, which anchor, and why.
- Detached spots or subsidiary elements are visually tied to the main mass.
- Negative page space has designed shapes rather than accidental holes.
- The result remains legible at the intended reproduction size.
- The vignette would lose something important if arbitrarily boxed into a complete rectangle.

## Notes
Loomis's vignette pages show floating arrangements, designs tied to one or more page edges, open spaces that connect through the subject, small spots attached to a main vignette, borders, silhouettes, relief-like treatments, and combinations of sketchy open masses with more solid image blocks. The portable principle is that a vignette is an open page composition whose missing rectangular boundary is itself part of the design, not merely a rendering trick at the edges.

`VAR_loomis_choose_background_by_value_range_and_depth_need` adds a selection test: open ground can isolate and clarify a subject, but it can also flatten value relationships or produce a cut-paper effect. Choose the treatment from the subject's value range and depth requirements, and use selective lost edges or interlacing when open page space needs to penetrate the subject more naturally.

`VAR_olofsson_anchor_product_sketch_with_partial_background_field` uses a simple value or color patch as a bounded backdrop. Its edge is not neutral: align it with the product's support and it can read as a table or floor; separate it and the same patch reads more graphically. Position the field according to the spatial implication you actually want.
