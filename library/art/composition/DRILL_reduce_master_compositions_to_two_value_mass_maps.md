# Reduce Master Compositions to Two-Value Mass Maps

## Practice Task
Choose several strong finished pictures and reduce each one to a broad two-value map that preserves the picture's major dark-light organization while discarding modeling, texture, local detail, and stylistic brushwork.

## Target Skill
See and compare whole-picture value structure as an arrangement of large masses rather than as a collection of shaded objects.

## Setup
Select three to five finished pictures with visibly different subjects or styles. Prepare small boxes matching each picture's outer proportion and use only one dark plus the untouched light ground.

## Instructions
1. Establish the frame and identify the few largest light and dark regions before making any marks.
2. Block the dark masses broadly and keep small internal details out unless they materially change the large pattern.
3. Ignore gradients, local modeling, texture, edge finesse, and decorative marks; translate them only when they merge into a mass that matters at thumbnail scale.
4. Reduce every chosen picture by the same rule so the resulting studies are comparable.
5. Place the reductions side by side and compare how differently the pictures distribute dark, light, balance, concentration, and empty space.
6. State what makes each mass plan distinct without turning the comparison into a formula to copy into unrelated pictures.

## Success Check
- Each reduction still carries the recognizable large value organization of its source picture at small size.
- Local shading and texture have not been mistaken for major compositional masses.
- The studies reveal materially different dark-light plans rather than being normalized into one preferred scheme.
- The learner can describe the structural difference between the pictures without claiming that one recurring mass pattern is a universal rule.

## Common Failures
- Copying outlines and details instead of reducing the picture to masses.
- Preserving every shadow change, which turns the exercise back into rendering.
- Forcing several different pictures into the same favorite black-and-white arrangement.
- Treating the exercise as permission to imitate a master's mass plan rather than as a way to recognize how different pictures solve the problem.

## Notes
This is an analytical counterpart to inventing tonal plans. Instead of recomposing one subject under several value organizations, the learner holds the reduction method constant and reverse-engineers several completed works. The constraint exposes each picture's particular mass structure and makes comparison possible without confusing it with surface finish.

`VAR_dow_map_relief_through_two_value_shadow_shapes` carries the same reduction into relief and sculptural study, where the chosen view and light convert projection and recess into a temporary two-value mass organization.
