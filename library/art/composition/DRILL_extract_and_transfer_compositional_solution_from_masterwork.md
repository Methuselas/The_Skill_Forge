# Extract and Transfer a Compositional Solution From Masterwork

## Practice Task
Choose a strong composition, isolate one concrete mechanism that makes it work, reduce that mechanism to a simple structural diagram, and apply the same mechanism to several unrelated subjects.

## Target Skill
Separate transferable design logic from literal imagery and style, then recombine that logic in new compositions.

## Setup
Select one finished work whose composition has a clear effect worth studying. Prepare small frames for structural diagrams and several unrelated subject prompts or reference sets for transfer attempts.

## Instructions
1. Describe the compositional effect in concrete visible terms rather than with praise such as dynamic, beautiful, or balanced.
2. Identify one mechanism that plausibly produces that effect: connected value pattern, focal isolation, asymmetrical placement, crop, directional convergence, visual-weight distribution, interval rhythm, or another specific relation.
3. Reduce the donor work to the minimum diagram that still shows that mechanism while discarding subject detail and surface style.
4. Rebuild the mechanism with a materially different subject whose objects, setting, and mark language do not permit literal copying.
5. Make at least two additional transfers so the mechanism has to adapt rather than becoming a disguised tracing of the first result.
6. Compare the transfers with the donor only at the structural level and note which parts of the mechanism remained stable versus which had to change for the new subject.

## Success Check
- The extracted mechanism can be named and diagrammed without referring to the donor's specific objects.
- The transfer compositions differ materially in subject and surface treatment while preserving the useful relation.
- Copying the donor's literal arrangement is unnecessary for the effect to survive.
- The learner can explain where the mechanism stops being useful instead of turning it into a universal formula.

## Common Failures
- Copying the master's objects, crop, or stylistic manner and mistaking resemblance for learning.
- Naming a vague quality without locating the visible relation that creates it.
- Extracting too many mechanisms at once, making it impossible to know what transferred.
- Treating one successful precedent as a mandatory composition rule.

## Notes
Strong precedents are most useful when they expand the repertoire of solutions rather than the repertoire of imitations. This drill converts admiration into a transferable structural move: isolate what the composition actually accomplishes, strip away the donor-specific surface, and prove the understanding by rebuilding the relation elsewhere.
