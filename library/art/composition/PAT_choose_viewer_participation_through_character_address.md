# Choose Viewer Participation Through Character Address

## Pattern Rule
**IF** character gaze, gesture, pose, or expression can make the audience feel either outside the scene or directly addressed by it
**THEN** decide the viewer's intended narrative relationship first, then coordinate character address so the audience reads as witness, participant, target, confidant, or another deliberate role
**ELSE** keep character attention inside the depicted event when no direct audience relationship is needed.

## Do
- Decide whether the viewer should witness a self-contained event or be actively acknowledged by the characters.
- Keep gaze and gesture inside the dramatic world when characters should attend to one another, an object, a threat, a task, or an off-frame event.
- Use direct eye contact, pointing, frontal gesture, or outward address deliberately when the viewer should become a participant, target, confidant, customer, or confronted presence.
- Coordinate head direction, eyes, hands, torso, and staging so the viewer relationship is consistent rather than carried by one isolated cue.
- Treat fourth-wall breaks as narrative devices that can support intimacy, confrontation, comedy, discomfort, appeal, or other intentional effects.

## Don't
- Do not let a character accidentally stare at the viewer when the story requires attention elsewhere.
- Do not ban direct address as a universal rule; use it when the intended relationship depends on acknowledging the audience.
- Do not rely on eye direction alone when the rest of the pose contradicts the intended social relationship.
- Do not confuse camera placement with viewer relationship: two scenes can share the same viewpoint while characters address the viewer very differently.

## Checklist
- The viewer's intended role in relation to the scene is explicit.
- Character gaze and gesture support that role.
- Any direct address is purposeful rather than an accidental pose artifact.
- Internal character interactions remain readable when the viewer is meant to stay outside the event.
- The audience relationship strengthens rather than contradicts the image's story job.

## Notes
Loomis's camera-dramatics discussion distinguishes scenes that play among the depicted characters from images that deliberately address the audience. His categorical warnings against looking at the viewer are too rigid to preserve as law. The durable decision is to choose the audience relationship consciously: inward gaze and gesture maintain a witnessed dramatic world, while direct address intentionally makes the viewer part of the communication.
