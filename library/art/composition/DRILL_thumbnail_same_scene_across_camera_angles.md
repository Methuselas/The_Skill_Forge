# Thumbnail the Same Scene Across Camera Angles

## Practice Task
Draw six small thumbnails of one unchanged story beat from six different camera positions, then select the view that best serves a named effect.

## Target Skill
Choosing viewpoint deliberately instead of accepting the first drawable angle.

## Setup
Write one simple beat in a single sentence and one intended effect such as urgency, menace, intimacy, scale, or calm. Keep both fixed for the entire exercise.

## Instructions
1. Make six thumbnails without changing who is present, what is happening, or the essential location.
2. Change camera height, distance, lateral position, and/or tilt enough that each thumbnail represents a genuinely different view rather than a small crop adjustment.
3. Keep the drawings cheap: broad silhouettes, major masses, and simple perspective cues only.
4. Rank the six views for the named effect and circle the strongest two.
5. Compare those two for action clarity and spatial constructibility, then choose one and state why it wins.

## Success Check
- All six thumbnails depict the same beat, so the camera rather than the story is being tested.
- The selected view has a specific dramatic advantage that can be named.
- The chosen camera still leaves enough spatial information to construct the scene coherently.

## Common Failures
- Changing pose, action, or story content so much that the thumbnails no longer isolate viewpoint.
- Making six near-identical eye-level crops.
- Choosing the strangest angle instead of the one that best serves the intended effect.

## Notes
The Marvel chapter teaches camera choice by direct comparison: ordinary and stronger views of the same situation are placed side by side so the reader can feel what the angle changes. This drill turns that comparison into a repeatable Stage 0 search.
