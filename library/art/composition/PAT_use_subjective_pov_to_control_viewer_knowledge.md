# Use Subjective Pov To Control Viewer Knowledge

## Pattern Rule
**IF** the audience should experience a beat through what one character can or cannot see or know
**THEN** Use a participant’s visual access, obstruction, instability, or limited information to make the audience share that character’s perception

## Do
- Decide whose knowledge the viewer should inhabit.
- Restrict or distort the frame according to that viewpoint when it strengthens identification or uncertainty.
- Return to objective framing when the story needs broader knowledge.

## Don't
- Do not label a shot subjective merely because the camera is near a character.

## Checklist
- The framing changes what the viewer can know or feel, not only where the camera sits.

## Notes

Anchor the subjective restriction to a specific perceiver and condition—position, obstruction, attention, impairment, or uncertainty. When the viewpoint changes hands or returns to an objective frame, make the transition legible so limited knowledge is not mistaken for missing continuity.
