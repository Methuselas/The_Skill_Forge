# Visualize Unillustrated Story Passages as Small Scene Roughs

## Practice Task
Choose a prose passage that has no illustration available to you and turn it into several tiny scene roughs that differ in narrative interpretation, not merely camera angle.

## Target Skill
Converting written story material into self-generated visual narrative by identifying the story beat, selecting what to reveal, and inventing action, relationship, environment, and plausible specifics without borrowing an existing pictorial solution.

## Setup
Choose a short story passage with enough surrounding text to understand the characters, setting, event, and information boundary. Avoid looking at existing adaptations or illustrations until the exercise is complete.

## Instructions
1. Read enough context to state the central beat, mood, characters, setting, and what the audience should or should not know at that moment.
2. Write two or three different visual interpretations of the passage in one sentence each before choosing camera or rendering style.
3. Make several very small scene roughs for those interpretations using only enough drawing to prove action, spatial relationship, and hierarchy.
4. Include environment, interaction, or a plausible secondary incident when it strengthens the story; reject solutions that reduce the passage to a single inert figure without narrative reason.
5. Keep at least two alternatives genuinely different in what aspect of the passage they emphasize, not merely in crop, lens, or pose.
6. Compare the roughs for story clarity, information control, character behavior, mood, and compositional potential.
7. Develop one promising rough farther only after the alternatives have been compared.

## Success Check
- The roughs can be traced back to the actual passage rather than generic genre imagery.
- At least two solutions emphasize meaningfully different narrative interpretations.
- Action, relationship, setting, or consequence makes the image function as a story scene rather than a neutral figure study.
- No solution depends on copying an existing adaptation or illustration.
- The selected rough preserves the intended story information boundary.

## Common Failures
- Drawing the first obvious noun from the passage instead of visualizing an event or relationship.
- Treating a new camera angle as a new interpretation when the narrative proposition is unchanged.
- Adding invented drama that contradicts character, setting, causality, or later story facts.
- Rendering one option much farther than the others and mistaking finish for narrative strength.
- Looking up an existing illustration before completing the independent interpretation pass.

## Notes
Practice independent story interpretation on passages that do not already carry an established illustration. Begin with small pencil conceptions and develop only the promising ones so the prose is transformed through the artist's own selection and staging rather than inherited from an existing visual answer.
