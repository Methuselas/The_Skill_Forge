# Refresh Visual Judgment With Mirror and Inversion

## Pattern Rule
**IF** prolonged familiarity with a drawing makes proportion, symmetry, perspective, or contrast problems hard to see
**THEN** temporarily mirror or invert the image to disrupt habituation, inspect the changed read, then return to normal orientation before deciding what to correct
**ELSE** judge directly while the drawing still reads freshly enough for reliable comparison

## Do
- Mirror a drawing when paired features, center alignment, silhouette balance, ellipse relationships, or hand-drawn perspective have become visually familiar.
- Rotate the image 180 degrees when judging the overall distribution and balance of contrast rather than subject meaning.
- Use the transformed view to locate suspicious relationships, then verify them again in the normal view before editing.
- Repeat the check during long development when a fresh read has again been lost.

## Don't
- Do not treat every difference noticed after a flip as an error; the transformed image is an inspection aid, not a new target.
- Do not correct only the mirrored copy and assume the normal orientation will automatically improve.
- Do not use inversion as a substitute for construction checks when the problem can be measured or perspectively proven.

## Checklist
- The transformed view exposes relationships that were difficult to notice in the familiar orientation.
- Suspected symmetry or perspective drift is confirmed in the normal view before correction.
- Contrast inspection is based on large value distribution rather than getting distracted by subject recognition.
- Repeated checks improve judgment without creating oscillating corrections from one orientation to another.

## Notes
A drawing can become perceptually stale simply because it has been looked at for too long. Mirroring changes the familiar directional pattern while preserving the underlying geometry, which makes bilateral drift and awkward perspective easier to notice. Turning the image upside down weakens semantic reading and can make the page's contrast distribution easier to assess as an abstract arrangement. The useful move is not the transformation itself but the temporary reset in visual judgment.
