# Generate Product Forms From Ambiguous Shape Seeds

## Practice Task
Generate an ambiguous primitive arrangement or abstract line mass before deciding what product it represents, then discover a coherent product form inside that seed.

## Target Skill
Escaping familiar product archetypes by interpreting open-ended spatial material before function and silhouette are fully fixed.

## Setup
Create several small seeds. For half, combine a few simple volumes such as wedges, cylinders, blocks, or tubes in arbitrary proportions and orientations. For the rest, make compact abstract doodles or masses with no predetermined product identity.

## Instructions
1. Inspect each seed spatially before naming it. Identify axes, overlaps, directional tendencies, and possible surface turns.
2. For a promising seed, list or sketch at least two plausible product readings when more than one interpretation is available.
3. Choose one reading and clarify only the geometry necessary to make its volume coherent; preserve the unusual relationship that made the seed interesting.
4. Add a small number of functional or identifying features so the discovered object can be read without collapsing back into a generic archetype.
5. Reject any feature that merely decorates the form without strengthening function, orientation, or identity.
6. Repeat from a different seed and compare how much of each final design came from discovery rather than from a preselected product silhouette.

## Success Check
- The final concept retains a distinctive spatial relationship traceable to the ambiguous seed.
- The object becomes functionally legible without simply reverting to the most familiar standard product shape.
- Added details clarify the discovered form instead of burying it.
- Different seeds produce materially different design directions rather than cosmetic variations of one archetype.

## Common Failures
- Naming the product before the seed is drawn and then forcing the seed to imitate the expected silhouette.
- Treating the initial doodle as sacred even when its geometry cannot be made coherent.
- Adding many product-signaling details before the larger spatial idea has been resolved.
- Producing only one interpretation of every ambiguous seed and mistaking first association for discovery.

## Notes
The exercise uses ambiguity as an anti-fixation device. A simple volume arrangement or shapeless doodle can suggest relationships that would be unlikely to appear if the task began with a fully named product and its conventional silhouette. The goal is not random novelty; it is to let an unexpected spatial proposition arrive first, then subject it to enough construction and functional clarification that it can survive as a design.
