# Match Sketch Resolution to Design Role and Audience

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a sketch must support a particular design decision or communicate with a particular audience
**THEN** choose its viewpoint, information density, finish, and explanatory detail from that role and reader instead of giving every sketch the same level of polish
**ELSE** keep the sketch at the cheapest resolution that still answers the active design question

## Do
- State what the drawing must help decide before choosing how much information to include.
- Keep early ideation numerous and inexpensive enough that alternatives can still change freely.
- Increase legibility, selected detail, and supporting views as the sketch moves from private search toward shared evaluation or technical discussion.
- Translate the same design differently for different readers: emphasize interaction and consequence for a nontechnical stakeholder, but expose structure, sections, or construction logic when engineering decisions are at stake.
- Let visible construction, extended guides, or roughness remain when they truthfully signal that the design is still open to revision.
- Use persuasive finish selectively when the job is to convey desirability, mood, or character; do not confuse persuasion with maximum information.
- In neutral explanatory work, suppress seductive finish that could bias an honest comparison between unresolved alternatives.

## Don't
- Do not polish every sketch to presentation level before the design question has earned that cost.
- Do not make an unresolved proposal look frozen merely because the drawing can be cleaned up.
- Do not assume the same drawing will communicate equally well to designers, engineers, clients, users, and management.
- Do not let persuasive surface finish obscure missing functional, structural, or dimensional information when those are the actual decision criteria.

## Checklist
- The drawing has a named job: search, compare, explain, coordinate, persuade, or document a decision.
- The chosen viewpoint and supporting views expose the information required for that job.
- The amount of finish matches how settled the design actually is.
- The intended reader can find the relevant information without decoding unnecessary drawing technique.
- A neutral comparison does not favor one concept merely because it received more polish.

## Notes
Design sketches are not one uniform artifact progressing toward a single ideal finish. Their useful form changes with the decision they serve and with who must read them. A private search sketch can remain fragmentary; a concept-selection sheet needs fair comparison and legibility; engineering communication may need sections or construction cues; a persuasive image can spend effort on mood and character while omitting irrelevant detail. Visual finish also communicates status: rough construction can invite revision, while a fully resolved rendering can imply decisions are already settled. Treat that implication as part of the communication design.
