# Search Product Silhouette in Profile Before Volumetric Development

## Pattern Rule
**IF** the active product-design question is primarily silhouette, proportion, or major graphic division and perspective depth would slow comparison
**THEN** search several alternatives in profile or another near-orthographic view first, select the strongest flat relationship, then develop its depth and sectional form in spatial views
**ELSE** begin directly in perspective when the design question depends on three-dimensional transitions from the start

## Do
- Use the simple view to compare outer contour, wheelbase or span, major mass proportion, openings, and large divisions quickly.
- Keep the alternatives inexpensive enough that noticeably different silhouettes can be tested before one is selected.
- Carry the chosen profile into a three-quarter or perspective view to solve depth, section curvature, surface transition, and attachment.
- Preserve the character-bearing silhouette relationship while allowing the spatial development to correct anything the flat view could not reveal.

## Don't
- Do not mistake a convincing side silhouette for a solved three-dimensional product.
- Do not force every spatial transition to obey a flat profile if the later perspective construction reveals impossible geometry.
- Do not use profile-first search when the defining idea is itself a depth relationship, twist, or spatial junction hidden in that view.

## Checklist
- The profile alternatives differ in meaningful silhouette or proportion rather than cosmetic details.
- A selected profile can be carried into a coherent spatial construction.
- The three-dimensional development preserves the useful character of the chosen flat study.
- Depth and sectional decisions are solved after selection rather than merely inferred from the side view.

## Notes
A simple projection can reduce search cost when the design problem is mainly two-dimensional. The advantage is not that profile is more truthful than perspective, but that it temporarily removes depth variables so silhouette and proportion can be compared faster. The selected result becomes a constraint for later volumetric work, not a substitute for it.
