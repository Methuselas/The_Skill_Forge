# Preserve an Expressive Seed Sketch Through Development

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an early sketch has been selected because its silhouette, proportion, rhythm, asymmetry, gesture, motif, or other dominant relationship carries character that the developed design is meant to retain
**THEN** identify those specific character-bearing relationships, keep the seed sketch visible as a development reference, and test later construction and refinement against those anchors while solving the design more precisely
**ELSE** allow later development to replace the early sketch freely rather than preserving it merely because it came first

## Do
- Name the few relationships that make the seed worth preserving before technical development begins; preserve those relationships, not every accidental mark.
- Keep the seed available beside later drawings so refinement can be compared against the original character instead of relying on memory.
- Let construction, proportion checks, mechanics, ergonomics, and manufacturable detail become more precise while repeatedly checking whether the selected expressive anchors still read.
- When a later solution must depart from the seed, change only what the stronger constraint requires and then re-establish as much of the intended character as the new condition permits.
- Use the seed as evidence during development: a later drawing is not automatically better simply because it is cleaner, more symmetrical, or more technically resolved.

## Don't
- Do not preserve mistakes, impossible mechanics, unsafe ergonomics, or arbitrary stroke accidents as if spontaneity made them requirements.
- Do not freeze the entire first sketch; a seed is a character reference, not a ban on correction or redesign.
- Do not sanitize asymmetry, proportion, rhythm, or silhouette automatically just because a cleaner construction is easier to model or render.
- Do not invoke the seed after the design intent has deliberately changed; choose or make a new reference when the target character itself changes.

## Checklist
- The reason this seed was selected can be named in observable visual terms.
- Later construction resolves real spatial and technical problems instead of simply tracing the seed.
- At least the intended silhouette, proportion, rhythm, asymmetry, gesture, motif, or equivalent character anchor remains recognizable after refinement.
- Necessary departures from the seed can be tied to a stronger design constraint rather than unconscious cleanup.
- The final developed form preserves the chosen character without preserving defects that make the design false, unusable, or unresolved.

## Notes
Early ideation drawings can contain a compact character that becomes weaker when each later pass replaces spontaneous relationships with cleaner but more generic ones. The useful discipline is selective continuity: decide what in the chosen sketch is actually carrying the design's identity, then keep that evidence present while the form becomes more exact. A radical workflow may follow the seed very closely, but the transferable rule is narrower — technical development should refine the chosen idea without automatically erasing the visual relationships for which that idea was selected.
