# Translate Source Form Language Across Scale and Product Type

## Practice Task
Analyze a visually distinctive source, reduce it to a reusable spatial form language, then apply that language to two substantially different design types or scales.

## Target Skill
Separating transferable structural relationships from literal contour copying and preserving those relationships through viewpoint, function, and scale changes.

## Setup
Choose one source with distinctive structure or rhythm: an animal, plant, machine, natural object, vehicle, or built form. Prepare at least two source views when available.

## Instructions
1. Study the source from more than one view and identify repeated relationships such as taper, segmentation, branching, shell overlap, directional thrust, proportion, or mass hierarchy.
2. Redraw those relationships as a simplified intermediate construction that omits source-specific texture and surface decoration.
3. Rotate or redraw the abstraction from a new viewpoint. Repair any part that only worked as a flat silhouette in the source view.
4. Translate the same abstraction into one small or hand-scale design and one materially different application such as furniture, vehicle, architecture, or another large-scale form.
5. Adapt function and proportions while preserving the chosen form-language relationships rather than copying the source outline.
6. Add scale cues appropriate to each application so the two results do not read as the same object merely enlarged or reduced.

## Success Check
- The intermediate abstraction survives a viewpoint change without depending on the original contour.
- Both designs visibly share a structural language while serving different functions.
- The source remains an influence rather than a literal copied silhouette.
- Each result communicates its intended physical scale through proportion, detail, viewpoint, or context.

## Common Failures
- Copying a recognizable outline and calling the result abstraction.
- Keeping ornamental texture while missing the source's deeper mass or sectional relationships.
- Producing two applications that differ only in size or label.
- Losing the selected form language once practical details are added.
- Designing an abstraction that cannot be reconstructed from another viewpoint.

## Notes
The useful bridge between reference and invention is an intermediate spatial abstraction. If the extracted idea can be redrawn from another view and can survive transfer into unrelated functions, it is probably a genuine form relationship rather than a traced contour. Changing scale is part of the test because detail density, context, and viewpoint must make the translated design belong to its new physical world.
