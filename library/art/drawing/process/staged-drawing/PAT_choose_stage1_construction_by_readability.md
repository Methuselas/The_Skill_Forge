# Choose Stage 1 Construction by Readability

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an approved Stage 0 composition is entering Stage 1
**THEN** choose blocking, scribbling, or a hybrid as the artist; construct each important subject only as far as needed for the artist to read the plan and build Stage 2 without guessing
**ELSE** preserve the active stage and do not force a construction ritual merely because one method is familiar

## Do
- Treat Stage 1 as a decision stage, not a cleanliness contest. The acceptance question is whether the artist can read the plan and build Stage 2 without guessing at important relationships.
- Choose the method artistically unless the user explicitly requests one. Use blocking when placement, perspective, inventory, proportion, or drift control dominates; use scribbling when gesture, rhythm, organic movement, or discovery dominates; combine them when the subject benefits from both.
- Follow the generic figure order **Torso → Legs → Arms → Head**. Rare deviations are allowed only when the actual support mechanics or an applicable Skill Card justify them.
- Account for the full figure through obstructions, especially in multi-figure scenes. The visible drawing may crop or hide parts, but Stage 1 should establish the complete positional body, its support, and its contact points.
- Include a horizon or eye-level line whenever it will materially support perspective, figure scaling, repeated objects, vehicles, architecture, or later depth construction.
- Spend construction selectively. A distant shelf may be one rectangle; a foreground car, spaceship, building, machine, creature, or other principal subject may need multiple nested primitives, axes, contact points, and perspective landmarks.
- Preserve the approved camera, crop, composition, action, major placements, negative spaces, broad value proposition, and story. Dream locally into better structure without dreaming sideways into another picture.
- Accept a heavier Stage 1 when the added lines resolve uncertainty rather than prematurely rendering Stage 2 masses or Stage 3 surfaces.

## Don't
- Do not impose uniform sparseness across the page. Different subjects carry different structural risk and deserve different construction density.
- Do not equate artistic freedom with recomposition. Method, notation, and local structural invention remain open; the approved picture remains the root.
- Do not insist on the fewest lines when additional construction is needed for perspective, multiple figures, hidden forms, vehicles, architecture, or complex machinery.
- Do not develop anatomy, costume, materials, polished contour, lighting, shadow, texture, or resolved object surfaces merely to make Stage 1 look impressive.
- Do not start a fresh composition for a continuation stage. Use the approved Stage 0 artifact as the controlling visual parent whenever the host supports registered editing.

## Checklist
- The artist chose blocking, scribbling, or a hybrid for a subject-specific reason.
- The full figure is accounted for where figure continuity matters.
- The horizon or eye level is present when later perspective will depend on it.
- Principal non-figure subjects contain enough structure for Stage 2 without guessing.
- Camera, crop, placements, action, and negative spaces still match the approved Stage 0 root.
- Stage 2 can be visualized and built directly from the construction.
- The page contains construction information, not premature rendering.

## Notes
Stage 1 is structurally sufficient, not uniformly simple. An editor should be able to read the plan and trust the artist to continue. The artist decides how much each element needs.

Use the bidirectional test: **dream forward into Stage 2 masses, then collapse those masses back into the same Stage 1 anchors and primitives.**
