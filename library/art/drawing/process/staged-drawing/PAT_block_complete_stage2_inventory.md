# Block the Complete Stage 2 Inventory

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an approved Stage 1 plan is entering Stage 2
**THEN** expand its anchors into the minimum connected solid masses needed to contain every subject, object, effect, and structural feature intended for Stage 3
**ELSE** do not invent missing Stage 3 content later and call it refinement

## Do
- Treat Stage 2 as the building phase. Convert Stage 1 notation into connected volume without redesigning the composition.
- Include every element that must exist in Stage 3 at its minimum block level. A later figure, building, car, effect, prop, attachment, support, or major design feature must already have a Stage 2 mass or structural placeholder.
- Build the figure in **Torso → Legs → Arms → Head** order unless a rare support-specific exception applies. Use a rib-cage mass, directional pelvic wedge, tapered limb forms, joint connections, and simple hand and foot masses.
- Expand environmental notation directly: boxes gain thickness, circles become spheres, rectangles become tubes or cabinets, tripod lines gain support volume, and cylinders gain overlap and end orientation.
- Preserve Stage 1 centers, axes, proportions, contacts, horizon, perspective relationships, overlap order, and negative spaces.
- Use draw-through, cross-contours, taper, joint interlock, overlap, foreshortening, and support to make the masses readable.
- Use only minimal tone when line alone cannot explain an overlap, turning plane, contact, or difficult depth relationship. Treat that tone as structural notation, not illumination.
- Allow an optional 2A primary-mass pass and 2B correction pass when the artist finds it useful, but do not turn them into mandatory approval gates.

## Don't
- Do not add a Stage 3 element for the first time after Stage 2 has been approved.
- Do not migrate, multiply, replace, or repurpose approved subjects and objects while giving them volume.
- Do not add anatomy, facial identity, costume folds, hair rendering, materials, texture, dramatic lighting, cast shadows, atmosphere, decorative hatching, or polished surface design.
- Do not repair a Stage 1 placement, inventory, pose, proportion, or perspective failure silently. Return to Stage 1, correct it, and rebuild.
- Do not let optional 2A and 2B subdivisions become a fixed ritual. The artist may solve Stage 2 in one pass.

## Checklist
- Every intended Stage 3 element already exists as a readable Stage 2 mass or structural block.
- The Stage 1 plan can be recovered by reducing the masses to their anchors and primitives.
- Figure masses connect and bear weight without anatomy or finish.
- Major objects have enough volume and support to be developed specifically in Stage 3.
- Shading is absent except for a necessary structural accent.
- Camera, crop, perspective, placements, overlaps, and negative spaces remain unchanged.
- Stage 3 can begin without inventing a missing subject or relocating a block.

## Notes
Stage 1 says where things go. Stage 2 states what solid forms exist there. Stage 3 decides what those forms specifically become.

The governing rule is: **if it will be in Stage 3, it must already be in Stage 2 at the minimum block level.**
