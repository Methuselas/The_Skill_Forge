# Commit Stage 3 Form Realization Against the Thumbnail

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an approved Stage 2 block-in is entering Stage 3
**THEN** use that exact artifact as the controlling edit parent, realize every block as its intended specific subject, keep the drawing working and line-dominant rather than presentation-finished, and repeatedly compare the developing drawing with the approved thumbnail
**ELSE** do not treat a fresh illustration of the same verbal premise as continuity

## Do
- Treat Stage 3 as the first serious realization pass and the principal form-commitment stage. This is where a person becomes a person, a building becomes a building, a car becomes a car, and a named effect becomes its actual designed effect.
- Expect this stage to carry the most drawing labor. Use the lines needed to resolve anatomy, form transitions, object-specific structure, architecture, machinery, clothing, effects, and selective surface information.
- Dream most here, but exercise the strongest control. Invent locally inside the approved masses; do not dream sideways into another composition or another story.
- Use the exact approved Stage 2 artifact as the registered edit target. A continuation Stage 3 should not be authorized as fresh generation when parent linkage can be preserved.
- Keep the approved Stage 0 thumbnail visible as a composition compass. Compare camera, crop, dominant gesture, large shapes, negative spaces, focal hierarchy, broad value grouping, and scene identity throughout the pass.
- Refine silhouettes, interconnections, contours, anatomy, design, mechanisms, and object identity while preserving the Stage 2 mass arrangement.
- Decide where later detail will concentrate and where it will remain quiet. For pencil or line-dominant drawing, use sparse local shading, hatching, or simple light-direction arrows only as needed to explain the intended form and lighting direction; leave comprehensive values, major detail, material finish, atmosphere, and texture for Stage 4.
- Permit local changes that clarify or improve the realization, but keep the overall thumbnail intact. A change that alters a frozen composition, pose, mass, overlap, or inventory must return to its owning stage.
- Judge the image reduced as well as close-up. The serious drawing must still carry the thumbnail's central proposition when detail is mentally removed.

## Don't
- Do not generate a new observatory, vehicle, building, figure arrangement, or scene merely because the verbal subject is unchanged.
- Do not move or repurpose Stage 2 blocks to make a more exciting local illustration.
- Do not add major subjects, props, effects, architecture, or furniture that were absent from Stage 2.
- Do not allow detail, texture, lighting, or atmosphere to conceal structural drift.
- Do not postpone specific form identity or the intended focal/detail locations to Stage 4. Do not, however, force Stage 3 to carry Stage 4-level values, materials, atmosphere, texture, or tertiary detail.
- Do not approve Stage 3 merely because it is impressive at full size. It must remain the same picture when compared against the thumbnail and parent block-in.

## Checklist
- The actual Stage 2 artifact is the edit parent and parentage is verifiable.
- Every Stage 2 block has become a specific intended form without migration or replacement.
- No major Stage 3 subject appears without a Stage 2 predecessor.
- The camera, crop, action, placements, overlaps, negative spaces, focal hierarchy, and scene identity still match the approved lineage.
- Anatomy, object design, contours, and selected details are serious enough to support final finish.
- The intended light direction and focal/detail locations are readable without a fully finished value or shadow treatment.
- A reduced or blurred comparison still resembles the approved thumbnail.
- The composition is strong enough that Stage 4 can finish it rather than rescue it.

## Notes
Stage 3 is the baking phase. Stage 4 is icing and decoration.

Once Stage 3 is approved, the realized shapes, designed forms, anatomy, clothing, object identity, major contours, and intended detail locations are committed. Stage 4 completes the current medium. Major structural revision after that point is a return, not a finishing adjustment.

`VAR_hda_u02_stop_anatomy_at_surface_form_threshold` adds Hogarth's artistic-anatomy ceiling: when internal muscle and bone description begins to interfere with the readable living surface, stop diagramming the parts and preserve the surface form and interrelated masses in motion. Use it to keep a serious anatomy pass from becoming an anatomical inventory; do not invoke it when the missing structure is exactly what prevents the pose, attachment, landmark, or teaching target from reading.
