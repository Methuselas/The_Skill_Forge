# Build a Comic Page From Assigned Beats to Pencils

## Objective
Carry a page whose panel beats are already assigned from a whole-page action skeleton into coherent finished pencils without letting one locally finished panel hide problems in the sequence.

## Steps / Flow
1. **Read the page as a sequence before drawing.** List what must happen in each assigned panel and what the reader must understand as the action moves from one panel to the next. This procedure begins after the story has already been divided into panel beats; it does not yet teach full comics pacing or script breakdown.
2. **Stage 1 — lay out the entire page at once.** Establish the panel arrangement and rough every panel's characters and action with stick figures, action centerlines, simple direction cues, and only enough environment to understand placement. Judge the page-wide flow before finishing any single drawing.
3. **Check continuity across the skeleton.** Confirm who is where, what direction the action travels, what changes between panels, and whether each panel hands the reader clearly to the next. Correct the page while the figures are still cheap to move.
4. **Stage 2 — build the whole page with masses.** Convert the page's stick figures into spheres, boxes, cylinders, and other simple forms. Draw through hidden structure wherever attachment, overlap, foreshortening, or placement actually needs proof; do not finish one panel while the rest remain unresolved skeletons.
5. **Stage 3 — flesh out the figures and settings.** Develop specific anatomy, costume, props, and environment from the accepted masses while preserving the action centerlines and panel-to-panel relationships established earlier.
6. **Review each panel as design inside the page.** Check that the major shape pattern and viewpoint of each panel are readable, and that adjacent panels do not fall into accidental sameness of scale, centering, or camera when variation would improve the sequence.
7. **Complete the pencil handoff without redesigning the page late.** The finished pencils should reveal the same sequence that worked in the page skeleton. Lettering, balloon placement, advanced pacing, page turns, and broader comics grammar remain later comic-study responsibilities unless another source explicitly supplies them.

## Notes
Marvel demonstrates the method on one six-panel Captain Britain page and then gives a second six-panel scenario as practice. The durable contribution is page-wide staged construction: the first draft exists only to place action and characters across the whole sequence; the next pass builds primitive masses; the next fleshes those forms into pencils. The book tells the learner to draw the entire figure even when it will be cropped, but SkillForge keeps the mature draw-through guardrail instead: solve as much hidden structure as the visible attachments and spatial relationships require.

This object is intentionally the **Stage-1 foundation of the comics page skillset**, not a claim that Marvel's short chapter exhausts sequential art. Later animation/storyboarding study and deeper comics sources can add shot continuity, acting, pacing, transitions, balloon flow, page turns, lettering space, and other specialized decisions without forcing those future concepts into this starter scaffold.
