# Use Thirds to Break Static Equal Divisions

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a composition or designed form has fallen into centered halves or repeated equal divisions and feels static
**THEN** test a one-third/two-thirds relationship before accepting the midpoint
**ELSE** keep deliberate symmetry or equal division when it serves the intended design

## Do
- Apply the test to the frame, a major mass, a head, or another dominant proportional relationship rather than treating thirds as a decorative grid.
- Compare the unequal division against the original midpoint and keep it only when hierarchy or character improves.
- Rotate or adapt the proportional field when the subject is primarily horizontal rather than assuming a single upright template.

## Don't
- Do not treat thirds as a biological law or a compulsory composition formula.
- Do not break meaningful symmetry merely to satisfy a numerical rule.

## Checklist
- The unequal relationship produces a clearer hierarchy than the equal split it replaced.
- The division supports the subject's intended orientation and character.
- A deliberate midpoint remains available when symmetry is actually the stronger choice.

## Notes
Mattesi calls this a law of thirds, but the transferable learner decision is a design heuristic for escaping automatic equality. It applies beyond animal drawing to characters, creatures, objects, vehicles, and page composition.
