# Simulate Overrange Brightness With Source-Colored Corona

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a source or reflection must feel brighter than the medium's available white
**THEN** reserve the hottest center, surround it with a restrained source-colored corona, and lift adjacent dark values as scattered light spills into them so the center reads as exceeding the literal value range
**ELSE** keep ordinary highlights compact when the source is within the medium's representable range.

## Do
- Preserve a clear value hierarchy from hot center through colored corona into the surrounding field.
- Let nearby silhouettes and dark passages rise slightly where glare or scattering would veil them.
- Match the corona's color to the source or illuminated environment rather than defaulting to white.
- Keep photographic starbursts, lens polygons, and ghosts separate unless a camera/lens look is specifically intended.

## Don't
- Make the corona a hard-edged halo unrelated to the surrounding atmosphere.
- Paint every highlight with a glow; reserve the device for genuinely overrange brightness.
- Darken the surroundings excessively if the intended effect depends on local light scatter lifting them.

## Checklist
- The source feels brighter than the literal white available.
- The corona grades naturally into neighboring values.
- Lens artifacts are absent unless deliberately part of the visual language.

## Notes
A painting or display cannot reproduce unlimited luminance. A corona creates the sensation relationally by using the surrounding value field and source-colored scatter to imply brightness beyond the medium's ceiling.
