# Compare the Same Subject Across Medium Behaviors

## Practice Task
Draw the same subject through three contrasting medium behaviors, then compare what each tool makes easy, difficult, crisp, broken, soft, or spontaneous before choosing a route for the intended result.

## Target Skill
Learn to select a medium by its native edge, spread, broken-mark, pressure, and stroke behavior instead of forcing one habitual technique through every subject.

## Setup
Choose one compact subject and hold its construction, viewpoint, and lighting constant. Use three media or materially different tool behaviors that make distinct kinds of marks.

## Instructions
1. Make three equal-size copies of the same simple mass so construction and scale stay constant while only the texture process changes.
2. On the first copy, work on a dry ground with a stable point or brush. Keep the edges comparatively controlled and test how little information is needed to imply the surface.
3. On the second copy, reduce the load or use half-dry opaque paint so broken paper and interrupted marks create a rougher texture. Keep the broken marks subordinate to the large form.
4. On the third copy, damp or mist the ground before applying a fluid medium. Let part of the edge spread naturally instead of trying to steer every branch or bloom.
5. If using a brush, make one broad stroke, one full-length stroke, and one fine stroke with the same brush by changing pressure, trailing, and lift. Note whether the brush is too large or too small for the scale of the study.
6. Compare the three results at thumbnail size. Write one sentence for each: what texture it implies, what control it gives up, and what it does more efficiently than drawing the surface literally.
7. Choose the strongest route and make one fresh final patch or small animal study without first drawing a detailed preliminary version in a different medium.

## Success Check
- The three studies differ because of medium behavior, not because the underlying animal mass changed.
- At least one soft or irregular treatment uses controlled surrender of edge precision rather than accidental mess.
- The chosen final route implies the target coat or skin quality with fewer literal marks than a hair-by-hair or crack-by-crack copy.
- You can explain why the selected process offers the right tradeoff between control, speed, freshness, and texture character.

## Common Failures
- Copying every strand or wrinkle so the exercise becomes surface inventory instead of texture implication.
- Trying to force wet-running media back into crisp repeated marks and losing the reason for using the wet ground.
- Letting half-dry paint collapse the animal into a flat hard-edged silhouette.
- Changing pose, lighting, or construction between samples, which makes the medium comparison impossible to judge.
- Making a detailed underdrawing in another medium that dictates the final marks before the chosen texture process has a chance to act.
