# Rank Edge Hierarchy Through Progressive Simplification

## Practice Task
Inspect one reference through progressively stronger low-pass or simplification states, rank its edges by relative strength, and render a compact study that preserves that hierarchy while checking the result at full clarity.

## Target Skill
Judge edges relationally instead of assigning isolated labels such as hard or soft, and distinguish boundaries that structurally survive simplification from weak transitions that should merge or disappear.

## Setup
Choose a reference with a useful range from crisp boundaries through soft and lost edges. Prepare several progressively simplified views of the source while keeping an unsoftened view available for final checking.

## Instructions
1. Identify one boundary that is unquestionably among the hardest and one that is unquestionably soft or lost.
2. Progressively simplify the source and record which boundaries disappear first, which merge into larger shapes, and which remain legible longest.
3. Rank several intermediate edges between the two anchors instead of treating edge strength as a binary choice.
4. Render a small study preserving the major hierarchy while allowing weak edges to merge into larger value/color shapes.
5. Inspect the output at normal clarity. Correct drawing, value, or edge errors that simplification could hide instead of applying the same blur to the result.
6. Repeat with another subject whose edge causes differ—form turn, cast event, atmosphere, material, motion, or focus—so the hierarchy is not tied to one optical condition.

## Success Check
- Edge rankings are relative to clear anchors rather than arbitrary labels.
- The strongest boundaries survive simplification longer than subordinate ones for an explainable reason.
- The output remains accurate at full clarity instead of depending on blur to conceal weak construction.
- Lost and soft edges still preserve enough large structure for the subject to read.

## Common Failures
- Blurring the finished study until incorrect edges merely become harder to see.
- Treating every high-contrast boundary as equally hard.
- Losing essential structural boundaries together with trivial detail.
- Copying the simplified source literally instead of using it to understand the hierarchy visible in the full reference.

## Notes
Progressive simplification turns edge judgment into a comparative test. Weak transitions and minor details tend to disappear before the strongest structural boundaries. The drill uses that disappearance order to calibrate relative edge authority while requiring the final output to withstand clear inspection.
