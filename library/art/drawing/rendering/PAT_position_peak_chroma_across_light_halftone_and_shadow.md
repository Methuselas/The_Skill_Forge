# Position Peak Chroma Across Light, Halftone, and Shadow

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a colored form feels dead because light and shadow colors are being blended together mechanically
**THEN** design where chroma is strongest across the light-to-shadow turn, allowing highlight, lit halftone, turning edge, and shadow to carry different amounts of color according to illumination and reflection rather than forcing saturation to change uniformly
**ELSE** keep the simpler transition when the material, lighting, or style genuinely calls for low chroma throughout.

## Do
- Judge highlight, light, halftone, turning region, and shadow as separate color roles instead of mixing only two endpoint colors.
- Allow a strong light source to reduce or bias local color at the brightest highlight while nearby lit halftones carry clearer surface color.
- Look near the light-to-shadow turn for opportunities to concentrate local chroma before the shadow becomes more influenced by reflected light or value compression.
- Reverse or modify the usual placement when backlighting, colored illumination, translucency, or other conditions make the shadow-facing region visibly more colorful.
- Keep chroma placement subordinate to the actual or designed form and lighting structure.

## Don't
- Do not create every transition by simply blending a light color into a shadow color; the midpoint can become dull or muddy.
- Do not turn "strongest color near the light" into an absolute law.
- Do not increase saturation everywhere in an attempt to make the object look more colorful.
- Do not let chroma changes contradict the material, value grouping, or direction of illumination.

## Checklist
- The color transition has intentional chroma structure rather than a uniform blend.
- The most saturated passage appears where the lighting and material plausibly support it.
- Highlights, halftones, and shadows remain distinct enough to describe the turning form.
- Exceptions such as backlight or colored reflected light are handled from observation or design, not forced into one formula.

## Notes
A useful color transition is not merely the average of a light-side color and a shadow-side color. Bright highlights may approach the color of the light, lit halftones can carry strong local color, and the turning region can become a strategic place for chroma before the shadow is tempered by reduced light and reflected influence. Treat this as a placement problem with exceptions, not as a fixed optical law.
