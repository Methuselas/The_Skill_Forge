# Pair Directional Line and Mass Marks as Complementary Roles

## Pattern Rule
**IF** line, broad patches, and texture marks are merely duplicating the same contour or filling one another's shapes
**THEN** assign them complementary jobs: let line carry direction, articulation, and travel while broader marks carry mass, internal shape, texture, or spread, then control their contrast, spacing, concentration, and edge quality so each makes the other more legible
**ELSE** keep a single mark language when it already communicates the intended form without redundant roles.

## Do
- Use line as a moving track that can connect, direct, and articulate form rather than only enclosing silhouettes.
- Let broader patches occupy area and establish mass, internal shape, or textural fields instead of simply coloring inside the line.
- Exploit contrast: broad marks can make a narrow line feel more directional, while line can sharpen the breadth and placement of mass marks.
- Welcome running, broken, or accidental effects when they generate useful texture or form, then selectively arrest the flow with a few controlled edges when articulation begins to disappear.
- Vary patch size, spacing, accumulation, and edge behavior as active design variables rather than treating all marks as equal fill.

## Don't
- Do not use a broad patch only to fill a contour that the line has already fully stated.
- Do not let every fluid mark run freely until the form loses joints, edges, or structural tension.
- Do not add line everywhere simply because a patch edge feels uncertain; use only the articulation the image needs.
- Do not confuse chance with absence of control. Accidents are useful when they are selected and integrated into a readable form.

## Checklist
- Line and mass marks perform visibly different but cooperating roles.
- Broad marks change mass, texture, or internal shape rather than merely filling outlines.
- The line system retains directional or articulating purpose.
- Chance effects are preserved where useful and constrained where they erase form.
- Removing either mark family would reduce a distinct part of the image's structural or expressive information.

## Notes
Treat line and patch as a “dialog”: line progresses toward a goal, while a patch spreads and occupies area. Their contrast becomes useful only when they do not duplicate one another. Wet ink can welcome controlled accident, but if every mark runs, articulation disappears; use a few firmer edges or details to confine the flow. The Pattern generalizes that role assignment beyond animal drawing while retaining the control-versus-chance tradeoff.

`VAR_guptill_assign_mixed_media_distinct_line_mass_and_accent_roles` turns the same division of labor into a staged mixed-media sequence: establish structure and selected detail with the primary line system, simplify broad values or restrained color with an area-based pass, then return only a few crisp finishing accents after that broader statement is dry/resolved. The hierarchy can be reversed when color or mass is meant to be primary; the durable rule is role separation, not pencil supremacy.

`VAR_martin_define_billowing_smoke_with_black_mass_and_negative_space` applies the same complementary-role logic to stylized smoke: begin with one coherent dark mass, then use the white shapes around and within it to separate lobes, overlaps, and light-facing passages. Solid, broken, or stochastic edge treatment can change the character of the smoke, but the effect must retain mass continuity instead of becoming a collection of disconnected dots.
