# Construct a Rainbow From Antisolar Geometry and Additive Light

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a primary or secondary rainbow must be placed in a scene
**THEN** center the arc on the antisolar point, use the appropriate angular radius and color order, and render the bow as added light over the background rather than as an opaque colored ribbon
**ELSE** omit the bow when the sun-view-rain geometry cannot support it.

## Do
- Locate the antisolar point directly opposite the sun and keep scene shadow direction consistent with it.
- Place a primary bow at roughly 42 degrees from the antisolar point, red outside and violet inside.
- For a secondary bow, place it farther out at roughly 50 degrees, weaker and with reversed color order.
- Allow the region between primary and secondary bows to read comparatively darker.
- Let landscape information remain visible beneath the bow while the additive light makes the arc locally lighter than its background.

## Don't
- Center the rainbow arbitrarily in the picture without reference to the sun.
- Paint the colors as opaque bands that erase the scene behind them.
- Use identical strength and color order for primary and secondary bows.

## Checklist
- Arc center and shadow directions agree about the antisolar point.
- Primary/secondary order and strength are correct for the chosen bow.
- The rainbow behaves as added light over the background.

## Notes
Rainbow construction becomes much easier when treated as geometry plus additive light. The approximate angular radii are useful placement guides; perspective composition still determines which portion of the arc is visible inside the frame.
