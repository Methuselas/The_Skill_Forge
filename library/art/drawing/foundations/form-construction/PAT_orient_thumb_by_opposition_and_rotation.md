# Orient the Thumb by Opposition and Rotation

## Pattern Rule
**IF** the thumb changes from lying beside the index side to opposing the fingers
**THEN** rotate the whole thumb branch through space and track the thumbnail plane as an orientation cue; do not model opposition as a flat sideways hinge
**ELSE** keep the simpler side-plane relationship when the thumb remains adjacent to the index

## Do
- Treat thumb orientation as a coupled change of direction and facing.
- Use the thumbnail plane to check whether the thumb has actually rotated into opposition.
- Let opposition bring the thumb tip toward the closing fingertips rather than merely sliding it across a flat palm drawing.
- Preserve the thumb as the hand's exceptional mobile branch.

## Don't
- Keep the thumbnail facing unchanged while swinging the thumb across the palm.
- Treat the thumb as a fifth long finger.
- Expose the chapter's degree measurements as rigid runtime laws; use them as demonstrations of limited ranges and changing planes.

## Checklist
If the thumb reaches the right location but feels pasted on or mechanically impossible, check its rotation and nail-plane change before changing its length.

## Notes
Thumb placement must be solved as a rotating oppositional member whose base plane and reach differ from the long-finger group.
