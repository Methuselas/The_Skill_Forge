# Design Surface Anatomy as Micro-Gesture on Parent Forms

## Pattern Rule
**IF** surface anatomy is being added to an already-established gesture, landmark, and volume construction
**THEN** treat each important anatomical group as a smaller gesture inside the larger figure: simplify its shape, place its attachments on the parent forms, decide its active/passive deformation from the action, and wrap its volume through the perspective of the structures it rests on or connects
**ELSE** return to the unresolved parent gesture, landmarks, or volumes before using anatomy to compensate

## Do
- Begin with the muscle group's useful action and attachment relationship rather than memorized contour.
- Reduce the group to a memorable designed shape only as a working scaffold.
- Distinguish active/compressed forms from passive/stretched forms so the two sides of an action do not become symmetrical copies.
- Let the anatomical volume inherit the perspective of the rib cage, pelvis, neck, limb, scapular system, or other parent structure beneath it.
- Use overlaps when several anatomical shapes compete for placement so front/behind order stays unambiguous.
- Preserve the original large gesture: a pectoral, trapezius, deltoid, abdominal, oblique, serratus, erector, or latissimus statement is a micro-gesture that supports the larger action.
- Alter simplified shapes for individual body type, character, sex traits, creature design, or exaggeration after their structural job is understood.

## Don't
- Do not paste a memorized muscle symbol onto a body whose underlying form or action disagrees with it.
- Do not make active and passive sides identical merely because the anatomy has the same named parts.
- Do not let an anatomical shape flatten or contradict the perspective of the larger volume it occupies.
- Do not preserve the mnemonic shape literally in the finished drawing after more specific form has replaced it.
- Do not use anatomy detail to rescue a gesture, support, or construction problem owned by an earlier stage.

## Checklist
- The local anatomical shape has a clear action/placement reason.
- Its compression or stretch agrees with the pose.
- Its visible volume belongs to the same perspective as its parent form.
- The larger gesture remains readable after anatomy is added.
- Removing the mnemonic label does not destroy the spatial explanation.
