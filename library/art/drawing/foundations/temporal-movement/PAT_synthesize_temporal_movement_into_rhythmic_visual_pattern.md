# Synthesize Temporal Movement Into Rhythmic Visual Pattern

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a fixed image must communicate a movement that unfolds through time
**THEN** organize the visible shapes, directions, repetitions, overlaps, and accents from knowledge of the surrounding action so the single image carries a rhythmic impression of direction, speed, and changing energy
**ELSE** describe the static state directly when temporal movement is not part of the intended read

## Do
- Use memory or sequential reference to understand the phases before and after the chosen instant.
- Select and arrange visible cues so the action reads as a continuous event rather than a disconnected photographic slice.
- Let different parts carry different speeds and directions when the movement requires overlapping actions.

## Don't
- Do not assume an instantaneous photograph automatically gives the strongest artistic statement of movement.
- Do not confuse this perceptual synthesis with the physical cause of the action; use force continuity to diagnose mechanics and this pattern to communicate temporal experience in a static image.

## Checklist
- The viewer can infer a direction and character of movement from the still image.
- Repetitions and overlaps support one rhythmic action rather than visual noise.
- Any apparent deformation remains compatible with the body's underlying attachments and mechanics.

## Notes
Luard distinguishes analytical snapshots of movement from the way a spectator remembers and links successive states. A static drawing has no literal time, so it must compress that temporal experience into a visual rhythm. `VAR_luard_allow_motion_conditioned_apparent_deformation` permits selective apparent elongation, merging, or suppression only when the movement itself supports that perceptual effect.
