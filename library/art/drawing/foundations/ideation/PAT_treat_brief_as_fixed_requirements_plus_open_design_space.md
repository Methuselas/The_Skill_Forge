# Treat a Brief as Fixed Requirements plus Open Design Space

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an illustration is governed by a client, editor, art director, assignment, or other external brief
**THEN** separate the genuinely fixed outcome and required units from the still-open pictorial decisions, then use that remaining design space deliberately
**ELSE** let the artist define the constraints needed to keep an open problem coherent.

## Do
- Extract required subject matter, communication outcome, assigned space, mandatory text/product relationships, deadlines, and other actual constraints before drawing.
- Resolve accuracy/fidelity separately from speed and finish: identify which forms, relationships, or details must remain exact, which may be abbreviated or omitted, how finished the result is expected to look, and what practical role the image must serve. A request for a rough, quick, or sketchy treatment does not by itself authorize factual inaccuracy.
- Distinguish requirements from suggestions about pose, staging, costume, camera, treatment, or arrangement that may still be negotiable.
- Ask what purpose a requested element serves so a better pictorial solution can preserve the requirement even when the literal first suggestion changes.
- Spend creative freedom on decisions the brief leaves open rather than defaulting to the safest literal execution.
- Confirm substantial departures before investing heavily when the change could violate a real requirement or approval boundary.
- When direction conflicts with your preferred solution, identify the underlying reason before either complying or resisting; preserve real constraints and negotiate only the pictorial choices that remain open.
- During revisions, compare each requested change against the original approved requirement: correct execution that missed the agreed target, but recognize a changed subject, staging, emphasis, treatment, or other requirement as a change of direction and clarify it before substantial rework.

## Don't
- Do not treat every descriptive sentence in a brief as equally fixed.
- Do not ignore explicit requirements under the banner of creativity.
- Do not obey a weak suggested staging literally when the underlying purpose can be met more effectively and the decision is open.
- Do not wait until final art to discover that an inventive deviation changed the job the client actually assigned.
- Do not confuse professional judgment with reflexive resistance, or cooperation with reflexive compliance.
- Do not let serial revisions silently rewrite the assignment; distinguish fixing the agreed work from changing what the work is now being asked to do.

## Checklist
- Fixed requirements are listed separately from open design decisions.
- Accuracy obligations, permissible abbreviation, finish level, time budget, and image role are distinguished rather than collapsed into a single idea of "rough" or "finished."
- Each major open choice has a reason tied to the intended communication.
- The solution preserves the purpose behind required elements.
- Any risky deviation is surfaced before expensive execution.
- When direction is disputed, the underlying requirement is stated explicitly and any proposed alternative still satisfies it.
- Creative freedom improves the assignment rather than merely making it different.
- Revision requests are classified as corrections to the agreed requirement or changes to the requirement itself.
- Any changed requirement is made explicit before substantial additional work begins.
