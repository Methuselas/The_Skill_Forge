# Preflight Observed Rendering for Constraints and Failure Points

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an observed or reference-based rendering is about to begin and later corrections would become expensive or destabilize the whole picture
**THEN** inspect the subject, light, reference, medium, time, and difficult passages first; decide the required compromise, scope reduction, or setup change before committing to the rendering
**ELSE** begin directly when the conditions are stable and no foreseeable constraint materially threatens the intended result.

## Do
- Identify the direction, quality, and color tendency of the main light together with the strongest light/dark and hard/soft extremes that will anchor later comparisons.
- Check whether the subject, light, viewpoint, or reference is likely to change and decide how the image will preserve one coherent state if it does.
- Locate drawing ambiguities, perspective or foreshortening risks, unusually difficult edges, and passages that may exceed the available value, color, detail, or medium range.
- Decide which visual event is most important, which areas can remain subordinate, and how much can realistically be resolved in the available time.
- Resolve known impossibilities early by changing scope, setup, reference support, palette/gamut, or intended treatment rather than postponing the problem until the surrounding work depends on it.

## Don't
- Do not rush into finish because the subject is attractive while known structural or lighting problems remain unanswered.
- Do not assume an overrange light, unstable subject, ambiguous drawing passage, or weak reference channel will somehow become easier after detail has accumulated.
- Do not turn preflight into exhaustive planning when the conditions are simple; inspect the failure points that could materially change the route.

## Checklist
- The dominant light and major comparison extremes are identified.
- Known instability, range limits, drawing risks, and reference weaknesses have a planned response.
- Scope and expected finish fit the available time and conditions.
- No major passage is knowingly deferred without a plausible execution route.

## Notes
The purpose of preflight is risk control, not rigidity. Observational work remains responsive, but foreseeable constraints should be handled while the picture is cheap to change. A few early decisions about light, range, stability, drawing difficulty, and scope can prevent later passages from being built on conditions that were impossible or contradictory from the start.
