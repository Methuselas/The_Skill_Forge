# Train Observation With Look-Hold-Draw and Blind Bursts

## Practice Task
Draw one moderately complex subject while deliberately shortening the loop between looking at the subject and making the next mark.

## Target Skill
Retaining one observed relationship long enough to draw it without replacing it with a remembered symbol.

## Setup
Place the subject where you can move your eyes between it and the page without changing viewpoint. Use a medium that permits light restatements.

## Instructions
1. Look at one small relationship—an angle, contour change, interval, or shape—and hold that visual fact briefly before drawing it.
2. Put down only that relationship, then return your eyes to the subject rather than staring at the drawing while inventing the next mark.
3. Several times during the study, keep your eyes on the subject for a short burst while the hand continues moving; reconnect with the page before proportion drifts far.
4. Restate inaccurate passages and resume the look-hold-draw loop.
5. End by comparing the whole drawing with the subject rather than judging the fluency of the line alone.

## Success Check
The drawing shows repeated accurate local observations without long passages that collapse into generic symbols, and the brief blind bursts do not cause uncontrolled proportional drift.

## Common Failures
- Looking mostly at the page and drawing from memory.
- Sustaining blind drawing so long that placement becomes unrecoverable.
- Treating the exercise as a test of beautiful continuous line instead of observation.

## Notes
Dodson's blind passages are short intensifications of an ordinary observational loop, not a demand to finish entire drawings without looking at the paper. Continuous access to the subject distinguishes this exercise from short-memory gesture drills that deliberately remove the reference.
