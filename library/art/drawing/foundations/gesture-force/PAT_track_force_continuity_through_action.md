# Track Force Continuity Through Action

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a living or articulated body is shown in action
**THEN** treat the pose as one state in a continuing physical event by reading where momentum or effort comes from, what redirects or resists it in the current body, and where that action continues next
**ELSE** use static structural construction when motion continuity is not the drawing problem

## Do
- Trace directional force through the major masses and joint chains instead of stopping the action at each anatomical part.
- Read applied force as the event that changes an existing direction: impact, support, gravity, resistance, contact, or muscular redirection.
- Use the action line as a graphic abstraction of the body-wide physical continuity, not as a decorative curve laid over unrelated masses.
- When comparing adjacent poses or panels, ask what state change connects them rather than treating each pose as a fresh start.

## Don't
- Do not reset momentum at every body part or keyframe.
- Do not use flowing curves as a substitute for explaining where weight, support, resistance, and redirection actually occur.
- Do not split a hybrid character into separate human-force and animal-force systems; once integrated, it is one acting body.

## Checklist
- The action has a readable incoming direction, event or redirection, and outgoing continuation.
- Major masses and support points agree with the claimed force path.
- A preceding or following pose could be inferred without inventing a completely unrelated action.

## Notes
Mattesi's directional FORCE, applied FORCE, rhythm, and coming-from/event/going-to vocabulary describes continuity through time as well as graphic flow. The same foundation applies to humans, animals, hybrid characters, and invented creatures. Borrowed anatomy should contribute its donor mechanics to one integrated system rather than function as pasted-on silhouette decoration. `VAR_mattesi_interlock_forceful_shapes_through_straight_curve_exchange` applies the same continuity to connected shape design; `VAR_mattesi_exchange_force_between_interacting_bodies` carries it across contact between bodies; `VAR_mattesi_preload_with_anticipatory_countermovement` adds a mechanically grounded preparation before a large action.
