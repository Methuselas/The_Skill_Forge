# Unify Finger Chains With Knuckle-Shank Rhythm

## Pattern Rule
**IF** constructed fingers read as disconnected beads, equal tubes, or stiff mechanical segments
**THEN** integrate the digit chain by alternating the fuller knuckle capsules with narrower shanks and carry that crest-and-trough rhythm through the fingertip
**ELSE** preserve the existing digit action without adding decorative undulation

## Do
- Let each knuckle expansion transition into a narrower shank before the next joint.
- Carry the rhythm continuously through bent and extended digit chains.
- Use the rhythm to unify the finger after the rod-and-ball construction establishes articulation.

## Don't
- Turn every finger edge into an identical sine wave.
- Apply the same apparent symmetry to a side view when the view does not show it.
- Sacrifice joint direction or finger action merely to make the contour rhythmic.

## Checklist
- Knuckles read as articulated expansions rather than isolated beads.
- Shanks compress between joint masses without breaking the digit chain.
- The fingertip completes the directional rhythm instead of ending as a blunt tube.

## Notes
Hogarth presents the rhythm as an integration of the rod-and-ball construction: the mechanical scaffold remains, but the finished finger reads as one continuous energetic form. His top and underside examples show a useful expansion/compression symmetry, while he explicitly notes that this symmetry is not apparent from side views.
