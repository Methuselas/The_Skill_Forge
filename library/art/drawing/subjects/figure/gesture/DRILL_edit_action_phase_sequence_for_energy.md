# Edit an Action Phase Sequence for Energy

## Practice Task
Design a five-cell action, construct it stage by stage, then reduce it to a stronger three- or four-cell sequence without losing the movement.

## Target Skill
Preserving action rhythm and energy while simplifying, combining, or rejecting phases in a multi-figure sequence.

## Setup
Choose one clear action such as a run into a leap, a throw, a strike, or a directional change. Leave enough space to compare all cells at once.

## Instructions
1. Draw every Stage 1 cell one at a time, checking the progression of action, spacing, and timing across the full sequence.
2. Complete every Stage 2 cell one at a time. Inside each cell, follow torso, legs, arms, head.
3. Compare the sequence as a whole and identify redundant, weak, or low-energy phases.
4. Remove one frame and combine useful information from two other frames into one clearer action statement.
5. Redraw the shortened sequence at Stage 1, then Stage 2, rather than cutting and pasting incompatible finished parts.
6. Advance all surviving cells through Stage 3 only after the Stage 2 sequence reads cleanly.

## Success Check
- The shortened sequence communicates the same action without requiring labels.
- Energy, direction, and timing are at least as clear as in the five-cell version.
- Every cell still obeys the internal construction hierarchy.
- No surviving figure is a literal collage of contradictory phases.
- The sequence advances stage by stage across all cells rather than finishing one cell while the others remain unresolved.

## Common Failures
- Removing frames until the movement becomes a jump cut with no readable transition.
- Keeping every phase because it was already drawn.
- Combining limbs from two moments while leaving the torso in neither moment.
- Constructing all torsos, then all legs, as a rule instead of completing each cell within the current stage.
- Polishing one favorite frame before checking the rhythm of the whole sequence.

## Notes
Hogarth's page compresses motion because of space and teaching economy. The five-step row establishes the rhythm; the lower sequence combines phases 1 and 3, rejects 2, and retains the later action. The lesson is not to merge drawing stages carelessly. It is to use notation to judge which moments carry the action most efficiently.

`VAR_loomis_choose_action_phase_from_whole_sweep` retains **Choose the Action Phase From the Whole Sweep** as a bounded alternative; use it only under the conditions recorded in the variant metadata.

`VAR_hogarth_search_neighbor_keyframes_from_pelvis_anchor` turns the sequence study into a correction tool: hold the pelvis as the persistent anchor, search nearby phases, and choose the keyframe with the action strength the composition actually needs.
