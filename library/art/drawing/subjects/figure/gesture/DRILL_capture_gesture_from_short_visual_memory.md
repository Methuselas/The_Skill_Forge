# Capture Gesture From Short Visual Memory

## Practice Task
Observe a person or posed reference briefly, remove the reference, and reconstruct only the gesture from memory. Repeat with several different actions.

## Target Skill
Practice the named decision until the structural result can be repeated deliberately without relying on surface copying.

## Setup
No special setup required.

## Instructions
1. Before drawing, look for the total attitude: head tilt, torso sweep, weight/support, limb directions, and the strongest pinch/stretch relationship.
2. Hide or look away from the reference.
3. Mentally rehearse the marks you would make before touching the page.
4. Draw the head attitude and the main head-to-torso action first, then add legs and arms as flowing relationships rather than copied contours.
5. Stop while the drawing is still a gesture; do not use anatomy or detail to repair forgotten action.
6. Reopen the reference, compare the total action rather than small contour accuracy, name the largest memory loss, and repeat the pose once.
7. Progressively shorten the observation time or use naturally moving people when the exercise becomes comfortable.

## Success Check
- The remembered pose is recognizable primarily from attitude and body language.
- Weight/support and the main torso action survive even when local proportions are imperfect.
- Lines describe relationships and flow rather than a memorized silhouette.
- The second attempt corrects the named action error without merely adding detail.

## Common Failures
- Staring at one limb instead of grasping the total pose.
- Trying to remember every contour point.
- Reopening the reference during each uncertain mark.
- Inventing anatomy to disguise forgotten action.
- Treating memory error as failure instead of evidence for what was not actually observed.

## Notes
This differs from reference-free invention practice. Vilppu uses memory to train **seeing and analysis of observed action** in situations where a model may not hold still. The production lesson is not to avoid reference; it is to make observation active enough that the action can survive after the subject moves.
`VAR_bammes_test_animal_motion_with_visual_memory_and_imagination` extends the same memory test to studied animal motion: after observing and understanding a movement process, remove direct support and reconstruct the action from visual memory or imagination. Use the uncertainty that appears as evidence for what has not yet been internalized; do not skip the observation/study phase that makes the reconstruction meaningful.
