# Carry the Underarm Curve Through the Elbow

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an arm's upper and lower forms must read as one member in a bent or extended pose
**THEN** locate the elbow and carry the underarm rhythm from the rear armpit through it to the outer base of the palm, using two meeting curves in a bend and one controlled long curve in extension
**ELSE** simplify the arm to the elbow and two endpoints before shaping the contour

## Do
- Use the elbow as the organizing landmark without treating it as a detached graphic hinge.
- In a bent arm, let the two curve sections form a clear V-like change; in extension, smooth them into a tensioned path comparable to a shaped Bézier line.
- Keep the rhythm visible through front, rear, and deep-space positions even when muscle bulges alter local contour.

## Don't
- Use the bone centerline as a substitute for the surface rhythm.
- Break the arm into unrelated bulges at the elbow.
- Reverse the curve arbitrarily when the view changes.

## Checklist
- The eye can travel from rear armpit to outer palm base without losing the arm.
- The elbow is clear but does not sever the upper and lower members.
- Bent and extended versions feel like the same structural rhythm under different tension.

## Notes
The persistent underarm curve is a large-form control, not a decorative line. It keeps the arm unified before detailed anatomy introduces smaller changes of contour.
