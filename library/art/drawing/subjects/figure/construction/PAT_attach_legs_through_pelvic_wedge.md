# Attach the Legs Through the Pelvic Wedge

## Pattern Rule
**IF** the torso is established and the legs are ready to be constructed
**THEN** clarify the pelvic wedge and its center division, seat each thigh into the side of that stable mass below the front corner, and build the leg action outward from those attachments
**ELSE** resolve the pelvic orientation before drawing independent leg cylinders

## Do
- Preserve the wedge as the underlying Stage 2 structure while allowing the visible hip, groin, and lower-belly forms to open, compress, or overlap with the leg action.
- Place the thigh origins deeply enough that the legs feel socketed into the pelvis rather than pasted to its outer contour. Route each upper leg from the deep socket through the femoral neck/trochanter region, then let the femur angle inward toward the knee instead of beginning the thigh at the visible outer hip bulge.
- Use the hip, knee, ankle, and appropriate S- or B-rhythm as one continuous chain.
- Let the leg action clarify how the lower-belly insert sits within the pelvic basin.

## Don't
- Widen or narrow the skeletal wedge arbitrarily to chase the leg silhouette.
- Attach the thighs at the upper corners of the box as though they hang from its rim.
- Draw convincing legs that have no clear origin inside the pelvic mass.
- Replace the Stage 2 wedge with unrelated Stage 3 hip contours.

## Checklist
- Both thighs enter one readable pelvic structure and do not create duplicate sockets; their visible outer masses can widen around the trochanters while the underlying femoral route remains anchored deeper inside the pelvis.
- The pelvic center division agrees with the torso centerline and leg separation.
- The visible pelvis may appear to spread with the legs, but the underlying wedge remains traceable.
- The lower-belly, groin, and hip contours can be developed in Stage 3 without relocating the attachments.

## Notes
The apparent spreading of the pelvis is a change in the visible compound form, not a literal expansion of the pelvic skeleton. As the legs move away from the center mass, the hip and groin contours separate and the lower-belly relationship changes, while the Stage 3 anatomy continues to reside inside the stable wedge established here.
