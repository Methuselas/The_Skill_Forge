# Choose S or B Leg Rhythm by View

## Pattern Rule
**IF** the whole leg needs a controlling rhythm before anatomy is refined
**THEN** use an S from hip to ankle for profile, many three-quarter, and seated orientations, or a B with a straighter inner stem and outer thigh-and-calf humps for front, rear, and deeply projected frontal orientations
**ELSE** decide the leg's fundamental view from the knee, anklebones, and major planes before choosing the rhythm

## Do
- Let the S reverse around the knee from front thigh toward rear calf.
- In the B, allow the inner stem to break with a bent knee while the outer thigh and calf retain the two-lobe relationship.
- Use exposed or enclosed anklebones as supporting view clues, not as a rule that overrides the constructed pose.

## Don't
- Switch to an S merely because a front-oriented leg is strongly foreshortened.
- Apply the rhythm to only the calf or thigh instead of controlling the entire hip-to-ankle member.
- Force the inside line perfectly straight when the shin's inward curve is visible.

## Checklist
- The selected rhythm matches the leg's overall orientation, not one local contour.
- The knee mediates the reversal or break without disconnecting the limb.
- Extreme crossed and compressed legs retain a readable S or B organization.

## Notes
Hogarth uses the two patterns as practical view families. Guided review simplifies the choice: S is commonly profile, three-quarter, or seated; B is commonly front, rear, hunched, crossed, or deeply projected while still facing front or back.
