# Link the Arm Pair Through the Shoulder Yoke

## Pattern Rule
**IF** the torso and support are stable and the arms are ready to be constructed
**THEN** link both arms through one shoulder-girdle yoke, using the collarbone path in front or the upper-shoulder path in back, then build each arm outward as an independently moving but mechanically restrained member
**ELSE** return to the torso when the shoulder tilt or arm origins cannot be stated as one system

## Do
- Draw the yoke literally during Stage 2 when it is needed to establish the pair.
- In rear views, invert the connection across the upper shoulders when the collarbones are hidden.
- Use transparent draw-through to locate obscured arm origins and overlapping members before selecting the visible contours.
- Combine the yoke with the continuous underarm rhythm and believable elbow chain.
- Let the yoke disappear once the shoulder-and-arm relationship is solved.

## Don't
- Attach and pose each arm independently without checking their shared shoulder system.
- Carry the yoke visibly into Stage 3 as though it were anatomy or finished contour.
- Hide an uncertain attachment behind an overlapping arm, prop, costume, or shadow.
- Treat free arm movement as freedom from the muscular and skeletal restraints of the torso.

## Checklist
- Both arms can be traced through one front or rear shoulder connection.
- Overlapping arms retain believable hidden origins and member paths.
- Shoulder tilt, collarbone or upper-back direction, and arm actions agree.
- Stage 3 can replace the yoke with specific anatomy without continuing to solve the attachment.

## Notes
The yoke is a literal Stage 2 construction tool. The arms can move independently, but the structures and muscles that power them still relate through the shoulder girdle and torso. With experience the artist may visualize the yoke without drawing it; if it is still required after Stage 2, the block has not done its job.
