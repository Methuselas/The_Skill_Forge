# Direct a Reference Subject Through Story State, Not Feature Posing

## Pattern Rule
**IF** live or photographic figure reference must carry a specific emotion, intention, or dramatic state
**THEN** give the subject the character's situation, objective, relationship, or emotional circumstance and let the visible expression emerge from that task instead of prescribing isolated facial features
**ELSE** use straightforward pose direction when emotional authenticity is not part of the reference job.

## Do
- Explain what has happened, what the character wants, fears, expects, resists, or reacts to.
- Give the subject a playable situation or intention rather than commands such as "raise the eyebrows" or "smile harder."
- Demonstrate or act the situation yourself when that communicates the state more efficiently than verbal feature instructions.
- Use story context, dialogue, imagined partners, props, or environmental cues to help the subject respond rather than pose.
- Encourage experimentation and capture useful fleeting states when they appear.
- Keep the final reference subordinate to the designed character and scene; select the response that serves the picture rather than the most exaggerated expression.

## Don't
- Do not manufacture emotion by adjusting facial parts one at a time while the body and situation remain neutral.
- Do not ridicule or punish failed attempts; self-consciousness usually makes expression more performative.
- Do not demand that a subject hold a transient expression so long that it becomes stiff.
- Do not assume a photographed expression is automatically right for the character merely because it looks vivid in isolation.

## Checklist
- The subject knows the dramatic situation or intention being played.
- Expression, posture, gaze, and gesture arise from one coherent state.
- Direction is framed in actions, circumstances, or relationships rather than isolated feature positions.
- The captured reference still serves the predesigned story beat and character.
- The result reads as a response to something, not merely as a facial pose for the camera.

## Notes
Loomis repeatedly treats expressive reference as an acting problem. Rather than directing eyebrows, mouth corners, or other features mechanically, he recommends establishing the story state, demonstrating the action when useful, and capturing the subject's response. This general Pattern sits above the child-specific `PAT_elicit_natural_child_expression_through_rapport`, whose special problem is how adult authority can suppress a child's natural expression.
