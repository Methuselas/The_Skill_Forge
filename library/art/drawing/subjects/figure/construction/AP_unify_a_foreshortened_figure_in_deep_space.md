# Unify a Foreshortened Figure in Deep Space

## Objective
Keep a deeply foreshortened figure readable as one connected three-dimensional system after overlap has divided its visible forms into partial masses.

## Steps / Flow
1. Begin from a coherent block-stage figure. Confirm the near-to-far order, major attachments, and visible fragments before adding specific anatomy; when those relationships are still undecided, return to the earlier construction rather than asking surface treatment to choose the pose.
2. Read each partial form as the visible portion of a complete member continuing behind the nearer mass. Preserve enough identity, direction, volume, and attachment for the hidden continuation to remain believable.
3. Locate the passages where overlap makes the figure resemble separate lumps or beads. At those passages, search lightly for real skin folds, tendon insertions, muscle interlaces, bony transitions, or other structural routes that can carry the eye from one mass into the next.
4. Derive every route from the natural form seen by the camera. Let high, low, front, rear, and three-quarter views change the projected overcurves, undercurves, compressions, and dominant masses without changing the underlying body.
5. Treat joints and attachment regions as parts of the continuous system. Let the knee redirect the leg, the deltoid merge arm and torso, the hip carry the leg into the pelvis, the elbow mediate the arm, and the neck structures join head and torso instead of inserting isolated connector blocks.
6. When local probing begins to compete with the total figure, stop adding connections. Strengthen the encompassing contour that coordinates the major outer flow, and suppress minor edges or internal divisions that keep the figure looking assembled from parts.
7. Add tone only when it is needed to consolidate mass more clearly than line. In production work, let the chosen light govern the values; use tone to carry resolved form and permit selected lines to soften, break, or disappear, not to disguise uncertain construction.
8. Keep early Stage 3 lines light while the specific form is being tested. Render further in Stage 3 when the image genuinely needs it, then harden only the lines and edges that have proved likely to survive into Stage 4.
9. Stop according to the image rather than a quota. Interconnection, contour, and tone are available controls, not three compulsory finishes; Stage 4 receives the proven solution and supplies the final degree of finish.

## Notes
The chapter begins with a paradox: overlap is necessary for recession, yet overlap also partitions the body. Hogarth answers with three escalating means of cohesion—linear interconnection, coordinating contour, and tonal fusion. The useful production lesson is not to maximize all three, but to recognize which one solves the present separation with the least interference.

This procedure records Hogarth's method as a guide and an emerging style variant, not a universal law. Different artists may preserve unity through different line economies, shape designs, edge systems, or value structures. Later reference study should compare those alternatives before any one route is promoted to a general rule.
