# Construct the Foot as a Soft-Tissue Support Wedge

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a planted or weight-bearing foot must be blocked before toe and tendon detail
**THEN** enclose the heel block, forefoot platform, and rising internal arch inside one continuous soft-tissue wedge, keeping the outer side broadly supportive and the inner side lifted but not hollow
**ELSE** simplify the foot to heel and forefoot contacts before shaping the arch

## Do
- Give the heel and forefoot distinct load-bearing volumes connected by a fleshed arch span.
- Keep the toes compact at the front platform; let the great toe rise while the smaller toes close down for traction and push.
- Show enough sole thickness under the inner arch that the foot remains a continuous living mass.

## Don't
- Carve an empty tunnel through the instep or expose the arch as if it were an external bone block.
- Treat the foot as a hand with long independent toes.
- Aim the foot independently of the shin rotation.

## Checklist
- The heel, arch, and forefoot read as one connected volume.
- Ground contact is plausible along the heel and forefoot, with a more continuous outer support edge.
- The inner contour rises without making the midfoot disappear.

## Notes
Hogarth's compound wedge is useful, but one plate overstates the openness of the arch. Guided review corrects the shorthand: skeleton, tendon, muscle, fat, connective tissue, and skin continue across the instep, so the external foot never becomes a hollow bridge.
