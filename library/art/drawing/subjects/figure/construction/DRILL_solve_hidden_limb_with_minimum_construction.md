# Solve a Hidden Limb With Minimum Construction

## Practice Task
Create a set of obscured limb problems and decide, case by case, whether the visible evidence needs no hidden path, a partial path, or a complete draw-through chain.

## Target Skill
Construct exactly enough unseen structure to place visible endpoints, fragments, attachments, and overlaps reliably.

## Setup
Prepare nine small torso-and-occluder layouts. In three, show only a visible thigh or forearm; in three, add a terminal hand or foot; in three, add a second fragment that must emerge after the obstruction.

## Instructions
1. Mark the visible fragments and their comparable width evidence.
2. Classify each layout as no hidden route needed, partial route needed, or full chain needed.
3. Draw the minimum hidden framework for the chosen class.
4. Add Stage 2 volume only where it changes the visible result.
5. Cover or erase the hidden construction and check whether the remaining fragments still imply one complete member.
6. Redo any case where the endpoint or second fragment forces a different route than the one first constructed.

## Success Check
- Simple cases remain simple and do not accumulate invented anatomy behind the occluder.
- Dependent endpoints and emerging fragments have a believable continuous route.
- Corresponding widths support the implied size of the hidden member.
- No two visible fragments demand incompatible hidden joints.

## Common Failures
- Drawing every concealed limb in full regardless of need.
- Guessing a hand or foot position that the hidden bend cannot reach.
- Treating visible fragments as unrelated pieces.
- Leaving hidden construction so dark that it competes with the intended final image.

## Notes
The exercise trains rational inference as a decision about dependency, not as a ritual of drawing through everything. It preserves Hogarth's visible-evidence logic while enforcing the guided minimum-sufficient boundary.
