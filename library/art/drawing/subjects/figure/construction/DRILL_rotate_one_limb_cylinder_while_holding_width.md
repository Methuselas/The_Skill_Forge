# Rotate One Limb Cylinder While Holding Width

## Practice Task
Draw one designed upper arm or thigh in a sequence of depth angles, then convert each simple mass into the same organic member.

## Target Skill
Preserve form identity through changing apparent length by coordinating constant comparable width, taper, and end-curve projection.

## Setup
Choose one figure-specific width at the broad end and one at the tapered end. Prepare six empty frames ranging from extended side view to nearly end-on.

## Instructions
1. Draw the first member in an extended view with clear near and far end curves.
2. Rotate the same member toward the viewer in the remaining frames without changing the chosen comparable widths.
3. Shorten the visible side walls as the end curves become fuller.
4. Preserve the taper toward the knee or elbow rather than making a uniform pipe.
5. Add temporary diameter checks to the first pass, then redraw the sequence once without explicit measures.
6. Convert every cylinder into the same thigh or upper arm at Stage 3 specificity without relocating the Stage 2 mass.

## Success Check
- Every frame reads as the same designed member from a different depth angle.
- Apparent length changes while corresponding widths and taper remain coherent.
- The organic conversion fits each block without stretching, shrinking, or moving it.
- The unaided second pass retains the spatial logic of the measured first pass.

## Common Failures
- Letting the end-on member become a larger or smaller body part instead of the same one.
- Keeping long side walls with a nearly circular end view.
- Measuring joint bulges rather than stable shank widths.
- Improving the anatomy by silently changing the underlying cylinder.

## Notes
This drill isolates the central Chapter 4 control before a full figure introduces overlap and hidden members. The first measured pass develops proof; the second develops visual judgment. Repetition is more valuable than one polished drawing.
