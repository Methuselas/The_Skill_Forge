# Coordinate the Figure With an Encompassing Contour

## Pattern Rule
**IF** intensive interconnection study has made the eye notice local parts before the total figure
**THEN** establish a persistent end-of-form contour that carries the major outer flow and subordinate the minor divisions that compete with it
**ELSE** retain the lighter local solution when the figure already reads as one mass

## Do
- Select the outer passages that let the gaze travel around contrary directions and large overlap barriers without becoming trapped in each bump.
- Diffuse, break, or suppress internal edges whose information is already carried by the larger silhouette, adjacent value, or neighboring form.
- Begin firming later in Stage 3 only after the contour has proved structurally correct and likely to survive the final pass.
- Vary the treatment according to the actual object, material, light, tension, and depth rather than applying one identical line weight everywhere.

## Don't
- Inventory every anatomical mound along the outside edge.
- Keep probing local insertions after they impair the overall concept.
- Mistake a continuous form for a requirement that every contour be literally unbroken.

## Checklist
- The whole figure is grasped before the minor internal anatomy.
- Suppressed edges do not make the form appear severed or structurally vague.
- The strengthened passages are confirmed decisions, not temporary marks made dark for convenience.

## Notes
Hogarth introduces contour precisely when detailed interlocking lines become a preoccupation. His examples compare interrupted aggregates with figures held by a coalescing external path, sometimes supported by tonal diffusion. The practical transition is subtractive: once local flow is understood, reduce whatever interferes with seeing the total mass.
