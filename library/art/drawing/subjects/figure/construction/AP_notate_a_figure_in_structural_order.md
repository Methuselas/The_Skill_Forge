# Notate a Figure in Structural Order

## Objective
Convert an accepted Step 1 action framework into a coherent Stage 2 figure by resolving the torso, legs, arms, and head in dependency order while preserving the original flow, support, and spatial direction.

## Steps / Flow
1. **Carry the action into the torso.** Preserve the accepted Step 1 action line as the Stage 2 torso centerline. Build the rib-cage barrel and pelvic wedge around it so their bend, turn, opposition, and spiral obey one governing flow.
2. **Resolve the torso before its dependents.** Clarify the orientation of both torso masses, their flexible waist connection, the pelvic center division, and the attachment regions for the neck and limbs. Do not let a later limb choice redesign the torso silently.
3. **Attach and bear the legs.** Seat the thigh masses into the pelvic wedge, choose the appropriate S- or B-rhythm for each leg view, and carry the leg force through the ankle into a credible foot stance, propulsion, or directional response. The visible hip region may spread or compress with the leg action, but the underlying wedge remains structurally consistent.
4. **Link the arms as a pair.** Construct both arms through a front collarbone yoke or rear shoulder yoke before treating either arm as an isolated member. Use transparent draw-through where one arm overlaps another, and preserve the underarm rhythm through the elbow.
5. **Resolve the head and neck last.** The Step 1 head oval and facing axis may remain for proportion and direction, but do not commit the Stage 2 cranial ball, facial wedge, or neck volume until the body action is stable. Choose the direction demanded by the scene, gaze, target, or narrative, then keep that choice within the actual range allowed by the neck attachment and shoulder girdle.
6. **Pass the notation exit gate on a plain construction.** Read the figure without detail: the torso centerline governs the action; weight, suspension, or momentum is believable; limbs attach once; hands and feet already look structurally plausible; and no yoke is still needed to understand the arm relationship. Remove modeled musculature, facial identity, hair or costume detail, polished contour, and lighting before judging the notation. Tag visible segmentation in deep recession for the Chapter 3 unity remedy rather than disguising it with anatomy or rendering.
7. **Advance without abandoning the order.** Stage 3 may apply the same order more loosely because the form is already established. Stage 4 may move around the image as line rejection, color, light, shadow, and integration demand, but it must preserve the Stage 2 solution.

## Notes
The default Stage 2 order is absolute: torso, legs, arms, head. Repetition of that order is part of the training because it prevents secondary forms from dictating the primary action. **Construction dependency order is not the same thing as kinetic or visual lead.** The head, gaze, hand, or another accent may appear to initiate the action in the finished pose even though the figure is still constructed from torso/support dependencies first. Rare construction-order exceptions are case-specific and follow the actual support mechanics of the pose rather than convenience; for example, a figure hanging from a bar may require torso, supporting arms, legs, then head.

Step 1 states the same root with symbols rather than volume: a head oval and facing axis; a simple flat rib-cage symbol; a simple pelvic symbol; lines for spine, neck, arms, and legs; circles for major joints; and mitten, wedge, or point markers for hands and feet. These are location marks, not ovoid, barrel, wedge-box, or cylinder constructions. Those three-dimensional masses begin in Stage 2. With practice, many Step 1 marks may be visualized and abbreviated, but the artist has not stopped accounting for them.

The centerline is the action's Wu Sao. It is not added afterward to explain independently placed masses; it is the Step 1 action carried forward as the governing constraint. Every later form must continue, oppose, balance, or answer that line without breaking the figure.

Chapter 2 identifies but does not solve the lumpy separation that appears in severe recession. A Chapter 2 exercise may pass its chapter-level check when the ordered notation and depth intention are correct, while recording that defect for Chapter 3. The complete drawing workflow must later reject disconnected forms once the unity methods are available.
