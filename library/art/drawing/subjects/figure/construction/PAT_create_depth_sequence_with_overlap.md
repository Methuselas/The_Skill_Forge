# Create a Depth Sequence With Overlap

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a figure or compound object must recede convincingly through deep space
**THEN** interpose the nearer masses over the farther masses so each partial form occupies a clear position in the near-to-far sequence
**ELSE** keep forms complete only when they are intentionally presented at roughly the same depth or when another spatial cue carries the relationship

## Do
- Let the nearest member show the most complete statement while successively farther members are partly hidden.
- Keep the visible fragment of an obscured form specific enough that its direction, volume, and attachment continue mentally behind the nearer mass.
- Use overlap at meaningful boundaries such as limb crossings, torso-to-limb recession, wrist turns, and foot placement rather than scattering arbitrary contour breaks.

## Don't
- Display every form entire and expect outline alone to create the same recession.
- Cut off a hidden member so abruptly that it reads as amputated, pasted on, or unrelated to its parent mass.
- Confuse more overlap with better depth when the ordering of the forms is contradictory.

## Checklist
- The viewer can number the major masses from nearest to farthest without labels.
- Every hidden member has a believable continuation behind the form that covers it.
- Removing the overlap would noticeably flatten the intended spatial relationship.

## Notes
The opening examples compare complete forms lying on the picture plane with interposed forms that establish numerical recession. The same logic is then applied to a tipped figure whose leg, body, and arm pass behind one another. Overlap supplies depth, but it also creates the separation problem addressed by the rest of the chapter.
