# Elicit Natural Child Expression Through Rapport

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a live or photographed child subject becomes stiff, guarded, or performative under adult direction and the goal is to capture their natural personality **THEN** reduce pose authority, meet the child on their own terms, and interact until an unforced expression emerges **ELSE** use direct posing only when a controlled expression matters more than spontaneous character.

## Do
- Treat rapport as part of reference acquisition rather than waiting for a natural expression to appear under a rigid pose.
- Give the child enough freedom to behave like themselves while you observe changes in expression.
- Capture the useful expression when it appears instead of demanding that the child hold it unnaturally.
- Prefer the expression that serves the intended picture, including quiet repose when the subject or story calls for it.

## Don't
- Assume stronger adult direction will automatically produce a more authentic child expression.
- Keep pushing a child who becomes afraid, resentful, shut down, or visibly unlike themselves.
- Replace structural observation with a generic symbol of childhood such as a forced grin.

## Checklist
- The expression looks spontaneous rather than commanded.
- The child still reads as the same individual rather than as a stock happy-child symbol.
- The captured reference supports the intended mood of the picture.

## Notes
Loomis frames this as a practical drawing-and-photography problem: adult authority changes the face being observed. The reusable lesson is not that every child portrait must be cheerful; it is that natural expression depends partly on the conditions under which the reference is obtained.
