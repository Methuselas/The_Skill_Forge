# Construct Only the Hidden Path Visible Forms Require

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an obscured limb or digit must explain a visible endpoint, emerging fragment, attachment, balance relation, or later overlap
**THEN** draw through the minimum hidden chain needed to make those visible decisions reliable
**ELSE** stop when the exposed form already supplies sufficient size, direction, and identity

## Do
- Reconstruct the full hidden bend when the unseen route determines where the knee, elbow, hand, foot, or following fragment must emerge.
- Use corresponding visible members and adjacent forms to set the hidden member's width and attachment.
- Keep hidden construction light in Stage 1, then add only the Stage 2 volume needed to validate the visible result.
- Let a correctly sized foot, heel, knee, forearm, hand, or finger fragment imply the rest when no later form depends on the exact route.

## Don't
- Invent an entire concealed limb merely because a limb must exist somewhere behind the body.
- Skip the hidden route when the visible endpoint cannot be placed consistently without it.
- Let two separate visible fragments imply incompatible versions of the same hidden chain.
- Expose extra construction in the final image after its explanatory job is complete.

## Checklist
- Every visible fragment belongs to one continuous parent chain.
- The hidden construction is no longer than the dependencies require.
- A later attachment or emerging form does not force an unplanned bend behind the occluder.
- Removing the hidden guide leaves a visible result that still implies the complete member.

## Notes
Hogarth calls the use of visible evidence to understand blocked members rational inference. Guided review turns that into a minimum-sufficient-construction rule. Sometimes the minimum is only a visible thigh or foot; sometimes it is the entire hidden limb. The deciding factor is not how much is unseen, but how many visible decisions depend on the unseen route.
