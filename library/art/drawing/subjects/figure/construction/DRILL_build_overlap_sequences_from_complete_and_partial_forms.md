# Build Overlap Sequences From Complete and Partial Forms

## Practice Task
Draw eight small depth studies: four abstract mass chains and four simplified figure passages in which forms progressively cover one another.

## Target Skill
Arranging complete and obscured masses into a readable near-to-far sequence.

## Setup
Divide a page into eight boxes. Use only simple ovoids, cylinders, wedges, centerlines, and light contour.

## Instructions
1. In the first two boxes, draw three to five complete forms with no interposition; note how they remain close to the picture plane.
2. Redraw those groups so the nearest form is most complete and each following mass is partially hidden in a numbered recession.
3. Make two additional abstract chains that turn, rise, or descend through space while preserving an unmistakable order.
4. For the last four boxes, use simplified arm, leg, torso-limb, and crossed-limb arrangements. Show only the visible portion of each obscured member, but keep its direction and attachment believable.
5. Remove any label or number and check whether the order still reads from the drawing alone.

## Success Check
- Every overlapped chain has one unambiguous nearest mass and a readable progression behind it.
- Partial members still feel complete beyond the occlusion.
- The figure passages gain depth without losing their parent attachments.

## Common Failures
- Leaving all forms equally complete and relying on size alone.
- Hiding so much of a member that its identity or direction disappears.
- Creating contradictory contour crossings that reverse the intended order.

## Notes
The source begins with abstract arrays before applying the same logic to the figure. That progression isolates the spatial effect first, then tests whether the artist can preserve a believable body when the visible information becomes partial.
