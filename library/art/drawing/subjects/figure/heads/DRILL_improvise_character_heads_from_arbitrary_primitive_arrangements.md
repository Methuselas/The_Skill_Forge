# Improvise Character Heads From Arbitrary Primitive Arrangements

## Practice Task
Create eight character heads that begin from **unplanned arrangements of two or three simple balls/circles** rather than from a predetermined face design.

## Target Skill
Using arbitrary primitive relationships as a Search prompt, then applying head construction as Control so the accidental suggestion becomes a readable three-dimensional character instead of a flat doodle.

## Setup
Divide one page into eight boxes. In each box, place two or three circles/balls of noticeably different sizes and overlaps before deciding what the face will become.

## Instructions
1. Place the primitive masses first. Vary size, overlap, spacing, and which mass is dominant.
2. Look for an implied face direction in the arrangement and add a centerline that turns around the proposed head rather than merely splitting the page shape.
3. Assign the masses useful structural jobs: cranial volume, facial projection, cheek/muzzle mass, jaw, or another clearly attached head form.
4. Add only enough eye, nose, mouth, ear, and jaw information to prove the discovered character. Keep those features subordinate to the mass arrangement.
5. Check that the final contour can be explained by the underlying solids. If a feature requires an impossible attachment or a centerline jump, rebuild the masses rather than hiding the problem.
6. On the last two heads, deliberately reject the first obvious reading and search for a second plausible character in the same primitive arrangement before choosing one.

## Success Check
- All eight heads arise from visibly different primitive arrangements rather than one repeated template.
- The centerline and feature placement agree with the chosen three-dimensional turn.
- At least six heads remain readable as coherent heads after the construction lines are mentally removed.
- The final character differences come from mass relationships, not only hair, accessories, or expression.

## Common Failures
- Deciding the face first and arranging the circles afterward to justify it.
- Treating the circles as flat graphic bubbles with no shared orientation.
- Adding features that ignore the centerline or float independently of the supporting masses.
- Keeping every accidental arrangement even when construction cannot make it coherent.
- Producing eight cosmetic variants of the same underlying head.

## Notes
Loomis's pp. 28-30 “trick stuff” and “stunt” exercises deliberately let arbitrary overlapping balls suggest a head before the artist knows the final character. The reusable lesson is a bounded Search/Control exercise: chance arrangement can generate possibilities, but the result still has to survive three-dimensional head construction before it is accepted.
