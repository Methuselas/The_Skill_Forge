# Resolve the Head and Neck Last From Context

## Pattern Rule
**IF** the torso, support, and arms already establish the body action and the temporary head indication now needs commitment
**THEN** choose the head direction that serves the scene, gaze, target, or narrative while remaining within the limited movement allowed by the existing neck attachment and shoulder girdle
**ELSE** keep the Step 1 head oval temporary and do not stretch or redesign the neck to force a preferred head pose

## Do
- Let context decide what the character must look toward, react to, avoid, or address.
- Test more than one physically possible head direction against the stable body when the composition remains uncertain.
- Seat the neck into the upper torso first, then rotate and tilt the ball-and-wedge head within that actual range.
- Preserve a deliberate look away only when it strengthens the narrative action.

## Don't
- Resolve the head first and force the body or neck to accommodate it.
- Treat the neck like an extensible limb with unrestricted reach or rotation.
- Aim the face away from the subject's target when the action requires visual engagement.
- Use an anatomically possible head position that contradicts the requested composition.

## Checklist
- The head direction communicates the required context or intention.
- The neck length, attachment, tilt, and rotation remain plausible from the established torso.
- Replacing the chosen head with another trial would not require rebuilding the body action.
- The head completes the figure rather than becoming a separate competing gesture.

## Notes
"Head last" means last to be resolved, not absent from the framework. A simple Step 1 oval and axis may establish proportion, but they carry no final authority. The body defines the available neck connection; the composition selects the best permitted head direction.
