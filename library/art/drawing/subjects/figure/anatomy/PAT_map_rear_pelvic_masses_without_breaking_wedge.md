# Map Rear Pelvic Masses Without Breaking the Wedge

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a rear-view pelvic wedge needs specific gluteal form during the rough stage
**THEN** keep the wedge intact and lay the upper hip masses and lower buttock masses as a conditional butterfly rhythm across its surface
**ELSE** leave the surface pattern subtle and let the wedge carry the construction

## Do
- Place the upper wing rhythm beneath the arched hip region and the lower close-set rhythm around the buttock masses.
- Increase the visibility of the pattern only when body mass or muscular development makes those volumes prominent.
- Use the centerline and wedge boundary to keep all four surface accents on one load-bearing pelvis.

## Don't
- Build four separate lobes and call them the pelvis.
- Force the same strong butterfly into every lean rear view.
- Add extra diagonals that imply fractures, shifted sockets, or disconnected hip fragments.

## Checklist
- The pelvic wedge remains readable before and after the surface rhythms are added.
- The pattern can be reduced or removed without changing the pelvis orientation.
- No surface line creates a competing joint boundary.

## Notes
The butterfly is a recognition aid for rear pelvic surface masses, not the universal block-in. Guided review narrows its use: it is often subtle, becoming conspicuous mainly in fuller or strongly muscular builds.

`VAR_bridgman_anchor_hip_surface_to_iliac_crest` retains **Anchor Changing Hip Surface to the Iliac Crest** as a bounded alternative; use it only under the conditions recorded in the variant metadata.
