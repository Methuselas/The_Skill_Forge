# Project the Hidden Torso Skeleton Into Living Form

## Practice Task
Train anatomical construction from the inside out so rib-cage and pelvic structure remain available beneath the living surface instead of being recalled as detached atlas facts.

## Target Skill
Practice the named decision until the structural result can be repeated deliberately without relying on surface copying.

## Setup
No special setup required.

## Instructions
1. Study or model a rib cage and pelvis, then draw them from several views. Before anatomical detail, set the **spatial axes** and reduce each mass to a simple solid that makes the view unmistakable.
2. Draw two or three imagined torso-skeleton views, including a three-quarter view and a foreshortened view. Prioritize spatial and functional coherence over small anatomical precision.
3. Choose a live-model or photographic torso reference. Mark only reliable surface landmarks and the major torso axis.
4. Construct the hidden rib cage and pelvis **inside** the observed body. Use corresponding left/right skeletal points and the visible landmarks to test their placement.
5. Join those skeletal cores through the flexible waist, then add the abdominal wall and other surface masses as layers resting on or bridging the internal structure.
6. Remove or suppress most of the internal scaffold. Check whether the final surface still implies the same rib-cage/pelvis orientation and whether compression, stretch, and overlap can be explained by that hidden structure.

## Success Check
- The rib cage and pelvis can be inferred beneath the live contour without distorting the reference.
- Foreshortened torso views remain spatially coherent even when anatomical detail is reduced.
- Surface anatomy explains the relationship of the skeletal cores rather than floating independently over them.
- Removing the internal construction does not destroy the torso's orientation.

## Common Failures
- Copying the visible torso contour first and fitting a skeleton into it afterward.
- Chasing rib-by-rib or pelvic detail before the axes and large skeletal cores agree.
- Treating the rib cage and pelvis as one rigid block.
- Using anatomy labels to compensate for an unresolved view or volume.

## Notes
Bammes explicitly prioritizes the realization of **spatial and functional events** over anxious anatomical precision during this exercise. The anatomy becomes more exact only after the internal construction is spatially convincing.
