# Route Animal Gesture Through a Governing Action Line

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an animal's parts are recognizable but the pose feels stiff, segmented, or directionless at the gesture stage
**THEN** establish the dominant whole-body sweep as a line of action and make the neck, trunk, tail, and selected limb paths continue, redirect, oppose, or accent that governing flow instead of behaving as unrelated curves
**ELSE** when the pose genuinely contains several competing actions, coordinate a small hierarchy of directional lines rather than forcing one decorative S-curve through the whole animal

## Do
- Find the broad directional sweep before detailed anatomy or surface contour. Reduce the pose far enough that one long line can explain its main travel, bend, or recoil.
- Treat the neck and tail as active directional members. Either can extend the principal flow; in another pose, a tail can work as a separate accent while a leg or trunk passage leads into the main action.
- Change the line language when the movement changes. A straighter passage can interrupt an over-curved rhythm, and a reversed arc can make a turn or counterforce legible instead of letting every contour continue the same way.
- Use repeated directions only when they strengthen the common rhythm. Let opposing belly, leg, neck, or tail lines answer the main sweep when that contrast belongs to the actual pose.
- In a launch or stretch, let the head, body, and reaching limbs participate in the same extension so the action reads as a whole-body event rather than as limbs flared around a passive trunk.

## Don't
- Do not trace each body region with an attractive local curve and expect the pieces to add up to one gesture.
- Do not make every edge equally wavy or graceful; uniform curvature can flatten structure and weaken changes of direction.
- Do not force the neck or tail into the main sweep when it works better as an accent, counterdirection, or independent secondary action.
- Do not turn Hultgren's compositional maxims into literal laws. Opposition, repetition, straight-versus-curve contrast, and horizontal/vertical/diagonal variety are tools to clarify the present action, not mandatory ingredients in every drawing.

## Checklist
- One dominant directional sweep can be traced through at least two major regions of the animal.
- Neck, trunk, tail, and important limb paths have a deliberate relationship to that sweep rather than separate decorative rhythms.
- Any reversal, straight passage, repetition, or opposing line corresponds to a real change in action, structure, or emphasis.
- The animal's direction and energy remain readable when anatomy and texture are reduced to a simple gesture or silhouette.
- A stretched or launching pose carries extension through the body as well as the extremities.

## Notes
Hultgren develops the lesson from simple straight and curved line families into animal examples. He first uses arcs to suggest movement, then shows straighter passages, reversed arcs, repetition, and opposition to prevent uniform flow. He next makes the line of action explicit and demonstrates that a long neck or tail may either join the main sweep or become an accent while another part leads the action. The final page pushes a takeoff stretch and separately tracks which leg is actually receiving weight. This Pattern keeps the whole-animal directional method while leaving precise support timing to the existing center-of-gravity skill.

`VAR_hultgren_weave_feline_masses_with_connecting_accents` retains Hultgren's cat-family mass-weaving alternative: when a clear action line still leaves the blocked body segmented, carry a few structurally true curves and connector accents through the major junctions instead of solving rhythm only with one centerline.

`VAR_hultgren_move_elephant_bulk_with_hind_leg_trunk_and_head_opposition` adds the u19 elephant route: let the hind legs and trunk carry major parts of the action, then use head-versus-body opposition only when it clarifies the pose. The variant is for making large elephant mass participate in gesture, not for inferring a universal gait formula from Hultgren's action drawings.

`VAR_hultgren_carry_bear_action_through_outer_forms_and_rear_to_front_leg` adds the u20 bear route: once the compact outer forms are established, let a rear-leg-to-front-leg action path travel through the body so the weighty mass joins the gesture rather than separating the limbs. It is a pose-reading method, not a fixed bear gait formula.
