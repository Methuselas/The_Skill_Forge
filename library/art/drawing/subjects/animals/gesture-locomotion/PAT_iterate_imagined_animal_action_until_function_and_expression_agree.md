# Iterate Imagined Animal Action Until Function and Expression Agree

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an invented animal pose is attractive or energetic but the intended event does not read convincingly from the gesture and body mechanics
**THEN** keep the idea in working-study mode, rebuild it from solid basic masses, diagnose what action the current configuration actually communicates, vary or exaggerate the functional relationships, and repeat until the intended action and expression agree before transferring the solved form into a finished version
**ELSE** preserve the convincing action and use the final pass to reinforce emphasis rather than rediscover the pose.

## Do
- Judge the first sketch by what it communicates, not by whether it is polished or appealing. An uncorrected first idea is allowed to be clumsy if it exposes the problem clearly.
- Rebuild the action with basic three-dimensional volumes. Make the trunk, girdles, limbs, and major overlaps concrete enough that the imagined motion has physical consequences.
- Name the mismatch in plain terms. If a collapsing animal still reads as running, jumping, or rearing, treat that reading as evidence rather than defending the intended story.
- Change the relationships that carry the event: support, limb direction, compression, extension, tilt, overlap, and the configuration of the major masses. Exaggerate when stronger contrast clarifies the action, but keep the result functionally plausible for the animal.
- When one variation fails, restart from reduced elements rather than decorating the same weak solution indefinitely. A new arrangement is preferable to polished ambiguity.
- Let textures, blots, or a different medium provoke alternatives when useful, but re-test the actual action afterward; an interesting surface is not proof that the gesture is solved.
- Once a working study is both expressive and functionally convincing, separate discovery from finish. Transfer the resolved form to the final drawing and use the final pass to reinforce the chosen points of emphasis.

## Don't
- Do not assume the subject reads correctly merely because you know what you meant to draw.
- Do not render, color, or texture a pose whose support and action still communicate the wrong event.
- Do not treat exaggeration as free distortion; the intensified components should clarify what is happening functionally.
- Do not preserve the first silhouette out of attachment to the idea when a new block-in communicates the event better.
- Do not make the final drawing carry the full burden of discovering the form. Solve the structural and expressive problem in working studies first.

## Checklist
- A viewer can identify the intended event from the animal's pose without being told the prompt.
- Major masses are solid enough that the action can be explained through support, direction, compression, and overlap.
- At least one unsuccessful attempt produced a named diagnosis rather than being discarded as merely "bad."
- Variations materially change the action-bearing relationships instead of adding only surface differences.
- The chosen working study is both expressive and mechanically believable before the final pass begins.
- The final version reinforces the solved emphasis rather than changing the underlying event again.

## Notes
Bammes develops the collapsing-horse theme as a diagnostic sequence rather than a march toward polish. Figure 79a records the first imagined idea without correcting it for attractiveness. Figure 79b increases contrast, yet Bammes explicitly rejects it because the horse still reads too much as running away. Figures 81-83 then return to basic volumes, three-dimensional solidity, altered gesture, and a new configuration whose components better express the function of collapse.

Figure 84 makes the production lesson explicit: the discovery of form belongs in the working study so the final drawing can reinforce emphasis. The reusable decision is therefore **diagnosis-led iteration**. Intended story does not outrank visible evidence; when function and expression disagree, re-block and restage the animal before finish.
