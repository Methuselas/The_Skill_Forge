# Block a Quadruped From the Dorsal Axis and Three Body Masses

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a four-legged animal is being blocked and its major torso proportions or limb attachments are still uncertain
**THEN** establish the dorsal/spinal route through the pose, organize the body around connected forequarter, middle trunk/belly, and hindquarter masses, and place the major limb pivots against those masses before refining the outer contour
**ELSE** use a subject-appropriate mass scaffold rather than forcing the three-region quadruped division onto another body plan.

## Do
- Let one dorsal route coordinate the front, middle, and rear regions so the animal reads as one body rather than three adjacent lumps.
- Compare the length, height, width, and bulk of the three regions before committing to surface anatomy; their relationship carries much of the animal's proportion.
- Locate the shoulder/forequarter and hip/hindquarter attachments before expanding the legs into full volume.
- Keep the middle trunk/belly region connected to both end masses while allowing its visible compression, stretch, and overlap to follow the pose.
- Change the proportions and shapes of the masses to fit the animal being observed; the scaffold is a construction order, not a universal mannequin.

## Don't
- Divide every quadruped into three equal ovals or bands.
- Let a finished silhouette relocate the shoulder, hip, or limb pivots after the structural block is established.
- Copy one species' forequarter-to-hindquarter proportions onto another merely because the construction vocabulary is shared.
- Treat the dorsal guide as a compulsory finished contour instead of a temporary organizing line.

## Checklist
- The dorsal route and the three connected body regions are readable before fur, muscles, or small contour changes are added.
- Each forelimb and hindlimb has one clear parent region and a plausible pivot chain.
- Removing surface detail still leaves a proportionally specific quadruped rather than a generic three-part tube.
- The middle region joins the two end masses without an unexplained gap or rigid connector.

## Notes
Hultgren's opening construction divides the body into forequarters, belly, and rear quarters, then uses a dorsal stripe and skeletal indications to keep the animal centered and proportioned. The later image-led examples in the same unit apply the scaffold to several differently proportioned quadrupeds. This Pattern therefore specializes the existing general mass-blocking foundation for quadruped construction without claiming that one fixed mass recipe fits every animal.

`VAR_hultgren_construct_long_haired_dog_from_hidden_core_and_sweeping_coat` adds the u14 long-coated-dog route: keep the hidden body and head construction legible first, then lay the coat over that scaffold with long sweeping line groups. Hultgren's collie is specifically lean beneath its hair; that observation is not generalized to every long-haired breed.

`VAR_hultgren_block_rabbit_from_head_chest_and_lower_body_then_bunch_or_spread` adds the u17 rabbit route: simplify the body to head, chest, and lower-body/hindquarter units, then preserve how those units bunch together in compact poses and spread through extended action. Hultgren's “double their length” wording is retained only as an exaggeration cue for visible extension, not a literal anatomical ratio.
