# Construct the Animal Nose-Mouth Region as a Coupled Soft-Tissue System

## Pattern Rule
**IF** the nose and mouth of an animal are being placed as separate symbols on a generic muzzle
**THEN** construct them as one coupled soft-tissue system whose projection and shape are supported by nasal cartilage, jaw and teeth, connective tissue, lip mass, loose cheek skin, and the species' feeding or scent function
**ELSE** keep the simpler muzzle block when those support relationships are already implicit and convincing.

## Do
- Compare how far the nose, upper lip, and lower lip project relative to one another before drawing nostril or mouth detail.
- Let cartilage and connective tissue give the soft nose its own volume instead of painting nostrils onto the end of the skull block.
- Use the jaw and tooth support to explain bulges around the lips, canines, and corners of the mouth where they are visible.
- Treat loose chops or spare skin as functional soft mass that changes the mouth opening rather than as arbitrary hanging contour.
- Adapt nostril direction, lip thickness, nose width, and muzzle projection to the actual species and individual.

## Don't
- Do not place a standard dog, horse, cow, or cat nose shape on every muzzle of that family.
- Do not separate nose design from the jaw, lip, and feeding/scent structures directly beneath it.
- Do not copy exact nostril silhouettes or breed extremes as universal anatomy.
- Do not let surface markings replace the underlying coupled nose-mouth volume.

## Checklist
- Nose, lips, jaw support, and mouth corner read as one connected volume system.
- The nose and lip project by species-appropriate amounts instead of sharing one generic end plane.
- Tooth/jaw support explains major local swellings where present.
- Nostril and mouth details sit on a coherent muzzle volume.
- The construction remains adjustable to breed, individual, and viewpoint.

## Notes
Bammes explicitly treats nose and mouth as mutually dependent in function and form. His horse, cow, dog, and big-cat examples differ in nasal cartilage, nostril direction, lip projection, loose cheek skin, canine support, and the relationship between the nose and mouth fissure. The portable skill is therefore to build one coupled support-and-soft-tissue system and then specialize it, not to memorize the source's listed nostril silhouettes.
