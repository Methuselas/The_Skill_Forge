# Establish Animal Type From a Proportional Ensemble

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an animal construction is recognizable only after paws, hooves, coat, horns, or other local identifiers are added
**THEN** establish the interrelated proportions of trunk length and height, ground clearance, limb length, neck, head, and major mass placement as one ensemble before descriptive detail, using flexible measurement that follows the actual animal rather than a standardized animal formula
**ELSE** keep proportion checks light when the intended study is deliberately generic and species or type distinction is not part of the task.

## Do
- Judge the largest relationships first: how long the trunk is relative to its height, how high it stands from the ground, and how the limbs, neck, and head scale against that body.
- Treat proportion as a relationship of parts to the whole. A useful change in one region should be checked against the rest of the animal instead of being edited as an isolated dimension.
- Strip away small identifiers temporarily and ask whether the framework and large masses still carry the intended type or conformation.
- Use boxes, modules, or comparison lines as temporary measuring devices only when they expose a relationship that is hard to judge directly.
- Investigate the actual animal, individual, age, and pose when the observed relationships disagree with a remembered formula or source example.

## Don't
- Do not force different animals into one standardized basic formula and then try to recover their identity with surface traits.
- Do not memorize a few fixed ratios and apply them before looking at the subject; Bammes explicitly rejects rigid a priori proportion rules in favor of investigation.
- Do not enlarge or shorten one feature in isolation while leaving the rest of the proportional ensemble unchanged when the intended type depends on several linked relationships.
- Do not treat functional explanations attached to one representative animal type as exhaustive biological laws for every member of a category.

## Checklist
- The intended animal type or designed conformation remains legible after most surface detail is removed.
- Trunk length-to-height, ground clearance, limb length, neck length, and head scale agree as one coherent set rather than as independent edits.
- Any measuring box or module can be discarded without the construction collapsing.
- A contradictory observed proportion causes the scaffold to change instead of being ignored to preserve a remembered canon.
- Local identifiers refine an already readable whole rather than rescuing a generic body.

## Notes
Bammes treats proportion as a first-priority description of animal form because the relationships of the parts can make the overall type recognizable before details such as hoof versus paw are inspected. His opening contrast is deliberately large-scale: a horse can read as a comparatively square arrangement while a cow can read as a lower rectangle. The point PASS retains is the hierarchy of evidence, not those examples as fixed species ratios.

The source also connects proportional builds with different ways of life, contrasting low, elongated stalking hunters with taller pursuit hunters and discussing running herbivores and primates. Those comparisons are useful prompts for structural analysis, but several of the causal statements are broader than this unit can justify as universal biology. Use them to ask which relationships matter in a given animal, then verify the actual subject.

`VAR_bammes_measure_animal_type_with_head_module_and_trunk_rectangle` adds the measured profile route developed across u06-u07: use head length as a temporary module, establish front height, trunk length, rear height, the enclosing rectangle, belly level, neck length, and head direction in that order. Use it when a side-view build is difficult to compare or the eye needs calibration. Bammes then explicitly releases the scaffold: estimate the same large relationships and return to exact measurement only when uncertainty remains. Drop the method when the view is strongly foreshortened or the module starts dictating the animal instead of measuring it.

`VAR_bammes_establish_animal_proportion_with_broad_overlapping_shapes` adds the complementary fast route from u08. Instead of measuring with a fine point, use the side of a broad medium to state the trunk as a large shape and swivel it into simple overlapping limb shapes. Use this when speed and whole-shape judgment matter more than exact modular calibration; abandon it when the flat pieces begin to substitute for the spatial or anatomical construction the task actually requires.

`VAR_bammes_use_backline_profile_as_type_signature` adds the u23 backline check: compare the highest point and the sequence of rises, falls, flats, and angle changes from withers through thoracolumbar transition and sacrum/croup before relying on coat or local identifiers. Use it as a type-calibration cue in a readable view, but let flexion, extension, sitting, jumping, or twisting override any memorized neutral profile.

`VAR_bammes_use_thorax_section_and_front_rear_expansion_as_type_signature` adds the u24 thorax-core check: compare width versus depth, shoulder-side compression versus posterior expansion, and major asymmetry or squareness as part of the type ensemble. Use it when a side silhouette is insufficient, but verify the actual animal instead of treating Bammes's comparative section diagrams as fixed species laws.
