# Build Animal Form From Skeletal Landmarks to Planes

## Practice Task
Choose one clearly visible animal region whose skeleton and major muscle groups have already been studied; begin with the hindquarter or hindleg if possible. Construct it from skeletal reference points into a planar solid study, then make one abbreviated repeat that preserves only the most important constructional information.

## Target Skill
Converting observed animal anatomy into a structurally convincing planar study without relying on copied outer contour or decorative shading.

## Setup
Use a reference in which the region's orientation and main landmarks are readable. Keep the relevant skeleton study nearby for the first attempt. Pencil is appropriate for the strict construction pass; a small full flat brush, crayon, or another broad tool may be used for the abbreviated repeat.

## Instructions
1. Mark the main skeletal reference points first and connect them so the hidden framework has a clear direction before any outer contour is developed.
2. From those marks, establish the viewing angle and identify the few large planes that describe the region in space: top/roof-like planes, outer and inner side planes, rear planes, and any major joint-facing changes that matter to the view.
3. State the first solid with concise straight or nearly straight construction lines. Delay soft contour curvature until the spatial relationships are clear; use the same discipline on narrow forms such as the tibia or distal foot.
4. Build the large masses around the framework and emphasize where important planes converge. Keep the study relatively block-like and unfaceted rather than fragmenting it into small anatomical details.
5. Clarify selected hollows and plane changes. Add hatching whose direction agrees with the plane angle, and place a few cross-section lines where they help you think around the volume.
6. Stop before painterly light-and-shade effects take over the study. The proof is the organized form, not a naturalistic rendering finish.
7. Compare the construction against the reference and name one place where outline-copying, lost skeletal anchoring, or uncertain plane direction weakened the result.
8. Make one faster repeat with a small flat brush, crayon, or similarly decisive tool. Preserve the same skeletal and planar logic while abbreviating secondary information; do not merely redraw the contour faster.

## Success Check
- The region can be understood from skeletal points and large planes even if the final contour is partly removed.
- Plane convergence, hatching direction, and any cross-sections agree with one three-dimensional orientation.
- The strict pass is solid without depending on decorative value modeling.
- Narrow limb segments remain constructed rather than collapsing into flat strips.
- The abbreviated repeat is more economical while preserving the same structural relationships.

## Common Failures
- Starting from the silhouette and trying to infer the skeleton afterward.
- Curving every contour immediately, which makes direction changes vague before the form is solved.
- Faceting every small muscle instead of organizing the few large structural planes.
- Using hatching as generic tone rather than as evidence for plane direction.
- Adding attractive light and shade to hide an unresolved construction.
- Making the second pass faster by omitting structure instead of abbreviating structure.

## Notes
Bammes completes the first-phase living-hindleg assignment from §5.2 by turning skeletal reference points into a constructional study of the living form. On printed pp. 57-60 he emphasizes large plane convergence, straight construction, selected cross-sections, and hatching that clarifies spatial gradients while explicitly separating this exercise from painterly light-and-shade rendering. The neighboring zebu and panther studies then show the pedagogical direction of travel: intensive structural observation can be compressed into increasingly fluent brush or line records once the construction has actually been learned.

The drill therefore has two linked passes: **strict structural proof first, abbreviated fluency second**. The abbreviation is earned by the first pass; it is not permission to skip the framework.
