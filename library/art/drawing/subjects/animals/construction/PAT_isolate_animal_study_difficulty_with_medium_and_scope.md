# Isolate Animal Study Difficulty With Medium and Scope

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an animal repose or movement study is failing because proportion, articulation, speed, medium handling, and expression are all being solved at once
**THEN** reduce the exercise to the smallest difficulty that needs training and choose a medium or construction aid whose normal behavior makes that one problem easier to see and repeat
**ELSE** recombine proportion, movement, and fuller description only after the separate demands can be handled without one masking another.

## Do
- Decide what the current exercise is actually training before choosing the tool. Bammes separates large-mass repose, articulated proportion-and-movement, rapid movement expression, and freer life/imagination study instead of forcing one procedure onto all four.
- Use a broad dry material when rapid concentration of the larger masses is the target; its width suppresses small detail and encourages a few decisive shapes.
- Reuse a movable proportion block when the main problem is changing pose while preserving segment proportion and pivot placement.
- When proportion plus locomotion is too demanding, temporarily study movement alone rather than producing a confused result that trains neither skill clearly.
- Use a loaded watercolor brush when the exercise needs speed, continuity, shorthand, and concentrated observation of movement rather than careful contour correction.
- Return to more combined studies after the isolated difficulty has become controllable; the simplification is a training condition, not the final artistic ceiling.

## Don't
- Do not choose a medium merely because it is familiar when its handling encourages the exact distraction the exercise is meant to suppress.
- Do not add proportional measurement, detailed anatomy, surface texture, and polished contour to a motion exercise whose purpose is rapid movement expression.
- Do not interpret a simplified study as proof that the omitted information is unimportant; it is being withheld so one problem can be trained deliberately.
- Do not keep raising the number of simultaneous demands when the current combination is already overtaxing the study.

## Checklist
- The exercise names one primary difficulty before drawing starts.
- The chosen medium or aid naturally favors the information being trained and discourages at least one competing distraction.
- The study can be repeated several times without becoming slower through added detail.
- Failure can be attributed to the target skill rather than to several unrelated problems happening at once.
- More complex proportion-and-motion work is reintroduced only after the isolated practice produces a stable result.

## Notes
Bammes opens §4.3 by deliberately minimizing difficulty. Broad chalk concentrates large masses, the movable proportion block links known proportions to pose changes, watercolor brushwork forces quick continuous movement notation, and pointed tools permit a freer approach from life or imagination. He explicitly says that when proportion plus movement is overtaxing, movement may be isolated first. The transferable lesson is a practice-design decision: choose the exercise scope and medium so the target problem becomes clearer instead of asking one study to solve everything simultaneously.
