# Map Primate Arm Mobility From Girdle to Hand Axes

## Practice Task
Draw one simplified primate arm three times: first as a bone chain, second with each major joint axis marked, and third as an axis-only abstraction. Then rotate the forearm from a more supinated to a more pronated state while keeping the shoulder and elbow relationships readable.

## Target Skill
Separating and coordinating the primate shoulder-girdle, shoulder, elbow, radio-ulnar, wrist, and hand axes so a change in hand orientation can be reconstructed through the whole arm.

## Setup
Use a simple front or three-quarter arm skeleton with a visible clavicle, scapula, humerus, radius, ulna, carpus, metacarpus, and thumb. Keep the first repetitions skeletal; do not add muscle rendering.

## Instructions
1. Draw the scapula and clavicle as the proximal base. Mark the internal clavicle joint near the sternum and the external clavicle/shoulder connection before placing the free arm.
2. Add the humerus and mark the shoulder's principal spatial axes rather than one circular socket symbol. Keep the arm's long turning direction visible from the shoulder downward.
3. At the elbow, draw the flexion-extension cross-axis separately from the forearm's longitudinal turning axis. Bend the elbow slightly so the distinction cannot hide in a straight limb.
4. Draw radius and ulna first in a more open relationship, then repeat the forearm with the radius crossing as the hand pronates. Show the turn at both the proximal and distal radio-ulnar relationships instead of twisting only the distal end.
5. Mark the wrist cross-axis and its side-to-side deviation direction. Redirect the hand once with wrist flexion-extension and once with thumb-side or little-finger-side deviation while keeping the forearm rotation unchanged.
6. Add the metacarpus, fingers, and thumb. Test one reach and one grip so the thumb opposition and finger independence read as the distal result of the whole arm chain rather than as an isolated hand exercise.
7. On the final repetition, remove most bone contours and keep only the girdle directions, total arm turning axis, elbow cross-axis, radio-ulnar turn, wrist axes, and a simple hand wedge. Redraw the same pronation change from that reduced map.

## Success Check
- The clavicle/scapula base is present before the humerus and does not behave like a fixed shoulder dot.
- Elbow flexion and forearm rotation remain visibly separate operations.
- Pronating the hand changes the radius-ulna relationship at both ends of the forearm.
- Wrist deviation can be changed without pretending that it created the forearm's pronation.
- The reduced axis-only version still predicts the final hand facing and thumb side correctly.

## Common Failures
- Marking every axis through one shoulder point and losing the two-part girdle.
- Drawing a crossed radius but leaving the hand in the old facing.
- Rotating the wrist to fake pronation while the radius and ulna stay unchanged.
- Treating the elbow's hinge axis as the same line as the forearm's longitudinal turning axis.
- Adding detailed fingers before the shoulder-to-wrist axis chain is stable.
- Copying one finished ape arm silhouette instead of rebuilding the rotation from the skeletal relationships.

## Notes
