# Construct a Horse Skeleton From Spine, Rib Cage, and Girdles

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a horse drawing needs enough internal structure to support believable construction, action, or later anatomy
**THEN** build the skeleton from a continuous spinal route and rib cage, attach the skull, shoulder blade and forelimb chain, then add the rear pelvic plate and hindlimb chain before surface forms
**ELSE** use the lighter quadruped mass-and-pivot scaffold when exact horse structure is not needed.

## Do
- Start with the spinal column as one flexible tapering route so neck, back, and tail belong to the same action before individual vertebral landmarks are added.
- Build the rib cage onto that route, then use the higher structural points over the fore and rear regions to organize where the limb-bearing structures attach.
- Attach the scapula around the outside contour of the rib cage, place the humeral socket from that blade, and continue one uninterrupted forelimb chain through elbow, foreleg, and hoof.
- Add the rear pelvic plate as a three-dimensional attachment rather than a flat hip symbol, then build the hindlimb downward from its socket through the visible bends to the hoof.
- Compare the resulting skeleton against the living horse from front, rear, side, and action views; let the same joints explain both the bony study and the finished surface.

## Don't
- Start with a finished horse silhouette and fit the bones inside afterward.
- Hang the foreleg directly from the rib cage while ignoring the scapular bridge that Hultgren places around it.
- Reduce the horse leg to a generic human-style knee-and-ankle rhythm when the observed joint chain and hoof placement show a different sequence.
- Memorize one rigid standing skeleton and reuse it unchanged in crouching, rearing, or running poses.

## Checklist
- The spine, rib cage, skull, scapula, pelvis, and all four limb chains can be traced as one connected system before muscle or coat is added.
- The forelimb visibly attaches through the scapula and humeral socket rather than appearing pasted to the chest.
- The rear limb can be followed from pelvic socket to hoof without a missing or invented joint.
- Front, rear, and side studies preserve the same underlying attachments even though their visible contours change.

## Notes
Hultgren develops the horse from inside outward: spinal column, rib cage, simplified vertebral landmarks, skull, scapula, forelimb, rear plate, and hindlimb. He repeatedly pairs bone studies with living drawings so the construction is judged by whether it explains the visible animal. The sequence is kept as a horse-specific specialization of the broader quadruped scaffold rather than as a universal mammal skeleton.
