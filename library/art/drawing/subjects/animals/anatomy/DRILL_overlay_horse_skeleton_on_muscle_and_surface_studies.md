# Overlay the Horse Skeleton on Muscle and Surface Studies

## Practice Task
Take clear horse references or anatomy studies and repeatedly reconstruct the hidden skeleton through them before copying the visible muscle or coat.

## Target Skill
Seeing how horse muscles and surface masses wrap around a stable hidden skeleton.

## Setup
Use one standing horse and two action poses in which the shoulder, rib cage, pelvis, and major limb bends can be inferred. Include at least one front or rear view so paired left-right structure cannot be solved only from profile contour.

## Instructions
1. Make a light copy or tracing-sized block of the visible horse without finishing texture.
2. Draw the spinal route, rib cage, skull, scapula, pelvic plate, and limb joint chains through the visible body, allowing hidden continuations to pass behind muscle masses.
3. Compare the overlay with a separate skeletal study and correct the attachments before adding more anatomy.
4. On a second pass, place the largest muscle masses over the same bones and mark where they wrap around, bridge between, or change direction at the joints.
5. Remove most of the internal lines and redraw the outer surface from the same pose. Check whether the visible bulges, hollows, and limb directions still make sense without the overlay.
6. Repeat on an action pose and verify that the skeleton moves with the pose rather than staying in the proportions of the first standing study.

## Success Check
- The same skeleton can explain both the anatomy overlay and the final surface drawing.
- Major muscles wrap around or bridge the bones instead of floating as unrelated shapes.
- Joint locations stay stable when the muscle layer is added.
- The action study preserves the same attachment logic while changing pose and foreshortening.

## Common Failures
- Copying the muscle drawing first and placing bones only where gaps remain.
- Treating the skeleton as a flat diagram that does not turn with the rib cage and pelvis.
- Moving joints after the muscle silhouette is established.
- Memorizing named muscles without checking what structure they actually wrap around or connect.

## Notes
Hultgren explicitly recommends drawing the skeleton over the horse's muscle study because doing so reveals where the muscles wrap around the bones. The surrounding pages reinforce the exercise by pairing skeleton and living form in matching poses and views. The drill therefore trains inside-out anatomical reasoning rather than atlas-label recall.
