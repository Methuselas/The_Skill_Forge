# Preserve Clean Paint Color by Limiting Wet Reworking

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a wet-paint passage depends on clean, distinct, high-chroma color and repeated manipulation would physically mix it with neighboring or underlying paint
**THEN** prepare the intended mixture before deposition, place it with as little wet disturbance as practical, and correct the boundary from adjacent paint when that preserves the main color body
**ELSE** blend or rework when the passage actually requires mixed transitions rather than maximum color separation.

## Do
- Decide the target value and hue family before committing a color whose purity matters.
- Protect the main body of a successful deposit while correcting its silhouette from neighboring paint when possible.
- Separate a wrong color decision from a damaged color application; correct the former even if the paint was placed cleanly.
- Use the method with brush, knife, or another paint behavior only when limited disturbance serves the intended result.

## Don't
- Do not turn clean application into a one-stroke fetish.
- Do not preserve an incorrect value, drawing, or hue merely because the deposit is fresh and saturated.
- Do not repeatedly churn a high-chroma passage and then compensate for the lost purity only by adding more pigment.

## Checklist
- The passage's color job actually benefits from separation rather than blending.
- Wet manipulation is not needlessly contaminating the intended mixture.
- Boundary corrections preserve the useful color body where practical.
- The result still agrees with the picture's value and gamut constraints.

## Notes
This is a physical paint-interaction decision, not a general rule that saturated color must always be untouched. It is useful when the act of wet manipulation itself is degrading a color relationship that was otherwise correct.
