# Control Watercolor Wash State With Moisture, Pigment, and Flow

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a transparent watercolor passage depends on fluid wash behavior
**THEN** treat surface wetness, pigment/water concentration, brush load, active liquid edge, gravity, and drying time as one coupled state system; choose and monitor those variables according to the intended spread, value transition, edge, and directional flow
**ELSE** use the general layering, edge, or rendering owners when no watercolor-specific fluid-state control is required.

## Do
- Distinguish useful surface states rather than treating wetness as binary: a saturated or shiny surface permits freer travel and more dilution, a damp surface gives softer but more contained blending, and a dry surface largely stops fluid integration with the preceding wash.
- Compensate pigment load for water already present in the support; the same brush mixture lands weaker and travels farther on a wetter sheet.
- Coordinate brush load with passage size and surface state so dilution, spread, and the active liquid boundary remain manageable together.
- Keep an intended continuous wash coherent while its working edge is active; do not let isolated areas dry accidentally while neighboring areas remain fluid.
- Remove or redistribute excess boundary liquid before it flows back into a settling passage and creates an unintended bloom or tide mark.
- Use support angle deliberately to even a field, encourage a chosen directional run, or oppose a run that exceeds the intended effect; stabilize the support once the target flow is reached.
- Treat support absorbency, thickness, preparation, dimensional stability, and painting size as timing variables because they change how long useful moisture states persist.
- Treat support absorbency as a retention and reversibility variable as well as a timing variable. On less-absorbent or nonabsorbent supports, calibrate apparent pigment strength, lifting ease, layer survival under later water, and whether any applied ground or coating actually adheres before trusting ordinary watercolor-paper assumptions.
- When a support keeps pigment largely surface-bound, exploit that reversibility deliberately when correction or reconstruction is useful, but choose a more retentive support or compatible preparation when accepted layers must survive later wet handling.
- Once a passage has fully dried, treat a later transparent application as a new layer or glaze unless deliberate reactivation, lifting, or rewetting is intended.
- Restore moisture only when doing so serves a known state target rather than as a reflex whenever an area begins to dry.
- When wet-into-wet softness is needed inside one bounded passage, control the geography of the wet state as deliberately as its intensity: wet only the intended region, keep adjacent regions dry when they must remain independent, and let the local field settle to the required moisture window before introducing pigment.
- If a bounded passage is too saturated to hold its intended extent, wait for it to settle; if it has dried beyond the desired soft-edge window, deliberately rewet it or switch to a dry-state operation rather than forcing wet-into-wet behavior into it.
- Let a confined wet passage reach a safe dry state before adding neighboring or overlapping work that must stay separate; support absorbency, thickness, preparation, and passage size determine how long that local working window lasts.
- For texture, decide the target effect first and choose the tool and moisture window together. Very wet surfaces favor broad diffusion or refill, damp surfaces favor more localized disturbance, and dry surfaces favor discrete marks.
- Calibrate uncertain texture interventions because the same salt, knife, dragged brush, spatter, or lifting action can produce a different result at a different point in the drying cycle.

## Don't
- Do not treat all wet paper as one controllable state.
- Do not change dilution without accounting for water already present in the paper and brush.
- Do not keep reworking a settling wash after its useful correction window has closed unless disturbed texture is intentionally wanted.
- Do not let an interesting texture effect erase the larger value, form, directional, or focal structure it is supposed to support.
- Do not assume one universal wash order across a painting; choose sequence from the state relationship each passage needs.
- Do not assume a drawn boundary alone will confine a wet passage; containment comes from the actual geography and moisture state of the wetted support.
- Do not transfer paper-based assumptions about staining, lifting, layer permanence, or correction cost to a less-absorbent support without testing them.
- Do not keep painting over a ground or coating that lifts, separates, or fails under the intended wet handling; treat that medium/support/preparation combination as incompatible and change the system.

## Checklist
- The current surface state is known well enough to predict whether the next mark will spread, contain, lift, or remain discrete.
- Pigment concentration and brush load are appropriate to the water already present in the support.
- Active beads, pools, and gravity are controlled intentionally rather than discovered after the wash settles.
- Dry-state gates are respected before crisp overpainting or glazing that should not reactivate the first passage.
- When only part of the image should interact wet-into-wet, the active wet geography is confined to that passage and neighboring regions remain in the state their later role requires.
- The chosen support has been calibrated for how strongly it retains pigment and how readily accepted layers survive later water; any ground or coating used with it remains mechanically compatible with the intended wash process.
- Texture interventions occur in a moisture window that produces the intended scale and edge character.
- Mixed wash types serve distinct picture jobs and their state dependencies have been planned rather than improvised blindly.

## Notes
Watercolor wash behavior is a coupled physical state, not a list of independent tricks. Moisture in the support changes dilution, spread, edge containment, gravity response, timing, and the availability of later lifting or glazing. Support absorbency also changes **retention and reversibility**: on conventional watercolor paper more pigment can become mechanically or chemically difficult to disturb, while on less-absorbent or nonabsorbent supports more of the deposit may remain surface-bound and vulnerable to later water. Control therefore includes **state intensity** (how wet, damp, or dry the passage is), **state geography** (which parts of the image are currently permitted to be wet and interact), and **state retention** (how securely an accepted deposit will survive the next wet operation). The useful question is not only which named wash to use, but what state the support, brush, pigment, active edge, support angle, local wet region, and retained layer must be in for that passage to behave as intended.

`VAR_garcia_build_gradated_wash_by_progressive_dilution` creates a continuous gradient by starting with the strongest intended transparent mixture and progressively increasing clean water while the wash remains active. On an already wet support, compensate for the additional dilution; if a crossing second gradient or glaze should remain distinct, wait for a safe dry state before applying it.

`VAR_garcia_map_mixed_washes_by_passage_job_and_state_dependency` assigns wash behavior by visual job and then records the state relationship between passages: interact while wet, remain isolated until dry, overpaint after drying, or deliberately rewet into a new flow state. Re-evaluate the whole image after major wash families so technique contrast does not outrank value, focus, or readability.

`VAR_garcia_time_watercolor_texture_intervention_to_surface_state` makes timing part of texture design. An intervention on a glossy wash can spread or refill; the same action on a damp wash can hold a tighter shape; on dry paper it may remain discrete or require deliberate reactivation. Use that state difference deliberately rather than cataloguing texture tools as fixed effects.
