# Capture Action Reference for Motion Legibility Rather Than Photographic Finish

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a camera is being used primarily to analyze an action
**THEN** optimize the capture for readable motion evidence—clear framing, sufficient sharpness and exposure, useful depth, and stable or deliberate camera movement—even when those choices are not aesthetically ideal photographs
**ELSE** let ordinary photographic goals govern when action analysis is not the purpose of the capture.

## Do
- Frame the subject so the body regions, contacts, or trajectories relevant to the action question remain visible.
- Use a short enough exposure when rapid motion must be frozen clearly enough to compare positions or phases.
- Keep enough of the relevant depth in focus that important joints, limbs, contacts, or props do not disappear into blur.
- Accept a technically imperfect image when the compromise preserves more useful movement information than a prettier but less legible capture.
- Keep a fixed camera when a stable coordinate frame will help comparison; when the subject must be followed, pan or track smoothly and deliberately so camera motion is not confused with subject motion.
- Learn the capture setup well enough that equipment handling does not distract from observing or directing the action.

## Don't
- Do not optimize an action-analysis capture for polish, shallow-focus beauty, or dramatic blur when those choices hide the mechanics being studied.
- Do not let random camera shake, erratic reframing, or inconsistent tracking create an extra motion signal that obscures the subject's movement.
- Do not preserve every body region at maximum detail when the research question only depends on a smaller set of landmarks or contacts.
- Do not turn Webster's example shutter, sensitivity, focal-length, or equipment values into fixed rules; the durable requirement is sufficient motion legibility for the task.

## Checklist
- The capture preserves the movement information named by the study question.
- Critical action states are sharp and exposed enough to inspect.
- Relevant body regions remain inside the usable frame and focus range.
- Camera movement is either intentionally fixed or deliberately matched to the subject.
- Photographic polish has not been prioritized over analyzable motion.

## Notes
Treat photography and video for action analysis as research tools rather than finished photographic products. The useful threshold is whether the recording preserves enough information to study the movement. A faster exposure, broader focus, less elegant framing, or other compromise can be the better research choice when it reveals action that would otherwise be lost. Camera behavior is part of the analysis design: a stable frame simplifies spatial comparison, deliberate tracking can keep a traveling subject readable, and accidental camera motion makes subject motion harder to interpret.
