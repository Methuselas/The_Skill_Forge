# Define Study Target Before Choosing Scope, Medium, and Detail

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a study is overloaded, drifting toward finish, or repeatedly failing without revealing what actually needs improvement
**THEN** name the primary skill or relationship being trained first, then choose the subject scope, medium, scale, repetition, and information density that make that target easiest to see, fail, correct, and repeat
**ELSE** allow a more integrated study when the combined demands are already manageable and integration itself is the training goal.

## Do
- Before beginning, state what improvement the study is working **for** in concrete terms, such as better proportion judgment, clearer perspective, stronger edge selection, freer action, or another observable target.
- Diagnose the purpose of the study before choosing how much to draw. Action, proportion, construction, light, value, color, edge, anatomy, surface, or detail require different amounts and kinds of information.
- Reduce simultaneous demands when they mask the target. A study is allowed to omit information that would be necessary in finished work if the omission makes the chosen problem clearer.
- Choose a medium whose handling supports the target. Broad or fast tools can suppress detail and expose mass or movement; more controllable tools can support measured construction or detailed investigation when that is the actual purpose.
- Match scale and duration to the information being sought rather than to a fixed idea of what a "proper" study should look like.
- Let quick sketches tolerate local incompleteness when their purpose is to capture action, mass arrangement, light pattern, color impression, or another large relationship before it disappears.
- Use sustained studies when the target genuinely requires slower construction, correction, comparison, or detailed analysis.
- Repeat the study enough times that the target skill, rather than accidental success in one drawing, becomes observable.
- Return isolated skills to integrated work and check whether the original weakness improves under normal artistic demands.
- Treat the ability to notice your own errors as diagnostic evidence: when the same detected error recurs, convert it into the next explicit study target instead of stopping at self-criticism.
- Set a limited visual problem that can actually be solved under the available time and conditions; deliberately ignore unrelated visible information when it does not serve that target.

## Don't
- Do not add finish, texture, anatomy, rendering, or detail merely because space remains on the page.
- Do not choose a familiar medium when its normal behavior encourages the exact distraction the exercise is meant to suppress.
- Do not make one study solve every attractive problem in the subject when that makes failure impossible to diagnose.
- Do not judge a quick study by the completeness standard of a sustained study, or a sustained study by the spontaneity standard of a quick sketch.
- Do not keep training an isolated subskill indefinitely after the real problem has shifted to integration.
- Do not rebuild an entire practice plan around one anomalous failure; target recurring weaknesses or a clearly chosen development goal.

## Checklist
- Before starting, the intended improvement can be stated in concrete, observable terms.
- The study has one primary target or a clearly ordered small set of targets.
- The chosen medium, scale, scope, and level of detail materially help reveal that target.
- At least one competing demand has been removed, delayed, or subordinated when overload was the problem.
- Failure can be attributed mainly to the target skill rather than to several unrelated problems at once.
- The exercise can be repeated without automatically accumulating more finish.
- There is a plan to test the isolated skill again in more integrated work.

## Notes
A study is a designed diagnostic environment, not simply an unfinished artwork. Decide what information the exercise needs before deciding how complete it ought to look. A drawing intended to capture action, mass, light, shadow, or color should not be burdened with unrelated finish, while a detailed investigation legitimately demands more information. Let recurring weaknesses exposed by practical work determine what deserves further study. Distinguish merely working **at** something from working **for** a concrete improvement: error awareness becomes useful when routed into the next explicit study target rather than treated as an endpoint.

`VAR_loomis_route_recurring_work_weakness_into_targeted_study` adds Loomis's feedback loop from applied work into practice: wait for a recurring, diagnosable weakness, isolate it in a targeted study, then return the improved skill to normal work and verify that the weakness actually decreases.

`VAR_bammes_isolate_animal_study_difficulty_with_medium_and_scope` preserves Bammes's animal-specific route that previously lived as its own Pattern. Broad dry media can isolate large masses, movable proportion blocks can isolate pose changes while preserving segment relationships, and loaded brushwork can force rapid continuous movement notation. The specialization now lives under the general practice-design owner because the governing decision is not animal-specific: reduce the study to the difficulty being trained and choose tools that make that difficulty legible.

`VAR_dow_isolate_structural_elements_then_recombine` adds a progressive practice sequence: isolate a structural variable, deepen control of it, then deliberately restore integration. The point is not to avoid complex work but to make the underlying relation trainable before recombination.

`VAR_dow_freeze_nontarget_variables_to_isolate_relationship` turns study simplification into an experimental control. Freeze variables that are not being trained, then reintroduce them once the chosen relationship can be judged reliably.

`VAR_dow_compare_diverse_exemplars_through_one_structural_question` adds cross-example structural comparison to study design. Hold the question steady while the surface form changes, then transfer the relation rather than copying a period style or decorative motif.

`VAR_dow_anchor_observational_study_to_live_design_question` begins from a current design need rather than an arbitrary study subject. Observe the specific evidence the work is missing, then return it to the image and test whether it solves the live problem.

`VAR_garcia_rehearse_watercolor_with_monochrome_wash_value_study` turns value planning into a miniature wash rehearsal: simplify the intended watercolor into broad light, middle, and dark territories, keep line secondary, build the wash from lighter toward darker values, then carry the accepted value/shape organization into color without assuming value alone guarantees the final painting.


`VAR_webster_match_evidence_type_to_research_question` adds Webster's evidence-routing distinction to study design. Let the question decide whether useful evidence must be measured, comparatively described, or approached with both methods; the method is subordinate to the information needed.
