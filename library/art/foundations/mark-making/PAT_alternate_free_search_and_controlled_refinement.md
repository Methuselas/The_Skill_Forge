# Alternate Free Search and Controlled Refinement in Mark Making

## Pattern Rule
**IF** exploration and precision are interfering with each other during a drawing
**THEN** shift deliberately between freer, larger, faster marks that discover action or possibilities and slower, shorter, more controlled marks that select and clarify what worked
**ELSE** stay in the current mode while it continues to solve the active problem

## Do
- Use a looser grip, larger arm action, connected strokes, and fewer interruptions when the problem is discovery.
- Move closer to the point of the tool, shorten the stroke, and compare more often when exact placement or finish is the current problem.
- Reopen free search locally when controlled refinement exposes a weak or unresolved solution.
- Let Stage 1 and Stage 3 carry the larger search functions while Stage 2 and Stage 4 control what the preceding search established.

## Don't
- Do not demand polished precision from the same marks being used to discover the drawing.
- Do not treat one loose pass followed by one tight pass as the only possible sequence; useful drawings can oscillate several times.
- Do not alter the Drawing-stage information ceilings merely because the hand changes operating mode.

## Checklist
- Search marks are free enough to discover alternatives without pretending to be final.
- Control marks clearly select, verify, or consolidate rather than merely adding detail.
- A local failure can reopen search without forcing an unrelated restart of the whole drawing.

## Notes
Dodson's two handwritings are complementary working modes. The general search/control cycle lives in metaskills; this specialization records how the distinction appears physically in drawing behavior.

`VAR_loomis_solve_uncertainty_on_disposable_study_then_reexecute_cleanly` separates discovery from clean execution when the surface itself is paying for every correction. Resolve the uncertain passage on a rough or spare support, then carry the chosen answer into the final with fewer hesitant repairs.

`VAR_dow_control_long_expressive_lines_with_slow_whole_arm_motion` expands controlled mark-making beyond short corrective strokes. For a long line, move with the whole arm slowly enough to guide the mark continuously; slight organic wavering can remain when the intended direction and character are still clear.

`VAR_eissen_search_product_shape_with_extended_lines_then_select` makes the line pass itself a discovery tool: long light trajectories can overshoot the expected object while proportion and direction are still changing, then the few governing relationships are selected and strengthened. `VAR_olofsson_probe_unresolved_product_form_with_selective_tone` searches through value instead: broad shadow, highlight, or paired light/dark masses can test a possible recess, turn, or overall expression before the final contour is committed. In both variants, search is temporary evidence; the chosen geometry must still be made coherent afterward.
