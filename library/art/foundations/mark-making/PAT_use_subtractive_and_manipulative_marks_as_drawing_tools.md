# Use Subtractive and Manipulative Marks as Drawing Tools

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an established passage needs lightening, softening, merging, reshaping, or selective highlight recovery
**THEN** use erasing, rubbing, wiping, smudging, masking, or scraping as intentional mark-making operations rather than treating them only as error removal
**ELSE** leave the additive marks intact when manipulation would weaken the intended edge or surface

## Do
- Knock a passage back partially when a full deletion would remove useful structure.
- Lift lights from an established tone when the medium permits a cleaner subtractive shape than drawing around the light.
- Use rubbing or smudging to alter edge character and value continuity deliberately.
- Match the manipulation to the support and medium so the surface can tolerate it.

## Don't
- Do not smear a failed value passage indiscriminately and call the blur a correction.
- Do not erase exploratory evidence before the replacement relationship is understood.
- Do not use abrasive or wet manipulation on a support that cannot withstand it.

## Checklist
- The operation has a named purpose: edge, value, shape, emphasis, or highlight.
- The manipulated area remains controllable enough for subsequent marks.
- Subtraction improves the drawing rather than merely hiding indecision.

## Notes
Dodson explicitly treats the eraser and bare hand as drawing tools. Subtractive and manipulative actions can sculpt the same visual relationships normally built with additive marks.
