# Track Force Continuity Through Action

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a living or articulated body is shown in action
**THEN** treat the pose as one state in a continuing physical event by reading where momentum or effort comes from, what redirects or resists it in the current body, and where that action continues next
**ELSE** use static structural construction when motion continuity is not the drawing problem

## Do
- Find the largest action statement before local anatomy: identify what the body is doing as a whole — stretching, thrusting, bending, pulling, bracing, or redirecting — before describing smaller parts.
- Trace directional force through the major masses and joint chains instead of stopping the action at each anatomical part.
- Treat each major force idea as one purposeful directional statement with a readable beginning, active passage, and destination instead of flattening distinct events into one generic sweep.
- Read applied force as the event that changes an existing direction: impact, support, gravity, resistance, contact, or muscular redirection.
- In human figures, test the rib-cage/pelvis relationship early when it carries the dominant action, then let the rest of the body answer that relationship.
- If the dominant direction is ambiguous, test opposing curve hypotheses and keep the one that best explains the pose's actual action and support.
- Use the action line as a graphic abstraction of the body-wide physical continuity, not as a decorative curve laid over unrelated masses.
- When comparing adjacent poses or panels, ask what state change connects them rather than treating each pose as a fresh start.

## Don't
- Do not reset momentum at every body part or keyframe.
- Do not let scratchy accumulation or multiple unresolved directional guesses replace a committed force idea; compare hypotheses deliberately, then choose the one that explains the action.
- Do not use flowing curves as a substitute for explaining where weight, support, resistance, and redirection actually occur.
- Do not split a hybrid character into separate human-force and animal-force systems; once integrated, it is one acting body.

## Checklist
- The largest action statement can be named before local anatomy is described.
- The action has a readable incoming direction, event or redirection, and outgoing continuation.
- Major force ideas remain distinct enough that their beginnings, redirects, and destinations can be traced.
- Major masses and support points agree with the claimed force path.
- A preceding or following pose could be inferred without inventing a completely unrelated action.

## Notes
Mattesi's directional FORCE, applied FORCE, rhythm, and coming-from/event/going-to vocabulary describes continuity through time as well as graphic flow. The same foundation applies to humans, animals, hybrid characters, and invented creatures. Start by finding the largest action statement rather than copying local anatomy; when a human pose is ambiguous, use the rib-cage/pelvis relationship and deliberately compared curve hypotheses as diagnostics rather than accumulating unresolved marks. Borrowed anatomy should contribute its donor mechanics to one integrated system rather than function as pasted-on silhouette decoration. `VAR_mattesi_interlock_forceful_shapes_through_straight_curve_exchange` applies the same continuity to connected shape design: derive the largest visible straight/curve relationship from function, force, and form, avoid parallel-sided or mirrored force collisions, let the straighter side direct or stabilize the energetic curve when that relationship fits the body, and pass the action through overlapping smaller shapes without turning the rule into a mechanical alternation. In animation, preserve the force/form logic rather than a frozen contour, so the specific shapes may change as the body turns and the action evolves. `VAR_mattesi_exchange_force_between_interacting_bodies` carries it across contact between bodies; `VAR_mattesi_preload_with_anticipatory_countermovement` adds a mechanically grounded preparation before a large action. `VAR_mattesi_locate_applied_force_with_curve_apex_and_leading_edge` sharpens applied-force diagnosis by locating the apex where a directional path is most strongly bent or compressed and the body region currently leading or catching the action, then following that leading edge as it transfers through sequential movement; use it only when those graphic clues agree with actual support, contact, resistance, and action. `VAR_mattesi_build_road_of_rhythm_through_oblique_force_handoffs` handles poses that require several linked force events: each directional force hands into a redirect and then into the next event, while mirrored force collisions and uncausal spaghetti flow are rejected. `VAR_mattesi_route_force_through_depth_as_roller_coaster` turns the connected force path into a spatial ride through the body's major masses: keep the dominant route coherent as it moves over, around, behind, through, toward, or away from the viewer, let local turns change tempo without severing the larger action, and infer only the hidden continuation required to keep separated visible segments mechanically connected. Use it when the pose is flattened by page-local rhythm even though the body clearly turns through depth.
`VAR_stanchfield_accent_action_with_opposing_body_directions` uses directional opposition between connected parts as an accent after the main mechanics are already coherent; it is useful when an action reads correctly but lacks a strong visual counterplay, and it should not override support, contact, balance, or the intended force path. `VAR_stanchfield_propagate_primary_action_through_whole_body_with_diminishing_influence` addresses the opposite failure: a local action that leaves the rest of the body inert. Carry supporting reactions outward through connected masses, usually with decreasing influence, so the whole body participates without competing with the primary event.
`VAR_stanchfield_build_pose_tension_between_related_landmarks_and_planes` applies the same continuity to relational angles: compare related landmarks, planes, and negative spaces, diagnose whether the pose has lost or added action relative to the intended gesture, then restore or reduce tension accordingly instead of drawing each endpoint independently or defaulting to more exaggeration.
`VAR_stanchfield_make_sustained_force_read_in_held_pose` handles near-static states where the force has not ended: trace the continuing effort through grip, lean, bracing, compression or stretch, support, and counteraction so a held load or resistance still reads as an active event rather than a relaxed position.
