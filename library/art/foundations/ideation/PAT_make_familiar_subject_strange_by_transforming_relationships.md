# Make a Familiar Subject Strange by Transforming Relationships

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a familiar subject is producing automatic, conventional, or visually stale solutions
**THEN** change one or more governing relationships while preserving enough identity for the transformed subject to remain meaningful
**ELSE** keep the ordinary relationship when recognition, instruction, or factual description is the primary job.

## Do
- Identify which relationship currently makes the subject predictable: viewpoint, relative scale, context, overlap, arrangement, distortion, orientation, or symbolic role.
- Change that relationship deliberately rather than adding random surface oddity.
- Preserve the structural or identifying evidence the viewer still needs to recognize what has been transformed.
- Test several degrees of transformation before choosing one.
- Let the new relationship create consequences throughout the drawing instead of remaining a pasted-on gimmick.

## Don't
- Distort every feature equally.
- Erase the subject's identity unless loss of identity is itself the intended concept.
- Confuse unfamiliarity with incoherence.
- Use transformation to hide unresolved construction, anatomy, perspective, or material problems.

## Checklist
- The source subject remains identifiable enough for the transformation to register.
- The altered relationship is clear.
- The transformation produces a new visual or conceptual read.
- The resulting drawing still obeys the structural rules that the chosen transformation has not intentionally replaced.

## Notes
The "make the familiar strange" route uses unusual viewpoints, scale changes, absurd combinations, distortion, coincidence, and altered context to force renewed perception of familiar material. Keep the decision at the relationship level so it can serve character, creature, object, environment, and concept design without turning any one example into doctrine.

`VAR_loomis_distort_character_with_rescaled_grid` adds a controlled caricature route: deform the coordinate grid and transfer corresponding crossings so exaggeration follows chosen proportional changes instead of arbitrary feature inflation. The reusable method is the rescaled grid, not tracing.
