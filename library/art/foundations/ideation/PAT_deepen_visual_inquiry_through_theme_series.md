# Deepen Visual Inquiry through a Theme Series

## Pattern Rule
**IF** one drawing cannot investigate the subject deeply enough or a promising question deserves sustained exploration
**THEN** define a bounded theme and let each related work expose a new question, variation, or consequence for the next
**ELSE** stop the series when repetition is no longer producing new information.

## Do
- State the inquiry or recurring theme in a way broad enough to permit variation but narrow enough to compare results.
- Make multiple related works rather than forcing one drawing to contain every possibility.
- After each work, record what changed, what remained stable, and what new question appeared.
- Vary viewpoint, scale, technique, context, structure, or emphasis while preserving the shared inquiry.
- Allow the criteria for the next work to become more precise as understanding deepens.

## Don't
- Treat a repeated subject label as a theme if the individual works ask no new questions.
- Change every variable at once when you need to learn what produced a difference.
- Continue a series only to satisfy an arbitrary count.
- Confuse a theme series with a serial animation/action sequence; the works may branch widely rather than depicting consecutive states.

## Checklist
- The works share one identifiable inquiry.
- Each new work contributes a distinct investigation.
- Comparisons across the series produce usable findings.
- The series can stop or redirect when the inquiry is exhausted.

## Notes
`VAR_dodson_use_bounded_formal_premise_to_keep_theme_generative` adds a tighter formal constraint when a broad theme needs more specific pressure to keep producing new questions.

Dodson describes theme work as heuristic: repeated engagement with a bounded idea lets one drawing generate questions for another. PASS rejects the exact six-drawing assignment as a canonical Drill because the reusable decision is the sustained inquiry itself, not the source's arbitrary count.
