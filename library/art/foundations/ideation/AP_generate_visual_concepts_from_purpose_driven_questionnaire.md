# Generate Visual Concepts from a Purpose-Driven Questionnaire

## Objective
Turn an abstract assignment into several drawable concept routes by questioning the subject, researching missing facts, clustering useful answers, and translating those relationships into visible situations before composition is chosen.

## Steps / Flow
**Entry Conditions**
- A subject, product, story, message, or illustration assignment exists.
- The desired result can be stated well enough to guide useful questions.

**Persistent Invariants**
- Questions serve the purpose of the picture rather than becoming trivia collection.
- Unknown facts are researched rather than invented as factual claims.
- A fact pool is raw material; it does not dictate one automatic concept.
- Concept routes must become visible situations, actions, contrasts, objects, or relationships before thumbnail composition begins.

**Flow**
1. **State the picture's purpose.** Write what the image needs to accomplish before generating answers.
2. **Question the subject broadly.** Ask about use, result, audience, setting, origin, behavior, problem, benefit, contrast, sensory qualities, consequences, and any other facts that could change the visual proposition.
3. **Separate known from unknown.** Research missing facts that matter to the assignment and discard questions that do not affect the picture.
4. **Build a fact pool.** Record concise answers without forcing them immediately into one composition.
5. **Cluster relationships.** Look for repeated benefits, frictions, contrasts, causes, consequences, settings, or human situations that can become themes.
6. **Promote a promising cluster into a concept premise.** State the relationship the viewer should grasp rather than naming only the subject.
7. **Translate the premise into visible form.** Convert abstract claims into actions, before/after contrasts, environments, interactions, props, or concrete situations that can actually be drawn.
8. **Branch several routes from the same evidence before judging them.** Let different clusters produce genuinely different visual propositions and keep the divergent batch generative long enough to discover alternatives; do not use the first acceptable route as the standard that every later route must immediately beat.
9. **Evaluate after the divergent batch exists.** Compare, reject, combine, or select only after several distinct propositions are visible enough to judge against the assignment purpose.
10. **Rough cheaply.** Move viable propositions into Stage 0 thumbnail search rather than polishing the questionnaire itself.
11. **Reweight instead of restarting blindly.** If feedback changes emphasis, keep facts that still serve the job and reorganize the concept around the newly important relationship.

**Failure / Rollback Rules**
- If the questions are producing facts but no drawable idea, return to clustering and ask what action, consequence, contrast, or human use those facts imply.
- If every route looks like the same idea with different props, return to the fact pool and select a different cluster rather than changing camera angle.
- If the first acceptable route becomes the benchmark that suppresses later search, suspend comparison, generate a small batch of genuinely different propositions, and resume evaluation only after the batch exists.
- If an appealing route depends on an unsupported fact, research it or discard that dependency before thumbnailing.
- If the questionnaire grows without improving the concept space, restate the purpose and cut questions that do not affect it.

**Completion Criteria**
- The assignment purpose is explicit.
- Important unknowns have been researched or marked unusable.
- At least two distinct concept premises can be traced to the same factual pool.
- Distinct propositions were generated before final comparison or selection, rather than being filtered one-by-one against the first acceptable idea.
- Each viable premise can be expressed as a drawable situation rather than an abstract slogan.
- Stage 0 composition search begins from a chosen proposition instead of a blank page.
- **Synthesize selected contributions.** After divergence, identify the specific useful feature or relationship in each promising rough and make a new synthesis sketch that resolves those contributions into one coherent product; do not paste fragments together without repairing their proportions and transitions.

## Notes
Loomis uses a questionnaire to turn an ordinary commercial subject into a pool of facts, then demonstrates that different clusters inside the same answers can generate substantially different idea families. Product-sketch ideation reinforces a useful timing rule inside that branching step: divergent drawing works best when several alternatives are allowed to exist before evaluation narrows the field. The durable procedure is purpose -> questions -> facts -> relationships -> visible propositions -> batch comparison -> roughs. It separates discovering what to picture from deciding how to compose it, and it prevents early judgment from collapsing the search around the first merely acceptable answer.

Combining alternatives should create a new resolved proposition, not a scrapbook. Name what each rough contributes, redraw those contributions together, and raise the synthesis only to the resolution needed to judge whether the combined design actually works.
