# Cycle Hampton’s Spatial Form Fundamentals

## Practice Task
Complete one short cycle of six spatial-form exercises before anatomy work.

## Target Skill
Maintain convincing three-dimensional form while rotating, combining, wrapping, and organically modifying simple primitives.

## Setup
No special setup required.

## Instructions
1. Draw several one-stroke spheres at different sizes.
2. Draw ellipses at different widths and check them with major and minor axes.
3. Draw wrapping lines alone, imagining flexible tubes or slinkies turning through space.
4. Pair ellipses to construct cylinders at different orientations.
5. Combine spheres, boxes, and cylinders into several simple organic masses.
6. Rotate a box through a falling/turning sequence so its planes clearly change orientation.

## Success Check
- The forms remain volumetric without shading or anatomy.
- Ellipse axes and cylinder directions agree.
- Wrapping lines reinforce rather than contradict the surfaces.
- Combined primitives read as one organic construction rather than a pile of symbols.
- The rotating box changes plane exposure coherently from step to step.

## Common Failures
- Drawing symbols for spheres/ellipses without checking their spatial orientation.
- Adding cross-contours that flatten or contradict the form.
- Leaving combined primitives merely touching instead of integrating their surfaces.
- Treating the drill’s sphere/box/cylinder vocabulary as the only valid way to construct a figure.

## Notes
Hampton frames these as recurring basic exercises rather than finished figure recipes. Their value is fluency in the spatial operations that later anatomy depends on.
