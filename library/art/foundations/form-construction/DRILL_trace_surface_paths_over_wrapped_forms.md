# Trace Surface Paths Over Wrapped Forms

## Practice Task
Train the eye and hand to follow lines as three-dimensional surface paths rather than flat graphic marks.

## Target Skill
Follow and invent cross-contour paths that stay attached to changing three-dimensional surfaces and preserve volume without relying on silhouette.

## Setup
No special setup required.

## Instructions
1. Place striped cloth over a simple object, or crumple striped material so the stripes bend through multiple plane changes.
2. Choose one stripe and trace its full path from start to finish.
3. Draw the path as if the pencil were traveling on the object's surface, not across the page.
4. Repeat with several stripes that move toward, away from, over, and around the form.
5. Redraw the same object without the literal stripes, adding only a few imagined cross-contours that preserve the same surface logic.
6. Compare whether the imagined lines still turn coherently through the volume.
7. For the Mattesi variant, repeat on an organic figure without relying on its outer contour: choose a force route, anchor it to useful internal centers such as the spine, ribcage center, knee, top of foot, facial center, or a major limb axis, and trace how that route travels over, around, toward, and away across connected masses.
8. Check the imagined route against the actual camera-facing form and correct any path that cuts into, flattens, or contradicts the volume.

## Success Check
- Each traced path changes curvature according to the form beneath it rather than preserving a flat graphic arc through every plane change.
- Lines wrap continuously toward, away from, over, and around the volume instead of becoming decorative stripes that ignore surface direction.
- Imagined cross-contours remain coherent after the literal stripe reference is removed, so the source pattern is not doing the spatial work for them.
- In the no-contour figure variant, internal surface information alone keeps connected masses coherent and carries the chosen force route without an enclosing silhouette rescuing the form.
- Whether this training improves later anatomy or contour placement cannot be closed in the same sitting; a later drawing must show the transfer, and predicting that improvement is not evidence that it occurred.

## Common Failures
- Copying the stripe as a flat 2-D shape without understanding the supporting form.
- Adding cross-contours that do not agree with the object's perspective.
- Treating the drill as a requirement to cover finished drawings with wrapping lines.
- Letting a plausible silhouette conceal surface paths that cut through the mass or fail to turn with the camera.

## Notes
Vilppu's “ant crawling over the surface” image captures the intended perception: the line is a path on a solid. The training scaffold should become increasingly implicit with mastery.

`VAR_hultgren_use_natural_stripes_as_surface_wrap_guides` retains **Use Natural Stripes as Surface-Wrap Guides** as an animal-specific transfer: natural markings can become built-in surface paths for checking volume, provided their observed pattern is respected rather than forced into idealized cross-contours.

`VAR_mattesi_trace_forceful_surface_paths_without_contour_scaffold` removes the literal stripe guide and transfers the same reasoning to an organic figure: trace a meaningful internal route across the surface and use structural centers to test volume, camera turn, and force continuity without allowing the perimeter to do the construction work.
