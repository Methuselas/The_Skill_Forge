# Recompose One Subject Across Tonal Keys and Plans

## Practice Task
Use one subject to make a compact set of small tonal studies that deliberately vary the overall key and dominant value organization while keeping the underlying subject substantially constant.

## Target Skill
Separating subject choice from tonal conception so value structure can be explored, compared, and selected rather than inherited passively from the first rendering attempt.

## Setup
Choose a subject with a clear large-value read. Make four to six small thumbnail boxes. Use any fast monochrome method that lets you state broad light, middle, and dark masses without local rendering.

## Instructions
1. Reduce the subject to its few largest value masses and identify the relationships that must remain readable for the subject to survive.
2. Make one high-key version in which most of the picture lives in the light family and darker values are reserved for subordinate or accent roles.
3. Make one middle-key version in which a middle-value family occupies most of the field and light/dark accents organize the read.
4. Make one low-key version in which dark values dominate and lighter passages become the main separators or accents.
5. Make at least one additional version that changes the dominant tonal plan rather than merely sliding every value up or down.
6. Compare the thumbnails at small size. Judge focal hierarchy, readability, mood, simplicity, and whether the large masses interlock cleanly.
7. Select the strongest plan for the intended picture, and record why it wins in terms of pictorial purpose, hierarchy, and readability before beginning a larger tonal development.

## Success Check
- The subject remains recognizable across the alternatives even though the tonal conception changes materially.
- Each thumbnail has a clear dominant value family or tonal idea rather than being a random redistribution of patches.
- The recorded selection reason ties the chosen plan to pictorial purpose, hierarchy, and readability; choosing the first thumbnail or merely naming a personal preference does not pass.
- Local modeling has not been allowed to obscure the comparison between the large tonal plans.

## Common Failures
- Making several copies with nearly identical value organization.
- Treating high-, middle-, and low-key as rigid formulas instead of broad exploratory constraints.
- Changing pose, crop, subject, lighting, and value plan all at once so the tonal variable can no longer be compared.
- Rendering one thumbnail so far that the exercise becomes a finish contest instead of a tonal-design comparison.

## Notes
Value organization is a design decision. Keep the subject substantially stable, deliberately vary tonal key and broad plan in thumbnails, compare the resulting pictorial effects, and enlarge only a selected option before full development. Named tonal plans are practical examples rather than an exhaustive universal taxonomy.

`VAR_dow_calibrate_three_flat_value_families_before_expanding_range` adds a three-family calibration pass before broader tonal development. Holding light, middle, and dark flat long enough to compare their intervals exposes weak grouping early; once the plan works, the study can regain intermediate values and softer transitions as needed.

`VAR_dow_use_temporary_multivalue_scale_to_stabilize_tonal_intervals` extends that calibration into five- or seven-value studies when intermediate tones begin to drift. The temporary scale keeps intended intervals legible during comparison, then gets discarded once the tonal organization can be handled freely.

`VAR_guptill_audition_material_value_vs_shadow_weight_on_fixed_outline` holds the outline stable while comparing whether local/material value or illumination should carry more of the picture.

`VAR_martin_permute_three_value_families_across_depth_planes` keeps the spatial drawing fixed while assigning light, middle, and dark to different foreground/middle-ground/background combinations. Judge whether the planes remain distinct, whether adjacent forms fuse usefully or accidentally, how visual weight shifts, and which large plan reads most clearly before detail returns.
