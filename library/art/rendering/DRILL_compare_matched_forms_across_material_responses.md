# Compare Matched Forms Across Material Responses

## Practice Task
Render the same simple form, or a tightly matched family of forms, as several materially different surfaces while keeping geometry, viewpoint, lighting, scale, and surroundings substantially constant.

## Target Skill
Learn to identify which optical cues actually distinguish one material from another instead of relying on labels, decorative texture, or changes in the underlying form.

## Setup
Choose one simple form or a small set of forms that are close enough in size and geometry to compare directly. Fix one viewpoint, one lighting setup, one scale, and one surrounding environment. Assign distinct material identities such as matte or porous, glossy opaque, reflective metal, transparent glass, and a softer natural surface.

## Instructions
1. Resolve the shared construction, viewpoint, and large light/shadow pattern before introducing material-specific effects.
2. Make one version for each material while preserving the same underlying geometry and illumination.
3. Change only the evidence that the material requires: reflection structure, highlight width and strength, transmission, edge behavior, roughness, local-value response, or other causally relevant optical cues.
4. Keep the surrounding environment stable so reflections and transmitted information can be compared rather than invented differently for each version.
5. Strip back ornamental microtexture where possible and test whether the material still reads from the larger optical response.
6. Compare the studies without material labels. For any pair that reads too similarly, diagnose which optical distinction is missing before adding more marks.
7. Make one corrected pass on the weakest study using the smallest change that resolves the material identity.

## Success Check
- The studies remain recognizably the same form and lighting situation.
- Different material identities are readable without changing the underlying geometry to help them.
- Reflective, transparent, matte, glossy, or soft surfaces differ through causal optical behavior rather than arbitrary texture symbols.
- The material read survives at reduced size after nonessential microtexture is removed.
- You can name the smallest optical cue that separates each material from its nearest confusing neighbor.

## Common Failures
- Redesigning the form between studies, which makes the material comparison ambiguous.
- Changing the light or environment to rescue a weak material read instead of fixing the missing optical cue.
- Covering every surface with descriptive texture before the large reflection, transmission, value, and edge behavior is working.
- Treating one highlight, one hatch pattern, or one color as a universal symbol for a material.
- Adding more detail when the real problem is that two materials share the same large optical response.

## Notes
The comparison works by holding the subject problem stable while material identity changes. Similar forms made from different substances expose whether the renderer understands the specific evidence carried by the surface itself. This drill complements medium comparison: one isolates what the depicted material must look like, while the other isolates what the chosen drawing or painting process naturally makes easy or difficult.
