# Compare a Fixed Subject Across Changing Light and Atmosphere

## Practice Task
Make a small series of studies of the same motif from the same viewpoint under materially different light, weather, or seasonal states.

## Target Skill
Separate stable subject properties from changes caused by illumination and atmosphere.

## Setup
Choose a motif with enough depth and directional planes to expose lighting changes. Fix the viewpoint, crop, and underlying drawing so geometry is held constant across the series.

## Instructions
1. Resolve one shared geometry/viewpoint and reuse it for every study.
2. Render the first observed condition from the evidence currently present, recording the large light family, sky, atmosphere, shadow color, and reflected-light relationships.
3. Return under a materially different condition and re-evaluate those relationships from observation; do not use the previous study as the color reference.
4. Repeat for enough conditions to expose meaningful variation in illumination or atmosphere.
5. Lay the studies together and label which changes came from light/air and which subject properties remained comparatively stable.
6. Use those observations to inform a later invented lighting study of the same or a related subject.

## Success Check
- Geometry, viewpoint, and crop remain consistent enough that color/light changes are the dominant variable.
- Each study independently reflects its observed illumination rather than drifting from the previous painting.
- The comparison yields explicit evidence about which visible colors belong to material identity versus changing light and atmosphere.

## Common Failures
- Redrawing the composition so much that geometry changes obscure the lighting comparison.
- Copying the first study's palette into later conditions instead of re-observing.
- Treating every color change as a local-color change in the object.

## Notes
The drill is a controlled observational experiment: same scene plus fixed geometry plus changing observed light provides evidence about illumination rather than subject identity. It complements invented color roughs by grounding them in real changes rather than alternative palette concepts chosen from imagination.
