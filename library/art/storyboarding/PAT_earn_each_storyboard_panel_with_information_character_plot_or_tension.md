# Earn Each Storyboard Panel With Information, Character, Plot, or Tension

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an intuitive first-pass storyboard is ready for analytical editing
**THEN** challenge each panel to earn its place by contributing new audience information, revealing or advancing character, advancing plot/action, or increasing/clarifying risk and tension
**ELSE** keep generating freely until the sequence exists well enough to judge without choking ideation with a checklist.

## Do
- Ask what new information the viewer receives from the panel.
- Ask whether the panel reveals, changes, or advances something important about a character or relationship.
- Ask whether the panel advances the plot, action, or causal chain rather than merely restating the previous beat.
- Ask what is at risk and whether the panel raises, clarifies, redirects, or releases tension in a purposeful way.
- Treat a panel that answers none of these questions as suspect: combine it, revise it, or remove it unless another clear sequence function can be stated.
- Apply the test after the intuitive boarding pass, when the job is revision and pruning rather than idea generation.

## Don't
- Do not force every panel to satisfy all four questions.
- Do not use the test mechanically during first-pass ideation; it is an editing tool, not a creativity gate.
- Do not delete a quiet panel merely because little overt action occurs if it supplies necessary information, character, tension, reaction, or transition.
- Do not preserve a redundant panel because the drawing itself is attractive.

## Checklist
- Every retained panel has a stated sequence job.
- Redundant panels have been combined, revised, or removed.
- Quiet beats still earn their place through information, character, plot, tension, or another explicit sequence function.
- The sequence was generated intuitively before this analytical filter was applied.

## Notes
Use four explicit post-first-pass questions for storyboard editing: what new information is supplied, whether the sketch advances character personality, whether it advances plot, and what risk or tension the scene contains. Do not apply the questions so mechanically that they inhibit the initial creative pass. The durable operation is a second-pass panel-value test: generate first, then make each retained storyboard image justify its contribution to the sequence.
