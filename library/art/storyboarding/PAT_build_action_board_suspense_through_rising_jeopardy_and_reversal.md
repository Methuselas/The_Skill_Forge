# Build Action-Board Suspense Through Rising Jeopardy and Reversal

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an action sequence risks becoming predictable because each exchange merely repeats the same danger
**THEN** escalate the dramatic problem through materially changing jeopardy and earned reversals so the audience must continually reassess who has advantage, what is at stake, and how the sequence may resolve
**ELSE** preserve the simpler action structure when repetition or inevitability is the intended dramatic effect.

## Do
- Let successive beats alter the actual problem through complications such as injury, lost or gained tools, changing position, time pressure, separation, failed plans, or another credible shift in advantage.
- Raise or redirect jeopardy instead of only making drawings louder or action faster.
- Use temporary advantages and apparent resolutions when they create a believable new dramatic question.
- Keep enough causal information visible that reversals feel earned once they occur.
- Let the sequence breathe when a short release makes the next complication stronger.
- Ask after each major beat what has become harder, more dangerous, more urgent, or less certain than before.

## Don't
- Do not begin at maximum danger and simply repeat the same threat level.
- Do not add arbitrary reversals only to defeat prediction.
- Do not hide causally necessary setup merely to manufacture surprise.
- Do not telegraph the final outcome so strongly that the remaining beats become procedural.
- Do not confuse visual intensity alone with rising story jeopardy.

## Checklist
- Each major escalation changes the dramatic problem rather than only the surface spectacle.
- Advantages and reversals have visible causal support.
- Stakes, urgency, or uncertainty rise or redirect across the sequence.
- The final outcome is not mechanically obvious from the opening exchanges unless inevitability is intentional.
- Visual escalation and story jeopardy are coordinated but not treated as the same thing.

## Notes
Bluth's action-board example increases suspense by changing the combatants' situation rather than merely repeating sword clashes: wounds, weapons, apparent defeats, deaths, recoveries, and renewed threats repeatedly alter what the audience believes about the outcome. The durable storyboard operation is progressive jeopardy plus credible reversal. Specific plot devices from the example are not formulas; the board must earn any reversal from the story's own causes and constraints.
