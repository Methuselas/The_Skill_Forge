# Stage Subtext Through Visible Behavior Without Forcing It Into Dialogue

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a storyboard scene depends on an emotional, relational, or motivational current that the characters do not state directly
**THEN** stage enough visible behavior around the spoken text for the audience to infer that underlying current without requiring explanatory dialogue
**ELSE** let direct dialogue remain direct when hidden meaning is not part of the scene's job.

## Do
- Identify separately what the character says and what the character wants, fears, resists, conceals, or waits for underneath the line.
- Express the underlying current through available visual behavior: facial expression, posture, hesitation, gaze, silence, blocking, distance, hand action, entrances/exits, or one character's reaction to another.
- Let pauses and non-actions carry meaning when a character is deciding, waiting, applying pressure, or refusing to commit.
- Use shot emphasis and staging to make the relevant reaction readable when the subtext depends on a small acting change.
- Keep the subtext supported by the story's established character relationships, objectives, and circumstances.
- Allow the audience to infer the underlying current rather than naming it in dialogue when the inference itself creates tension or involvement.

## Don't
- Do not invent a hidden motive merely to make a straightforward scene seem more sophisticated.
- Do not make the subtext so faint that the intended relationship or decision disappears at storyboard-reading speed.
- Do not force every spoken line to mean its opposite.
- Do not let explanatory dialogue duplicate a visual behavior that already communicates the necessary underlying meaning unless the story specifically requires both.
- Do not confuse subtext with withheld plot information; a character can reveal the plot facts while still carrying an unstated emotional or relational current.

## Checklist
- The spoken text and the visible behavior can be described separately.
- The audience can infer the intended underlying current from the board without an explanatory caption.
- The behavior is supported by the character, relationship, and scene objective.
- The shot and staging make the relevant reaction legible without overemphasis.
- Removing the subtext would measurably flatten the dramatic beat rather than simply make it less cryptic.

## Notes
Bluth's NIMH story-meeting sequence repeatedly looks for what is happening beneath the literal lines. Brisby struggles to volunteer before she says it; Nicodemus can pressure her through waiting and silence; Justin's reactions can carry concerns not fully verbalized. The board therefore has to stage meaning that exists underneath the text without automatically converting that meaning into additional dialogue. This Pattern concerns visible dramatic inference, not plot concealment alone.
