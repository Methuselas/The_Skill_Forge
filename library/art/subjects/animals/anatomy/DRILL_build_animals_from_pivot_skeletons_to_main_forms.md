# Build Animals From Pivot Skeletons to Main Forms

## Practice Task
Make a page of quick structural studies from clear animal references, using at least three differently proportioned quadrupeds. In every study, stop once the pivot skeleton and main forms agree; do not finish fur, facial detail, or rendering.

## Target Skill
Locating a quadruped's major skeletal pivots and preserving them while the main body forms are added.

## Setup
Choose side or three-quarter references in which the spine direction, forequarters, hindquarters, and major limb bends can be inferred. Work small enough that each study stays structural rather than becoming a contour drawing.

## Instructions
1. Mark the dorsal/spinal route and the broad direction of the head before drawing the body silhouette.
2. Place the shoulder/forequarter and hip/hindquarter pivots, then trace each visible limb as one joint chain with simple lines and joint marks.
3. Check the relative lengths of the limb segments against the reference before adding volume.
4. Build the forequarter, middle trunk/belly, and hindquarter masses around the existing pivots without moving the joints to make the silhouette easier.
5. Add simple limb volumes over the same chains, drawing through an overlap when a hidden continuation is necessary to preserve attachment.
6. Repeat the sequence on differently proportioned quadrupeds and compare which relationships changed while the construction order stayed the same.

## Success Check
- Shoulder, hip, knee, hock, paw, and other major pivots remain in place when body and limb volumes are added rather than migrating to make a preferred silhouette easier.
- The three quadrupeds preserve visibly different limb lengths, trunk ratios, and forequarter/hindquarter relationships instead of being one generic animal template with altered contour.
- Forequarter, middle trunk, hindquarter, and limb volumes wrap the established skeleton without disconnecting joints from the masses they belong to.
- Hidden or overlapping limbs retain a plausible joint-chain continuation back to the parent mass rather than ending as detached visible fragments.
- When fur, facial detail, and finish are absent, the reduced pivot skeleton and three main body regions still identify the intended proportional differences.

## Common Failures
- Drawing the outer contour first and inventing the skeleton afterward to fit it.
- Using the same limb lengths and body-mass ratios for every species.
- Moving a shoulder, hip, knee, hock, or paw after volume is added because the original pivot placement was never checked.
- Treating an overlapped limb fragment as a detached shape instead of continuing its joint chain through the hidden region.

## Notes
The unit moves explicitly from simplified skeleton knowledge and pivot points into main-form sketching, and its image-led pages apply that sequence to several quadruped types. The Drill practices that transfer rather than memorizing a single animal skeleton. It deliberately avoids promoting the unit's broader species-stance claims as anatomical rules.
