# Match Fish Mouth Orientation to Feeding Layer and Action

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a fish mouth must be constructed or interpreted from feeding behavior
**THEN** orient and project the jaws according to where and how the fish normally takes food: forward for terminal feeding, upward for surface-biased feeding, or downward for bottom-biased feeding, then add specialized extensions only when reference supports them
**ELSE** preserve the observed mouth orientation when species reference is more specific than a general feeding inference

## Do
- Use a terminal mouth at the front of the head when the feeding action is directed mainly ahead of the fish.
- Use a superior upturned mouth when the body is biased toward taking food from the surface or upper water.
- Use a subterminal or inferior mouth when the fish is structurally biased toward feeding from the bottom.
- Preserve protractile or otherwise specialized mouth motion when it materially changes how food is reached or removed.
- Add barbels when reference shows tactile or taste-assisted food location around the mouth.

## Don't
- Do not treat mouth orientation as proof that a fish feeds only at one water level.
- Do not add barbels, projecting jaws, or protractile structures as generic fish decoration.
- Do not infer exact jaw shape from feeding zone alone when reliable species reference gives a different solution.

## Checklist
- The mouth points toward the fish's dominant feeding layer or target direction.
- Jaw projection and opening mechanics agree with the feeding action.
- Specialized appendages have a functional reason supported by reference.
- The design allows exceptions rather than turning terminal, superior, and subterminal into absolute behavioral categories.

## Notes
Webster distinguishes terminal, superior, and subterminal mouth configurations as useful clues to feeding strategy. He also cautions through his examples that morphology indicates a bias, not an exclusive behavioral rule. The useful Art decision is to let orientation suggest how the head meets food while keeping species evidence authoritative.
