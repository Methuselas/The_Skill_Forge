# Construct a Bird Leg From Hidden Knee, Visible Ankle, and Digitigrade Foot

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a bird leg must be constructed beneath feathers or from a simplified silhouette
**THEN** locate the femur and knee high near the body, continue through the long lower segment to the conspicuous ankle, then build the distal foot from that ankle into the toe support
**ELSE** follow the visible joint chain directly when the anatomy is already exposed and unambiguous

## Do
- Place the hidden proximal joint before copying the exposed zigzag of the lower leg.
- Treat the familiar backward-looking bend as the ankle region rather than automatically labeling it as a knee.
- Let feather masses hide the proximal chain without relocating the underlying attachment.

## Don't
- Do not attach the long visible lower segment directly to the pelvis as though the hidden femur did not exist.
- Do not construct the bird leg as a mammal-style knee-and-shin ending at an ordinary plantigrade foot.

## Checklist
- The high knee can be traced back toward the body even when feathers cover it.
- The conspicuous distal bend is read as the ankle before the foot and toes continue.
- The final toe contact supports the body without breaking the hidden joint chain.

## Notes
Mattesi's ostrich and heron studies use the digitigrade comparison to expose a common visual mistake: the most visible bend is not the bird's knee. The construction remains useful when the upper leg is almost entirely concealed by plumage.
