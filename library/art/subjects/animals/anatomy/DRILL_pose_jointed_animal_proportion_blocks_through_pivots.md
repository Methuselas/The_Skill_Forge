# Pose Jointed Animal Proportion Blocks Through Pivots

## Practice Task
Build a simplified jointed animal from separate proportion blocks for the trunk and limb segments, connect the pieces at the major pivots, and use the same pieces to make several different poses without changing segment size.

## Target Skill
Preserving segment proportion and joint identity while changing an animal's pose by rotating a simplified articulated model around fixed pivot locations.

## Setup
Choose one animal whose proportions and major joints are already understood from a clear side view. Cut or draw reusable flat pieces for the trunk and principal limb segments. Let adjacent limb pieces overlap at each joint. Mark the pivot consistently on both pieces and connect them with a removable pin, brad, or other hinge. If physical pieces are impractical, reproduce the exercise digitally with separate rigid layers and fixed rotation points.

## Instructions
1. Establish the animal's large proportional ensemble first. Size the trunk and limb pieces from the same subject rather than from a generic mannequin.
2. Mark each major pivot through the overlapping ends of the neighboring pieces. Check that the holes or digital rotation points coincide before changing the pose.
3. Assemble a neutral or standing pose and trace, stamp, or print it once. Do not resize any piece after this point.
4. Rotate the same pieces into a second pose. Change only joint angle and overlap; preserve the segment lengths and the identity of every pivot.
5. Make at least three more poses, including one with strong flexion and one with clear extension. If a limb seems to require a new bend, diagnose the pivot placement instead of inventing another joint.
6. Compare the sequence. Mark where the silhouette changes even though the underlying segment proportions remain constant.
7. Optional printmaking pass: vary pressure or value so the nearer limb reads more strongly than the farther limb, but keep depth accents subordinate to the articulation exercise.

## Success Check
- The same set of pieces can produce several readable poses without resizing the segments.
- Every joint rotates around a stable, repeatable pivot rather than drifting along the limb.
- Strong flexion and extension change overlap and direction while preserving the animal's basic proportion.
- The pose sequence remains recognizable even with no fur, muscle rendering, or finished contour.
- Any near/far value difference clarifies overlap without hiding bad pivot geometry.

## Common Failures
- Cutting or drawing pieces before the animal's basic proportions have been established.
- Moving the hinge location between poses because a silhouette is inconvenient.
- Resizing limb segments to force a pose to fit.
- Treating the blocks as anatomical bones rather than simplified proportional pieces centered on the important pivots.
- Spending more time on color, ink texture, or print effects than on segment proportion and articulation.

## Notes
A small movable-block exercise bridges proportion study into movement. Individual limb shapes overlap, holes are placed where the pivots occur, and a pin keeps each adjoining piece aligned with the same joint while the pose changes. The exercise reinforces proportion while introducing the first two-dimensional movements.

Optional presentation variations include uneven inking, overprinting, blank areas, different colors, and heavier printing for the nearer legs. Those are not the core skill. The core is that a fixed proportional model can change pose by rotating around stable pivots, making any accidental change of segment length or joint identity easy to see.
