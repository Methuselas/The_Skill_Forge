# Construct Contrasting Big-Cat Poses From Studied Anatomy

## Practice Task
Without using a live animal as the immediate model, construct two contrasting big-cat actions from internalized study: one crouched and compressed, one stretching. Keep both in working-study form until the anatomy, spatial organization, and intended psychological/functional read agree.

## Target Skill
Translating studied big-cat anatomy into contrasting imagined actions while preserving trunk mechanics, carnivore foreleg and paw structure, spatial recession, and the intended expressive read.

## Setup
Complete the relevant carnivore foreleg, paw, and muscle-mass studies first. Use a simple line or block medium for the construction pass. Reserve enough page space to compare both poses at the same general scale before developing either one toward finish.

## Instructions
1. Begin each pose with the trunk as one dominant cylindrical mass and state its overall tension before local anatomy. For the crouch, keep the trunk taut and compressed toward action; for the stretch, make the lumbar-to-chest relationship visibly hollow into a concave bend.
2. Place the shoulder region and scapular direction as part of the trunk action rather than as detached limb sockets. In the crouch, carry the shoulders and forelegs forward expressively; keep the hindlegs folded beneath the rear mass so the body reads as gathered rather than merely lowered.
3. Build the forearms and paws from the studied carnivore mechanism. Let planted contact affect apparent forearm width and paw spread instead of pasting a standard paw symbol onto the end of the limb.
4. In the stretching pose, splay the forelegs widely and let the metacarpal mass fan toward the digits. Show the claws only where the intended stretch and the species construction support them.
5. Preserve the contrast between the relatively small shoulder area nestled against the steep side of the thorax and the broader distal spread of forearm and paw. Use that change of mass to keep the limb attached to the body while still making the paw feel expansive.
6. Strengthen depth by allowing the advancing forearm and paw to occupy more apparent volume when the view calls for it. The enlargement should clarify spatial recession and the action, not detach the paw from the forearm or change the underlying anatomy.
7. Add only the head and facial cues needed to protect the intended read. In Bammes's stretching example, closed, drowsy eyes and a closed mouth prevent the exposed claws and wide forelegs from turning the pose into a threat display.
8. Compare the two studies at block level. If either pose reads as the wrong action, return to trunk curve, shoulder placement, limb compression/extension, paw attitude, or mass recession before adding finish. Once one study is structurally and expressively clear, it may be carried into a more responsive finishing medium without redesigning the solved construction.

## Success Check
- The crouch and stretch read as clearly different actions before fur, texture, or rendering is added.
- Trunk curvature organizes the pose instead of functioning as an outline drawn after the limbs.
- Shoulder/scapular placement and hindleg folding support the action rather than floating independently of the torso.
- Forearm rotation, paw fan, digit direction, and claw state remain consistent with the studied carnivore construction.
- Near forearm/paw enlargement strengthens recession without producing a disconnected oversized terminal form.
- The stretching pose remains nonthreatening when the intended head cues are added.
- A failed read is corrected in the block rather than hidden by finish.

## Common Failures
- Drawing a generic cat silhouette first and trying to insert the anatomy afterward.
- Lowering the body for a crouch without gathering the hindquarters or driving the forequarters forward.
- Making the stretch only a long outline while leaving the lumbar/chest relationship mechanically unchanged.
- Splaying the paws while keeping the metacarpus as one rigid, narrow block.
- Exaggerating the nearest paw for perspective without propagating the change through the forearm and shoulder connection.
- Using open claws or facial detail as the sole source of expression when the trunk and limb behavior still communicate something else.
- Treating the final medium as another chance to discover the pose instead of carrying forward the working study that already solved it.

## Notes
Bammes uses the crouching-and-stretching big cat as a visualization test after the carnivore foreleg has been studied structurally and muscularly. The source does not ask for generic invention from nothing: it asks the student to reuse the earlier construction logic, reduce the animal to stable masses and planar directions, then make the anatomy serve two different functional and psychological situations.

The key learning contrast is that the same studied animal structure must survive a change of action. In the crouch, the trunk, forward shoulder/foreleg thrust, and folded hindquarters produce gathering and tension. In the stretch, the hollow lumbar curve, widely splayed forelegs, fanned metacarpus, and enlarged near paw produce expansion and recession. A finished-media comparison demonstrates the handoff principle: once the idea study is established, a different, more responsive medium may free the finish, but the structural discovery should already have happened upstream.
