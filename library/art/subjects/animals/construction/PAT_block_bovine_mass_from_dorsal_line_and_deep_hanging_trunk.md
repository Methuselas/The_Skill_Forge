# Block Bovine Mass From a Dorsal Line and Deep Hanging Trunk

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a cow or bull construction keeps collapsing into a generic barrel with legs attached to its underside
**THEN** establish the dorsal/spinal route as the upper organizing edge, suspend a deep rib-and-belly mass beneath that route, anchor the shoulder and pelvic regions with visible bony landmarks, and only then attach the limb chains and subject-specific neck/forequarter mass
**ELSE** use the broader quadruped mass scaffold when the animal does not need the cattle-specific deep-body emphasis.

## Do
- Establish the upper dorsal route before drawing the lowest belly contour so the body has an organizing support direction rather than only an outer silhouette.
- Treat Hultgren's basket-like hanging-body idea as a construction metaphor: place the deep trunk below the spine and let its depth, width, and sag explain the animal's weight.
- Keep the shoulder, pelvis, and major limb pivots readable through the bulk so the legs attach to a constructed body instead of to an undifferentiated oval.
- Use front, rear, and three-quarter checks to keep the deep body three-dimensional; a heavy side silhouette still needs a believable centerline and left-right width.
- Adjust the amount of neck, shoulder, and forequarter emphasis to the actual cow or bull being drawn rather than copying one fixed source example.

## Don't
- Interpret the hanging-basket metaphor as a literal biomechanical statement about how cattle weight is physically suspended.
- Let a large abdomen erase the rib cage, shoulder, pelvis, or limb attachments.
- Make every cattle body equally deep or equally front-heavy; observed conformation, age, sex, breed, and pose still matter.
- Use surface traits such as horns, hide markings, or a tail tuft to compensate for a generic underlying body block.

## Checklist
- The dorsal route and the deep trunk mass are both readable before surface detail is added.
- Shoulder and pelvic landmarks still explain where the four limb chains attach.
- The body reads as deep and weight-bearing without becoming a flat side-view oval.
- The construction can be turned toward or away from the viewer without losing its width or centerline.
- Any cow-versus-bull difference is tied to the observed subject rather than treated as a universal formula.

## Notes
Organize the heavy bovine body from an upper dorsal route into a deep hanging trunk with readable skeletal anchors. A basket-like mass can clarify how the body hangs below the spine, while a bull may concentrate more visible mass around the neck and shoulders. Treat this support-and-hanging language as an artistic construction metaphor, not literal veterinary biomechanics.
