# Construct a Foreshortened Animal From Backline, Cross-Axes, and Core Masses

## Practice Task
Build one reclining, turning, or strongly three-quarter animal in four successive studies: spatial reference system, core masses, articulated body, and reduced-guide final. Keep each pass small enough that the same pose can be repeated without turning the exercise into a finished illustration.

## Target Skill
Building a turned or reclining animal as one coherent three-dimensional body by sequencing hidden longitudinal structure, transverse axes, core masses, limbs, and finally surface evidence.

## Setup
Choose one clear reference in which the trunk is not a simple profile and at least one part of the back route is partly concealed. A striped or banded animal is useful for the last pass, but not required. Use a medium that allows light construction and a darker restatement.

## Instructions
1. In the first study, draw the longitudinal route from muzzle toward tail without breaking it at overlaps. Add only a few transverse axes at spatially important points, including shoulder and pelvic landmarks when visible or inferable.
2. Check the tilt of each cross-axis against the pose. If both ends sit level by habit while the body is turning or reclining, correct the axis before adding volume.
3. In the second study, place the major body sections along the established trail. Keep them simple but pose-specific: distinguish the head, neck, thorax, lumbar connection, pelvis, and tail instead of repeating one generic oval.
4. Add the limbs as simplified directions and flat or blocky forms attached to the already oriented shoulder and pelvic regions. Preserve overlap and foreshortening from the axis solution rather than redrawing the trunk around the limbs.
5. In the third study, sharpen the useful intersections where masses pass into one another and let the construction become more organic. Reduce guide lines that are no longer needed, but keep enough visible to diagnose any depth contradiction.
6. In the fourth study, redraw the pose with most or all scaffolding implicit. If the animal has stripes or other transverse markings, use several of them as an independent wrap check: their curvature and spacing should turn around the same solids already constructed.
7. Compare all four studies. If the final version flattens after the guides disappear, return to the first pass and identify which longitudinal or transverse relationship was never truly understood.

## Success Check
- The hidden continuation of the body route remains believable through overlap.
- Shoulder and pelvic cross-axes tilt consistently with the pose and do not default to horizontal.
- The core masses occupy one shared depth system instead of reading as separate stickers or beads.
- Limbs attach to the oriented trunk without forcing late changes to the main masses.
- The third and fourth passes retain the same foreshortening as the explicit-axis pass.
- When natural markings are present, they wrap the solids rather than contradicting them.

## Common Failures
- Drawing a convincing outer silhouette first and inventing the spatial guide afterward to justify it.
- Using a row of identical ellipses or ovoids whose axes do not correspond to the animal's actual shoulder, pelvis, or trunk turn.
- Letting the back route terminate at an overlap instead of continuing it mentally through the concealed section.
- Adding limbs before the shoulder and pelvic orientations are settled, then distorting the torso to make the attachments fit.
- Erasing all scaffolding before testing whether the final organic version still occupies the same space.
- Forcing coat markings into perfect geometric rings rather than observing how the real markings vary as they wrap the body.

## Notes
Bammes presents almost exactly this progression: begin with the center/back route and intersecting cross-axes, expand that system with simplified body masses, then let the guide recede while the animal becomes more organic. In the final stage, the tiger's stripes reinforce the solid impression because the transverse markings wrap around the body like changing cross-sections. A finished example shows the construction fully absorbed into the drawing rather than left visible as a formula.

The stripe check is deliberately secondary here because the library already owns natural markings as surface-wrap evidence. The distinct practice target of this Drill is the sequence from longitudinal structure to cross-axes to masses and then to a guide-free foreshortened animal.
