# Model Animal Trunk Torsion With a Four-Sided Block

## Practice Task
Solve one twisted animal trunk first as a rounded four-sided mass, then as an organic trunk. If possible, make the same simple mass in Plasticine/modeling clay, twist its two ends in opposite directions, draw it from several views, and finally rebuild the animal form without looking at the model.

## Target Skill
Converting axial trunk torsion from an abstract spatial block into a believable organic animal form and then reconstructing the same twist from imagination.

## Setup
Use a pose in which the front and rear of the trunk clearly disagree in orientation. Keep the first pass free of limbs, coat, and muscle detail. A small piece of Plasticine or modeling clay is useful because a hand-twisted model makes the torsion visible.

## Instructions
1. Draw a simple four-sided trunk block with slightly rounded corners. Mark a center/vertebral route and at least three cross-sections so each end has an unambiguous facing direction.
2. Rotate the front and rear sections in opposite directions. Let the intermediate sections turn progressively between them instead of snapping from one orientation to the other.
3. Draw the twisted block from front, side, and three-quarter views. Use foreshortening to prove that the section changes survive a difficult camera angle.
4. If using Plasticine/modeling clay, form the same rounded block physically and twist the ends carefully in opposite directions. Compare the real plane/edge changes with the drawn version and correct any impossible crossings.
5. Wrap an animal trunk around the block: place the thorax and pelvic relationship, then soften the planes into organic form while preserving the progressive turn of the sections.
6. Remove the model and redraw the organic trunk from memory or imagination. Do not copy the previous silhouette; reconstruct the turn from the vertebral route and section sequence.
7. Finish with one simplified animal pose using only the amount of contour and anatomy needed to make the torsion readable.

## Success Check
- Front and rear trunk sections face different directions for a structural reason rather than because the silhouette was skewed.
- Intermediate sections rotate progressively through the trunk.
- The same torsion remains understandable in front, side, and three-quarter views.
- The organic version preserves the block's spatial logic without looking faceted or mechanical by accident.
- The final imagination pass can be rebuilt from the axis and sections after the model is removed.

## Common Failures
- Twisting only the outline while all internal sections remain parallel.
- Rotating the thorax and pelvis independently with no transitional turn between them.
- Copying the Plasticine silhouette rather than learning the section change that produced it.
- Keeping the temporary block too literal in the finished animal.
- Adding limbs and surface anatomy before the torsion itself is solved.
- Treating one front-view solution as sufficient and discovering later that the twist collapses from another angle.

## Notes
Reduce a difficult twisted trunk to a four-sided mass when the front of the body and thighs occupy conflicting directions. For a direct hands-on test, shape a rounded four-sided piece of Plasticine, twist the ends in opposite directions until the form resembles a propeller, and use that simplified object to understand the organic problem. Progress from the modeled block through front and side studies into an approximated organic form and finally an imagination study.

The drill keeps that pedagogical progression intact: **simple mass -> physical/visual proof -> organic translation -> reconstruction from imagination**.
