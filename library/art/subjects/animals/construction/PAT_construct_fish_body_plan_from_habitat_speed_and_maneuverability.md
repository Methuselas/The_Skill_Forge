# Construct Fish Body Plan From Habitat, Speed, and Maneuverability

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a fish must be constructed or designed and a generic streamlined body would erase the movement demands of its habitat
**THEN** choose body depth, elongation, compression, flexibility, and fin distribution as a functional solution for the fish's required speed, maneuverability, station-holding, and use of space
**ELSE** preserve the observed species body plan when reliable reference already establishes those relationships

## Do
- Use a long streamlined body when efficient travel or holding position in stronger flow is the dominant requirement.
- Use a deeper or more laterally compressed body when tight maneuvering matters more than sustained high speed.
- Use an elongated flexible body when the animal must generate strong undulation or turn through confined spaces.
- Use dorsoventral flattening, extreme depth, globular mass, or other departures from the typical streamlined form when the habitat and locomotor role support them.
- Read fin configuration together with the body shape; flexibility, body depth, and fin placement jointly affect the available swimming gait.

## Don't
- Do not reduce fish construction to one torpedo silhouette with species details added afterward.
- Do not assume every aquatic body should maximize streamlining.
- Do not copy one body's proportions while assigning it a locomotor behavior that requires a different balance of flexibility, speed, and maneuverability.

## Checklist
- The body plan implies a plausible swimming strategy before surface detail is added.
- Body depth and elongation agree with the intended speed/maneuverability tradeoff.
- Flexibility is consistent with the amount of whole-body undulation the form could support.
- Fin placement and body shape read as one locomotor system.

## Notes
Webster presents fish morphology as a range of functional solutions rather than one canonical fish shape. Deep-bodied, streamlined, eel-like, flattened, and other forms produce different swimming possibilities. The transferable construction decision is to make the body plan answer the locomotor problem imposed by habitat and behavior.
