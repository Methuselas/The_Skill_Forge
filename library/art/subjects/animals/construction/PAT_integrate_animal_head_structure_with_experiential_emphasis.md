# Integrate Animal Head Structure With Experiential Emphasis

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a finished animal head is either structurally correct but lifeless or expressive but no longer preserves the species-defining head construction
**THEN** keep the skull type and soft-form unity as the stable base, then let texture, tone, mark density, omission, or other finish choices emphasize the particular living impression you want to communicate without contradicting that construction
**ELSE** stop at the structural study when interpretation would add finish without a clearer statement.

## Do
- Preserve the large head type after soft tissue is added; living flesh should round and complete the skull rather than replace its cranial/facial organization.
- Decide what experiential quality matters before choosing finish behavior, then make the marks serve that quality instead of distributing description evenly.
- Let coat-direction hatching, when used, follow surface turn so texture also carries plastic form.
- Use blank areas, selected tonal notes, broken marks, or controlled jottings when suggestion communicates the living appearance more efficiently than exhaustive description.
- Allow personal interpretation only after the practical structure is understood well enough that omission does not make the head generic.

## Don't
- Do not sacrifice species structure in the name of expression.
- Do not assume detailed finish is more truthful than suggestive finish, or that loose finish excuses weak construction.
- Do not copy the horse prism, carnivore ovoid, lioness pentagon, or any historical artist's mark language as a mandatory style.
- Do not elevate Bammes's color/mood example into a general color doctrine; this source only supports the larger structure-first, interpretation-later relationship.

## Checklist
- The finished head still preserves the same skull type and soft-form relationships as the construction underneath.
- The dominant experiential emphasis can be named and is supported by the chosen marks.
- Texture or tone describes form where it is explicit and omission remains structurally intelligible where it is suggestive.
- The finish strategy is chosen rather than applied uniformly.
- Removing decorative effects does not reveal a broken head construction.

## Notes
Bammes closes his head sequence by integrating the object and the artist's subjective experience. He insists that building with masses and structures comes first, then shows multiple legitimate finish strategies: Pisanello's coat-following hatching uses texture to model relief; Menzel uses controlled jottings, tonal suggestion, and blank paper; the orangutan study uses selective emphasis to communicate a personal response. These examples support freedom of finish only after the head's practical structure is secure.
