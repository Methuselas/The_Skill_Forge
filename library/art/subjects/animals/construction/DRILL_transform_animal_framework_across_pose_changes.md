# Transform an Animal Framework Across Pose Changes

## Practice Task
Make a sequence of stripped-down animal skeleton studies that use only the vertebral direction, principal limb segments, and major pivots. Begin with a relatively clear standing or neutral pose, then redraw the framework in movement and in at least two poses that radically change the body orientation, such as crouching, sitting, stretching, rearing, or an upended posture.

## Target Skill
Preserving an animal's articulated anatomy while changing the directions of its spine and limb framework across movement and non-standing poses.

## Setup
Choose one well-documented quadruped or closely related set of animals with reference for several poses. Work small. Use only enough marks to show spine direction, shoulder/hip attachment, major joints, limb segments, and terminal contacts; do not add coat, muscle rendering, or finished contour.

## Instructions
1. Draw a neutral framework and mark the vertebral route plus the main pivots of all visible limbs.
2. Redraw the same structural idea in a moving pose, changing the spine and limb directions rather than simply tilting the neutral diagram.
3. Choose a pose that reorients the trunk strongly—such as rearing or another upended action—and rebuild the framework from the new vertebral direction outward.
4. Choose a compact or folded pose such as sitting, crouching, or stretching and show how the same joint chain changes angle and overlap.
5. Compare the studies side by side. Circle any joint that seems to have changed identity, disappeared, or become an invented bend; redraw that study until the chain is traceable.
6. Only after the framework works, add one light mass pass to a single study to confirm that the stripped construction can support the visible animal.

## Success Check
- The same major anatomical chain remains traceable across all pose changes.
- Spine and limb directions change materially with the pose instead of remaining a memorized standing scaffold.
- Joint order stays consistent even when overlap or foreshortening changes visibility.
- The framework alone communicates whether the animal is standing, moving, upended, sitting, or stretching.
- Adding a simple mass pass does not require relocating the main pivots.

## Common Failures
- Bending a stock standing skeleton without rebuilding the vertebral and limb directions.
- Letting silhouette changes create extra joints or erase necessary ones.
- Adding enough volume that a weak framework becomes hard to diagnose.
- Copying the horse pivot map exactly onto another species without checking its actual proportions and joint placement.
- Treating the exercise as gesture-only and losing the articulated sequence of the limbs.

## Notes
Use a **step-by-step approach to structural animal design**: establish the directions of the vertebral column and limbs, then adapt that framework for movement. Test it with poses that radically alter the studied directions, such as an upended, sitting, or stretching large cat. This differs from pivot-to-main-form practice by delaying volume until after the framework has survived several pose changes.
