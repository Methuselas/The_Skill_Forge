# Couple Splayed Limb Walking With Lateral Trunk Rotation
## Pattern Rule
**IF** a walking animal carries its limbs substantially out to the sides rather than chiefly beneath the trunk
**THEN** coordinate the stepping pattern with visible lateral trunk and shoulder-pelvis rotation instead of animating the body as a rigid beam over independent legs

## Do
- Let side-to-side body rotation participate in advancing the limbs and shifting support.
- Increase or decrease trunk elevation and rotational amplitude with speed and the animal's actual gait strategy.
- Track the tail as part of the whole-body response when the species and gait make it relevant.
- Verify the specific amount and timing from the actual animal rather than turning the pattern into generic "reptile motion."

## Don't
- Do not paste an under-body mammal walk onto a genuinely splayed-limb construction.
- Do not keep the shoulders, pelvis, and trunk locked while the lateral limbs alternate.
- Do not exaggerate lateral rotation beyond the animal's structure merely to signal species identity.

## Checklist
- Limb placement agrees with the body's splayed construction.
- Trunk rotation and support shift are temporally coordinated.
- Body height and tail response fit the gait and speed.
- The motion reads as one integrated locomotor system rather than four independent legs.

## Notes
A crocodilian walk makes the relationship easy to see: lateral limb placement is accompanied by conspicuous side-to-side body action, and the body can lift higher as speed increases. The transferable point is the coupling between limb orientation and trunk rotation.
