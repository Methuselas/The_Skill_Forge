# Switch Kangaroo Support Strategy Between Slow Shuffle and Fast Hop
## Pattern Rule
**IF** animating kangaroo locomotion across slow and fast speeds
**THEN** change the support system rather than merely retiming one gait: use forelimb-assisted hind-foot repositioning with the tail participating in slow support, then shift to synchronized hind-leg hopping with the tail functioning primarily as counterbalance at speed

## Do
- At slow speed, let the forelimbs take support while the paired hind feet move forward together.
- Let the hind legs and tail create a temporary support base that frees the forelimbs to reposition.
- At speed, drive locomotion with the paired hind legs acting together through repeated hops.
- Shift the tail from a support role in the slow pattern toward dynamic counterbalance in faster hopping.
- Keep the small forelimbs subordinate to propulsion during fast hopping unless the observed action gives them another task.

## Don't
- Do not animate a fast kangaroo by simply speeding up the slow shuffle.
- Do not keep the tail's mechanical role identical across both gaits.
- Do not turn the forelimbs into major propulsive limbs during ordinary fast hopping without reference support.

## Checklist
- Slow locomotion includes the forelimb and tail support strategy.
- Fast locomotion clearly switches to paired hind-leg hopping.
- Tail function changes with the gait.
- The transition remains coherent with the animal's center of mass and support.

## Notes
The slow and fast patterns solve balance and propulsion differently. The distinctive value of the example is not merely that kangaroos hop, but that their tail and forelimbs participate in one support system at low speed and reorganize when the animal switches to high-speed hopping.
