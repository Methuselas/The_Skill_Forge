# Animate Pulse-Jet Aquatic Propulsion as Acceleration and Recharge

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an aquatic animal propels itself by drawing in water and expelling it under pressure
**THEN** phrase each jet cycle as an acceleration pulse during expulsion followed by intake or recharge with reduced acceleration or deceleration, smoothing the visible speed change only when pulses become frequent enough to overlap perceptually

## Do
- Make the expelled-water phase the moment when the body gains the clearest forward impulse.
- Let the refill or recharge phase carry less propulsive force so the animal coasts or loses some speed before the next pulse.
- Increase pulse frequency when the animal accelerates or maintains faster travel if reference shows that strategy.
- At very high repetition rates, reduce the apparent between-pulse speed fluctuation so the path can read as nearly continuous motion.
- Keep the body orientation and jet direction consistent with the intended travel direction.

## Don't
- Do not animate a slow pulse-jet animal at perfectly constant speed through both expulsion and refill.
- Do not make the refill phase generate the same impulse as the jet phase unless the species uses an additional propulsive mechanism.
- Do not preserve large visible surges when pulse frequency is high enough that the motion should visually smooth out.
- Do not point the expelled jet in a direction that contradicts the body's resulting acceleration.

## Checklist
- Expulsion and forward acceleration are temporally linked.
- Recharge produces a believable coast or reduced acceleration.
- Faster pulsing reduces the apparent separation between individual surges.
- Jet direction and body travel form a coherent action-reaction pair.

## Notes
Webster describes jet propulsion as inherently pulsatile: the animal loads water, expels it to gain speed, then must refill before the next useful thrust. At low repetition this produces a clear accelerate-and-coast rhythm. As the pulses become rapid, the fluctuations can merge into a smoother overall travel speed.
