# Reverse Animal Effort Against a Load

## Practice Task
Draw the same horse-load problem twice: once driving a heavy load forward and once pulling against that resistance backward.

## Target Skill
Making whole-body animal action change when the direction of effort against resistance reverses.

## Setup
Use a simple harness or implied load direction rather than detailed equipment. Keep both studies at gesture/skeleton stage so the comparison is about drive, support, and body organization instead of rendering.

## Instructions
1. Mark the load direction and the horse's intended travel before drawing the pose.
2. Design a forward-driving version with a clear line of action, planted support, and body masses organized to push or pull the load in that direction.
3. Redraw the same problem with the horse working backward against the load instead of merely mirroring the first silhouette.
4. Compare how the head, neck, trunk, pelvis, and planted limbs re-coordinate when the effort reverses.
5. Strengthen the version whose direction of effort is ambiguous by changing the whole pose, not by adding speed lines or facial expression.
6. Repeat with a lighter imagined load and reduce the amount of bodily drive so the exercise also trains degree, not only maximum strain.

## Success Check
- The direction of effort is readable without arrows or captions.
- Reversing the load changes the organization of the whole animal rather than only flipping the legs.
- Support contacts and body lean agree with the direction of resistance.
- The heavy-load version carries more visible drive than the lighter-load repeat.

## Common Failures
- Mirroring the first drawing instead of redesigning support and body commitment.
- Showing strain only in the neck or face while the trunk and legs remain neutral.
- Adding extreme stretch everywhere regardless of which parts are actually carrying or resisting the load.
- Finishing anatomy before the direction of effort reads clearly.

## Notes
Hultgren assigns this exact reversal problem after his action pages: give the horse a heavy forward pull, get as much drive into the pose as possible, then reverse the problem so the animal works backward against the load. The drill keeps that contrast while treating it as an action-design exercise rather than a claim about exact harness biomechanics.
