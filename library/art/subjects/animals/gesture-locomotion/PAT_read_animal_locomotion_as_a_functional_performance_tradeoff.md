# Read Animal Locomotion as a Functional Performance Tradeoff
## Pattern Rule
**IF** an animal's locomotion is being analyzed or invented from body form alone
**THEN** read the gait as a functional compromise among speed and acceleration, maneuverability, endurance and energy economy, stability, size and mass, environment, and the animal's immediate behavioral objective

## Do
- Separate top speed from acceleration, turning ability, sustained effort, and stability.
- Ask what the animal is trying to accomplish now—pursuit, escape, travel, wading, stalking, climbing, or another task—and which performance qualities that task rewards.
- Let size, mass, limb proportions, body plan, and environment constrain the available tradeoffs.
- When exact species reference is unavailable, use animals of similar type, size, and shape as bounded analogues for plausible principles rather than exact timing.
- Compare more than one plausible locomotor solution when the design could emphasize different functional priorities.

## Don't
- Do not reduce locomotive performance to maximum speed.
- Do not assume the fastest animal also turns, accelerates, or endures best.
- Do not copy a proxy species' exact gait timing into a different animal without verification.
- Do not maximize every performance trait at once when the body plan implies compromise.

## Checklist
- At least two relevant performance demands have been compared.
- The chosen locomotion agrees with body plan and environment.
- The movement strategy serves the current behavioral objective.
- Any analogue species is used as evidence, not as an exact template.

## Notes
Animal locomotion is a functional solution rather than a showcase of one maximum attribute. Predators and prey may succeed through different combinations of acceleration, turning, endurance, stability, and energy cost. Reading those compromises makes both observed and invented movement more specific.
