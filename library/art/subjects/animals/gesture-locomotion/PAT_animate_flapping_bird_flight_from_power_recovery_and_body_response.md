# Animate Flapping Bird Flight From Power Recovery And Body Response

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a flying bird uses repeated flapping and the wingbeat reads like two rigid paddles reversing through the same shapes
**THEN** separate the beat into a load-bearing power stroke and a lower-resistance recovery stroke, let the wing and feathers reorganize for those different jobs, and coordinate the body response with the changing wing force and flight mode
**ELSE** use a simpler held-wing or gliding solution when active flapping is not the motion being shown.

## Do
- Treat the power/downstroke and recovery/upstroke as functionally different phases instead of one motion played forward and backward.
- On the power stroke, present a comparatively broad effective wing surface and let the stroke visibly contribute lift or thrust.
- On the recovery stroke, reduce resistance by partially folding, rotating, or otherwise reorganizing the wing when the species and reference support it.
- Let wingtip and primary feathers trail, flex, or separate relative to the changing direction instead of rotating as one rigid fan.
- Coordinate the body with the beat: allow the body to rise, settle, pitch, or otherwise respond to the wing cycle rather than hovering motionless while only the wings move.
- Change the apparent stroke path with flight mode. Forward flight, climbing, hovering, and landing can require different body attitudes and wing trajectories.
- Scale beat tempo and amplitude to the apparent bird, flight condition, and shot rather than imposing one universal cycle rate.

## Don't
- Do not use identical wing shape and projected area on both the power and recovery strokes when the reference shows aerodynamic reorganization.
- Do not make the wingtip feathers a rigid fan with no trailing, flex, or separation.
- Do not keep the body vertically and rotationally frozen while the wings perform the entire cycle.
- Do not reverse the same drawings symmetrically and assume the two strokes will read as equally convincing.
- Do not assign one wingbeat timing to birds of very different scale or to different flight modes without checking reference.

## Checklist
- The power and recovery strokes have visibly different jobs and shapes.
- Wing surface and feather organization change in a way that supports those jobs.
- The body responds to the wing cycle rather than remaining mechanically fixed.
- The wingtip or primaries show appropriate trailing, flex, or separation.
- The stroke path and timing fit the bird's flight mode and apparent scale.

## Notes
Distinguish the effective downstroke from the recovery upstroke and move the body with the wing cycle rather than holding it fixed. Let primaries trail from the wrist and separate during the upstroke when the species supports it; hovering or landing also changes body attitude and the apparent wing path. Animate a wingbeat as an asymmetric force cycle whose shape, feather behavior, body response, and tempo change with flight condition, not as a mirrored hinge motion.
