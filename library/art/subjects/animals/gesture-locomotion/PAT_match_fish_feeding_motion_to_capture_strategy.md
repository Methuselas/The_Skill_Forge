# Match Fish Feeding Motion to Capture Strategy

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a fish must capture or remove food on screen
**THEN** animate the mouth and body according to the actual capture strategy—pursuit and snap, rapid suction-like opening, or specialized projection—rather than using one generic bite
**ELSE** keep the feeding action constrained to reliable species reference when the mechanism is unclear

## Do
- Combine a forward body lunge with a rapid closing action for active pursuit predators that overtake and seize prey.
- For ambush feeding that uses a rapidly expanding mouth, make the opening itself part of the capture event so surrounding water and prey are drawn inward rather than staging the action as a simple jaw clamp.
- Extend projecting mouthparts only when the species uses them to reach, sift, expose teeth, or remove food from a surface.
- Coordinate eye, body orientation, and final mouth action when the feeding mechanism depends on precise targeting.

## Don't
- Do not animate every predatory fish as a terrestrial animal biting with only jaw closure.
- Do not add a large forward lunge to a stationary suction-based capture when the body is meant to remain comparatively still.
- Do not use telescoping or projecting mouth action as a general fish exaggeration.
- Do not let mouth orientation alone determine the exact capture timing.

## Checklist
- The body contribution agrees with the feeding strategy.
- The mouth action explains how the prey is actually seized or drawn in.
- Specialized projections have a clear functional reason.
- The motion remains compatible with the fish's established mouth construction and habitat.

## Notes
Webster's examples separate fish feeding into visibly different motion problems. A pursuer closes distance before the snap; an ambush feeder can use a rapid mouth expansion to move water and prey toward the mouth; specialized species can project mouth structures to reach or manipulate food. The animation should reveal that mechanism instead of hiding all three inside the same bite symbol.
