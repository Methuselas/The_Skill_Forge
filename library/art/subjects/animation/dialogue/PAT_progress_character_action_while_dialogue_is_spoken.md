# Progress Character Action While Dialogue Is Spoken

## Pattern Rule
**IF** dialogue risks freezing the character into a talking pose instead of continuing purposeful behavior
**THEN** Let thought, posture, gaze, task, or relationship develop through a spoken line instead of freezing the performance and decorating it with mouth shapes

## Do
- Listen to the actual recorded performance before designing mouth shapes. Identify the emotional attitude, phrase rhythm, important words, energy changes, and major accents that drive the acting.
- State what changes during the sentence.
- Choose body, head, gaze, hand, and posture progression that carries that change through the spoken phrase.
- Let those actions reinforce how the line is performed rather than illustrating the literal wording one word at a time.
- Make the mouth serve the ongoing action rather than become the only moving system; detailed lip synchronization sits inside the larger acting plan.

## Don't
- Do not hold a neutral acting pose for an entire line unless stillness is the intended performance.

## Checklist
- The character goes somewhere emotionally or physically while speaking.
- The body-level acting follows the recorded performance's attitude, rhythm, and accents before detailed mouth synchronization is added.

## Notes

Define a readable start and end condition for the character's thought, task, or relationship before subdividing the line. Too many independent changes can compete with the words; preserve the few actions that make the internal progression visible even with the mouth temporarily hidden.
