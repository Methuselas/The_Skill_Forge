# Review Completed Animation From Intent to Evidence and Revision

## Objective
Evaluate a completed or substantially complete animation against its intended job, separate observation from repair, and turn the review into a repeatable revision loop rather than a vague like/dislike judgment.

## Steps / Flow
1. **Restate the intended job.** Identify what the shot or sequence was meant to accomplish in story, action, performance, timing, staging, or another concrete area before judging whether it succeeds.
2. **Watch the whole at real speed several times.** Record the first overall impression before slowing, scrubbing, or single-framing; do not let detail analysis erase the audience-speed read.
3. **Separate evidence of success from evidence of failure.** Note what works as deliberately as what does not, and locate observations by frame, time, drawing, pose, or shot region when possible.
4. **Diagnose before prescribing.** Name the problem and its evidence first, then allow a reflection step before choosing a correction. Do not collapse "I noticed X" into an instant untested fix.
5. **Inspect detail only where the whole-view pass requires it.** Use frame-by-frame comparison, timing analysis, or component isolation to explain a specific issue, then return the finding to normal-speed playback.
6. **Judge in the intended context.** Include sound and neighboring shots when they are part of the audience experience; a motion that works alone may fail in sequence, and the reverse can also occur.
7. **Create distance from maker blindness.** When practical, step away before review or use an informed peer so familiarity does not make errors invisible.
8. **Make critique actionable.** Peer feedback should explain why an element works or fails and identify a testable direction for improvement rather than stopping at praise or dismissal.
9. **Preserve successful causes.** Identify the decisions that produced strong timing, staging, performance, or motion so revision does not accidentally destroy them and so they can be repeated elsewhere.
10. **Revise, then run the review again.** The cycle closes only when the correction survives another whole-speed evaluation against the original intent.

**Completion check**
- The review began from the shot's intended job, not personal taste alone.
- Whole-speed impressions and detailed findings both exist.
- Problems were identified before fixes were chosen.
- Successes were analyzed as causes, not merely praised.
- Revisions were replayed in context after correction.

## Notes
Webster distinguishes animation testing during construction from reflective analysis after substantial work exists. The review should move from intent to whole-speed evidence, through bounded detailed diagnosis, and back into revision. Real-time playback remains the authority for whether a local correction actually improves the experienced motion.
