# Design Animation Hold From Stability Readability And Style

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an animated pose must remain on screen long enough to register, think, listen, react, punctuate an action, or sustain a gag
**THEN** first make the held state mechanically sustainable, then choose its duration and degree of internal motion from the dramatic read and the visual style
**ELSE** pass quickly through unstable extremes instead of freezing them unless the instability itself is the intended effect

## Do
- Check whether the pose or object is in stable equilibrium before treating it as a hold; a balanced relaxed or tense pose can remain readable far longer than an unsupported off-balance extreme.
- Choose hold duration from what the audience needs to perceive, read, or feel rather than from a fixed frame-count recipe.
- Let a fast accent resolve into a readable freeze when the audience needs time after the motion to understand what happened.
- Permit more absolute stillness when the design is strongly graphic or stylized and that stillness belongs to the visual language.
- For more realistic figures, use a restrained moving hold when complete immobility would read as frozen or lifeless; preserve the pose while allowing only small credible signs of continued life.
- Keep tension, thought, listening, or quiet readable without adding motion merely to prevent a frame from being held.

## Don't
- Do not hold an obviously off-balance pose as though gravity and momentum have stopped acting unless the break from physics is deliberate.
- Do not add arbitrary blinks, shifts, or secondary gestures that weaken the meaning of a strong held pose.
- Do not use one universal hold length for reactions, looks, titles, gags, or quiet acting; their readability demands differ.
- Do not confuse a held pose with a dead image; the choice between stillness and a moving hold should follow style, realism, and dramatic purpose.

## Checklist
- The held state is mechanically plausible for as long as it remains on screen, or its implausibility is intentional.
- The audience has enough time to read the pose or story point without waiting after the meaning is already clear.
- Any internal motion during the hold preserves rather than competes with the pose's primary idea.
- The degree of stillness matches the design language and shot realism.

## Notes
A hold is both a physical and dramatic decision. The animator must ask whether the pose can actually remain where it is and whether keeping it there helps the audience. A stable pose may sustain a long pause; an unstable extreme generally needs to flow onward. Graphic characters can often tolerate clean stillness, while a realistic figure may need a subtle moving hold so the pose stays alive without surrendering its clarity.
