# Damp Oscillation By Contracting Extremes Toward Rest

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a flexible or spring-like object must continue oscillating after an impulse and gradually lose energy
**THEN** alternate the motion across its rest position while reducing successive extreme amplitudes until the object settles
**ELSE** use irregular staggered vibration when the required effect is shake or tremble rather than an ordered decaying oscillation

## Do
- Establish the rest position, then place alternating extremes on opposite sides of it so the direction reverses repeatedly.
- Contract the distance of successive extremes as energy dissipates instead of repeating the same amplitude.
- Scale the oscillation period and amount of travel to the apparent mass, length, and flexibility of the object; a large heavy springboard reverses more slowly than a small light spring.
- Allow the spacing around a reversal to be asymmetric when coming out of the extreme more slowly produces the required snap and recovery.
- When a short exact loop begins to look mechanical, extend it with a longer or near-repeat variation so consecutive cycles do not expose the same positions in the same rhythm.

## Don't
- Do not repeat equal-amplitude reversals when the object is supposed to be losing energy.
- Do not substitute random frame order for an oscillation whose defining feature is an ordered back-and-forth return toward rest.
- Do not use the same period for objects of visibly different mass, scale, length, or flexibility.
- Do not let the final frames snap abruptly to rest after the established motion has been damping gradually.

## Checklist
- Successive extremes alternate across a stable rest position.
- Extreme amplitude contracts over time until the remaining motion becomes negligible.
- The period and spacing make the object's mass and flexibility believable.
- The settle does not expose an unintended short mechanical repeat.

## Notes
Damped oscillation is distinct from an irregular shake. The motion keeps reversing in an ordered way, but each reversal carries less displacement as energy is lost. This makes springs, boards, tails, flexible fixtures, and similar systems feel as though they are spending stored motion rather than looping a generic vibration.
