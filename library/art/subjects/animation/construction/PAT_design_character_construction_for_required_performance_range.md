# Design Character Construction for the Required Performance Range
## Pattern Rule
**IF** a character design must be animated through specific actions or performances
**THEN** test and construct the character so its form, volume, articulation, and structural design can actually produce the required pose and movement range while preserving the important design qualities

## Do
- Test representative required poses before treating a static design as animation-ready.
- Preserve volume, form, balance, and anatomical or mechanical articulation through turns and extremes.
- In drawn work, keep the construction capable of surviving perspective and pose changes without losing identity.
- In built or 3D work, translate the concept into joints and deformable regions that permit the narrative's required actions.
- Revise the construction when the design itself blocks necessary performance instead of asking animation polish to hide the limitation.

## Don't
- Do not judge animation readiness from one appealing static view.
- Do not preserve a graphic design feature so rigidly that the character cannot perform the actions the story requires.
- Do not expect animator skill to compensate fully for missing articulation or structurally impossible motion.

## Checklist
- Required key poses are achievable without destroying the character's identity.
- Volume and form remain coherent across the tested range.
- Joint or deformation strategy supports the intended movement.
- The construction can be reproduced consistently from more than one view or pose.

## Notes
Solid drawing and solid modeling share the same practical obligation: the character's construction has to carry performance. A successful static concept is only a starting point; the final construction must remain believable and usable as it changes through time.
