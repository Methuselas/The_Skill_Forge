# Use Symmetry Or Asymmetry Intentionally In Body Language

## Pattern Rule
**IF** body symmetry is affecting the acting read and must be chosen for a specific character or rhetorical effect
**THEN** Use symmetry when order, authority, balance, openness, or rhetorical certainty serves the acting, and break it when a more natural or dynamic asymmetry is needed

## Do
- Name the acting effect before choosing symmetry.
- Use matched sides deliberately for formal or declarative states.
- Break the match through timing, depth, tilt, or differentiated limb roles when symmetry is not the message.

## Don't
- Do not treat “twinning” as automatically wrong or automatically desirable.

## Checklist
- The degree of symmetry supports the character’s current statement.

## Notes

Judge symmetry in the character's three-dimensional body rather than only in the screen contour; perspective can make equal limb roles appear unequal. Preserve matched function when formality is intended, and use unequal roles—not arbitrary offsets—when breaking the pose.
