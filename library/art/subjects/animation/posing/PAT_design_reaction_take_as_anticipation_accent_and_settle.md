# Design Reaction Take As Anticipation Accent And Settle

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a character reaction needs a readable build, peak, and recovery instead of one undifferentiated change
**THEN** Build a reaction take from perception/preparation into a clear accent and then a readable settle or recovery

## Do
- Identify the stimulus.
- Allow a perception or processing interval before the body reacts when the character, situation, or intended characterization requires one.
- Prepare or contract the body as appropriate.
- Coordinate face, torso, limbs, hands, and stance so the take reads as one character-wide response rather than a facial change pasted onto an unrelated body.
- Hit one clear reaction accent or overshoot.
- Let the body recoil, continue, or settle into the new state.
- Make the final readable settle or hold carry the clearest completed expression when the overshoot itself is too brief for the audience to study.

## Don't
- Do not equate a take with maximum displacement; a small reaction can be stronger.
- Do not use one fixed reaction delay for every character; processing time can be part of characterization.
- Do not assume the most extreme overshoot is also the pose the audience will read longest.

## Checklist
- The reaction has a readable cause, processing interval where needed, accent, and aftermath.
- Face and body agree on the reaction rather than competing with one another.
- The accent creates the reaction energy and the settle/hold communicates the completed state clearly.

## Notes
A take can contain two different readable jobs: the overshoot or accent supplies the shock, while the following settle or hold often supplies the expression the audience actually has time to inspect. The delay between stimulus and reaction is therefore a performance variable, not a universal frame count.
