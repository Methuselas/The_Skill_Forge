# Transfer Observed Gesture Onto a Target Character Design

## Practice Task
Take a live model, photographic pose, or live-action frame and a target character design whose proportions materially differ from the source performer. Transfer the observed action into the target character without copying the performer's anatomy, body type, costume, or contour.

## Target Skill
Preserving observed action, weight, support, and attitude while remapping them through a target character whose proportions and construction differ materially from the source performer.

## Setup
Choose a source pose with a readable action and a target model or character sheet with clearly different proportions, masses, limb lengths, or costume shapes. Keep both visible. Work at rough-pose density so detail cannot disguise a failed translation.

## Instructions
1. Before drawing the target, state the source action in a short verb phrase and identify its main weight, support, tension, attitude, and directional relationships.
2. Identify the target character's established head-to-body ratio, torso and pelvis masses, limb proportions, attachment points, and major animatable shapes. Treat those as the body that must perform the action.
3. Block the source gesture through the target construction. Preserve what the action is doing while allowing joint locations, silhouette, spacing, and proportions to change as consequences of the target design.
4. Ignore incidental source costume, facial anatomy, body type, and contour unless one of those facts is actually necessary to the observed action.
5. Check support and balance before refinement. A translated pose that copies the source silhouette but breaks the target character's weight or attachments has failed.
6. Strengthen the target pose only where the action needs clearer animation readability; do not add arbitrary exaggeration simply because the target is cartoony.
7. Compare source and target at the level of action rather than contour similarity. Ask whether the same reach, recoil, lean, compression, push, hesitation, or other gesture survives in a body built differently.
8. Repeat with a deliberately mismatched source and target body type so the exercise cannot collapse into tracing or proportional substitution.

## Success Check
- The source action remains recognizable without the source frame beside the target drawing.
- The target character remains structurally itself rather than becoming the source performer in costume.
- Weight, support, attitude, and important directional relationships survive the translation.
- Differences from the observed silhouette can be explained by the target character's design or by a deliberate readability improvement.
- The pose reads as an authored animation pose rather than a copied photographic state.

## Common Failures
- Copying the source performer's anatomy and then pasting target-character features over it.
- Forcing the target model to match the source silhouette even when its limb lengths or masses make that construction impossible.
- Preserving local contour while losing support, weight, or the reason for the action.
- Ignoring the target's established attachments and proportions in the name of gesture.
- Adding cartoon exaggeration before the basic observed action has survived the transfer.

## Notes
Stanchfield's “double vision” exercise asks the animator to look at the live model while mentally seeing the target cartoon character, carrying over the gesture instead of the performer's literal anatomy. The governing decision already belongs to `PAT_translate_live_action_reference_into_story_readable_animation_extremes`; this Drill turns that decision into deliberate practice by forcing a source/target mismatch large enough that tracing cannot succeed.
