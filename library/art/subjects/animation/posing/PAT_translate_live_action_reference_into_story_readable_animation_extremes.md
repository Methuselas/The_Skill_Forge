# Translate Live-Action Reference Into Story-Readable Animation Extremes

## Pattern Rule
**IF** live-action, photographic, performance-capture, motion-capture, rotoscope-derived, or observed motion is being used as reference for an animated scene
**THEN** analyze what the action means, identify the gesture and story-bearing states, and redesign those states as clear animation extremes rather than tracing the literal reference contours
**ELSE** preserve the observed pose more literally when documentation or faithful study is the actual purpose

## Do
- Treat the reference as evidence for what the character is doing, feeling, and moving through rather than as a sequence of contours, captured transforms, or tracked poses to copy.
- Treat performance-capture and motion-capture data as a starting performance that supplies staging, scale, mechanics, and broad acting rather than as automatically finished animation; retime frames and plus key poses when clearer intent, exaggeration, or nuance is needed.
- Identify the action's meaningful extremes: the states where the gesture, intent, contact, direction change, or story point reads most clearly.
- Strengthen those extremes when the observed action is too continuous, indecisive, or visually minced to communicate cleanly in animation.
- Let animation-specific devices such as clearer silhouette, overlap and drag, squash and stretch, or a temporarily off-balance stance support the intended action when the reference alone does not state it strongly enough.
- Preserve the reference's underlying logic: weight, direction, character identity, contact, and the reason for the action should survive even when the pose is caricatured beyond the camera evidence.
- When the source performer and target character have materially different proportions or construction, remap the action through the target character's established masses, proportions, attachments, and animatable shapes instead of copying the actor and model sheet literally at the same time.
- Compare adjacent extremes as parts of one action so the designed states imply a believable path through the movement rather than becoming unrelated attractive poses.

## Don't
- Do not trace live-action frames, photostats, or captured motion data and assume literal fidelity will produce finished animation.
- Do not preserve weak or accidental intermediate postures merely because the camera captured them.
- Do not exaggerate away the action's weight, contact, character identity, or story purpose.
- Do not force the live-action anatomy onto a differently designed character or distort the target model merely to preserve the actor's literal silhouette; translate the action into the character's own body logic.
- Do not add animation devices mechanically; use them only when they clarify the action or make the designed extreme more expressive and readable.

## Checklist
- The designed extreme communicates what the character is doing without needing the source frame beside it.
- The pose carries the gesture and story point rather than merely matching the photographed anatomy.
- Departures from the reference strengthen clarity, action, or character without breaking the reference's underlying mechanics.
- When proportions differ, the target character remains unmistakably on-model while still performing the source action.
- Adjacent extremes still imply one continuous action with a plausible path between them.
- The result reads as an authored animation pose rather than a traced photographic state or untouched capture solve.

## Notes
Live action and captured performance often contain continuous, transitional movement that works as recorded evidence but does not automatically yield strong animation extremes. Performance capture can establish scale, staging, broad mechanics, and an acting base while still needing key-frame intervention to complete the animated performance. The transferable operation is interpretive: study the real action, extract its gesture and intent, then retime and design states that make those essentials unmistakable. When the performer and target character differ in build, the action must be translated through the character's own construction rather than split between literal actor anatomy and literal model-sheet copying. This is not permission for arbitrary distortion. The reference remains the mechanical and behavioral evidence; animation clarifies and caricatures that evidence so the action reads as a deliberate sequence of expressive extremes.

- Use live-action or sequential reference to discover truthful mass and support mechanics, then translate the useful structure into clearer animation extremes rather than tracing reference indiscriminately.
