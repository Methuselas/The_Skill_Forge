# Test Animation Acting as Pantomime Without Supporting Channels

## Practice Task
Take an acting pose or short animation phrase and temporarily remove dialogue, explanatory background, and other nonessential support so the body must carry the intended action and attitude as pantomime.

## Target Skill
Testing whether animated acting communicates its primary attitude and action through body language when dialogue, background, and other supporting channels are temporarily removed.

## Setup
Choose one pose or short phrase whose story intent is already known. Preserve the character and necessary contact geometry, but prepare a stripped version with dialogue muted and background information minimized. If a prop is indispensable to the literal action, keep only enough of its contact or spatial role to judge the acting rather than asking the viewer to identify an invisible object.

## Instructions
1. State the intended action and attitude in one short phrase before inspecting the stripped version.
2. Remove or mute dialogue, captions, decorative background cues, and secondary information that could explain the beat for the character.
3. Read the body at silhouette scale. Check whether weight, spine direction, head attitude, gaze, hands, opposition, and major negative shapes still point toward the same action and emotional state.
4. For a short phrase, compare the main poses in sequence and ask whether the transition itself preserves the intended motive instead of relying on dialogue to supply it.
5. If the acting becomes ambiguous, repair the largest communicative failure first: pose choice, weight, silhouette, gaze, action direction, or body-wide participation. Do not begin with facial detail or polish.
6. Restore the supporting channels and verify that they reinforce the body language rather than contradicting or rescuing it.
7. Repeat with a second pose that uses a different attitude or action so the test does not become memorization of one stock gesture.

## Success Check
- The stripped pose or phrase still communicates the intended primary action and attitude without explanatory dialogue.
- Major body parts participate in one coherent statement rather than reading as unrelated local poses.
- The silhouette, weight, gaze, and directional relationships support the same story conclusion.
- Restored dialogue, props, and background strengthen an already readable performance instead of becoming its only explanation.
- When a prop is essential, the character's intent and bodily relationship to the action remain readable even if the exact object cannot be identified.

## Common Failures
- Judging only the face while the torso, hands, and weight remain neutral or contradictory.
- Removing an indispensable physical contact and then treating failure to identify the missing prop as an acting failure.
- Adding more detail when the real problem is an unreadable pose or weak action direction.
- Letting dialogue explain a motive that the body actively contradicts.
- Exaggerating every joint equally until the pose becomes noisy rather than clearer.

## Notes
Stanchfield treats mime as a useful test because animated acting should not depend on verbal explanation alone. The durable exercise is not literal stage mime; it is a channel-removal diagnostic. By temporarily suppressing dialogue and other explanatory support, the model can test whether the character's body language carries the action, attitude, and relationship strongly enough to survive on its own. The exception for indispensable props keeps the drill focused on acting rather than turning it into a guessing game about absent objects.

`VAR_mattesi_blind_read_character_and_moment_from_pose_before_reveal` runs the same readability problem in reverse. Hide the intended action, attitude, or dramatic moment from the evaluator, infer the most likely read from the body alone, then reveal the intent and compare. Use it when an independent decoder test is more useful than judging a pose whose intended meaning is already known; do not demand exact wording or invent unsupported biography when the essential action and attitude already agree.
