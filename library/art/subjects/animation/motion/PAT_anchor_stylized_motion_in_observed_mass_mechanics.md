# Anchor Stylized Motion In Observed Mass Mechanics

## Pattern Rule
**IF** stylized movement is being pushed beyond realism and risks losing believable weight, support, or inertia
**THEN** Ground stylized motion in credible mass, support, balance, and weight transfer, then exaggerate selectively without losing the cues that make the action believable

## Do
- Identify the real support and mass behavior before stylizing.
- Preserve the minimum truthful cues that make the motion physically legible.
- Exaggerate shape, timing, or path only after those anchors are understood.

## Don't
- Do not confuse literal realism with believability.
- Do not use surface style to conceal impossible support logic.

## Checklist
- The motion can be exaggerated yet still feels caused by mass and support.

## Notes
- Preserve truthful support, weight transfer, and continuity even when joints bend beyond literal anatomy, locomotion becomes eccentric, or silhouettes are stretched and compressed. Believability is the anchor; realism is optional.
- For apparent load, show the body reorganize around support and resistance rather than merely making the object look heavy.
