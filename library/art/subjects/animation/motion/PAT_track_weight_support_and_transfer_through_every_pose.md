# Track Weight Support And Transfer Through Every Pose

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a pose sequence risks losing clarity about where body mass is supported and where that weight moves next
**THEN** Track where mass is, what supports it, where force comes from, and where weight transfers next at every important pose

## Do
- Mark the current support base.
- Locate the main mass relative to that support.
- Identify the force or effort driving the transition.
- When contact friction supplies braking or redirection, track where the continuing body mass lies relative to the planted contact instead of treating the support as an instant stop for the whole figure.
- Arrange the mass and support so braking resolves momentum deliberately; if the feet stop while the upper mass keeps traveling, show how that continuing momentum is absorbed or redirected.
- Let the supporting contact visibly bear the stopping load when useful through compression, planted area, lean, or local deformation.
- Reorganize the body again as speed approaches zero so a strong braking lean does not become an unintended fall in the opposite direction.
- Verify the next support before advancing the action.

## Don't
- Do not judge weight from shape alone.
- Do not stop the support and assume the rest of the mass stops simultaneously when inertia would carry it onward.
- Do not preserve an extreme braking lean after the momentum it was countering has already dissipated.

## Checklist
- Every important pose has a believable support and transfer explanation.
- Braking or redirection shows where contact force enters the system and how continuing mass is resolved.
- The final support arrangement is stable after the stopping force has done its work.

## Notes
- For quadrupeds, apply the same support audit to front and rear systems separately, then track how the trunk transfers between them. Pelvis and chest may oppose one another vertically while both remain mechanically coherent.
- During braking, support and mass can temporarily oppose one another: friction may arrest the planted contact while the rest of the body continues through inertia. The animation should show both the braking load and the subsequent reorganization into a stable support state.
