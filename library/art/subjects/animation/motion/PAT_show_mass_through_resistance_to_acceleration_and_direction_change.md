# Show Mass Through Resistance To Acceleration And Direction Change

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an animated mass starts, stops, reverses, or redirects and its apparent weight depends on how readily it changes motion
**THEN** Reveal apparent mass through how much preparation, braking, recovery, and commitment are required to start, stop, reverse, or redirect it

## Do
- Increase preparatory organization as mass or resistance increases.
- Show braking and overshoot or recovery where appropriate.
- Make light masses able to change state more readily than heavy ones.
- Let the response to force unfold over time: a brief weak impulse may barely disturb a heavy mass, while a sustained force can gradually accelerate it into substantial motion.
- Use spacing, acceleration, deceleration, and persistence of travel to carry the mass cue even when the drawn shape itself is simple.

## Don't
- Do not communicate mass only with drawing size.
- Do not let a supposedly heavy object start, stop, or redirect instantly unless the scene supplies enough visible force to justify that change.
- Do not assume a detailed or convincingly rendered heavy object will read as heavy if its temporal response behaves like a light one.

## Checklist
- Start/stop/reversal behavior is consistent with the intended mass.
- The amount and duration of applied force plausibly match how quickly the mass changes state.
- The spacing pattern itself supports the intended weight even before surface rendering is considered.

## Notes
Apparent weight is a behavioral property as much as a drawing property. Two similarly shaped objects can read as radically different masses when one resists acceleration and preserves momentum while the other changes speed or direction almost immediately. The useful animation test is therefore not only "does this look heavy?" but "does it respond to force over time like the intended mass?"

- `VAR_whitaker_signal_apparent_scale_through_inertial_timing` applies the same behavioral principle as a scale cue: larger or more massive subjects often read through slower changes of motion and broader natural periods, while smaller or lighter subjects can change state more sharply. Treat this as perceptual evidence rather than a law; supplied force, material, propulsion, style, and character intent can override it.
