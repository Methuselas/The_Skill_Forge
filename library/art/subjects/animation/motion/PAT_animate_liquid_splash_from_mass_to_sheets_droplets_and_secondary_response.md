# Animate Liquid Splash From Mass To Sheets Droplets And Secondary Response

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a splash, thrown liquid mass, or impact-driven water effect must break apart while retaining convincing fluid causality
**THEN** move the liquid from a coherent displaced mass into stretched sheets or streams, break those forms into independent droplets, and let separated pieces continue on their own trajectories while secondary water responses follow the original displacement
**ELSE** keep the water as a simpler unified shape when the shot is too small or stylized to benefit from explicit breakup

## Do
- Start from the force that displaces the liquid and make the first mass radiate, stream, or stretch away from that cause.
- Let the coherent mass thin into sheets, tongues, or elongated directional shapes before fragmenting into smaller pieces.
- Break sheets into droplets progressively as cohesion fails instead of converting the whole splash into particles at one instant.
- Once droplets separate, let them continue on independent ballistic paths rather than forcing every part of the splash to deform as one shape.
- Stagger the disappearance of minor droplets while preserving their downward or outward travel; simplify unimportant endings without making many particles vanish on the same frame.
- When an object enters an open body of water, allow a later secondary jet or rebound where displaced water collapses back into the cavity and drives water upward.
- Adapt the response to the container and available water volume; a constrained vessel may throw water outward without producing the same secondary vertical jet.

## Don't
- Do not keep a splash as one rubbery blob after its thin sheets should have broken apart.
- Do not make separated droplets share one rigid path when their trajectories have diverged.
- Do not delete many droplets simultaneously merely to finish the effect quickly.
- Do not apply the same secondary jet to every impact regardless of surrounding water geometry.

## Checklist
- The splash begins from a clear displacement or impact cause.
- The effect progresses coherently from mass to sheets or streams to droplets.
- Separated droplets continue on individually plausible trajectories.
- Breakup and disappearance are staggered rather than synchronized.
- Any secondary jet or rebound follows from the water's actual opportunity to collapse back into the displaced space.

## Notes
The useful abstraction is a change of organization through time. Water can begin as one displaced mass, spread into thin connected sheets, then lose cohesion so individual droplets behave as separate projectiles. This allows a complex splash to be simplified without losing the causal sequence that makes it read as liquid rather than as arbitrary shape noise.
