# Deform Animated Form With Squash and Stretch While Preserving Volume

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an animated action needs stronger elasticity, impact, expression, or preparation across successive states
**THEN** redistribute the form into purposeful squash and stretch while preserving a believable total volume and letting nearby connected regions react to the same action
**ELSE** keep the deformation subtle when the action is restrained, rigid, or would lose character identity under broader distortion

## Do
- Build the deformation around the action phase: an anticipation may open and lengthen the pose, contact may compress the main mass while other parts extend, and termination may restore more ordinary proportions.
- Let several regions respond at once when the mechanics call for it; a body can compress while arms, ears, cheeks, or other connected structures stretch in the same state.
- Propagate a local action into adjacent soft structures instead of moving one feature in isolation; a broad facial change can alter lips, nostrils, cheeks, eyelids, and nearby flesh as one connected response.
- Preserve the sense that material is being redistributed rather than created or removed; exaggerate contour and proportion without making the form arbitrarily gain or lose bulk.
- Establish a readable normal state before a major deformation when the audience would otherwise have no baseline for recognizing the stretch or squash as a deviation.
- Grade connected reactions outward from the primary action so nearby structures generally respond more strongly and distant structures less strongly unless the design supplies another mechanical reason.
- Use changing negative spaces as evidence of the deformation, especially when an opened anticipation collapses into contact and then reopens during recovery.

## Don't
- Do not treat squash and stretch as a uniform scale operation applied equally in every direction.
- Do not freeze neighboring soft parts while one feature changes dramatically; isolated motion makes the deformation feel pasted on.
- Do not use broad distortion where the character's recognizable construction or the object's rigidity requires a narrower range.
- Do not introduce an unfamiliar character at maximum deformation when the intended normal construction would become ambiguous.
- Do not give every connected region the same reaction strength when the primary action should fall off through the surrounding structure.
- Do not return every region to neutral at the same instant when the action still carries momentum into connected parts.

## Checklist
- The deformed states still read as the same character or object with coherent volume.
- Compression in one region is balanced by expansion, displacement, or redistribution elsewhere rather than unexplained mass change.
- The amount of deformation matches the force and dramatic emphasis of the action.
- Connected soft structures visibly participate when the primary deformation should affect them.
- A strong deviation reads against a recognizable baseline when the audience needs one to understand the deformation.
- Reaction strength falls off or redirects through the surrounding structure in a mechanically readable way rather than spreading uniformly.
- Negative spaces and silhouette changes reinforce the intended anticipation, action, and recovery phases.

## Notes
Squash and stretch is most useful as a relationship between action states, not as a decorative distortion added after posing. Its range can run from nearly imperceptible facial compression to broad whole-body deformation. The important constraint is coordinated response: shapes change to intensify the action while volume, attachment, and character identity remain convincing. A readable norm can increase the impact of a later deviation, and connected reactions usually weaken with distance from the primary deformation instead of propagating at one uniform strength.

- Squash and stretch should strengthen already-believable timing, spacing, contact, and mass behavior rather than manufacture motion after the temporal structure fails. Preserve the underlying amount of mass even in extreme distorted intermediates.
