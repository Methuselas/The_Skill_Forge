# Generate Walk Variants From Fixed Contact Positions

## Practice Task
Hold the main contact positions constant and generate distinct walk characters by systematically changing the passing pose and other structural variables.

## Target Skill
Using fixed contact poses to isolate how passing height, torso and limb action, head and foot phase, bounce, timing, and spacing cause gait character, then recombining only compatible variables into a coherent walk.

## Setup
Use a minimal subject or short sequence so the named variable can be compared without polish hiding the result.

## Instructions
1. Fix the opposite contact poses.
2. Make one conventional middle phase.
3. Make variants by changing passing height, torso bend, joint direction, head phase, foot action, arms, bounce, timing, or spacing one variable at a time.
4. Combine successful variables into several coherent gait designs.
5. For at least one successful combined gait, record why you kept its contributing variables based on what you observed during playback.

## Success Check
- The opposite contact poses remain fixed across the comparison; a gait that feels different because its contacts moved does not count as a variant of the same step structure.
- Single-variable trials make the effect of passing height, torso bend, joint direction, head phase, foot action, arms, bounce, timing, or spacing readable before variables are combined.
- The variants differ in character during playback rather than only in isolated pose drawings or labels.
- Combined gait designs use compatible changes that reinforce one character instead of accumulating unrelated eccentricities.
- For at least one successful combined gait, the reason for keeping its contributing variables is stated in terms of the observed motion they produce, not merely a preference for that version.

## Common Failures
- Changing every variable at once so the cause of the new character is unreadable.

## Notes

Label each isolated trial by the single variable changed and view variants at the same scale and playback rate. Stop combining changes when the cause of the gait character can no longer be named; that is the point to return to the isolated comparisons.
