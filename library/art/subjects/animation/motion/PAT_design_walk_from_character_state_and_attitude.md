# Design Walk From Character State And Attitude

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a walk must communicate who the character is or how they feel, not merely satisfy a generic gait cycle
**THEN** Design gait from age, condition, weight, confidence, mood, personality, and urgency instead of defaulting to a universal walk

## Do
- State the character condition that should be visible in the gait.
- Alter tempo, stride, passing height, bounce, torso, feet, arms, and spacing in service of that condition.
- Preserve enough locomotion structure for the gait to remain readable.
- Let an inexperienced young walker widen the support base, shorten the step, or hold the arms outward when balance—not propulsion—is the immediate problem.
- Let cautious or physically limited walking reduce stride, increase knee bend, shorten single-support exposure, seek extra support, or become a shuffle when reference supports those mechanics.
- When body mass or mass distribution changes balance demands, allow more lateral weight transfer and torso compensation instead of changing speed alone.
- Let mood alter several coordinated variables—tempo, stride, posture, head attitude, arm swing, and springiness—rather than relying on one symbolic cue.

## Don't
- Do not paste personality onto an otherwise unchanged generic cycle.
- Do not turn age, body type, or emotion into a fixed gait dictionary; use these examples as possible responses and verify the actual character or reference.

## Checklist
- The walk would help identify the character even without costume or dialogue.
- Mechanical changes in balance, stride, posture, and arm use agree with the chosen character state rather than a stereotype.

## Notes

**Blair strut-phase variant — `VAR_blair_invert_walk_high_low_phase_to_create_strut`.** One stylized way to turn a walk into a strut is to invert the ordinary vertical emphasis: bring the body high at or near contact, drop lower during the lifted-foot/recovery phase, and coordinate head, torso, arms, and hands with the display. Preserve credible support and treat this as one gait solution rather than a universal strut formula.
