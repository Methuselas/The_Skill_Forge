# Animate Fire And Smoke From Rising Flow Breakup And Dissipation

## Pattern Rule
**IF** fire or smoke must read as animated matter driven by rising hot flow rather than as a sequence of unrelated changing silhouettes
**THEN** organize the effect around upward-moving flow, let forms break up and dissipate as they rise, and vary timing and shape enough to preserve continuous motion without exposing a mechanical repeat
**ELSE** use a simpler graphic treatment when the effect is deliberately symbolic and does not need volumetric flow behavior

## Do
- Establish a persistent upward flow through the effect so successive flame or smoke forms appear to travel and transform rather than stick in one screen position.
- In flame, let motion generally be more active near the hot base and slow as forms rise, taper, split, and disappear.
- Let surges of flame appear quickly and die away more gradually when that asymmetry supports the force of the effect.
- Permit gaseous forms to change apparent volume more freely than solid-character animation while preserving directional continuity from one state to the next.
- Scale temporal breadth to apparent effect size: a large fire or smoke mass can take longer to develop and travel through its full visible structure than a small one.
- Vary repeated smoke puffs, eddies, or cycle shapes when identical repetition becomes visible; use alternate forms, longer cycles, or phase differences rather than random noise.

## Don't
- Do not let flame shapes drift downward or remain pasted in place without a causal reason when the surrounding flow is rising.
- Do not repeat one identical smoke puff or flame cycle long enough for the viewer to identify the loop.
- Do not preserve solid-object volume mechanically when the effect is a changing mass of gas.
- Do not use one timing scale for every fire regardless of apparent size, intensity, or context.

## Checklist
- The effect has a readable dominant flow direction.
- Flame or smoke forms transform while continuing through that flow instead of sticking in place.
- Breakup and disappearance occur progressively rather than as synchronized deletion.
- Timing and cycle design fit the apparent size and energy of the effect.
- Repetition, if used, remains concealed or intentionally visible according to the shot.

## Notes
Fire and smoke are animated as moving flow systems, not as solid shapes that merely change contour. Rising hot air provides the durable directional model: new forms develop lower in the effect, travel and deform upward, then taper, split, and dissipate. Stylization can simplify or exaggerate this behavior, but the motion remains easier to read when the transformation has a consistent causal direction.
