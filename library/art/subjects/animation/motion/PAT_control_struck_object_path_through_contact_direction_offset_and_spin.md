# Control a Struck Object's Path Through Contact Direction, Offset, and Spin

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a ball or comparable object changes motion because it is kicked, hit, batted, or otherwise struck
**THEN** design the outgoing path from the direction and location of contact as well as the amount of force, using off-center or oblique impact when spin or deflection is intended
**ELSE** use the object's existing motion and environmental forces without inventing an impact response

## Do
- Establish the intended outgoing path before choosing the striking surface's orientation at contact.
- Use a more centered, square contact when the task calls for a comparatively direct transfer of motion.
- Offset or angle the contact when the action requires spin, deflection, or a curved subsequent path.
- Treat the foot, racket face, bat, club, or other striking surface as a directional control at the instant of impact, not merely a carrier of force.
- Let the follow-through agree with the contact geometry and intended outcome so the strike, impact, and departing object read as one physical event.
- Calibrate the visible spin response to the actual object and environment rather than assigning every off-center hit the same curve.

## Don't
- Do not decide the object's path from strike strength alone.
- Do not show an oblique or off-center contact while sending the object away as though it were struck squarely unless another force explains the difference.
- Do not give every spinning projectile a canned sideways arc; surface, air, object construction, speed, and context affect the visible result.
- Do not orient the striking surface independently of the trajectory it is supposed to create.

## Checklist
- Contact direction and offset are readable at the impact moment.
- The departing trajectory is plausible for that contact rather than merely for the swing direction.
- Intended spin is supported by asymmetric or oblique contact.
- Follow-through, striking-surface orientation, and object departure describe the same action.
- The amount and effect of spin remain appropriate to the object and conditions.

## Notes
A strike determines more than speed. Where and how the force reaches the object changes the direction of travel and can introduce spin, so the geometry of the contact surface is part of trajectory design. Exact aerodynamic or surface response remains reference-dependent; the transferable animation decision is to make the outgoing motion answer the actual impact.
