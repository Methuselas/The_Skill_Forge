# Build the Living Head Over a Constructed Skull

## Practice Task
Train causal transfer from skull construction to living head form so features and soft tissues are placed **because of** the underlying structure rather than copied as independent symbols.

## Target Skill
Practice the named decision until the structural result can be repeated deliberately without relying on surface copying.

## Setup
No special setup required.

## Instructions
1. Choose a clear head view. Establish the whole head orientation with a symmetry axis and spatial cross-axes before drawing features.
2. Reduce the head to the two large skull systems: cranial mass and facial skull. Give the cranial ovoid readable forehead, temple, crown, occipital, and base directions; keep these planes curved/spheroidal rather than turning the head into a rigid cube.
3. Construct the facial support beneath it: jaw, central facial support, cheek/zygomatic structure, and the major socket relationships. Draw through enough **hidden edges and opposite-side reference points** to prove that both sides belong to the same three-dimensional skull.
4. On tracing paper, a new registered layer, or a second clean pass directly over the construction, design the living head over that skull. Keep the eye as a spherical form embedded in the socket environment; give the nose a ridge and side planes; let the mouth/dental region bulge and recede as a volume rather than a flat line.
5. Add only the soft forms needed to explain the present head. Preserve the skull's turn, tilt, proportions, and major support relationships while allowing individual soft-tissue variation.
6. Suppress most of the construction. Compare the living result with the skull beneath it. If a feature must be relocated, flattened, or enlarged merely to make the surface look plausible, return to the skull stage and diagnose the structural mismatch.

## Success Check
- The living head preserves the same turn, tilt, width/depth relationship, and cranial/facial attachment as the skull study.
- Major features occupy one common spatial system and do not appear pasted onto a mask.
- Hidden-side landmarks can be mentally continued through the head without contradiction.
- Removing the construction leaves a believable living head whose important surface events can still be explained by the skull beneath it.

## Common Failures
- Copying the visible face first and retrofitting a skull afterward.
- Treating the cranial mass as a flat circle or the facial skull as a front-facing mask.
- Drawing only visible edges so opposite-side landmarks drift independently.
- Rendering eyes, nose, lips, or ears in detail before the whole head and skull agree.
- Preserving the teaching scaffold literally in the finished head instead of letting living form replace it.

## Notes
Bammes's transparent-paper test is the core exercise: the skull study should be able to **generate** a living head. This is anatomy as construction, not an anatomy-chart overlay.
