# Map Facial Wrinkles by Flow and Cause

## Practice Task
Use several real faces or clear photographic references to make small head studies in which the **large wrinkle routes and their causes are mapped before individual crease rendering**. The goal is not to collect wrinkles; it is to understand why the visible lines travel where they do.

## Target Skill
Reading facial creases as consequences of one deforming head surface—regional flow, active tension or pressure, and age/material sag or shrinkage—then simplifying that read into a usable drawing.

## Setup
Choose three references with different visible conditions: one active expression, one pose with obvious head/jaw/neck compression or stretch, and one older or more relaxed face where gravity/material effects are visible. Keep the head construction simple so surface study does not replace the skull and feature masses.

## Instructions
1. On each head, lightly establish the cranial/facial construction and the major feature masses. Do not begin with wrinkle lines.
2. With a second pass, mark only the dominant **regional flow** you can actually see: central/frontal, oblique from the inner eye/cheek, or lateral around the outer eye/ear/jaw. Omit any family that the reference does not support.
3. For each major crease cluster, write or mentally assign its most useful drawing cause: **tension**, **pressure/compression**, **sag**, **shrinkage/material change**, or mixed/uncertain.
4. Redraw the face cleanly using only the strongest crease routes needed to explain those causes. Let folds wrap the parent forms and weaken or split where the reference shows them losing force.
5. Compare the simplified drawing to the reference. If a wrinkle can be removed without losing the action, age, or surface structure, remove it. If a line remains but you cannot explain what tissue event produced it, re-observe before keeping it.
6. Finish with one memory rep from the weakest reference: reconstruct the head and redraw only the major flow/cause map, then check it against the source.

## Success Check
- Wrinkle routes wrap the constructed head and follow its perspective instead of sitting on the face as stock forehead, crow's-feet, or nasolabial symbols.
- Each major crease cluster is assigned a supported cause such as tension, compression, sag, material change, or mixed/uncertain; naming a cause that the reference does not show is not a stronger answer.
- Multiple causes can coexist across one face without being separated into artificial diagram zones or given equal line weight everywhere.
- The clean redraw keeps the expression, age, or compression read with fewer lines than the reference rather than proving effort by copying every visible crease.
- The memory rep preserves the major flow/cause map, and comparison with the source identifies where remembered routes drifted instead of accepting familiar wrinkle patterns as correct.

## Common Failures
- Copying every visible line at equal strength.
- Drawing stock forehead, crow's-feet, or nasolabial symbols without checking their present direction and cause.
- Forcing all three Hogarth flow families onto every face.
- Labeling every old-age crease as sag or every expression crease as muscle tension when the actual cause is mixed or unclear.
- Adding wrinkles to compensate for weak head construction or unclear feature deformation.

## Notes
Hogarth's chapter is most useful as an **observation organizer**, not a universal wrinkle atlas. His three flow families and four cause labels are scaffolds for asking better questions of a real face. The reference remains the authority on which routes exist, how strong they are, and where they merge or disappear.
