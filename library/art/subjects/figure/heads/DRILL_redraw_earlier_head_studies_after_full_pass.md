# Redraw Earlier Head Studies After a Full Pass

## Practice Task
Return to one or two head studies that gave you trouble earlier in the learning sequence. Without tracing or correcting the old drawing, solve the same kind of head again from scratch with your current construction method, then compare the old and new attempts at the structural level.

## Target Skill
Testing whether study has changed the default way you construct a head, rather than mistaking familiarity with the lessons for usable improvement.

## Setup
Choose an earlier drawing whose problem is still identifiable: turn/tilt, cranial-to-facial proportion, planes, feature placement, age/type, or tonal modeling. Keep the original drawing visible **only after** the new attempt is complete. If the old study used a reference, reuse the same reference when possible so the comparison tests learning rather than a different subject.

## Instructions
1. Name the original difficulty in one short phrase before redrawing: for example, “jaw detached from cranium,” “eyes flatten in three-quarter view,” or “child head reads adult.”
2. Hide the old drawing and make a fresh construction. Establish the cranial mass, facial plane, centerline/cross-contour, proportions, and major planes before local features or finish.
3. Stop once the new drawing reaches roughly the same stage as the old one. Do not win the comparison merely by rendering the new version more heavily.
4. Put the two drawings side by side and compare them in order: whole head envelope → turn/tilt → cranial/facial relationship → jaw/planes → feature placement → age/type → tone or finish.
5. Identify the **earliest** relationship that improved. Then identify the earliest relationship that still fails or drifts.
6. If the same structural failure remains, revisit the specific construction rule or exercise that owns it and make one immediate third attempt. Change that relationship deliberately rather than polishing around it.
7. Record one sentence: “The redraw improved ___; the next weak point is ___.” Use that weak point to choose the next practice rep.

## Success Check
- The new drawing solves at least one earlier structural problem before extra finish is added.
- The comparison isolates a specific changed decision rather than declaring the newer drawing generally “better.”
- A remaining failure is assigned to the earliest stage that owns it.
- The third attempt, when needed, changes the diagnosed relationship rather than adding detail.

## Common Failures
- Choosing an easy new subject instead of revisiting a real earlier difficulty.
- Comparing a rough old study with a polished new one and calling finish “progress.”
- Correcting only eyelashes, hair, wrinkles, or contour while the cranial/facial construction is unchanged.
- Looking at the old drawing throughout the redraw and unconsciously copying its solution or its error.
- Treating one improved redraw as proof that every head problem is solved.

## Notes
Loomis closes the head section by asking the learner to revisit material that was difficult earlier and compare new drawings with the first attempts. The useful practice principle is a controlled **return test**: repeat a previously hard problem after the full study sequence and judge whether the construction decisions themselves have changed.
