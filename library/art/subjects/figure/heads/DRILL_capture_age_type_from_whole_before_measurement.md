# Capture Age Type From the Whole Before Measurement

## Practice Task
Train rapid recognition of age-specific figure proportions so a child, adolescent, or young adult does not collapse into a scaled adult mannequin.

## Target Skill
Recognize and preserve developmental age through coordinated whole-body proportions rather than a single head-size cue or surface detail.

## Setup
No special setup required.

## Instructions
1. Use an unposed or naturally moving reference rather than a deliberately staged academic pose when possible.
2. Before drawing, take one quick whole-figure read: overall height, apparent head-to-body relation, torso versus leg dominance, shoulder/hip relationship, limb thickness, and joint prominence.
3. Make a fast Stage 1 sketch without pencil measuring. Preserve the **whole age impression first**.
4. Add only a few age-relevant structural cues that the reference actually supports: relative head size, leg length, torso fullness/slenderness, limb proportions, muscle development, or soft-tissue fullness.
5. Compare against the reference. Diagnose the error as a **relationship error** rather than fixing it by adding facial or clothing detail.
6. Repeat with several developmental stages so the sketches cannot be solved by one generic “child” symbol.

## Success Check
- A viewer can distinguish the broad developmental stage from the body proportions even with facial detail removed.
- The figure reads as an integrated body type rather than an adult figure with a larger head pasted on.
- Head size, leg length, torso mass, and limb character agree with one another.
- The sketch remains loose and quick without becoming arbitrary.

## Common Failures
- Drawing “a child” as a single universal proportion recipe.
- Using a large head as the only age cue.
- Correcting an age read with hairstyle, clothing, or facial detail instead of body relationships.
- Measuring so slowly that the natural posture and whole impression are lost.

## Notes
Bammes's sketchbook rule is not “be approximate.” Rapid work still requires a clear target. For youth figures, the target is the coordinated age-specific whole.
