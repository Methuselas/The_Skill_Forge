# Design an “About To…” Pose From a Support Shift

## Practice Task
Create six minimal gesture figures that each read as being about to do something: step, turn, throw, rise, catch, or change direction.

## Target Skill
Practice the named decision until the structural result can be repeated deliberately without relying on surface copying.

## Setup
No special setup required.

## Instructions
1. Begin each figure with the head-to-weight-bearing-side gesture and a simple gravity line.
2. Deliberately avoid a fully mirrored, settled stance.
3. Shift the head, rib cage, and pelvis so the pose carries tension relative to its current support.
4. Add the second support or destination contact where it can plausibly catch or complete the action.
5. Keep the drawing at gesture level; do not use anatomy or costume to sell the action.
6. Under each figure, write the implied verb and check whether the pose communicates it without the label.

## Success Check
- Each pose has a readable current support and a believable next support or directional outcome.
- The six poses do not collapse into the same generic lean.
- At least four imply the intended verb before the label is read.
- The figures remain structurally plausible despite deliberate imbalance.

## Common Failures
- Making every pose merely diagonal without changing support.
- Moving limbs theatrically while leaving the three major masses vertically stacked.
- Confusing asymmetry with random distortion.
- Treating the exercise as a rule that stable or symmetrical poses are always bad.

## Notes
Hampton presents “about to…” as one corrective exercise, not the solution for every pose. Symmetry can legitimately communicate power, strength, immobility, or stasis.
