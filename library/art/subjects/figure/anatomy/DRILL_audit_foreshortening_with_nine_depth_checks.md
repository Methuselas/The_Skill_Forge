# Audit Foreshortening With Nine Depth Checks

## Practice Task
Take one Stage 2 or early Stage 3 figure with a strong advance/recession problem and make a diagnostic copy. Run Hogarth's nine depth principles as **questions**, one at a time, to identify the smallest spatial failure that explains the bad read. Correct only the failed relationship, then redraw the pose without the diagnostic scaffolds.

## Target Skill
Diagnosing a foreshortening failure before solving it with more anatomy, more rendering, or a complete restart.

## Setup
Choose a pose with at least one limb or torso mass moving strongly toward or away from the camera. Preserve the intended camera, action, and major mass arrangement. Keep one untouched copy beside the diagnostic version so every correction can be compared against the same starting problem.

## Instructions
1. **Overlap:** number the major masses from near to far. Check whether nearer contours actually interrupt farther forms and whether accidental tangencies flatten the intended order.
2. **End-on section:** inspect the most foreshortened cylindrical member. Ask whether its side walls shorten while its cross-sectional width and end-curve read remain believable for the same member.
3. **Joint positions:** reduce the doubtful limb to socket, intermediate joint, and terminal point. Place those locations before rebuilding the masses between them.
4. **Contour compression:** when a tapered member points strongly into depth, check whether the broad-to-narrow change compresses over the shortened projected length instead of stretching into a long side-view taper.
5. **Receding planes:** only after the structure is coherent, test whether a restrained value or form-following stroke can clarify which planes turn away. Do not use tone to conceal an unresolved overlap or attachment.
6. **Pivot reach:** if an endpoint still looks too long, too short, or disconnected, use the carrying joint as a temporary pivot and test the legal reach of the next joint or terminal form.
7. **Clear-view projection:** if the deep view remains hard to visualize, solve a simple side, front, or back correspondence view and transport the important landmarks into the target orientation before restoring form.
8. **Perspective tracking:** check the figure against a coherent ground or scene perspective field. Use temporary tracks through comparable landmarks or contacts when the feet, pelvis, hands, or surrounding objects drift out of the same spatial system.
9. **Joint interlock:** at a bent knee, elbow, or knuckle, decide which member advances and verify that the decisive joint contour interlocks with that advancing member rather than reversing the depth read.
10. Circle only the checks that exposed an actual failure. Correct those relationships at the stage that owns them, remove the temporary guides, and redraw the figure once from the corrected construction.

## Success Check
- You can name the specific depth failure instead of saying only that the pose “looks off.”
- The corrected version preserves the intended action while clarifying near-to-far order, member identity, reach, and joint direction.
- Tone or detail is added only after the structural checks pass.
- A clearer source view or perspective track is used only when direct construction is genuinely uncertain.
- The final redraw no longer needs all nine visible guides to remain convincing.

## Common Failures
- Treating the nine checks as compulsory geometry on every foreshortened drawing.
- Running later checks to compensate for an earlier unresolved overlap, joint, or proportion error.
- Changing the pose or camera during diagnosis so the original problem disappears rather than being solved.
- Letting an ellipse, projection line, value patch, or grid overrule a more convincing articulated figure merely because the guide looks technical.
- Applying every correction at once and losing the ability to identify which spatial control actually mattered.

## Notes
Treat the nine depth checks as flexible analytical aids rather than fixed laws. Each check exposes a different class of foreshortening failure, so use only those the present pose needs. Overlap, width/end-curve control, articulated chains, pivot reach, joint interlock, tone, and perspective retain their own dedicated owners; this drill's distinct value is the ordered audit when the failure category itself is uncertain.
