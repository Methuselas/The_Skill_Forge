# Construct the Whole Figure From Mass Relations Before Contour

## Practice Task
Train whole-figure proportion and mass perception so the body is assembled from large relationships rather than copied as an enclosing outline.

## Target Skill
Capture a specific figure's body type, proportion, and support from large mass relations before contour or anatomical detail.

## Setup
No special setup required.

## Instructions
1. Establish the figure's top, bottom, central plumb/symmetry reference, and a few major height divisions before drawing local contour.
2. Block the head, rib-cage region, pelvis/hip region, and leg direction as broad strips, rectangles, or flat-brush masses. A paper-strip version is valid when practicing physically.
3. In the broad-material version, begin from an interior structural center such as the pelvis or rib-cage center and **grow the mass outward toward the periphery** while watching the reference continuously.
4. Judge width and height together as the figure expands. For profile studies, keep the rib cage and pelvis offset according to the observed stance instead of stacking them as centered blocks.
5. After the whole mass and silhouette relationship reads, add only the corrections needed to recover the specific model: characteristic width changes, overlaps, a few internal forms, and limited line refinement.
6. Compare the result against the reference at thumbnail size. Repeat if the model-specific body type disappears when local detail is removed.

## Success Check
- The figure remains recognizable as the specific observed body type before detailed contour or anatomy.
- Major width/height relations and support direction agree with the reference.
- Profile masses feel counterbalanced rather than vertically stacked.
- Contour refinement clarifies an already coherent whole instead of creating the whole after the fact.

## Common Failures
- Drawing the external outline first and mechanically filling the interior.
- Treating proportional divisions as a fixed canon instead of measurements of the present model.
- Adding anatomy or portrait detail before the large mass relations are stable.
- Centering every torso/pelvis/head block on one vertical in profile regardless of the observed stance.

## Notes
Several media can isolate the same perceptual problem: paper strips, flat brush, broad dry brush, and later line correction. The durable lesson is the work order: **large relational mass first, contour and specificity second**.

`VAR_mogilevtsev_audit_big_figure_silhouette_as_single_patch` adds a temporary diagnostic pass before reopening those internal masses: read the entire live figure as one filled outside patch, compare its overall height, width, lean, and placement, correct the earliest large mismatch, then restore internal negative spaces and construction. The patch audit is intentionally incomplete; it is a check on the whole envelope, not a replacement for mass construction.
