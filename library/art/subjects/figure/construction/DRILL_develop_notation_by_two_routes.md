# Develop One Notation by Two Routes

## Practice Task
Take one invented action through two development methods: a small exploratory notation enlarged after selection, and a final-size notation developed continuously on the same working area.

## Target Skill
Preserving the governing action while developing a notation from thumbnail to large format and through a continuous final-size route.

## Setup
Choose one action idea and reserve two equal final-size areas. Keep the first route's thumbnail separate so it can be compared with the enlargement.

## Instructions
1. **Route A — thumbnail to large.** Make several small notations, choose the strongest action, then enlarge it into a Stage 2 construction while preserving the torso centerline, attachments, balance, and limb directions.
2. Develop the selected Route A construction through Stage 3 and a Drawing Stage 4 finished-pencil study without silently replacing the action.
3. **Route B — continuous final size.** Begin at the intended final scale with light probing marks and several local arm or leg alternatives around one governing torso.
4. Reject the weaker alternatives, commit one coherent Stage 2 figure, and continue on registered layers through Stage 3 and a Drawing Stage 4 finished-pencil study.
5. Compare which route preserved spontaneity, which exposed structural errors earlier, and where either route became flat or stale.

## Success Check
- Both final studies preserve the same intended action even though their search methods differ.
- Route A enlarges the selected notation without losing its visual impact.
- Route B rejects alternatives before Stage 3 rather than carrying multiple limbs into specific form.
- Construction order remains visible in the process even when the finished drawing no longer shows it.
- The comparison identifies a concrete reason to choose one route for a future task.

## Common Failures
- Treating enlargement as permission to redesign the pose.
- Tightening the thumbnail until it loses the action before enlargement.
- Letting final-size probing accumulate into multiple unresolved bodies.
- Jumping from a rough trial directly to finish without a coherent Stage 2 commitment.
- Calling a new, more interesting pose a refinement of the original instead of recognizing it as a new notation.

## Notes
The two routes solve different working problems. Small notation separates exploration from development; final-size notation can preserve a sudden visual idea by carrying the same drawing forward continuously. Repeated neighboring sketches may also reveal that the primary idea is flat or stale. In that case, select the stronger notation deliberately rather than forcing the first attempt to survive.
