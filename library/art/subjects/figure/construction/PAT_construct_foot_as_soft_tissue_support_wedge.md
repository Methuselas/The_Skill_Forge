# Construct the Foot as a Soft-Tissue Support Wedge

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a planted or weight-bearing foot must be blocked before toe and tendon detail
**THEN** enclose the heel block and forefoot platform inside one continuous soft-tissue wedge, organize support through the longitudinal and transverse arches over a practical heel/great-toe-ball/little-toe-ball base, and keep the outer side lower and more continuous while the inner side rises higher without becoming hollow
**ELSE** simplify the foot to heel and forefoot contacts before shaping the arch

## Do
- Give the heel and forefoot distinct load-bearing volumes connected by a fleshed arch span.
- In an ordinary planted foot, organize the support plan through the heel, great-toe ball, and little-toe ball, then build the higher inner longitudinal arch, lower outer route, and transverse forefoot arch over that base. Treat these as support regions, not three isolated visible points.
- Preserve the foot's asymmetry: the outer/little-toe side is generally lower and flatter through the heel-to-forefoot support, while the inner/great-toe side rises through the stronger arch; let the ankle relationship inherit the same asymmetry when the view reveals it.
- Keep the toes compact at the front platform; let the great toe rise while the smaller toes close down for traction and push.
- Show enough sole thickness under the inner arch that the foot remains a continuous living mass.

## Don't
- Carve an empty tunnel through the instep or expose the arch as if it were an external bone block.
- Treat the foot as a hand with long independent toes.
- Aim the foot independently of the shin rotation.
- Force the ordinary three-region support plan onto toe-only push-off, pointe, kneeling, unusual footwear, or another contact state whose actual pressure pattern is different.

## Checklist
- The heel, arch, and forefoot read as one connected volume.
- Ground contact is plausible for the actual pose; in an ordinary planted stance, the heel and both sides of the forefoot cooperate as the main support regions with a more continuous outer route.
- The inner contour rises without making the midfoot disappear, and the transverse arch prevents the forefoot from reading as a flat slab.

## Notes
Hogarth's compound wedge is useful, but one plate overstates the openness of the arch. Guided review corrects the shorthand: skeleton, tendon, muscle, fat, connective tissue, and skin continue across the instep, so the external foot never becomes a hollow bridge.

`VAR_zarins_wrap_heel_core_in_weight_bearing_fat_pad` retains **Wrap the Heel Core in Weight-Bearing Fat Pad** as a bounded alternative; use it only under the conditions recorded in the variant metadata.
