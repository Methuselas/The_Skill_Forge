# Unify a Foreshortened Figure in Deep Space

## Objective
Restore whole-body continuity when a structurally correct deeply foreshortened figure has become visually fragmented by overlap, without using surface integration to hide incorrect Stage 2 size, attachment, or depth order.

## Steps / Flow
1. **Pass the entry gate.** Enter only when the Stage 2 masses, attachments, near/far order, and advancing/receding size relationships are already trustworthy but the Stage 3 figure reads as disconnected pieces. If apparent fragmentation is actually caused by wrong size/hidden-path logic, return to `AP_control_foreshortened_form_size_in_stage_two`; if attachment/support is wrong, return to `AP_notate_a_figure_in_structural_order`.
2. **Read every visible fragment as part of a complete hidden member.** Preserve enough direction, volume, identity, and attachment that the form can continue coherently behind nearer masses.
3. **Locate the true continuity breaks.** Identify passages where correct overlap nevertheless makes the figure resemble separate beads/lumps and determine whether the cheapest remedy is structural interconnection, encompassing contour, edge suppression, or tone.
4. **Use only valid anatomical/form routes.** Search for real folds, tendon paths, muscle interlaces, bony transitions, or other camera-valid structures that can carry the eye across a separation. Do not invent connector lines merely because two masses need to feel related.
5. **Respect camera projection.** Let high, low, front, rear, and three-quarter views alter overcurves, undercurves, compressions, and dominant masses while preserving the underlying body.
6. **Carry continuity through joints and attachments.** Let knee, hip, shoulder/deltoid, elbow, neck, and other transitions redirect the form as parts of one system rather than inserting neutral connector blocks.
7. **Escalate to whole-contour control when local lacing competes.** If many little connectors make the figure busier but not more unified, strengthen the encompassing outer flow and suppress internal divisions that keep the body assembled-looking.
8. **Use tone only as downstream consolidation.** When the chosen light can fuse resolved masses more clearly than line, let values soften/break selected boundaries. Tone may consolidate a solved body; it may not conceal uncertain construction.
9. **Pass the whole-figure unity gate.** Reduce the drawing and verify that the figure reads as one continuous body while retaining the intended near/far order and overlap. If unity depends on explaining every hidden connection explicitly, simplify and retest.
10. **Complete with the cheapest sufficient integration.** Stop when interconnection, contour, edge control, and/or tone make the structurally correct figure read as one body at whole-figure scale. Do not maximize every cohesion device merely because it is available.

## Notes
This AP owns a **continuity** problem, not a Stage 2 reconstruction problem. The primary rollback distinction is therefore structural error versus visual fragmentation: wrong size/attachment/depth returns upstream; correct structure with a segmented read stays here.

Hogarth presents linear interconnection, coordinating contour, and tonal fusion as escalating means of cohesion. PASS treats them as optional controls whose value is measured by the whole-figure read, not as three compulsory finishes.
