# Lace Separated Forms With Valid Interconnections

## Pattern Rule
**IF** overlap makes adjacent masses read as detached lumps even though the underlying construction is sound
**THEN** carry a small number of structurally true surface passages from an outer contour into the next form so the eye can follow one continuous system
**ELSE** leave the connection implicit when the silhouette, attachment, and existing form already read clearly

## Do
- Search at places where the body actually produces connective evidence: skin wrinkles, tendon insertions, muscle interlaces, bony prominences, stress channels, and transitions between large masses.
- Let an outside edge become an inside line when that turn accurately describes how one form passes into its neighbor.
- Keep exploratory routes light until the specific form is confirmed, then retain only the passages that improve continuity.
- Follow the object's own structure; the same principle can transfer to mechanical or designed forms when their real construction provides a valid joining path.

## Don't
- Memorize Hogarth's long routes as fixed formulas for every body, build, pose, or viewpoint.
- invent decorative lines merely because a passage looks empty.
- Keep adding connective marks after they begin to obscure the larger figure.

## Checklist
- Every retained line corresponds to a believable anatomical or functional transition.
- The eye crosses the overlap without mistaking the connected masses for separate objects.
- Removing a retained passage would weaken the intended flow; removing an unnecessary one would improve clarity.

## Notes
Knuckle channels, finger insertions, foot wrinkles, Achilles and hamstring tendons, torso routes, limb joints, shoulder passages, and neck complexes do not share one universal line map. Their common principle is that a true local connection can restore continuity where overlapping forms have visually interrupted it.
