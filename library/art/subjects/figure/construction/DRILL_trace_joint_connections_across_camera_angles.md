# Trace Joint Connections Across Camera Angles

## Practice Task
Construct one knee, one shoulder, and one head-neck attachment from four camera positions, then trace only the connective routes that each view actually exposes.

## Target Skill
Preserving one connected anatomical system while its visible routes change with viewpoint.

## Setup
Use twelve small boxes: high, low, front-three-quarter, and rear-three-quarter views for each region. Keep the first construction lines light.

## Instructions
1. Block each region as connected volumes before adding any surface route.
2. Mark which mass is nearest to the camera and which surfaces turn away.
3. On the knee studies, carry thigh, patella or condyle region, tendons, shin, and calf through one leg without inserting a separate hinge.
4. On the shoulder studies, connect the deltoid into the arm and into the visible torso passages rather than attaching an arm cylinder to a socket symbol.
5. On the neck studies, select among sternomastoid, trapezius, neck-funnel, and implied spinal routes according to what the view reveals.
6. Compare the high and low views. Identify where natural curves reverse from overcurve to undercurve because the camera moved, not because the anatomy changed.
7. Erase every route that was memorized from another box but is unsupported in the present view.

## Success Check
- Knee, shoulder, and head-neck sets read as the same underlying regions from different cameras rather than one memorized line map copied into four boxes.
- Connective routes appear only where the current view supports them; a route remembered from another view is erased instead of being kept because the anatomy name is correct.
- The knee redirects thigh into shin, the deltoid carries into arm and torso, and the neck routes join head and trunk without a separate hinge, socket symbol, or floating connector.
- High and low views show the expected overcurve/undercurve reversals where camera position changes what surface is exposed, not because the anatomy itself was redesigned.
- The final boxes explain their curves through form and depth evidence rather than a page-up or page-down rule.

## Common Failures
- Copying one anatomical line map into all four views.
- Treating the knee, deltoid, or neck as an isolated connector.
- Darkening exploratory lines before the view has proved them.

## Notes
Hogarth's long pathways are best used as demonstrations of structural continuity, not tracing formulas. Later reference review should repeat this exercise across different bodies and artists so the learner sees which routes belong to anatomy, which belong to viewpoint, and which belong to a particular style.
