# Block Connected Torso Masses Across Views

## Practice Task
Make six torso-only blocks that preserve the same basic proportions while changing view, tilt, and opposition.

## Target Skill
Orienting the rib cage and pelvis independently while preserving a continuous waist and centerline.

## Setup
Use no head or limbs except tiny attachment marks. Divide the page into six boxes.

## Instructions
1. Draw a barrel, pelvic wedge, broad waist connection, centerline, and one cross-contour on each mass.
2. Include front, rear, three-quarter, side, forward bend, and opposed turn solutions.
3. At the end of the block pass, erase or suppress any guide that proposes a second construction.
4. Reduce every torso to its centerline and two masses to confirm that the original view still reads.

## Success Check
- Front, rear, three-quarter, side, forward-bend, and opposed-turn blocks are distinguished by centerline, cross-contour, and mass orientation rather than by six altered outer silhouettes around the same front-facing construction.
- The rib-cage barrel and pelvic wedge keep the chosen basic proportions across the set instead of changing size to make difficult views easier.
- The waist remains a broad load-bearing transition; a narrow mechanical connector between two floating masses does not count as connection.
- In the most extreme block, centerline and mass overlap still explain how load passes through the torso without an unexplained jump or fracture.
- After competing guides are suppressed, reducing each study to centerline and two masses preserves its intended view rather than exposing an unresolved second construction.

## Common Failures
- Repeating the same front-facing shapes and merely changing their outline.
- Using a narrow mechanical connector instead of a body-width transition.
- Leaving so many competing guides that the pelvis or rib cage appears fractured.

## Notes
Planes and directional guides are temporary explanations of mass alignment. Keep those marks long enough to solve the block, then remove any that no longer support one coherent torso.
