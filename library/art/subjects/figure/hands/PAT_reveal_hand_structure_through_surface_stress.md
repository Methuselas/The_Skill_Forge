# Reveal Hand Structure Through Surface Stress

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a hand action needs stronger structural clarity
**THEN** reveal only the landmarks that the action puts under stress: let bony wrist/knuckle points emerge, let dorsal finger tendons ride over and between the knuckles, let the thenar and hypothenar masses change fullness with thumb/palm action, and aim creases or skin tension along the active force
**ELSE** suppress those landmarks rather than drawing a permanent anatomical map over a relaxed hand

## Do
- Treat visible tendons, creases, pads, hollows, and bony points as consequences of pose and pressure.
- Let dorsal finger tendons become clearer as they cross stressed knuckles; use their route to connect finger action back into the hand.
- Let the thenar mass flatten when compressed toward the index side and swell/elevate as the thumb rises and rotates toward the palm.
- Keep the hypothenar side as a lower elongated support mass rather than mirroring the thenar mound.
- Use skin stress and creasing to continue the direction of finger bends, thrusts, and wrist action.

## Don't
- Do not expose every named tendon, carpal, vein, or wrist landmark in every pose.
- Do not make stress marks decorative wrinkles disconnected from the hand's action.
- Do not make the two palm-side masses symmetrical.
- Do not substitute surface detail for the palm wedge and articulated digit construction underneath.

## Checklist
- Every retained stress mark clarifies force, compression, tension, or a structural connection.
- Tendon and crease directions agree with the posed palm, digits, and wrist rather than crossing them decoratively.
- Compressed and stretched regions differ in mark character and placement where the action makes that difference visible.
- Removing a retained landmark would weaken the perceived action or structure; removable marks are treated as descriptive noise.
- The palm wedge and articulated digit chains remain readable without depending on surface detail to conceal weak construction.

## Notes
Surface creases and bulges are most useful when they reveal underlying load, compression, stretch, or joint action rather than becoming texture for its own sake.
