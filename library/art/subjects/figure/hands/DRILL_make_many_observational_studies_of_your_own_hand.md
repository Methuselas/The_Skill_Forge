# Make Many Observational Studies of Your Own Hand

## Practice Task
Use one of your own hands as the live model for a short series of hand studies. Change the view and action between studies so the palm, finger fan, thumb, knuckle spacing, and foreshortening cannot all be solved by one memorized symbol.

## Target Skill
Learning to see and reconstruct the hand that is actually in front of you while preserving a coherent palm-and-digit construction.

## Setup
Work small enough that several studies fit on one page. Keep each study at block or rough stage: palm mass, thumb branch, finger roots, major joints, nail/end planes, and only the surface information needed to explain the pose.

## Instructions
1. Draw one open palm, one back-of-hand view, one side or three-quarter view, one partially closed hand, one foreshortened hand, and one hand interacting with a simple object.
2. In every study, place the palm mass first and identify its facing before separating all four long fingers.
3. Compare the middle-finger length, palm width, finger-base spacing, and knuckle arc against the live hand instead of applying one fixed textbook ratio.
4. When the hand turns in depth, judge the visible spacing between knuckles and finger roots before adding contour detail.
5. On the object study, observe the hand just before and during contact; note how the fingers begin adapting to the object's contour.
6. After each sketch, compare it directly with the live hand and mark only the earliest structural mismatch: palm orientation, thumb placement, finger root, segment length, or depth spacing.
7. Repeat the weakest view immediately once, correcting that one structural relationship before adding more finish.

## Success Check
- The studies look like different views of a real hand rather than six copies of one hand symbol.
- Finger placement follows the observed palm and knuckle spacing in each view.
- At least one study contains convincing foreshortening without hiding the fingers in a mitten shape.
- The object-contact study shows a plausible adjustment of the hand to the object rather than an arbitrary grip.
- The learner can name one recurring structural error that became easier to see through repetition.

## Common Failures
- Drawing the same familiar hand pose repeatedly.
- Measuring every finger from memory while ignoring the observed spacing of the current view.
- Rendering nails, wrinkles, or tendons before the palm and finger roots agree with the reference.
- Avoiding foreshortened or partially closed poses because the open hand is easier.
- Treating one successful study as completion instead of using repetition to expose recurring errors.

## Notes
The artist's own hand is an always-available live reference for repeated study of proportion, spacing, and depth. The exercise does not make one person's anatomy universal; it trains palm-and-digit construction until it survives varied views.
