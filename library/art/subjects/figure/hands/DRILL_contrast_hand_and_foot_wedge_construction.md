# Contrast Hand and Foot Wedge Construction

## Practice Task
Draw five matched hand-and-foot pairs from front, side, three-quarter, up, and down views.

## Target Skill
Building hand and foot from related wedge families without making their functions or internal structures interchangeable.

## Setup
Use simple wedges and joint chains only; omit nails, wrinkles, veins, and surface texture.

## Instructions
1. For each hand, establish the palm wedge, finger base line, four digit chains, and opposing thumb.
2. For each foot, establish heel, forefoot, soft-tissue arch span, compact toes, and ground contacts.
3. Place each pair at the same approximate scale and compare width, thickness, taper, articulation, and support.
4. Circle any pair in which the hand looks load-bearing or the foot looks finger-like, then rebuild that pair.

## Success Check
- The hand reads as a mobile tool and the foot as a stable support before contour detail.
- The two forms remain recognizably related as wedges without sharing the same internal construction.
- The foot arch remains fleshed and the thumb remains opposed.

## Common Failures
- Using one generic terminal block for both extremities.
- Over-articulating the toes while under-constructing the palm.
- Drawing the arch as empty space rather than a raised, filled span.

## Notes
The source places hand and foot together precisely to expose their difference. Repetition should preserve the family resemblance while making function change the construction.
