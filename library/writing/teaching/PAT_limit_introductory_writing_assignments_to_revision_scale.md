# Limit Introductory Writing Assignments to Revision Scale

## Pattern Rule
**IF** beginners must learn several writing genres in a short term and each piece is expected to receive meaningful revision and feedback
**THEN** assign complete short forms small enough that a weak draft can be restarted and every piece can be closely read instead of letting page volume consume the course
**ELSE** reduce the number of genres or projects until the longer form still has room for a full revision cycle

## Do
- Budget backward from the feedback setting. Starkey's classroom comparison is practical: a ninety-minute meeting can closely examine several concise professional works or discuss four or five short student pieces instead of only two long ones.
- Choose a complete form whose boundaries fit the unit, such as a lyric poem, short-short story, short creative nonfiction piece, or ten-minute play.
- Spend the saved page budget on another look: structural rethinking, a clean new draft, peer discussion, and detailed instructor comments.
- Set the length low enough that deleting a failed opening or beginning again is a credible choice rather than a punishment students will avoid.

## Don't
- Don't confuse fewer pages with lower standards; the point of the constraint is to make closer analysis and more thorough revision possible.
- Don't ask students to rush through multiple genres at lengths that leave time only for first drafts.
- Don't treat the short work as a fragment of the “real” assignment. Require it to solve the promises of its own form.

## Checklist
- Can a student restart after discovering the draft's governing idea is wrong?
- Does every submitted piece fit the available workshop or commenting time?
- Is at least one substantial revision expected, not merely proofreading?
- Does the assignment produce a complete work rather than an abandoned opening?

## Notes
The source links scale to behavior rather than convenience. Students who are three pages into a four-page story are more willing to start over than students ten pages into twelve, and instructors can exchange raw page volume for close reading and careful response. Shortness is therefore a revision and feedback control, not just a way to survey more genres.
