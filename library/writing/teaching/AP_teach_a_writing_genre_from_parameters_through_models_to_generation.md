# Teach a Writing Genre from Parameters Through Models to Generation

## Objective
Carry beginners through one bounded genre unit so they can recognize its broad possibilities, see its craft elements working in complete texts, generate a feasible draft, and reserve enough time to revise that draft.

## Steps / Flow
1. **Activate the shared writing baseline.** Name what students already know from other writing and what the new genre will ask them to notice or do differently.
2. **Sketch the genre's broad parameters.** Give students an orienting boundary without presenting one definition or example as the only legitimate form.
3. **Close-read a small set of complete models.** Use concise, excellent works whose scale matches the coming assignment; select models that make different valid uses of the genre visible.
4. **Analyze one craft element at a time.** Point to the decision in the text, compare how the models handle it, then translate the observation into a move students can attempt in their own work.
5. **Introduce terms at the moment they become useful.** Define the vocabulary beside the first live example instead of front-loading a detached glossary lesson.
6. **Move from recognition to generation.** Give several bounded kick-starts or starting strategies so a student with no immediate subject can begin making material rather than waiting for inspiration.
7. **Draft a complete short work and return to it.** Keep the assignment within restart and workshop scale, then use close feedback and revision to consolidate the genre decisions the models exposed.

## Notes
Starkey describes the same repeated architecture for poetry, fiction, creative nonfiction, and drama: an orientation, three analyzed models, element summaries, terms in context, generation exercises, and a wider anthology. The preface also supplies the missing production constraint—each genre uses a short complete form so the course can afford close reading and substantial revision. The sequence is a reusable teaching loop, not a claim that three models or four genres are universal counts.
