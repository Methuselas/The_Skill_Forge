# Match Writing Models to the Assignment Scale

## Pattern Rule
**IF** you are selecting model texts for an introductory writing assignment
**THEN** choose excellent works close enough to the assignment's length and complexity that students can realistically read, analyze, and emulate the whole form within the allotted unit
**ELSE** isolate one bounded craft move in the larger work and state that it is not a whole-assignment model

## Do
- Measure the assignment before choosing readings: target length, weeks available, number of unfamiliar genre decisions, and whether students must revise a complete draft.
- For a four-page story, prefer a deft short-short as the primary whole-form precedent rather than asking a 6,000-word story to model what four pages can accomplish.
- Keep the quality threshold high while reducing scope. Concision is the constraint; weak or artificially simplified prose is not the answer.
- Tell students whether they are studying the model's complete architecture or only one local move such as dialogue, compression, or an ending.

## Don't
- Don't use a famous, carefully wrought long work as direct production precedent merely because it is canonical; students may admire it without being able to convert its scale into a feasible draft.
- Don't assign a brief form and then surround it with examples whose page count and complexity silently teach a different task.

## Checklist
- Can students close-read the entire model during the time budget?
- Can they produce and revise a complete work of comparable scope?
- Is the selected work strong enough to reward imitation without requiring students to copy its subject or voice?
- If the example is intentionally larger, have I named the exact craft move it demonstrates?

## Notes
The source makes its test concrete by rejecting Raymond Carver's roughly 6,000-word “Cathedral” as the primary model for a short classroom story while retaining the much briefer “Popular Mechanics.” The distinction is not masterpiece versus beginner work. Both examples must be excellent; the model earns its place by making the assigned scale visible and achievable.
