# Sustain Poetic Intensity Beyond Meter and Lineation

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a poem does not rely on conventional meter, rhyme, or line breaks to announce its genre
**THEN** make its language concentrated, vivid, rhythmically alive, musical, or figuratively heightened enough to create sustained poetic intensity
**ELSE** ensure the formal devices deepen the language rather than serving as the poem's only evidence of craft

## Do
- Compress wording so that image, sound, rhythm, and implication carry more than ordinary exposition would.
- Read for the accumulated pressure of the language across the whole piece, whether it is formal verse, free verse, or a prose poem.
- Let lineation, syntax, sonic texture, and figurative choices cooperate when more than one of them is present.

## Don't
- Assume that broken lines automatically turn ordinary prose into a poem.
- Treat freedom from inherited form as freedom from deliberate craft.
- Force every poem into meter or rhyme merely to make its genre recognizable.

## Checklist
- The language remains charged when read without relying on page shape alone.
- Images, sounds, rhythms, or figures create effects that plain exposition would not.
- Formal choices intensify meaning instead of decorating it.
- The piece sustains its poetic character from beginning to end.

## Notes
Poetry is broader than metrical verse. A poem may use inherited form, free verse, or prose, but each mode still needs language whose concentration and patterned energy distinguish it from merely reformatted prose.

`writing_poetry_variant_build_prose_poem_through_sentence_music` makes the sentence carry work that lineation would otherwise perform. Use compactness, metaphor, image, unconventional sound, and the rhythm of natural speech to sustain pressure across the paragraph; exploratory or surreal content still needs careful sentence craft. The method is a poor fit when line breaks are indispensable or when uncharged exposition is being relabeled rather than transformed.
