# Make Performance Poetry Stand on Its Language

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a poem is written for live delivery
**THEN** make its wording, imagery, rhythm, and turns strong enough to work without charisma, music, or automatic audience cues
**ELSE** preserve the same linguistic rigor while adapting the poem to its quieter reading context

## Do
- Test the text in a neutral reading so the words, not the performer's force, carry the experience.
- Revise predictable phrasing, borrowed emotional cues, and rhythms that create excitement without precision.
- Use voice, pacing, gesture, and accompaniment to reveal strengths already present in the poem.
- Consider both the immediate audience response and what remains after the performance ends.

## Don't
- Let volume, speed, persona, or music conceal clichés or thin imagery.
- Treat applause at a live moment as sufficient evidence that the written poem is finished.
- Strip away performative design; make it answerable to the language it presents.

## Checklist
- A plain reading still communicates the poem's central movement and emotional pressure.
- Memorable moments arise from specific language rather than generic cues.
- Performance choices clarify or intensify textual choices.
- The poem rewards attention beyond its first live impact.

## Notes
Performance is a legitimate poetic medium, not an exemption from poetic craft. Delivery can enlarge rhythm, voice, and communal energy, but durable work gives the performer substantial language to embody.
