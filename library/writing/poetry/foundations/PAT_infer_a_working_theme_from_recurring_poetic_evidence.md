# Infer a Working Theme from Recurring Poetic Evidence

## Pattern Rule
**IF** several poems revisit similar subjects, images, situations, or attitudes but their larger concern remains vague or has been declared only as an aspiration
**THEN** separate surface subjects from recurring pressures, cluster concrete evidence across the poems, and state a working theme that explains both repetition and meaningful difference
**ELSE** keep the current theme as a revisable hypothesis while testing new material that may deepen, contradict, or outgrow it

## Do
- Inventory recurring places, objects, relationships, actions, tonal stances, questions, turns, and exclusions before naming an abstraction.
- Ask what treatment repeatedly gives a subject significance: reverence, resistance, recovery, estrangement, comic deflation, witness, or another observable attitude.
- Draft at least two interpretations of the same recurrence and prefer the one that explains more strong poems with fewer forced inclusions.
- Include unfinished and unsuccessful work when it reveals persistent attention, while keeping artistic quality separate from evidence of concern.
- Test the candidate against an apparent outlier and revise its scope when the outlier exposes a more accurate relation.

## Don't
- Promote a sentence about what the writer hopes to address into a theme before existing poems support it.
- Treat a broad category such as place, family, politics, or loss as the completed interpretation.
- Force every poem under one label, or discard contradictory evidence solely to protect a flattering account of the writer.
- Convert one current pattern into a permanent author identity or a restriction on future subjects.
- Assume a critic's or reader's interpretation settles the writer's only legitimate theme.

## Checklist
- The surface subjects and the inferred recurring concern can be stated separately.
- Multiple concrete features from more than one poem support the interpretation.
- A credible alternative was tested rather than suppressed by the first attractive label.
- At least one difference or counterexample helped calibrate the theme's boundary.
- The result guides inquiry and selection without dictating what the writer must write next.

## Notes
A theme is useful here as a corpus-level reading, not as destiny. The same material can sustain more than one defensible interpretation, and a chosen project may deliberately begin from a concern before a larger body of evidence exists. In both cases the working account remains answerable to what the poems actually notice, repeat, alter, and omit. Subject names what a poem visibly attends to; theme proposes why that attention keeps mattering, and either label may need revision when the critical frame changes.
