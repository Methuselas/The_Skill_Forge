# Keep Free Verse Rhythmically Accountable

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a free-verse poem lacks an audible current or its flexible lines have become verbose and slack
**THEN** shape a discernible rhythm through stress, breath, syllable density, syntax, and deliberate changes in line length without imposing unwanted meter
**ELSE** preserve its elasticity while checking that freedom continues to produce energy rather than formlessness

## Do
- Read through the ear and breath as well as the eye, noticing where bodily pace rises, settles, or loses force.
- Scan representative lines to see their actual stress patterns without pretending the whole poem follows one metrical scheme.
- Let broken phrases create short bursts of energy or let syntactically complete lines provide more settled units.
- Change line length in response to the poem's movement rather than varying it merely to advertise freedom.

## Don't
- Assume that rejecting traditional meter releases the poem from rhythmic responsibility.
- Let unconstrained lines expand until their language becomes indistinguishable from slack prose.
- Add regularity so rigidly that a useful free-verse cadence turns into an accidental formal meter.

## Checklist
- The poem has a rhythm that can be heard even when it cannot be assigned one recurring foot.
- Changes in breath, stress, syntax, and length correspond to changes in thought or feeling.
- Flexible lines remain concise and intentional.
- Any local regularity supports the poem without erasing productive variation.

## Notes
Free verse exchanges a predetermined metrical spine for active listening and control. `writing_poetry_variant_stabilize_free_verse_with_shared_syllable_counts` introduces roughly equal syllable counts when an emerging poem needs more pulse but not a traditional meter; abandon that constraint when changing breath or line elasticity carries the stronger effect.
