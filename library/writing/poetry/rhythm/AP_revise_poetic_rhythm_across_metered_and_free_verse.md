# Revise Poetic Rhythm Across Metered and Free Verse

## Objective
Bring a lineated poem to an intentional, audible rhythm whose pattern or flexibility supports its thought, feeling, voice, and line movement without mechanical regularity or slackness.

## Steps / Flow
1. **Set the rhythmic intent.** Start with a complete enough draft to hear across several lines. State whether a recurring meter is meant to govern the poem, whether the poem is deliberately free verse, or whether the current mode is accidental. Name the movement the reader should feel before changing syllables.
2. **Hear the existing poem before diagramming it.** Activate `writing_read_draft_aloud_to_defamiliarize_it` and mark where the pulse strengthens, drags, becomes monotonous, or disappears. Preserve the intended voice and emerging content as invariants; technical correction may not flatten either one.
3. **Choose the diagnostic branch.**
   - **Metered branch:** activate `writing_poetry_scan_and_vary_meter_deliberately`. Identify the prevailing foot and line length, then locate substitutions, extra syllables, and rhyme-emphasized stresses. **Advance gate:** the base pattern is audible, and each important deviation either performs a chosen effect or is marked for repair.
   - **Free-verse branch:** activate `writing_poetry_keep_free_verse_rhythmically_accountable`. Map representative stresses, breath units, syllable density, syntax, and changes in line length without forcing a recurring foot. **Advance gate:** the poem has a discernible current, and its flexibility does not excuse verbose or slack lines.
4. **Repair without erasing life.** In meter, correct accidental disruptions but retain or introduce variations that prevent a metronomic sound. In free verse, tighten slack passages and activate `writing_poetry_variant_stabilize_free_verse_with_shared_syllable_counts` when a temporary equal-count constraint would reveal a missing pulse. If the constraint stiffens useful breath or movement, remove it and return to the free-verse branch.
5. **Test lineation against rhythm.** Activate `writing_poetry_break_lines_to_control_pace_and_expectation`. Check that endings, enjambments, and line lengths cooperate with the heard rhythm rather than contradicting it accidentally. If a rhythm works aloud but the page imposes the wrong pause, revise the boundary; if the boundary is essential, reshape the words around it.
6. **Run the form-fit gate.** Compare the governing mode with what the poem has become. If prescribed meter or rhyme now distorts the emerging thought, activate `writing_poetry_variant_release_prescribed_meter_that_no_longer_fits` through `writing_revise_whole_draft_toward_intended_form`, recast the draft in free verse, and return to step 2. If freedom has produced formlessness and a recurring structure would help, test a manageable meter or the shared-syllable variant, then return to the appropriate branch.
7. **Complete by ear and structure.** Read the revised poem aloud without looking at stress marks. Stop only when its rhythm remains perceptible, technical choices serve rather than consume the poem, variations sound purposeful, line breaks support the movement, and no unresolved mismatch remains between form and content.

## Notes
The protocol branches because meter and free verse fail differently. Formal verse needs enough consistency to establish its pattern but enough deviation to stay alive; free verse needs enough elasticity to follow speech and thought but enough control to avoid slackness. Scansion is diagnostic evidence, not an authority over the ear, and changing rhythmic mode is a recovery path rather than an admission that the first approach was illegitimate.
