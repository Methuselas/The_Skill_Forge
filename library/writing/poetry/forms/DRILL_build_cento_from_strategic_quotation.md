# Build a Cento from Strategic Quotation

## Practice Task
Compose a poem entirely from intact quotations whose new sequence creates an effect not present in the source passages individually.

## Target Skill
Close reading and meaning-making through poetic juxtaposition.

## Setup
Choose a bounded body of writing whose reuse is permitted in the exercise context. Confirm the attribution or submission expectations before composing, then collect substantially more candidate lines than the poem will need.

## Instructions
1. Copy candidate lines exactly and keep their authors or works attached in the working notes.
2. Sort the lines by image, tone, syntax, sound, subject, and possible points of contrast rather than by their original order.
3. Choose a desired movement, such as surprise opening into clarity, humor darkening into recognition, or several voices converging on one question.
4. Sequence quotations so each adjacency creates a new relation; remove lines that function only because they are famous or attractive in isolation.
5. When the collage remains loose, impose a compatible stanza or repetition pattern and test whether that added pressure makes the yoking more strategic.
6. Read the complete poem aloud without supplying connective explanation. Reorder or cut until syntax, tone, and progression can carry the intended effect.
7. Preserve the required attribution outside the poem and verify that the finished use fits the exercise, classroom, or venue expectations established in setup.

## Success Check
- Every word in the poem comes from the selected source material.
- Adjacent quotations generate discernible new relationships rather than a random scrapbook.
- The sequence develops an effect across the whole poem.
- Removing a selected line would subtract a specific transition, contrast, or accumulation.
- Source tracking and reuse expectations remain clear even though the poem itself is a transformed arrangement.

## Common Failures
- Depending on a recognizable quotation to supply importance without changing its context.
- Adding original connective phrases when stronger selection or ordering should solve the transition.
- Preserving source order so closely that the result merely excerpts rather than recomposes.
- Treating attribution and submission expectations as concerns to investigate only after the poem is finished.

## Notes
Collage turns reading into composition. Because the language is borrowed intact, authorship shifts toward selection, juxtaposition, pacing, and formal pressure. The constraint exposes how strongly meaning depends on neighboring sentences and can reveal compositional possibilities in familiar work that ordinary rereading leaves hidden.
