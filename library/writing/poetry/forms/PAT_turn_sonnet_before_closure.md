# Turn a Sonnet Before Closure

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a sonnet's later lines merely continue the opening argument, image, or tone toward a predictable conclusion
**THEN** introduce a directional change that makes the remaining lines reconsider, complicate, answer, or reframe what came before
**ELSE** keep the existing turn while testing whether the ending realizes its consequence

## Do
- Identify the pressure accumulated by the opening movement, then choose a counterclaim, changed scale, new temporal position, tonal reversal, or unexpected consequence that can answer it.
- Place the turn where the chosen sonnet structure can support it: after an octave, before a closing couplet, at a stanza boundary, or later when delay strengthens the effect.
- Signal the change through meaning first; punctuation, a new stanza, or a transitional word may clarify a turn but cannot create one alone.
- Make the closing movement spend the new direction by revising the earlier material's significance rather than starting an unrelated second topic.

## Don't
- Treat the arrival of a couplet or a new rhyme as a turn when the thought continues unchanged.
- Force every sonnet to pivot at the same numbered line despite using different stanza and rhyme structures.
- Save the only consequential idea for the final line without preparing enough pressure for it to reorient the poem.
- Return to the opening claim unchanged after announcing a reversal.

## Checklist
- The poem has a before-and-after relationship that can be stated without reference to its rhyme labels.
- The turn arrives before closure and changes the reader's understanding of the opening movement.
- Its position agrees with, or productively resists, the chosen stanza structure.
- Formal signals support a real shift in argument or tone.
- The ending develops the turn rather than merely naming it.

## Notes
Sonnet schemes can mix quatrains, octaves, sestets, and couplets, so the volta is more portable than a fixed line number. The essential action is a swerve toward an unexpected place before completion. A structural marker can expose that change, but the turn exists in the poem's thought and tone rather than in punctuation or white space alone.
