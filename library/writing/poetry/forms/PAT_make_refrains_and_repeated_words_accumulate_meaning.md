# Make Refrains and Repeated Words Accumulate Meaning

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a poetic form requires a line, phrase, or terminal word to return in changing contexts
**THEN** choose repeated material with enough semantic and syntactic flexibility for each return to gain a new tone, implication, or degree of force
**ELSE** remove the recurrence or replace its anchor before the surrounding poem hardens around it

## Do
- Test a prospective refrain in every required position before drafting the intervening lines; it must remain grammatical while permitting more than one reading.
- End a rhymed refrain on a word with enough useful correspondences that later lines are not forced toward filler or distorted syntax.
- Make the intervening material change the reader's knowledge, emotional pressure, or view of the speaker so identical language cannot mean exactly what it meant before.
- Decide whether repeated end words should blend into new sentences or strike like emphatic hammers, then maintain that strategy or vary it for a named effect.
- Modify a returning phrase slightly when the form permits and the change sharpens its altered context without making the recurrence unrecognizable.

## Don't
- Repeat a line unchanged while leaving its surrounding situation static.
- Choose an anchor so specific to its first context that later appearances sound pasted in.
- Let the convenience of already-written repeated lines substitute for development between them.
- Hide every recurrence when the form needs insistence, or hammer every recurrence until emphasis becomes monotony.

## Checklist
- Every required recurrence fits its local sentence and stanza.
- The poem changes enough between returns to alter how the repeated language is heard.
- Rhyme options remain broad enough to protect accurate diction.
- The degree of variation preserves recognition of the governing pattern.
- The final return carries pressure accumulated from the whole poem rather than merely completing a diagram.

## Notes
Repetition becomes structural when context does new work on the same words. A preoccupation may make a refrain feel psychologically inevitable; a sequence of concrete developments may turn a neutral opening into resignation, anger, grief, or insight. Repeated terminal words present a related choice: unobtrusive syntax can conceal the mechanism, while conspicuous recurrence can make insistence itself the subject.
