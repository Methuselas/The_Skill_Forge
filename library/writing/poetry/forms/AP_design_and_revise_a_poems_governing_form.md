# Design and Revise a Poem's Governing Form

## Objective
Develop a poem whose rhythmic, line, stanza, rhyme, repetition, and turning behaviors create recognizable expectations while remaining responsive to its voice, thought, imagery, and discoveries.

## Steps / Flow
1. **Name the material's movement.** Start with enough generated language to identify what opens, develops, repeats, turns, or closes. Preserve the strongest voice, images, and relations as invariants; do not select a shape solely because its name or prestige is attractive.
2. **Choose the source of formal pressure.** Use `writing_poetry_learn_form_before_varying_its_rules` when an inherited form supplies the governing constraints. Use `writing_poetry_variant_extend_emerging_local_form` through `writing_poetry_draft_through_discovery` when a charged passage has produced a repeatable local pattern. Keep a deliberately flexible form only when lineation, rhythm, and grouping can still teach the reader how the poem moves. **Advance gate:** the governing expectations can be stated as observable behavior.
3. **Specify the working architecture.** Map applicable line counts, stanza units, rhythmic tendencies, rhyme positions, repetitions, and structural turns. Activate `writing_poetry_shape_stanzas_around_movement_and_pause` to assign each grouping a unit of thought, image, action, or interruption. Mark constraints that govern recognition separately from conventions that may vary.
4. **Draft inside the pressure.** Let the form generate associations, compression, sequence, and turns that an unconstrained paraphrase would not produce. Recast language rather than padding a unit, distorting syntax, or substituting an inferior word. **Recovery:** when compliance consumes attention without generating value, loosen one non-governing convention and test again.
5. **Coordinate sound and temporal movement.** Activate `writing_poetry_ap_revise_rhythm_across_metered_and_free_verse`; then activate `writing_poetry_make_rhyme_serve_sense_and_effect` when rhyme is present. Confirm that audible patterning, line endings, and stanza boundaries reinforce the same movement instead of creating unrelated systems.
6. **Test expectation and variation.** Read the poem aloud and on the page. Mark what a reader can reasonably learn from recurrence and what each departure changes. Strengthen a baseline that is too faint to make variation legible; remove a recurrence that has become empty decoration.
7. **Run the form-fit gate.** Compare the shaped draft with the poem's emerging center. If another inherited structure might generate a better movement, activate `writing_poetry_variant_audition_alternate_form_for_same_material`. If prescribed structure now distorts the poem, use the release-form variant in `writing_revise_whole_draft_toward_intended_form`. If a local discovery has become the stronger law, rebuild around it and return to step 3.
8. **Close as one habitable structure.** Stop when the reader can perceive the poem's governing expectations without explanation, each unit carries necessary material, deviations perform named effects, and form has changed or clarified the poem rather than merely containing a prepared statement.

## Notes
Form can be inherited, discovered during composition, or established locally by a free-verse poem. These routes differ in where their rules begin, not in their obligation to create intelligible pressure. A successful shape is learned through the poem itself: repetition establishes expectation, variation spends it, and stanza-sized movements make the larger structure traversable.
