# Rehabilitate a Received Phrase Through Literalization or Context

## Practice Task
Take one overused expression through two drafts: one that makes its concealed image literal and one that makes the phrase inevitable in a specific situation.

## Target Skill
Reactivating dormant imagery in familiar language without relying on recognition alone.

## Setup
List five expressions heard repeatedly in a real speech community. Choose one whose history, tone, and ordinary use you understand.

## Instructions
1. Identify the phrase's literal objects, actions, implied speaker, and worn figurative meaning.
2. Draft a poem in which its dormant objects and actions physically occur without explanation.
3. Draft another around a speaker and pressure for which the familiar wording becomes the only exact concluding line.
4. Compare whether literalization creates more than a joke and whether context gives recognition new force.
5. Revise the stronger version and remove other received phrases that entered unnoticed.

## Success Check
- The phrase comes from understood use rather than borrowed local color.
- Its concealed image becomes concrete or its final use becomes necessary.
- The draft works for a reader who does not share the writer's private history.
- Familiarity no longer supplies the entire effect.

## Common Failures
- Selecting an expression because it sounds quaint or exotic.
- Producing a literal gag with no further pressure.
- Explaining the phrase before the poem can reactivate it.

## Notes
Received language is not automatically dead. Reuse becomes deliberate when the writer understands its use, exposes the perception hidden by repetition, and gives readers a new reason to encounter it.
