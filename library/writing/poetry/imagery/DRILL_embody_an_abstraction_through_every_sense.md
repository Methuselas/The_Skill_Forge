# Embody an Abstraction Through Every Sense

## Practice Task
Imagine an abstract quality as an unknown presence in a container, discover it through successive senses and actions, and shape the encounters into a poem.

## Target Skill
Converting an abstract quality into a developing field of concrete sensory behavior.

## Setup
Choose a value, emotion, condition, or recurring concern. Imagine it inside an opaque container; all tasting remains imaginary.

## Instructions
1. Record texture, temperature, weight, movement, and resistance without naming the abstraction.
2. Describe its smell, sound, possible speech, and imagined taste through exact physical equivalents.
3. Remove it and decide whether it can be held, whether it responds, and what it appears to want.
4. Drop, release, or disturb it and record the resulting action.
5. Arrange the strongest discoveries so the presence changes, drafting without the abstract label.
6. Restore the label only if it frames rather than explains the images.

## Success Check
- Several senses generate distinct information.
- The abstraction acquires behavior, limits, and relation.
- An unexpected image retains emotional force after its origin is concealed.
- The sequence develops through contact and consequence.

## Common Failures
- Naming the intended meaning after every image.
- Making each sense report the same generic quality.
- Treating random contradiction as depth.

## Notes
The container delays explanation and forces contact with particulars. The goal is a sensory field whose behavior lets a general concern become encounterable and capable of surprise.
