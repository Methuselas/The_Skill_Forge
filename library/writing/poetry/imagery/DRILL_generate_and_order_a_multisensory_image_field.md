# Generate and Order a Multisensory Image Field

## Practice Task
Produce an abundant set of images for one ordinary object, then turn the unordered field into a short poetic movement through selection and arrangement.

## Target Skill
Generating concrete comparisons and selecting an order that creates rhetorical movement.

## Setup
Place a safe ordinary object or substance within reach. Prepare separate spaces for visual, auditory, tactile, olfactory, gustatory when safe, kinetic, functional, and associative observations.

## Instructions
1. Observe for fifteen minutes and record comparisons in every relevant category without arranging them.
2. Mark the exact feature that licenses each comparison and discard candidates based only on familiar phrasing.
3. Circle five to ten images with transformative potential, including one that changes scale or mode.
4. Try two orders: one coherent progression and one containing a purposeful return or disruption. Name one image whose force comes from where it sits rather than from what it is, and say what the position did for it.
5. Draft from the stronger order, then remove isolated dazzlers and strengthen transitions through recurrence, syntax, or lineation.

## Success Check
- The field reaches beyond sight without filling a quota per sense and without tasting what should not be tasted.
- Every retained comparison names the feature licensing it, and candidates resting on familiar phrasing are discarded.
- Both orders are drafted, so the coherent progression and the disrupted one are compared rather than one being imagined.
- The chosen order changes perception across the sequence instead of grouping similar images together.
- At least one image gains force from its position, and the gain is attributable to placement rather than to the image.
- Isolated dazzlers are removed even when they are the best single lines, and the transitions carry what they were doing.

## Common Failures
- Stopping after the first obvious visual likeness.
- Treating abundance as the finished poem.
- Keeping an image because it is clever although it interrupts the pressure.

## Notes
Generation and ordering are separate decisions. Abundance provides alternatives; sequencing converts them into thought. An ordinary object makes close attention, rather than spectacle, supply the transformation.
