# Order Image Sequences to Carry Poetic Thought

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a poem's several images read as an inventory or a display of isolated cleverness
**THEN** arrange their scales, senses, recurrences, contrasts, and implications into a movement that discovers or enacts the poem's pressure
**ELSE** let one decisive image stand without manufacturing a sequence

## Do
- Group images whose vehicles share material, action, scale, or emotional direction, then test where the relation can deepen or turn.
- Try movements such as domestic to expansive, near to distant, build to recession, recurrence with alteration, or coherence interrupted by one charged disjunction.
- Place the strongest transformation where preceding images prepare its force rather than automatically at the opening.
- Let stanza shape, syntax, lineation, and sound reinforce the movement without becoming formal costume.
- Retain a mysterious link when it intensifies a pattern readers can feel even if they cannot paraphrase it fully.

## Don't
- Assume that accumulating fresh comparisons creates development by itself.
- Force every image under one conceit after the poem discovers a necessary break.
- Explain the argument after the sequence has made its pressure available.
- Use a cultural, sacred, or technical field as decoration without accurate knowledge and accountable context.

## Checklist
- The order produces a change in scale, perception, emotion, or implication.
- Adjacent images create relations stronger than their isolated effects.
- A climax, return, or disruption is prepared by earlier material.
- Formal organization supports rather than merely labels the governing relation.
- Removing an image either improves the sequence or reveals the job it performed.

## Notes
Image order can behave rhetorically without becoming logical proof. A chain may gather resonance, circle back through changed context, or break an expected relation so readers experience a disturbance the poem cannot state abstractly. Arrangement becomes a method of thinking rather than a catalogue.
