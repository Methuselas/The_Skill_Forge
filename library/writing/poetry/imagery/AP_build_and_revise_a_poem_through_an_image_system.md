# Build and Revise a Poem Through an Image System

## Objective
Develop a poem whose concrete perceptions and comparisons generate thought, acquire purposeful order, and carry tonal or thematic pressure without becoming a list of ornaments, stereotypes, or abstract statements with scenery attached.

## Steps / Flow
1. **Choose a live field.** Begin with an ordinary object, observed event, bodily sensation, or abstraction that needs embodiment. Record exact particulars before deciding what they mean. **Advance gate:** the subject offers material in at least two senses or modes of action.
2. **Generate beyond the first likeness.** Activate `writing_replace_abstractions_with_specific_sensory_images` and `writing_make_figurative_language_unexpected_and_fitting`. Produce more candidates than the poem needs across texture, sound, smell, temperature, movement, use, scale, and history. Remove unsafe sensory tests and culturally stereotyped vehicles.
3. **Test the bridge.** State what connects subject and comparison. When the first vehicle is predictable, transfer it to another subject or search farther. When a partly inexplicable image has unusual charge, postpone judgment until the whole movement can be tested.
4. **Select an image logic.** Activate `writing_poetry_order_image_sequences_to_carry_thought`. Choose a coherent progression, controlled recurrence, build-and-recede motion, or deliberate disruption. **Recovery:** if every image merely renames the subject, change scale, sense, or governing relation.
5. **Compose through the relations.** Let images change the poem's questions, syntax, lineation, and movement rather than decorate a predetermined conclusion. Preserve the concrete world while allowing the pattern to discover implications unavailable to literal explanation.
6. **Audit inherited language.** Activate `writing_replace_received_language_with_precise_distinctive_phrasing`. Replace unnoticed phrases and mixed metaphors. Rehabilitate a familiar expression only when literal development or exact context restores its force.
7. **Test alignment and productive mystery.** Read for what the system makes readers perceive, infer, and feel. Keep a mysterious link when sound, recurrence, tone, or position supports it; cut dazzlers that do not serve the poem. **Advance gate:** the imagery and governing pressure cannot be separated without loss.
8. **Complete on page and aloud.** Verify that sequence, stanza shape, line breaks, and sonic texture support the movement; that response is invited rather than presumed; and that culturally or technically specific comparisons are accurate and accountable.

## Notes
An image can transmit perception and create thought: once one thing is seen through another, the borrowed field supplies actions, implications, and possible order. Coherence is not compulsory, but relation is. Even destabilizing imagery needs enough local energy and whole-poem pressure to distinguish productive uncertainty from randomness.
