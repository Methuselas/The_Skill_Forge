# Select and Order a Coherent Poetry Manuscript

## Objective
Turn a bounded pool of finished or viable poems into a purposeful reading experience whose selections, sequences, intervals, opening, closing, and title create a larger structure without sacrificing the integrity of individual pieces.

## Steps / Flow
1. **Define the actual pool and use context.** Gather complete texts, distinguish finished poems from viable revisions, and name the intended reading set, submission, pamphlet, or collection without assuming a universal length. Exclude drafts that cannot become ready within the available time. **Advance gate:** every candidate can be read and compared as a whole.
2. **Separate quality from fit.** Use `writing_poetry_variant_cull_by_quality_and_manuscript_fit_separately` through the judgment drill. Mark standalone quality first; then create a second disposition for this manuscript: necessary, possible, redundant, disruptive, or withheld. Preserve strong exclusions for another project rather than downgrading them to justify the cut.
3. **Infer the governing pressure.** Activate `writing_poetry_infer_working_theme_from_recurring_evidence`. Cluster concrete subjects, attitudes, images, questions, and formal recurrences; test alternate accounts and at least one outlier. **Recovery:** if every candidate fits only under a broad category, narrow the pool or choose a more exact relation before ordering.
4. **Build necessary internal groups.** Activate `writing_poetry_make_poems_interdependent_in_sequence` where two or more poems gain transition, narrative, expansion, or thematic resonance together. Keep stand-alone poems outside a sequence when grouping would falsely promise dependence. **Advance gate:** every internal group has a larger effect that survives a removal or reorder test.
5. **Audition whole-manuscript orders.** Compare at least three arrangements, such as thematic recurrence, narrative or temporal movement, and a constellation organized through contrast. Track energy, tonal saturation, scale, speaker proximity, formal density, and the charged spaces between poems. Reject an order that merely follows composition date unless chronology itself performs necessary movement.
6. **Choose entrances, returns, peaks, and closure.** Let the opening establish a trustworthy field without explaining the whole project. Space central recurrences so the reader can recognize a spine without meeting identical poems in a block. Place stronger pieces where their function produces the needed lift or turn rather than following a fixed first-middle-last formula. Make the ending alter or complete the manuscript's pressure instead of merely using the last available poem.
7. **Frame the group.** Activate `writing_poetry_variant_title_sequence_or_collection_as_active_frame` through `writing_poetry_use_title_as_active_frame`. Test the title before the opening and after the closing; add an epigraph only when it changes entry or relation enough to earn its presence, and verify all borrowed language before selection.
8. **Read, repair, and close at manuscript scale.** Read continuously and note repeated information, abrupt register changes, weak transitions, and poems whose individual revision is now exposed by their neighbours. Return local defects to the relevant poem, then repeat the order test. Stop when every inclusion has both a standalone and manuscript disposition, each sequence earns interdependence, the order produces intelligible development or resonance, and title plus ending make the whole feel complete without claiming faultlessness.

## Notes
Manuscript shaping resembles revision at a larger scale, but a good poem is not automatically a good inclusion. The governing decisions are relational: what the reader carries forward, where recurrence becomes recognition or fatigue, and how one poem changes the next. Narrative, thematic spine, and constellation are legitimate architectures; manuscript size and the placement of peaks remain context-sensitive rather than fixed formulas.
