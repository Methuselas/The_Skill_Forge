# Audition Competing Orders for a Small Poetry Manuscript

## Practice Task
Arrange the same bounded group of poems through several ordering logics, read each continuously, and select or hybridize by the larger experience each produces.

## Target Skill
Comparing whole-manuscript reading effects while holding the selected poems constant.

## Setup
Choose six to twelve complete poems with at least two recurring concerns and meaningful variation in tone, scale, speaker distance, or form. Print, copy, or represent each poem as one movable unit without reducing it to its title alone.

## Instructions
1. Record a compact profile for every poem: opening energy, ending pressure, subject, tone, scale, form, and any recurrence that may connect it to another.
2. Build three complete orders while holding membership constant: one by thematic recurrence, one by narrative or temporal development, and one by contrast or constellation.
3. Read every order continuously, marking entry clarity, adjacency effects, repetition fatigue, abrupt transitions, energy peaks, and the work performed by the ending.
4. Move one strong poem away from an obvious peak position and test whether function matters more than isolated quality at that location.
5. Identify the best transition and worst transition across all versions; explain what each pair causes the reader to carry forward.
6. Select one order or build a hybrid using only effects observed in the comparison, then give every placement a brief relational reason.
7. Remove one poem and repeat the closing read to determine whether the manuscript becomes tighter or loses a necessary pressure.

## Success Check
- Every version contains the same poems until the explicit removal test.
- Each order follows a distinguishable logic rather than random shuffling.
- Selection cites transitions, recurrence, energy, and closure instead of preference alone.
- Strong individual poems are not automatically assigned fixed positions.
- The final arrangement gives every inclusion and boundary a defensible job.

## Common Failures
- Changing membership and order simultaneously so their effects cannot be distinguished.
- Grouping all similar poems together until recurrence becomes saturation.
- Calling composition chronology natural without testing what a reader experiences.
- Choosing the smoothest order when productive disruption better serves the whole.
- Treating a title list as an adequate substitute for rereading the poems.

## Notes
Ordering is revision by adjacency. The same poem can establish a field, deepen a recurrence, interrupt saturation, or close a movement depending on its neighbours. Holding membership constant long enough to compare versions reveals those jobs before culling introduces another variable.
