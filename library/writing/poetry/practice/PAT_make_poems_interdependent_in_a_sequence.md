# Make Poems Interdependent in a Sequence

## Pattern Rule
**IF** poems have been grouped by a shared subject or title but reading them together adds no consequential movement beyond their separate meanings
**THEN** give each poem a distinct angle and arrange the group so transitions, recurrences, contrasts, elapsed time, or accumulating detail create a larger experience
**ELSE** preserve the grouping only while every member continues to earn both its independent and relational work

## Do
- State the contribution of each member in one phrase: establish, complicate, contradict, widen, revisit, accelerate, pause, or close.
- Track what changes between pieces, including speaker relation, knowledge, skill, season, setting, scale, tone, or temporal position.
- Repeat an object, image, phrase, formal feature, or situation with altered significance so recognition carries development rather than redundancy.
- Choose a narrative progression when causal or temporal change matters; choose a constellation when adjacency and recurrence create the stronger whole.
- Test the group by removing and moving pieces, then repair the precise transition or missing function the experiment exposes.

## Don't
- Treat common subject matter as proof that the poems need one another.
- Make every member restate the governing theme in the same tone and scale.
- Preserve a weak piece because it supplies information another poem could carry more powerfully.
- Require every poem to stand equally well alone when dependence is part of the design, or use dependence to excuse incomprehensible fragments.
- Add framing prose to explain a relationship that sequence and recurrence could make perceptible.

## Checklist
- Each poem contributes a function or perspective unavailable from its neighbours.
- The order produces transition, accumulation, contrast, or resonance that disappears when the pieces are isolated.
- Recurrent elements change meaning or pressure as they return.
- Removing or moving a member either improves the whole or reveals the job it performed.
- The final group is more than an inventory while individual pieces remain legible enough for their assigned roles.

## Notes
Sequential order is one available logic, not the definition of a sequence. A narrative group can make elapsed time, causality, or changing relationship visible; a constellation can let separated pieces vibrate through recurrence and contrast without a linear plot. Interdependence is the governing test. Even two poems can form a sequence when their adjacency creates a third effect, while dozens of related poems remain only a category if nothing changes between them.
