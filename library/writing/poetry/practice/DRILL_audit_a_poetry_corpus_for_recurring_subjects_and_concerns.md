# Audit a Poetry Corpus for Recurring Subjects and Concerns

## Practice Task
Review a bounded body of poems as evidence, distinguish repeated surface subjects from their treatments, and test competing accounts of the concern that connects them.

## Target Skill
Inferring a working corpus-level concern from recurring concrete poetic evidence without forcing identity or uniformity.

## Setup
Choose six to twelve complete poems by one writer, including at least one apparent outlier and one piece judged less successful. Remove author statements about theme until after the first analysis.

## Instructions
1. For each poem, record concrete subjects, recurring objects and places, speaker position, dominant action, tonal attitude, central question, turn, and ending pressure.
2. Cluster repeated features without naming an abstract theme; note differences in treatment inside each cluster.
3. Write two working concerns that could explain why the strongest recurrences matter.
4. Test both accounts against every poem, marking support, contradiction, irrelevance, and forced interpretation.
5. Select or revise the account that explains the most consequential evidence while preserving meaningful differences and outliers.
6. Reveal any author statement or external interpretation, compare it with the evidence-based result, and retain it only as another reading rather than automatic authority.
7. Name one new subject that could test or expand the working theme instead of merely repeating its existing imagery.

## Success Check
- Subject inventory precedes abstract interpretation.
- The chosen account explains treatment and pressure, not only shared nouns.
- An alternative and an outlier materially test the result.
- Artistic quality and evidence of recurring concern remain separate judgments.
- The final statement remains revisable enough to admit growth and precise enough to guide selection or inquiry.

## Common Failures
- Reading only celebrated poems and mistaking editorial selection for the writer's complete pattern.
- Treating frequency alone as significance.
- Choosing the most flattering description of the author.
- Forcing every poem to confirm one identity claim.
- Using an author's explanation to skip analysis of the poems.

## Notes
The temporary removal of declared intention prevents aspiration from becoming the answer key. Reintroducing it later tests whether the statement illuminates the poems, oversimplifies them, or identifies a concern that the selected corpus does not yet demonstrate.
