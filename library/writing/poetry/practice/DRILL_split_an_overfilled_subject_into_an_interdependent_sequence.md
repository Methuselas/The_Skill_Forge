# Split an Overfilled Subject into an Interdependent Sequence

## Practice Task
Replace one poem that tries to cover an entire subject with several focused poems, then order them so the group creates movement unavailable from a compressed catalogue.

## Target Skill
Giving distinct facets of one poetic subject enough independence and relational pressure to form a sequence.

## Setup
Choose a draft crowded with several times, places, speakers, relationships, images, or arguments. Preserve the original and list every distinct facet it tries to contain.

## Instructions
1. Group the facets into three to five focuses that can each sustain a separate poem without summarizing the whole subject.
2. Assign each focus a different angle, such as observer position, time, scale, tone, formal pressure, or stage of change.
3. Draft each member independently, repeating only elements whose altered return can carry recognition or development.
4. Arrange one narrative version and one constellation version; write the transition or resonance created at every boundary.
5. Remove each member in turn and note what disappears from the larger experience.
6. Revise, merge, or cut any member whose job is duplicated, then compare the sequence with the original crowded draft.

## Success Check
- Each member has a coherent focus rather than one slice of an arbitrary word count.
- The pieces differ in treatment while remaining recognizably related.
- At least one recurrence changes significance across the group.
- The selected order creates transition or resonance that the alternate does not produce as strongly.
- The sequence explores the subject more deeply without merely spreading the original excess across more pages.

## Common Failures
- Converting every paragraph or stanza into a separate poem without redefining its focus.
- Repeating background information in every member.
- Using a common title to substitute for actual relationship.
- Keeping a weak bridge poem solely because the planned number requires it.
- Assuming chronological order is necessary when no consequential change depends on time.

## Notes
Expansion earns its space when different angles alter the reader's understanding of the subject. The removal test makes interdependence observable: a missing transition, tonal counterweight, stage of change, or transformed recurrence identifies the member's job; no change identifies redundancy.
