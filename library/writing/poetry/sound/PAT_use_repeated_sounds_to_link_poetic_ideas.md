# Use Repeated Sounds to Link Poetic Ideas

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a poem needs musical continuity or a subtle relationship between particular words and ideas
**THEN** recur selected consonant or vowel sounds across the relevant language and regroup them until the audible connection accents the intended meaning
**ELSE** avoid adding echoes that function only as ornament

## Do
- Use initial consonants when a clearly entering sound should gather several words into one phrase or movement.
- Carry a consonant through stressed syllables even when the surrounding vowels differ to create a less conspicuous link.
- Repeat a vowel sound across words with different terminal consonants when the passage needs a sustained tonal current.
- Trace a chosen sound beyond adjacent words; a recurrence across separate lines can bind ideas without announcing a formal rhyme scheme.
- Read the passage aloud and revise by hearing, since spelling alone can conceal or falsely suggest a correspondence.

## Don't
- Scatter a conspicuous letter through the poem merely to demonstrate a named device.
- Assume that a poem without consistent end rhyme has no designed sound relationships.
- Crowd every phrase with echoes until verbal display overwhelms syntax, image, or thought.
- Let an attractive sound bind words whose resulting semantic association works against the intended effect.

## Checklist
- The recurring sound can be heard in the selected words rather than inferred only from spelling.
- The words joined by sound also have a useful similarity, contrast, progression, or tension in meaning.
- Removing or relocating the recurrence would alter the passage's emphasis or musical movement.
- The pattern remains proportionate to the poem and does not force inaccurate diction.
- Sound organization contributes poetic intensity whether or not the poem uses end rhyme.

## Notes
Alliteration repeats initial consonant sounds; consonance carries consonants through other positions, especially stressed endings; assonance repeats vowel sounds without requiring the same final consonants. The labels are useful for locating the mechanism, but the craft decision is relational: ordered sound should make selected language cohere and should help the reader hear how its ideas belong together.
