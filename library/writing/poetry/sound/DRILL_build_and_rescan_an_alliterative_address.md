# Build and Rescan an Alliterative Address

## Practice Task
Write a short address to a nonhuman subject through stressed sound recurrences, then rescan and revise it until the audible pattern supports rather than distorts the language.

## Target Skill
Hearing and revising stressed alliteration as an accentual line structure.

## Setup
Choose a concrete nonhuman addressee—a food, tool, appliance, vehicle, place, monument, fictional being, or planet—and list precise actions, qualities, and relations before choosing recurring sounds.

## Instructions
1. Draft 8–12 lines addressed directly to the subject, aiming for two clearly related stressed sounds in each line and occasionally testing a third.
2. Vary line length and the placement of recurrences; keep related sounds close enough to remain audible without forcing every line into identical syntax.
3. Read the draft aloud and mark the ordinary stressed syllable inside each multisyllabic alliterating word.
4. Mark additional words that receive comparable emphasis from the phrase, then count the major beats and the lighter syllables between them.
5. Circle matches visible in spelling but weak in pronunciation, along with forced diction, distorted syntax, and sound links that accent the wrong idea.
6. Revise for a perceptible beat pattern across the poem. Replace weak matches, move recurrences, or permit an exception when stricter alliteration would damage meaning or movement.
7. Read the unmarked revision aloud once more and verify that the pattern remains audible without exaggerated performance.

## Success Check
- The address stays directed at the subject throughout rather than sliding into description of it.
- Related stressed sounds sit close enough to remain audible without every line taking the same syntactic shape.
- Scansion marks the ordinary stressed syllable inside each multisyllabic alliterating word, so recurrence is judged where the stress actually falls rather than at the start of the word.
- Words drawing comparable emphasis from the phrase are marked too, and major beats are counted with the lighter syllables between them.
- Any line where the pattern distorted diction, word order, or sense is revised, and the lost sound is accepted rather than argued for.
- The lines read aloud as speech to something, with the pattern supporting the address rather than announcing itself.

## Common Failures
- Counting an unstressed initial syllable as a strong recurrence.
- Ignoring equally stressed non-alliterating words when describing the beat pattern.
- Packing every line with the same letter until the poem becomes a tongue twister.
- Choosing vague praise because sound generation has displaced attention to the addressee.
- Performing the pattern with artificial emphasis that ordinary reading does not support.

## Notes
The nonhuman address supplies a stable semantic field while sound is isolated for practice. Rescanning matters because an apparent pattern on the page may disappear in speech: stress can fall later in a word, and syntax can promote a small word that does not participate in the selected recurrence. The last unmarked reading tests whether revision created real musical organization rather than a diagram that requires coaching to hear.
