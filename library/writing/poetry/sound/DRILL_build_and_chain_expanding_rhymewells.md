# Build and Chain Expanding Rhymewells

## Practice Task
Search outward from one charged word through progressively looser sound relations, restart from a productive outer discovery, and draft from both fields.

## Target Skill
Expanding audible correspondence into generative diction without confusing discovery with final selection.

## Setup
Choose one exact word from a live draft and prepare two sets of concentric rings or graded lists.

## Instructions
1. Fill the nearest ring with the fullest pronounced matches.
2. Move outward through slant, consonantal, assonantal, stressed-syllable, rhythmic, and phrase-level echoes.
3. Stop when the relation can no longer be heard, then move one surprising edge word to the center of the second field.
4. Repeat the search and draft for ten minutes from associations across both fields.
5. Mark discoveries that strengthen sense and cut words retained only to prove the search occurred.

## Success Check
- Degrees of relation are judged by sound rather than spelling.
- The second field opens diction unavailable from the first center.
- At least one association develops beyond a rhyme list.
- Final selection rejects forced discoveries.

## Common Failures
- Treating every suffix match as equally strong.
- Stopping at exact rhyme.
- Preserving the whole inventory in the draft.

## Notes
The widening search exteriorizes associative listening. Its purpose is choice: distant echoes may generate the most useful surprise and still be removed when they weaken the poem.
