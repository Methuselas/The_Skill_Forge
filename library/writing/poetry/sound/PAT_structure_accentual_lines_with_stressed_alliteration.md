# Structure Accentual Lines with Stressed Alliteration

## Pattern Rule
**IF** repeated initial or near-initial sounds are intended to organize a poem's lines as an audible accentual pattern
**THEN** place the recurrences on pronounced stressed syllables, regulate the major beats and the lighter syllables between them, and bend the pattern when strict compliance damages the poem
**ELSE** use quieter sound recurrence without asking it to carry the line's rhythmic structure

## Do
- Identify the stressed syllable inside each multisyllabic candidate before counting it as part of the recurrence.
- Speak the complete phrase to hear which words syntax, contrast, repetition, or meaning promotes beyond their ordinary lexical stress.
- Keep related sounds close enough for the ear to group them, while allowing line length to follow the movement the phrase requires.
- Count every major beat, including stressed words outside the alliterating set, then notice how many lighter syllables separate successive beats.
- Establish enough recurrence for the pattern to become audible before using an exception as expressive variation.
- Retain the most accurate and forceful word when a convenient sound match would weaken sense, voice, or tone.

## Don't
- Count matching initial letters as an audible pattern without testing pronunciation and stress.
- Treat a word as a beat merely because it begins with the selected letter when a later syllable carries its main stress.
- Cram several sound matches into every line until syntax becomes distorted or diction becomes absurd without purpose.
- Confuse a recurring number of strong beats with a fixed syllable count or a regular sequence of metrical feet.

## Checklist
- The governing recurrence can be heard with the text out of sight.
- Its important instances occur on syllables that receive real lexical or contextual stress.
- The number and spacing of major beats establish a perceptible rhythm across several lines.
- Deviations are legible as variations against an established pattern rather than accidental lapses.
- Sound-organized words remain exact enough to serve the poem's subject and movement.

## Notes
Accentual verse measures recurring strong beats while allowing the number of lighter syllables between them to vary. Alliteration can expose that structure because stressed sound recurrences give the ear conspicuous landmarks, but the alliterating words are only a minimum: other words may carry equal weight. The pattern therefore has to be heard in the phrase, not counted from its first letters. A near match or missing recurrence is defensible when it keeps the poem moving and the larger rhythmic expectation remains audible.
