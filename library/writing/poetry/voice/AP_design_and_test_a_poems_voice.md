# Design and Test a Poem's Voice

## Objective
Create a flexible, poem-specific speaking presence whose attention, language, rhythm, knowledge, omissions, and reader relation jointly produce the intended effect without collapsing the speaker into the author or substituting verbal costume for perception.

## Steps / Flow
1. **Name the speaking situation.** Identify who appears to speak, what pressure makes speech necessary now, whom the poem addresses, what the speaker can perceive or know, and what remains unavailable. When the speaker resembles the writer, preserve the distinction between a selected textual self and the whole biographical person. **Advance gate:** the poem has a usable relation among speaker, subject, and implied listener.
2. **Diagnose the current language.** Activate `writing_poetry_treat_voice_as_flexible_poem_specific_system`. Mark anonymous phrases, conspicuous private quirks, recurring word families, syntactic habits, rhythms, leaps, and omissions. If the available vocabulary feels narrow or unexamined, run `writing_poetry_drill_counterbalance_word_inventory_for_voice`. **Advance gate:** at least three interacting choices can be revised or retained as parts of the voice.
3. **Set register and linguistic access.** Activate `writing_choose_diction_to_serve_purpose_and_tone`. When dialect, community language, multilingual wording, or unconventional grammar matters, activate `writing_make_nonstandard_language_deliberate`; verify access, accuracy, and the intended reader route through context or explanation. **Recovery:** remove a marker that functions only as authenticity display and rebuild the effect through exact attention, syntax, or rhythm.
4. **Choose the speaker branch.** For a witness or alternate human idiolect, compare what that speaker notices, values, and omits. For an animal, object, historical figure, or other constructed speaker, activate `writing_poetry_construct_persona_through_perception_desire_and_verbal_form`. **Advance gate:** changing speakers changes the selection and organization of experience, not merely pronouns or accent.
5. **Choose the discourse branch.** If personal lyric supplies the needed movement, keep it. If the poem needs unfamiliar concepts, structures, or tonal pressure, activate `writing_poetry_expand_voice_by_borrowing_another_discourse`. If it tells an event whose explanation is flattening the pressure, activate `writing_poetry_withhold_motive_when_detail_supports_inference`. Return to the speaking situation whenever the borrowed mode displaces the speaker's reason to speak.
6. **Coordinate the poem's other systems.** Test whether line boundaries, stanza gaps, sound recurrences, figurative habits, title, and any gloss amplify the selected voice. Use `writing_poetry_break_lines_to_control_pace_and_expectation` when syntax and lineation produce different speakers or levels of control. Use `writing_poetry_add_paratext_for_external_framing` only when readers need external support.
7. **Run the transfer test.** Move one sentence or line into a contrasting speaker or neutral register. Revise if it survives unchanged despite a different attention, knowledge, motive, and relationship. Read the complete poem aloud and mark places where delivery must manufacture distinctions absent from the language.
8. **Complete without fixing an identity.** Stop when the speaker's access remains coherent, conspicuous language is accurate and purposeful, omissions invite supported inference, the poem works on the page as well as aloud, and every deviation from ordinary register performs a named job. Preserve the resulting method as one successful voice in a range, not as the author's permanent signature.

## Notes
Voice is an effect created by interacting choices rather than an essence deposited in every poem. Recurring obsessions and verbal affinities can generate material, but flexibility matters: another subject, addressee, persona, or discourse may require a different system. The protocol therefore starts from the present speaking situation and closes by protecting future range.
