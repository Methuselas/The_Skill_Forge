# Diagnose Voice Through a Counterbalanced Word Inventory

## Practice Task
Build a fixed-size inventory of favored words, counterbalance each choice with a contrasting word, and use the resulting pairs in constrained writing to discover tendencies without turning one sample into a permanent identity.

## Target Skill
Detecting and expanding the verbal affinities that contribute to a poem-specific voice.

## Setup
Use the alphabet or another fixed set of 20–30 prompts. Keep a dictionary available only for prompts that stall the collection, and choose a short name, phrase, or theme to supply an acrostic constraint.

## Instructions
1. For every prompt, record one word you are strongly drawn to without trying to make the list coherent.
2. Mark whether sound, meaning, association, visual shape, or more than one quality drove each choice.
3. Add a counterbalancing word beside every favorite: plain beside ornate, abrasive beside soothing, concrete beside abstract, comic beside solemn, or another useful opposition.
4. Write one brief sentence or line for each letter of the chosen acrostic, drawing from both sides of the inventory and allowing unexpected combinations.
5. Read the result aloud. Mark recurring rhythms, sounds, semantic fields, leaps, anonymous phrasing, and private quirks that readers could not yet interpret.
6. Revise three lines by replacing the most predictable favorite with its counterbalance or a third alternative; note which changes enlarge the voice and which merely create novelty.
7. State two testable tendencies from this sample and one reason each might fail to recur in another poem.

## Success Check
- Every initial choice has a meaningful counterbalance rather than a random antonym.
- Favored words enter relations and sentences instead of remaining an inert list.
- The constrained piece reveals at least two interacting tendencies in sound, syntax, subject, or association.
- Revision distinguishes productive surprise from arbitrary strangeness.
- Conclusions remain hypotheses about this sample, not declarations of a fixed authorial voice.

## Common Failures
- Treating the inventory as an identity statement or a vocabulary that future poems must repeat.
- Equating rare words with distinctiveness and common words with anonymity.
- Forcing all entries into one theme before their independent attractions become visible.
- Choosing counterbalances so unrelated that they cannot create usable pressure.
- Preserving the inventory as a permanent tracking system instead of using it as a bounded laboratory.

## Notes
The fixed prompts prevent the exercise from stopping after familiar words, while counterbalances resist self-caricature. The writing step matters because voice emerges through selection, combination, rhythm, and relation—not from isolated vocabulary. Repeating the exercise on another occasion can test persistence, but one inventory establishes only current affinities.
