# Collect Passage-Anchored Critique Before Revision

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a critique conversation should help you choose revisions rather than collect everyone's first reaction
**THEN** circulate the complete draft for passage-anchored annotation before meeting, cluster the responses, and use the live discussion to test diagnoses and revision options
**ELSE** reserve a silent annotation period at the start of the conversation before discussing the piece

## Do
- Keep the draft and preliminary feedback together so repeated reactions, conflicting readings, and unexamined passages remain visible at once.
- Ask readers to attach each note to exact language and name the effect, question, or point of confusion it creates.
- Have readers scan existing annotations before adding another; they can reinforce a pattern, offer a contrasting reading, or cover an untouched passage.
- Before discussion, sort comments into two or three consequential clusters and write the revision decisions each cluster might change.

## Don't
- Don't spend the live conversation reading the draft for the first time when an earlier annotation pass is feasible.
- Don't equate the platform with the method. Margin notes, shared comments, or a collected worksheet can support the same sequence.
- Don't count duplicated comments as broad coverage; consolidate repeated reactions and focus the conversation on consequences and alternatives.

## Checklist
- Did every reader receive the complete draft before the conversation?
- Is each comment attached to a passage rather than offered as a floating verdict?
- Can I name the strongest recurring response and the most important disagreement?
- Does the live agenda move from reactions to concrete revision choices?

## Notes
The source presents this sequence through a discontinued course platform, but its portable contribution is independent of classroom software: preliminary annotations centralize feedback so a later conversation can begin with the draft's recurring problems and possibilities already visible. For personal writing, the writer uses that synthesis to arrive at the meeting with questions and leaves with ranked revision options.
