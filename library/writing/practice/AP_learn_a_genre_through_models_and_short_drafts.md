# Learn a Genre Through Models and Short Drafts

## Objective
Enter an unfamiliar genre by connecting its broad possibilities to complete models, observable craft decisions, a feasible first piece, and substantial revision.

## Steps / Flow
1. **Name the writing target.** Choose one genre and a complete short form that can be drafted, read closely, and revised within the available time.
2. **Sketch broad parameters.** Establish an orienting boundary without treating one definition or example as the genre's only legitimate shape.
3. **Close-read a small set of complete models.** Select concise, excellent works at the scale of the intended piece and notice how they make different valid use of the form.
4. **Analyze one craft element at a time.** Point to the decision in each text, compare its effects across models, and translate the observation into a move to attempt.
5. **Learn terms beside live examples.** Add vocabulary when it clarifies a decision already visible in the models instead of front-loading a detached glossary.
6. **Generate several possible starts.** Use bounded prompts or starting strategies to make material before deciding which possibility deserves a full draft.
7. **Finish one short work, obtain close feedback, and revise it.** Keep the piece within restart scale so a weak governing choice can be replaced rather than merely polished.

## Notes
Starkey describes this repeated architecture for teaching poetry, fiction, creative nonfiction, and drama: orientation, analyzed models, element summaries, terms in context, generation exercises, and further examples. Recast as personal practice, it is a writer's study loop. The durable sequence moves from recognition to production and revision; it does not require the source's exact model count or classroom setting.
