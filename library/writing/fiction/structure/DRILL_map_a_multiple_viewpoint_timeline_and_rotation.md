# Map a Multiple-Viewpoint Timeline and Rotation

## Practice Task
Build and stress-test a whole-story map that separates when events occur from when each viewpoint presents them.

## Target Skill
Arrange multiple viewpoint strands so chronology, narrative contribution, allocation, and every transition remain deliberate and legible.

## Setup
Choose a planned or drafted story with at least two viewpoint positions and five prospective sections or scenes. Prepare one row for every unit of presentation.

## Instructions
1. Write the complete underlying chronology without using the draft's current order.
2. For every proposed unit, record chronological time, presented position, active viewpoint, location, knowledge entering and leaving, viewpoint-specific job, and intended transition cue.
3. Give each viewpoint its own line across the chronology. Mark encounters, shared events, exclusive events, long absences, returns, and clusters of consecutive units.
4. Measure each viewpoint's approximate share, then state the function that justifies the allocation instead of treating equality as the target.
5. Test one regular rotation and one consequence-driven order. Record how each changes momentum, disclosure, allegiance, and orientation.
6. Cover headings and labels on the opening of every switched unit. Mark the first phrase that identifies the active access position and repair any opening that reveals private knowledge before relocation is legible.
7. Remove the smallest viewpoint from the map and actually reroute its indispensable information through another character's observation, dialogue, testimony, document, or report. Record the effect that remains lost, or delete that viewpoint if none does.
8. Choose the arrangement whose demonstrated gains justify its transitions, then update every row affected by the choice.

## Success Check
- The underlying chronology and presented order are both complete and distinguishable.
- Every unit names a viewpoint-specific job; “it is this character's turn” is identified as a failed justification.
- Every long absence, dense cluster, and unequal share has either a functional reason or a recorded repair.
- The regular and consequence-driven orders were both mapped, and the chosen order is justified by observed effects rather than symmetry or preference.
- With labels covered, each switch establishes the new access position before using its private knowledge.
- The smallest-viewpoint removal test was applied to the map, and retention names a consequential loss that rerouting could not preserve.

## Common Failures
- Copying the draft's order and calling it the chronology.
- Counting sections without recording what each one changes.
- Equalizing shares by padding low-pressure viewpoints.
- Treating headings as sufficient while the opening voice and knowledge remain ambiguous.
- Predicting that removal would fail without attempting the alternative information routes.

## Notes
The map turns a reader's relocation burden into visible design. It does not prescribe equal shares or a fixed cycle. Its purpose is to show whether every return arrives when that viewpoint can change the story and whether the underlying lives remain coherent beneath the selected order.
