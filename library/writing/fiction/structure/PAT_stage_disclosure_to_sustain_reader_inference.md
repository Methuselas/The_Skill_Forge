# Stage Disclosure to Sustain Reader Inference

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a fiction draft explains its important information before readers can use it or withholds orientation merely to manufacture confusion
**THEN** schedule each disclosure at the point where it supplies necessary footing, creates a productive question, or changes the meaning of prior evidence
**ELSE** preserve the existing information rhythm when readers can remain oriented while actively inferring what matters

## Do
- List the facts the writer knows and mark when the viewpoint character, narrator, and reader can credibly know each one.
- Give readers enough immediate situation, relation, and physical context to interpret action without unloading the full history.
- Name the question each withheld fact should sustain and the event or evidence that will eventually answer, deepen, or redirect it.
- Deliver exposition through motivated action, speech, perception, summary, or consequence when that route makes the information perform another job.
- Let partial disclosures accumulate so readers can form and revise hypotheses before receiving confirmation.
- Reveal a fact earlier when concealment depends on unnatural silence, impossible viewpoint behavior, or missing information the scene needs.

## Don't
- Treat every unknown as mystery; an absent foothold produces disorientation rather than inference.
- Make characters hide shared knowledge from each other solely so readers cannot hear it.
- Delay a fact after readers have already inferred it unless the delayed confirmation changes consequence or trust.
- Explain the complete backstory at the first mention of a person, place, or object.
- Resolve every question immediately or preserve every question indefinitely.

## Checklist
- Readers know enough at each stage to understand the current action and form useful questions.
- Every consequential omission has a named inferential or emotional function.
- Disclosures arrive through a credible source and at a consequential moment.
- New information confirms, complicates, or redirects prior evidence rather than merely filling a blank.
- Exposition is distributed without forcing characters or narrators into unnatural behavior.
- Remaining uncertainty is intentional and distinguishable from missing causality or continuity.

## Notes
Disclosure controls the reader's changing relation to the story. Mystery asks why something happened or what it means; suspense asks what may happen; exposition supplies the world and history needed to interpret both. The useful rhythm is neither maximum concealment nor maximum clarity. It gives readers enough evidence to work while timing each additional fact so knowledge itself becomes part of the narrative movement.
