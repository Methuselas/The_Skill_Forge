# Foreshadow a Consequence While Withholding Its Route

## Pattern Rule
**IF** readers need a future-facing question to organize anticipation across the story
**THEN** reveal a consequential outcome, image, phrase, or condition early enough to create that question while withholding the chain that will fulfill or reinterpret it
**ELSE** omit foreshadowing when the present action already supplies sufficient pressure or advance knowledge would weaken discovery

## Do
- Name the exact question the signal should make readers carry, such as who is affected, how an outcome occurs, or why a promised change matters.
- Plant the signal in an opening, title, image, object, statement, or brief forward glimpse whose importance can survive the intervening narrative.
- Reveal enough consequence to create anticipation while preserving uncertainty about cause, route, meaning, or final context.
- Let later events complicate the reader's first interpretation rather than merely delaying an answer already obvious.
- Fulfill, transform, or deliberately frustrate the signal before the story closes so its placement proves structural rather than decorative.

## Don't
- Announce an outcome whose only function is to manufacture generic suspense.
- Repeat the signal so frequently that anticipation becomes instruction about what to feel.
- Conceal the promised route through arbitrary missing information the viewpoint would naturally supply.
- Use a title as a hook when the story never makes its wording consequential.
- Foreshadow every important event; surprise and unmarked development need room to operate.

## Checklist
- The early signal creates a specific prospective question.
- Readers know less about the route than about the promised consequence or condition.
- Intervening material changes or deepens the signal's possible meaning.
- The fulfillment feels prepared without becoming mechanically predicted.
- Removing the signal would materially change anticipation, interpretation, or structural unity.

## Notes
Foreshadowing trades one kind of uncertainty for another. Telling readers that something will happen can remove surprise about the fact while intensifying curiosity about cause, identity, process, or meaning. The useful signal is therefore not a vague warning but a controlled piece of future information whose placement makes readers imaginatively active.
