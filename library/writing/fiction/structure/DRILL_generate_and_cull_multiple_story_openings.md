# Generate and Cull Multiple Story Openings

## Practice Task
Draft several short-story openings in one sitting, create distance, and select the one whose live pressure most strongly earns continued reading.

## Target Skill
Recognize and produce an opening that begins where consequential action, decision, or curiosity is already active.

## Setup
Prepare three to five story kernels or fragments and enough uninterrupted time to draft one short opening for each. Plan to leave the results unread for at least one night.

## Instructions
1. Write an opening of one to three paragraphs for each kernel without polishing the previous attempt before starting the next.
2. Give every opening a character, a present situation, and a pressure or question that cannot remain static.
3. Stop each sample before explaining its full background or planned ending.
4. Put all samples away long enough for their composing momentum to cool.
5. Reread them in a different order and mark the first point at which each creates curiosity, orientation, and consequential movement.
6. Choose the opening that reaches those effects with the least preamble, then continue that story rather than repairing every rejected sample.
7. Retain any strong fragment from a rejected opening only if it belongs causally in the selected story.

## Success Check
- Every sample contains active story pressure rather than only atmosphere or biography.
- The delayed reading changes the basis of selection from composing excitement to reader experience.
- The chosen opening supplies enough orientation without explaining everything first.
- A later starting point would remove necessary footing, while an earlier one would add preamble.
- Selection produces one draft to continue instead of an expanding set of polished beginnings.

## Common Failures
- Writing several versions of the same first sentence rather than several sustained entries.
- Choosing immediately because the newest attempt feels most vivid.
- Mistaking unexplained danger or oddity for meaningful curiosity.
- Polishing all candidates until comparison becomes a way to avoid continuing one.
- Combining favorite lines from incompatible premises into an opening with no causal center.

## Notes
Generating in a batch weakens attachment to the first available idea; delaying the decision weakens attachment to the feeling of having just written it. The practice target is not maximal novelty but entry into a story whose present pressure can continue beyond the opening.
