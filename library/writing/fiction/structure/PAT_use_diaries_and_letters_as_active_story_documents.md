# Use Diaries and Letters as Active Story Documents

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a diary, letter, journal, testimony, or comparable document carries part or all of a fiction narrative
**THEN** establish its telling situation and distinct voice, give it local narrative movement, and place it where its production or discovery changes the active story
**ELSE** summarize the past when rendering the document adds no additional pressure, access, or interpretation

## Do
- Define who makes the document, for whom, on what occasion, under what motive, with what knowledge, and through what plausible route of circulation or preservation.
- Differentiate the document's voice from the surrounding narrative through register, syntax, rhythm, selection, assumption, and omission rather than decorative formatting alone.
- Research the writer's period, social position, medium, and communicative conventions before choosing historically marked language.
- Give each rendered document or extract local movement: a question pursued, decision made, confession risked, contradiction exposed, discovery registered, or understanding changed.
- Place the document where writing, withholding, sending, losing, finding, misunderstanding, or reading it alters pressure, knowledge, conduct, relation, or interpretation.
- Decide how much of the document readers need; render only the material whose exact voice or sequence performs work that summary cannot.

## Don't
- Use a document to recite facts every relevant character and reader already know.
- Make every diarist, correspondent, and surrounding narrator sound interchangeable.
- Substitute antique spelling, ornate typography, or indiscriminate archaism for a researched voice.
- Treat a private diary as audience-free speech; self-concealment, self-performance, habit, fear, and imagined readership can still shape it.
- Require every extract to contain a complete standalone plot when a smaller local turn performs its structural job.
- Transform a real document without first declaring the truth contract and distinguishing evidence from invention.

## Checklist
- The document's maker, audience, occasion, motive, knowledge, and route into the story are credible.
- Its voice is distinguishable from the surrounding narrative and appropriate to period and position.
- Every rendered extract contains movement rather than static background.
- Its placement changes present or downstream pressure, knowledge, action, relationship, or interpretation.
- The exact document form provides access, tension, or ambiguity that ordinary summary would lose.
- Any real source material is used within a declared truth contract and without copied distinctive expression.

## Notes
A story document is both language and event. Its contents matter, but so do the circumstances of making, withholding, transmitting, preserving, discovering, and interpreting it. The document need not tell a complete independent story; it must complete a local narrative action and produce a consequence in the larger one.

`writing_fiction_variant_use_one_discovered_document_as_present_tense_turn` concentrates the method in a single artifact. Build the present scene around the reader-character's changing interpretation, then require the discovery to alter what that character does, believes, risks, or asks next.

`writing_fiction_variant_interleave_documentary_past_with_present_action` makes the document strand a sustained structural carrier. Map both chronologies, order each document so it advances its own past pressure, and place each alternation where new knowledge complicates the present rather than merely explaining it.
