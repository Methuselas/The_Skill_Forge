# End the Selected Story Without Exhausting the Larger Life

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a fiction ending trails into explanation or forces every surrounding question to close after the governing action, relation, or pattern has reached its necessary turn
**THEN** stop where the selected story becomes complete while leaving broader lives, circumstances, and productive mysteries beyond the frame
**ELSE** retain fuller resolution when the genre promise or governing complication genuinely requires it

## Do
- Restate the selected story separately from the characters' complete biographies and the world's continuing history.
- Identify which action, pressure, relation, or pattern must culminate for this story to feel whole.
- Prepare the ending through earlier evidence while preserving uncertainty about its exact form, context, or emotional meaning.
- Resolve or transform the central reader question even when subsidiary facts remain unknown.
- Let a changed action, image, perception, relation, or texture carry the final shift when explicit insight would narrow it.
- Test each paragraph after the culmination and retain it only if it completes a remaining consequence or materially changes interpretation.

## Don't
- Continue until every character's later fate has been reported.
- Add marriage, death, confession, reconciliation, or another conventional terminal event merely to manufacture closure.
- Call an arbitrary cutoff mysterious when the governing movement has not reached a consequence.
- Withhold the central answer after the story has promised a genre whose essential satisfaction depends on it.
- Explain the ending's meaning after the final evidence has already made the shift available.

## Checklist
- The governing story reaches a consequence, culmination, or decisive variation.
- The ending feels prepared in retrospect without having been mechanically predictable.
- Unresolved material belongs to the larger life or deepens the completed story rather than exposing a missing step.
- The size of the final change fits the accumulated pressure and available length.
- Readers can infer why the story stops here even if they cannot settle every implication.
- Deleting the final explanatory passage strengthens or weakens the ending for a named reason.

## Notes
Completeness belongs to the selected story, not to the represented life as a whole. A narrative can finish after a slight emotional shift while relationships, motives, and circumstances remain complex. Productive openness differs from incompletion because the governing movement has reached a consequential boundary; what remains unknown enlarges that completed experience instead of postponing it.
