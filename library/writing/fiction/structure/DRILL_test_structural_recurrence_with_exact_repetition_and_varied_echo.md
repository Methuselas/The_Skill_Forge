# Test Structural Recurrence with Exact Repetition and Varied Echo

## Practice Task
Draft a bounded story containing one exactly repeated element and one transformed echo, then revise until both recurrences reinforce the governing movement without making the design schematic.

## Target Skill
Use exact recurrence and transformed echoes to make narrative change perceptible.

## Setup
Choose a planned or drafted story with a legible change in character, relation, situation, or understanding. Name one event, object, location, phrase, or condition that could recur exactly and a second element that could return with a changed context, participant, scale, or emotional charge.

## Instructions
1. State the governing movement in one sentence and write what each recurrence should let readers remember, compare, or infer.
2. Draft a compact version of the story with the exact element appearing at least twice and the echoed element appearing first in one form and later in a recognizably altered form.
3. Mark the context surrounding each appearance: who notices it, what it means at that moment, and what has changed since the prior occurrence.
4. Remove any recurrence that duplicates meaning without increasing pressure, contrast, or unity.
5. Move the remaining recurrences farther apart or closer together and compare how placement changes recognition, anticipation, and emphasis.
6. Revise the final recurrence so it gathers or overturns an earlier meaning without explaining the connection after the evidence.
7. Read the story once with the recurring language highlighted and once without highlighting. Keep the pattern only if it remains perceptible in ordinary reading but does not dominate every transition.

## Success Check
- The exact repetition establishes a useful continuity or expectation.
- The varied echo is recognizable and records a consequential difference.
- Both recurrences connect to the governing movement rather than merely decorating the prose.
- Placement gives readers enough distance to remember and reinterpret each element.
- The final recurrence produces inference without an explanatory gloss.
- Removing either pattern creates a named structural loss.

## Common Failures
- Repeating conspicuous wording so often that the story announces its design.
- Changing the echo so completely that readers cannot recognize the relation.
- Using recurrence as a substitute for causal or temporal orientation the story still needs.
- Explaining what the repeated element symbolizes instead of letting context change its meaning.
- Keeping several motifs when one exact recurrence and one variation already perform the work.

## Notes
The exercise separates recurrence from ornament by assigning every repeated element a structural job. Exact repetition supplies a stable point of comparison; variation measures movement against it. The highlighting pass exposes excess, while the ordinary reading tests whether the scaffold supports the story without becoming its visible cage.
