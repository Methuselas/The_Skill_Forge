# Allocate Scene and Summary by Narrative Pressure

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a draft gives routine transitions and consequential turns the same narrative space
**THEN** expand moments whose action, perception, speech, or inference carries the story into scene, and compress connective duration or background into selective summary
**ELSE** retain the current proportion when its changes of speed already match changes in pressure and importance

## Do
- Enter scene when a choice, encounter, discovery, or relational turn needs exact action, sensory presence, thought, or speech to produce its effect.
- Use summary to cross routine intervals, establish relevant backstory, move between locations, or cover repeated conditions whose individual instances do not need enactment.
- Select only pressure-bearing details in either mode; a scene is not permission to inventory everything present, and a summary is not permission to explain everything known.
- Let summary leave productive questions open, then disclose or dramatize the information when its timing changes the reader's understanding.
- Blend brief summary with enacted beats when readers need both temporal reach and immediate evidence.
- Treat narrative space as a signal of importance: expand only what deserves the expectation that close attention creates.

## Don't
- Apply "show, don't tell" as an absolute rule.
- Dramatize waking, eating, travelling, greetings, or other connective business merely because it happened.
- Summarize a climax or irreversible exchange when exact behavior and timing create its force.
- Inflate an insignificant incident with vivid detail that falsely promises later consequence.
- Replace scene with abstract emotional labels when readers need evidence from which to infer the state.
- Unload a character's full history in one block when smaller deposits can enter through present pressure.

## Checklist
- Changes in narrative speed correspond to changes in consequence, pressure, or required reader attention.
- Every expanded scene supplies an effect that summary would weaken or erase.
- Every compressed passage preserves the orientation and causal change readers need.
- Selected details invite inference without crowding the reader with explanation or decorative abundance.
- Backstory arrives when it alters the live action rather than merely proving the writer knows it.
- The combination of scene and summary feels selective but complete for the governing story.

## Notes
Showing and telling are relative degrees of narrative expansion, not opposing measures of quality. A sentence can cross years while a few seconds occupy a page. The useful question is what the reader must experience directly and what can be carried by an informed narrator without losing consequence. Under-expansion leaves important events inert; over-expansion crowds out inference, slows movement, and assigns false importance.

`writing_fiction_variant_sustain_summary_for_tale_or_parable_logic` makes compression the governing texture. Move rapidly through the causal sequence, pause only for speech or detail that sharpens the reversal, and let the overall arrangement of events produce the effect. The variant succeeds when summary is deliberate form rather than an undeveloped substitute for scenes the story actually needs.
