# Design and Render a Story Setting

## Objective
Choose, establish, and render a story's time and place so geography remains credible, selected details intensify character and conflict, and description supplies atmosphere and orientation without stopping the action.

## Steps / Flow
1. **Name the setting's story job.** State which character relation, conflict, action limit, opportunity, or atmosphere the time and place must intensify. Preserve that pressure and the scene's required movements as invariants. **Advance gate:** replacing the setting with a generic room or town would remove a named effect.
2. **Choose the consequential time and place.** Activate `writing_fiction_choose_setting_to_intensify_character_and_conflict`. Test how history, season, hour, weather, enclosure, distance, and social use alter what characters can do. When a workplace or institution regulates access, belonging, or consequence, activate `writing_fiction_build_institutional_settings_as_rule_bound_social_worlds` and establish the operating order the character must navigate. If localization only decorates the premise, return to the conflict and choose a condition that bears on it.
3. **Ground the design.** Activate `writing_fiction_ground_real_and_imagined_settings_in_accurate_detail`. Research consequential facts for real places and borrow coherent physical or cultural particulars for invented ones. Separate what must be accurate for action from attractive information the story does not need.
4. **Establish spatial continuity.** Use `writing_fiction_drill_map_setting_for_spatial_continuity` for recurring or action-dependent locations. Trace entrances, exits, sightlines, distances, and travel time. **Advance gate:** every consequential movement is possible and repeated locations retain stable relations.
5. **Choose the perceptual filter.** Coordinate `writing_fiction_choose_point_of_view_by_access_distance_and_effect` with `writing_fiction_filter_setting_through_character_consciousness`. Decide who notices the place, what familiarity makes invisible, and what fear, desire, knowledge, or class makes salient. Route an authorial inventory back through the active observer.
6. **Select implication-bearing details.** Activate `writing_fiction_use_specific_sensory_details_to_imply_larger_conditions`. Choose sensory evidence that simultaneously orients the reader and suggests atmosphere, history, relation, or approaching trouble. When an inhabited space or its possessions must characterize an occupant, activate `writing_fiction_reveal_character_through_possessions_and_inhabited_space` and select traces whose use or meaning creates the inference. If a detail requires explanatory interpretation, replace it with observable consequence or let the implication remain.
7. **Control density by need.** Activate `writing_fiction_control_setting_detail_density_by_story_need`. Expand where geography, unfamiliarity, mood, or action requires anchoring; compress where pace and focal attention reject a survey. For flash fiction, favor one location or a few recurring details unless a transition earns more space.
8. **Audit and complete.** Read scene movements against the map and remove facts that neither orient, constrain, reveal, nor imply. Stop when the setting creates useful pressure, characters move through it credibly, every explicit detail belongs to the viewpoint and moment, and readers receive enough of the larger place to imagine beyond the described edge.

## Notes
The writer's setting knowledge should exceed the prose. Research and mapping create a stable private model; viewpoint and story need determine its visible edge. This order prevents description from becoming either generic backdrop or indiscriminate display, while allowing sparse passages to carry a strong sense of place through exact, consequential selection.
