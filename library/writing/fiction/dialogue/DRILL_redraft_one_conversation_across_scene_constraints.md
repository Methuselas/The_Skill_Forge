# Redraft One Conversation across Scene Constraints

## Practice Task
Redraft one consequential conversation under contrasting locations, deadlines, and participant states while holding its speakers, essential facts, and governing wants constant.

## Target Skill
Select scene conditions that alter attention, permitted speech, conduct, inference, or outcome instead of decorating an otherwise unchanged exchange.

## Setup
Choose two characters whose conversation must change knowledge, power, commitment, relationship, or available action. State each speaker's immediate want, the essential facts, what each knows, and the intended scene change. Preserve these as invariants across every version.

## Instructions
1. Draft a neutral baseline containing only the necessary turns. Mark where privacy, witnesses, objects, movement, interruption, time, or participant readiness could exert pressure.
2. Choose two contrasting locations. Inventory ownership, privacy, witnesses, noise, access, exits, interruptions, concurrent tasks, and objects each place supplies.
3. Draft the conversation in both locations. Let each condition change what can be said, heard, attempted, concealed, or interrupted; do not add scenic description that leaves behavior untouched.
4. Choose an event that will end or transform the opportunity to talk. State what becomes impossible, exposed, lost, or newly consequential when it occurs, then redraft one location version under that deadline.
5. Redraft again under a different credible participant state, such as fatigue, hope, pain, distraction, intoxication, grief, urgency, or divided attention. Change selection and conduct without converting the state into a fixed personality or automatic outcome.
6. Underline every condition-specific turn. Remove a background event, symbolic detail, or countdown that neither changes action nor redirects interpretation.
7. Compare the versions and select the conditions that best serve the larger story. Record the causal reason for the selection rather than choosing the busiest or most dramatic surface.

## Success Check
- Every version preserves the same speakers, essential facts, governing wants, and intended scene change.
- Each location changes at least one permission, interruption, action, inference, or consequence.
- The deadline has a defined expiry event and a concrete cost rather than an arbitrary count.
- Participant state changes attention or conduct without functioning as a stereotype.
- Retained environmental details belong to the setting and affect the exchange.
- A near miss containing picturesque background or a clock that changes nothing is identified and rejected.

## Common Failures
- Changing the underlying conflict so the settings cannot be compared.
- Adding noise, weather, witnesses, or objects that no character notices or responds to.
- Treating another character's home as a different backdrop rather than a change in ownership, permission, and power.
- Imposing a deadline whose expiry has no consequence.
- Making fatigue, time of day, or mood dictate identical behavior for every person.
- Selecting the version with the most incidents even though none belongs to the governing story.

## Notes
Scene conditions become structural when they alter available choices. Privacy may permit candor, a witness may force indirection, a task may occupy the hands while freeing speech, and a deadline may make delay into a decision. Comparative redrafting isolates those effects by preserving the human and informational problem while changing the conditions around it.
