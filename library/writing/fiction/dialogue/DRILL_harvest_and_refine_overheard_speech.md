# Harvest and Refine Overheard Speech

## Practice Task
Transcribe a short stretch of naturally occurring public conversation, analyze its spoken texture, and reshape selected moments into a fictional exchange that moves.

## Target Skill
Hear the difference between natural spoken rhythm and the purposeful selection required by fictional dialogue.

## Setup
Choose a busy public place where ordinary conversation is audible. Omit names and identifying details; the practice target is speech texture, not the speakers' identities.

## Instructions
1. Record several minutes of speech as closely as possible, including fragments, interruptions, repetitions, and abandoned sentences.
2. Mark the phrases that reveal relation, attitude, desire, or an unexpected rhythm.
3. Cross out greetings, filler, and repeated information that adds no new pressure.
4. Recombine the strongest moments into a brief fictional exchange between invented characters in a specific situation.
5. Add only the minimum action or silence needed to expose what the speakers do not state.
6. Read the result aloud and revise any line that sounds written rather than spoken or natural but purposeless.

## Success Check
- The transcription preserves fragments, interruptions, repetitions, and abandoned sentences rather than tidying them as it records.
- Phrases are marked for revealing relation, attitude, desire, or rhythm, not for being quotable.
- Greetings, filler, and repeated information are cut, including lines the writer enjoyed.
- The invented exchange has a situation, so recombined moments carry pressure instead of sitting as a collage.
- Added action or silence is the minimum needed to expose what the speakers do not state.
- The reading aloud rejects lines that sound written, and equally lines that sound natural while doing nothing.

## Common Failures
- Correcting the initial transcription into grammatical sentences before studying it.
- Preserving a colorful line that performs no job in the invented scene.
- Copying an identifiable conversation instead of transforming its verbal material.
- Removing every fragment and repetition until all spoken texture disappears.
- Treating realism as permission to keep dull routine.

## Notes
Close listening trains the ear to notice that real speech is broken, repetitive, relational, and rhythmically odd. The second half of the exercise trains the equally important act of refusal: most overheard language is raw material to sift, not dialogue to preserve.
