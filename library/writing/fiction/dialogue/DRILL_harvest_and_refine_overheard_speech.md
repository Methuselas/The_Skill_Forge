# Harvest and Refine Overheard Speech

## Practice Task
Observe a short stretch of non-sensitive public conversation, analyze its spoken texture, and use those abstract features to create an original fictional exchange that moves.

## Target Skill
Hear the difference between natural spoken rhythm and the purposeful selection required by fictional dialogue.

## Setup
Choose a public place where ordinary conversation is audible without defeating a reasonable expectation of privacy. Do not collect sensitive content, record people without permission, or retain names and identifying details. The practice target is speech behavior, not the speakers' identities or distinctive expression.

## Instructions
1. Make brief anonymized notes about fragments, interruptions, repetitions, abandoned sentences, turn length, pace, and topic shifts. Stop when the content becomes private, sensitive, or identifying.
2. Mark abstract features that reveal relation, attitude, desire, or an unexpected rhythm. Do not select a line merely because its exact wording is memorable.
3. Cross out greetings, filler, and repeated information that adds no new pressure.
4. Write wholly new lines that reproduce selected rhythms, interruptions, evasions, or turn-taking behavior in a brief exchange between invented characters in a specific situation.
5. Add only the minimum action or silence needed to expose what the speakers do not state.
6. Read the result aloud and revise any line that sounds written rather than spoken or natural but purposeless.

## Success Check
- The notes preserve fragments, interruptions, repetitions, abandoned sentences, and turn behavior without retaining sensitive or identifying content.
- Features are marked for revealing relation, attitude, desire, or rhythm, not because an exact phrase is quotable.
- Greetings, filler, and repeated information are cut, including lines the writer enjoyed.
- The invented exchange has a situation, so recombined moments carry pressure instead of sitting as a collage.
- Added action or silence is the minimum needed to expose what the speakers do not state.
- The reading aloud rejects lines that sound written, and equally lines that sound natural while doing nothing.
- The invented exchange contains no copied distinctive line and does not use accent, age, class, region, or background as a substitute for individual voice.

## Common Failures
- Correcting the initial transcription into grammatical sentences before studying it.
- Preserving a colorful line that performs no job in the invented scene.
- Copying an identifiable conversation instead of transforming its verbal material.
- Preserving a stranger's distinctive phrase or sensitive disclosure because it seems dramatically useful.
- Turning an observed accent or community marker into a stock identity label.
- Removing every fragment and repetition until all spoken texture disappears.
- Treating realism as permission to keep dull routine.

## Notes
Close listening trains the ear to notice that real speech is broken, repetitive, relational, and rhythmically odd. The second half trains both selection and transformation: the writer carries forward an abstract behavior of speech while inventing the actual expression. Public audibility is not blanket permission to preserve another person's identity, private matter, or distinctive language.
