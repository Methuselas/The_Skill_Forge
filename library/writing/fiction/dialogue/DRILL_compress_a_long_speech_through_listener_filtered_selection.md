# Compress a Long Speech through Listener-Filtered Selection

## Practice Task
Draft one extended fictional speech as reported dialogue, direct dialogue, and a selective blend, then choose the version whose speaker presence and listener filtering best produce the required scene change.

## Target Skill
Control the information density, immediacy, voice, and reception of a long speech without reproducing a transcript or using listener activity as decoration.

## Setup
Choose a speech, account, explanation, briefing, interview answer, presentation, or monologue that must change knowledge, power, commitment, relationship, interpretation, or available action. State the speaker's purpose, the listener's present knowledge and agenda, and what must be different when the speech ends.

## Instructions
1. Divide the complete content into information units. Mark each as already known, necessary now, deferrable, inferable, disputed, or irrelevant to the required scene change.
2. Mark where exact wording matters because of voice, timing, promise, threat, evasion, ambiguity, emotional force, or later recall. Everything else remains eligible for report, compression, or omission.
3. Draft a reported-only version of no more than 200 words. Let the viewpoint select, order, paraphrase, judge, misunderstand, or omit material according to the listener's access rather than presenting neutral minutes.
4. Draft a direct-only version of no more than 500 words. Give the speaker a social purpose and movement through claims, examples, turns, concessions, concealments, or decisions rather than one undifferentiated block.
5. Draft a blended version of no more than 300 words. Use report to cross procedural or repetitive material, enter direct speech where exact language changes force, and leave quotation after its consequence registers.
6. In every version, make the listener do at least two consequential things: question, interrupt, mishear, infer, resist, remember, observe, act, withdraw, or revise a decision. Remove reactions that merely announce an emotion while leaving reception unchanged.
7. Compare the three versions for clarity, pace, voice, information retention, viewpoint character, and scene consequence. Do not favor the blend by default; select the form whose costs serve the larger story, and record the effect that decided it. Keep the two you did not select rather than deleting them, since the reason only holds against something to compare it with.
8. Restore only material whose absence creates a causal, emotional, or orientation gap. Confirm that no retained quotation exists merely to add color and no environmental detail or prop remains without changing delivery, interpretation, or action.

## Success Check
- Necessary, deferrable, disputed, and irrelevant information units are distinguished before drafting.
- Exact quotation is reserved for wording whose voice, timing, ambiguity, conflict, or later consequence matters.
- The reported version carries a discernible viewpoint filter rather than neutral summary.
- The direct version develops through verbal action instead of reproducing a complete transcript.
- Listener behavior changes access, interpretation, relation, decision, or consequence in every version.
- The selected treatment is defended by effect, and both rejected treatments remain available for comparison.

## Common Failures
- Quoting an isolated phrase merely to make summary livelier.
- Reporting the crucial turn because writing the speaker's voice is difficult.
- Preserving every point from a realistic briefing, interview, or presentation.
- Adding questions, emotion, thoughts, props, or room description that change nothing.
- Splitting exposition among extra speakers whose agendas and information do not differ.
- Making the listener inattentive only to hide information the reader needs fairly.
- Assuming the blended version is automatically more sophisticated.

## Notes
Extended speech becomes a scene when delivery and reception act upon each other. Direct wording supplies presence; report supplies compression and a narrator's selective frame; a blend changes distance at chosen pressure points. The listener is not a device for breaking up paragraphs. Attention, resistance, questions, and action matter only when they change which information arrives, what it means, or what someone can do next.

The variant `writing_fiction_variant_stage_briefing_interview_or_presentation_as_active_exchange` treats the event's format as a power structure. Define who controls questions, pacing, evidence, visual aids, interruption, and exit; summarize routine procedure and directly render only the moments when that structure changes delivery, credibility, interpretation, or consequence.
