# Choose Direct or Reported Dialogue by Effect

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a draft must decide whether readers hear a character's exact words or receive them through a narrator's account
**THEN** use direct dialogue for immediate vocal presence and reported dialogue for compression, distance, or a revealing filter imposed by the narrator

## Do
- Quote the speech when timing, tone, verbal conflict, or the experience of hearing the character carries the scene's force.
- Report a conversation when its outcome matters more than its wording or when the narrator's summary itself reveals detachment, bias, or control.
- Blend report and quotation when extended speech needs compression but selected wording must preserve voice, timing, ambiguity, conflict, or a later consequence; change distance at the pressure point rather than at a fixed interval.
- Let reported speech preserve character through the reporting consciousness's diction, emphasis, judgment, misunderstanding, and omission instead of flattening the account into neutral minutes.
- Compare both versions of a pivotal line to identify whether voice or mediation creates the stronger effect.
- Preserve the same causal information when testing the alternatives so the comparison isolates distance rather than content.

## Don't
- Report the climactic exchange merely to avoid formatting or inventing distinct voices.
- Quote a routine conversation at full length when a sentence of summary would preserve everything consequential.
- Assume reported speech is neutral; the reporting character selects and frames what another person said.
- Mix direct and reported forms so loosely that readers cannot tell whether words are exact or paraphrased.
- Insert a colorful direct phrase merely to enliven summary when its exact wording changes no inference, relation, or action.

## Checklist
- The chosen form serves a named need for immediacy, compression, distance, or characterization.
- Exact wording earns quotation through tone, rhythm, conflict, or revelation.
- Summary carries a discernible narrator filter when that filter matters.
- In a blended passage, each transition between report and quotation performs a named change in distance, voice, timing, or consequence.
- The reader can distinguish spoken words from paraphrase.
- Switching forms would measurably change the scene's force.

## Notes
Direct speech increases impact because readers encounter the speaker's language without summary standing between them. Reporting reduces that impact, but reduced immediacy can be the point: a narrator who habitually paraphrases others may expose indifference or self-absorption through the omission of their voices.
