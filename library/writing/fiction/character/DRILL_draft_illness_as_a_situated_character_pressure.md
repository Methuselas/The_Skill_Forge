# Draft Illness as a Situated Character Pressure

## Practice Task
Research one illness, injury, diagnostic process, or acute bodily stress, then draft an ordinary disruption, an encounter with care or diagnosis, and a relational decision without reducing the character to the condition.

## Target Skill
Make bodily stress change capacity, attention, access, privacy, and available action while preserving the character's existing desires, competencies, relationships, and agency.

## Setup
Choose a character with a governing desire and relationship that exist independently of the condition. Define the illness, injury, diagnostic uncertainty, or acute state narrowly. When it falls outside your experience, prepare questions about symptoms, variability, course, treatment, care settings, access, and ordinary life, then consult varied credible perspectives and appropriate medical sources.

## Instructions
1. Record the character's baseline routines, capabilities, obligations, coping habits, knowledge, resources, and current story goal before the bodily pressure begins.
2. Map the condition's researched effects on energy, pain, cognition, sensation, movement, sleep, privacy, communication, and risk. Mark variability and uncertainty rather than choosing every available symptom.
3. Map external conditions: available care, cost, work and family obligations, transportation, institutional rules, stigma, misinformation, support, and who controls consequential information.
4. Draft an ordinary disruption in which one selected effect changes a task, choice, or relationship. Do not announce a personality conclusion from the response.
5. Draft an encounter with care, testing, or diagnosis. Make the institution, information route, uncertainty, and participant knowledge constrain what can happen rather than using a generic clinical backdrop.
6. Draft a relational decision: disclosure, concealment, request, refusal, delegation, boundary, or altered commitment. Preserve the other person's motives and let the decision create a consequence beyond sympathy.
7. Compare behavior across the three scenes. Attribute differences to symptoms, fear, fatigue, medication, information, access, witnesses, and stakes before treating any response as a stable trait.
8. Audit the sequence for medical invention, inevitability, cure or decline shortcuts, inspiration, punishment, pity, and a condition that exists only to deepen another character. Revise until the person remains active beyond the diagnosis.

## Success Check
- The condition is narrow enough to research, and uncertainty and variation remain visible.
- Baseline desires, competencies, relationships, and obligations exist before illness becomes pressure.
- All three scenes are drafted and each changes a concrete choice, access condition, relationship, or consequence.
- Institutional and material conditions affect the care or diagnosis scene rather than serving as scenery.
- Acute behavior is attributed to situated pressures before any stable-character inference is made.
- The relational decision changes the story without making pity, tragedy, inspiration, cure, or revelation the condition's only function.

## Common Failures
- Combining unrelated symptoms into a dramatic but medically incoherent condition.
- Treating one researched account as representative of everyone with the diagnosis.
- Using pain, fear, exhaustion, or medication effects as proof of the character's essential self.
- Making the hospital or clinic frictionless, placeless, and free of institutional rules.
- Giving the ill character no goal except recovery or no relationship except receiving care.
- Resolving the condition only to reward virtue, punish vice, or complete another person's growth.

## Notes
Bodily stress changes what a person can do in a particular moment, but constraint is not essence. The same character may respond differently as symptoms, information, privacy, support, money, obligations, and available choices change. Research establishes plausible conditions; characterization comes from the person's situated decisions within them.
