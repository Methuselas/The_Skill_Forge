# Render One Character through Five Portrayal Channels

## Practice Task
Portray one character through interpretation, appearance, action, thought, and speech in a single bounded scene, then cut or redistribute redundant channels until each retained one performs a distinct job.

## Target Skill
Choose how readers learn each piece of character information instead of defaulting to exposition, dialogue, interiority, or a mechanical showing-and-telling formula.

## Setup
Choose a scene in which one character wants something, encounters resistance, and makes or avoids a consequential choice. Prepare five labels: interpretation, appearance, action, thought, and speech.

## Instructions
1. Under each label, draft one possible piece of evidence about the character that the scene could credibly deliver through that channel.
2. Make interpretation compress history, motive, or judgment that enactment would handle inefficiently; keep the interpreting authority and possible bias legible.
3. Put appearance into movement, attention, or social response so the body and clothing do more than complete a static portrait.
4. Choose an action that changes the situation or reveals a habitual response under pressure.
5. Use thought for private knowledge, contradiction, or misreading unavailable from the outside; keep it within the chosen viewpoint.
6. Give speech a listener and a social purpose. Let wording reveal what the character seeks, performs, evades, or risks in that relation.
7. Assemble a short scene using all five candidates once as a diagnostic constraint. Underline repeated information and explanations that arrive after evidence has made them inferable.
8. Remove, compress, or relocate any channel that performs no unique job. The final scene may use fewer than five when its effect is stronger without the quota.

## Success Check
- All five channels deliver evidence the scene could credibly carry, drafted before any is cut.
- Interpretation compresses history, motive, or judgment that enactment would handle inefficiently, and the interpreting authority stays legible along with its possible bias.
- Appearance arrives through movement, attention, or social response rather than completing a static portrait.
- The action changes the situation or reveals a habitual response under pressure, rather than illustrating a trait already given.
- Channels delivering the same evidence twice are cut or redistributed, and at least one is actually cut. A scene that keeps all five has not been tested.
- Each retained channel does a job the others cannot, stated for each rather than assumed from its presence.

## Common Failures
- Treating the five labels as a permanent formula for every scene.
- Repeating one trait through description, thought, action, and dialogue.
- Using interpretation to announce the correct judgment before readers encounter evidence.
- Reporting a thought the viewpoint cannot access.
- Adding physical business or speech that decorates the scene without changing inference or pressure.

## Notes
The five-channel draft is a diagnostic overproduction pass. It reveals both neglected resources and redundancies: exposition may compress years efficiently, appearance may become social action, thought may expose contradiction, and silence may outperform a spoken line. Revision completes the exercise by selecting the smallest combination that gives readers the intended evidence.
