# Inhabit Unsympathetic Characters from Inside

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a character's conduct invites the writer to condemn, mock, or flatten that person from the outside
**THEN** understand the need, wound, value, or self-justification that makes the conduct defensible to the character without requiring the story to excuse it

## Do
- State the character's case in language that person could sincerely believe rather than in the prosecutor's version of it.
- Find a moment of tenderness, fear, competence, restraint, or vulnerability that complicates the dominant judgment.
- Hint at the conditions that shaped a defect when those conditions help the reader understand present action.
- Leave room for redemption or refusal through choices, not through an authorial declaration that the person is secretly good.

## Don't
- Confuse understanding a motive with endorsing its consequences.
- Give an antagonist only appetites that serve the protagonist's plot.
- Humanize through a sentimental detail that never affects perception or choice.
- Explain every cruelty with a complete origin story when a charged glimpse would suffice.

## Checklist
- The character could describe the disputed action without adopting the writer's contempt.
- At least one detail resists the simplest moral label without canceling harm.
- Past influence, when included, changes how present behavior is understood.
- The story allows judgment to arise from action and consequence.
- The character remains a person even when no redemption occurs.

## Notes
Interior access creates dimensionality, not acquittal. The useful question is not whether a difficult person deserves affection, but what arrangement of desire and belief lets that person continue acting as they do. That answer gives scenes motives stronger than villainous convenience.
