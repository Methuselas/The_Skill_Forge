# Build a Hidden Character Profile

## Practice Task
Spend fifteen to twenty minutes making a private profile for one central character, then select only the details that can exert pressure on the current story.

## Target Skill
Develop more character knowledge than the draft states while revealing only what action and reader orientation require.

## Setup
Choose a central character from a planned or incomplete fiction draft. Have the story's immediate problem or situation available.

## Instructions
1. Write quickly about birthplace, age, appearance, work, commute or daily routine, tastes, attachments, recurring dream or fear, chief desire, and one contradiction.
2. Add one characteristic gesture, one speech habit, and one category of detail the character reliably notices.
3. Mark the three profile facts most likely to alter a decision under the story's present pressure.
4. Draft a short scene that reveals at least two marked facts through action, speech, or attention without stating the profile.
5. Cut any background sentence the scene does not need for orientation, causality, or emotional force.

## Success Check
- The profile is written quickly and privately, covering the listed ground including the contradiction.
- The three facts most likely to alter a decision under present pressure are marked before the scene is drafted.
- The scene reveals at least two marked facts through action, speech, or attention, with the profile never stated.
- Background sentences the scene does not need for orientation, causality, or emotion are cut, including ones the writer enjoyed producing.
- What goes unused stays in the profile rather than being worked into the scene to justify the time spent on it.
- The scene is legible to a reader who has not seen the profile, which is the only test of whether the pressure transferred.

## Common Failures
- Treating the prompts as a questionnaire whose every answer must enter the draft.
- Generating colorful preferences that cannot affect any choice.
- Converting profile notes into an introductory paragraph.
- Choosing observations the character lacks the background to make.
- Hiding facts the reader needs to understand the immediate scene.

## Notes
The exercise separates design knowledge from disclosure. A submerged biography gives the writer reasons for choices and verbal texture, but its value disappears when the draft displays the whole worksheet. Repeat with another central character when motives feel interchangeable.

`writing_fiction_variant_pressure_hidden_profile_through_unsent_document` inserts a private voice experiment between profile and scene. Choose whether the character writes only for the self or toward a specific person, then establish the medium, privacy expectation, relationship, immediate pressure, risk, and purpose. A diary may still rehearse, rationalize, omit, perform for an imagined reader, or misread the self; a letter or message changes further according to what its recipient knows and what the writer wants that person to believe or do. Let diction, syntax, rhythm, repetition, selection, accusation, evasion, formality, attention, judgment, omission, and self-justification reveal a situational voice rather than an authentic essence.

When private and relational voices blur together, write both a self-directed entry and an unsent message about the same pressure. Mark what changes with the addressee and what remains characteristic. Treat every discovery as a hypothesis, then draft the ordinary scene and retain only language, perception, speech, relation, or conduct that the live situation supports. The document need not appear in the story, and its best sentences do not earn transfer merely because they were productive to write.

`writing_fiction_variant_hot_seat_character_with_responsive_questions` makes character design conversational and adaptive. Begin with a specific interviewer relationship or neutral rehearsal frame, ask about a live desire or problem, and follow language that evades, contradicts, boasts, minimizes, or unexpectedly specifies. Answer in the character's available diction without forcing immediate consistency. Afterward, separate useful discoveries from improvisational noise and retain only answers that can alter motive, relation, speech, or choice in the story. The interview generates hypotheses; the drafted behavior decides which become character facts.

`writing_fiction_variant_reconstruct_education_and_work_through_private_resume` uses a familiar document to expose both capability and self-presentation. List schooling, qualifications, every consequential job including temporary work, skills acquired, interests, and the qualities the character would advertise. Then ask what is omitted, inflated, regretted, obsolete, or contradicted by conduct. Transfer only consequences into the story: what the character notices, can perform, resents, conceals, expects from institutions, or believes work has proved about the self.
