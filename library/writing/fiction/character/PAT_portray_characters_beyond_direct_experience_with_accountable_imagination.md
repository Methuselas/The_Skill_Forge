# Portray Characters Beyond Direct Experience with Accountable Imagination

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a character's identity, body, community, work, history, or social position includes consequential experience the writer does not share
**THEN** identify the material gaps, research specific conditions and perspectives, build the person through desire and contradiction, and test every identity-linked choice against stereotype and power rather than relying on confidence or presumed universality
**ELSE** keep testing familiar experience for assumptions that proximity has made invisible

## Do
- Separate broadly shareable motives such as belonging, ambition, fear, or loyalty from the particular conditions that determine how those motives can be expressed.
- Turn consequential unknowns about access, risk, custom, language, work, embodiment, history, and institutional power into bounded research questions.
- Consult varied, credible perspectives from people with relevant knowledge instead of treating one account as representative of a whole group.
- Give the character relationships, competencies, defects, pleasures, and private priorities that are not reducible to the axis of difference.
- Let identity matter where it changes how the character is perceived, what choices are available, or what a situation costs; let it recede where another pressure governs the moment.
- Revise when informed readers identify a false note, while distinguishing specific craft evidence from demands that one character represent everyone.

## Don't
- Treat empathy, imaginative boldness, or good intentions as substitutes for knowledge.
- Change demographic labels while preserving assumptions drawn entirely from the writer's own circumstances.
- Use accent, clothing, food, disability, trauma, or oppression as detachable proof of authenticity.
- Make a marginalized character carry only suffering, instruction, moral purity, or symbolic importance for another character.
- Assume that research grants ownership of another person's testimony or removes the need for selective invention.
- Equate unlike experiences merely because they share an abstract emotion or theme.

## Checklist
- The writer can name what required research and how the findings changed the character or scene.
- The character wants and does things not dictated solely by an identity category.
- Social and material conditions affect choices without mechanically determining personality.
- Speech, behavior, and bodily detail arise from this individual in this context rather than from a stock type.
- Harm, access, and power remain visible where the story makes them consequential.
- Removing the identity label would not erase the character's particular desire, contradiction, and agency.

## Notes
Imagination permits fiction to cross the boundary of the writer's biography, but permission is not competence. Accountable portrayal combines invention with inquiry: the writer learns enough to understand constraints and possibilities, then creates a particular person rather than a demographic demonstration. Research can correct ignorance; it cannot make one character definitive of a group or turn borrowed testimony into decoration.
