# Propagate One Counterfactual Change through a Fictional Life

## Practice Task
Change one consequential fact in a fictional character's starting material, trace the resulting chain of effects, and draft a scene that could not occur under the original conditions.

## Target Skill
Transform lived or factual character material by making invention alter relationships, opportunities, pressures, and choices rather than merely disguising the model.

## Setup
Choose a character seed under a fiction truth contract. Record five plain facts about the person's circumstances, relationships, history, work, or social position without yet interpreting them.

## Instructions
1. For each fact, write one counterfactual that changes a meaningful condition rather than only a name or surface trait.
2. Select the change with the strongest credible pressure. State what becomes newly possible, newly difficult, or newly expected because of it.
3. Trace at least three consequences in order: an immediate material effect, a changed relationship or social response, and a later choice or coping behavior.
4. Check the chain for unsupported stereotype. Replace assumed behavior with a consequence grounded in the character's particular circumstances, desires, and available options.
5. Revise any other seed fact that the chosen change makes inconsistent; do not preserve the original biography when its causal conditions no longer exist.
6. Draft a short scene in which the transformed character acts under one downstream pressure. Do not explain the counterfactual exercise inside the scene.
7. Compare the scene with the original seed. Mark the decisions, details, and relationships that now arise from the changed life rather than from cosmetic concealment.

## Success Check
- Each counterfactual alters a meaningful condition rather than a name or a surface trait.
- The selected change names what becomes newly possible, newly difficult, or newly expected, so the pressure is stated before it is traced.
- Three consequences follow in order, and each is caused by the one before it rather than merely listed after it.
- The chain is audited for unsupported stereotype, and at least one assumed behaviour is replaced by a consequence grounded in this character's circumstances, desires, and available options.
- The drafted scene could not occur under the original conditions, and what makes it impossible is identifiable.
- The change earns its place by what it opened, not by being the most dramatic candidate available.

## Common Failures
- Changing several facts at once so no consequence can be traced clearly.
- Treating identity categories as costumes while leaving access, expectations, and relationships untouched.
- Preserving favorite incidents even after the new premise removes their cause.
- Equating unlike experiences because they share an abstract theme.
- Producing a profile of possibilities but never testing one through action.

## Notes
A counterfactual becomes generative when it propagates. One changed condition can alter how others respond, which resources are available, what the character fears or expects, and which later decisions become plausible. Tracing that chain creates distance from lived material while preserving the irregular specificity that made the seed useful.
