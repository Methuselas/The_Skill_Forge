# Treat Character Bodies as Lived Conditions

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a character's body matters to perception, conduct, access, relationship, or consequence
**THEN** distinguish physical facts from environmental interaction, social treatment, self-interpretation, adaptation, and change so embodiment shapes a particular life without becoming a personality shortcut
**ELSE** omit bodily inventory that performs no story or orientation function

## Do
- Establish only the physical facts the work needs, including natural variation, current condition, capability, pain, energy, sensory access, presentation, and change over time.
- Test how the body interacts with actual environments, objects, clothing, tools, distances, tasks, and institutional arrangements.
- Separate what other people perceive or assume from what the character experiences and from what the narrative can establish as fact.
- Give the character an individual relation to the body that may include comfort, indifference, pride, pleasure, frustration, adaptation, ambivalence, or changing interpretation.
- Let repeated experience produce skills, habits, accommodations, avoidance, presentation choices, or strategies rather than treating the body as a static label.
- Research consequential bodily experience outside the writer's knowledge and preserve variation among people who share a condition.
- Track changes caused by age, health, injury, treatment, work, environment, or choice when those changes affect the story.

## Don't
- Infer morality, intelligence, temperament, class, sexuality, competence, or destiny from facial structure, eye color, teeth, posture, body size, disability, or another physical feature.
- Treat social prejudice as proof that the stereotype is true of the character.
- Label bodily difference as a flaw when the actual dramatic defect lies in conduct, judgment, desire, or contradiction.
- Bundle ancestry, ethnicity, gender, age, or disability into a stock package of appearance and behavior.
- Build an exhaustive body inventory whose details never affect perception, action, relation, access, or consequence.
- Make one response to embodiment representative of everyone with a similar body.

## Checklist
- Physical fact, environmental interaction, social interpretation, and self-interpretation remain distinguishable.
- At least one bodily condition changes a concrete action, access condition, relationship, or consequence.
- Adaptations and capabilities arise from this person's repeated circumstances rather than a generic type.
- Appearance-based judgments remain attributable to an observer and can be confirmed, complicated, or corrected by later evidence.
- Consequential unfamiliar experience has been researched through varied credible perspectives.
- Every retained bodily detail performs orientation, characterization, pressure, continuity, or causal work.

## Notes
Embodiment is a continuing relation among a body, a person, other people, and a material world. The same physical fact can create different consequences under different architecture, work, resources, relationships, histories, and self-understandings. Character emerges from how a particular person perceives, adapts, chooses, and is treated—not from a body functioning as a moral diagram.
