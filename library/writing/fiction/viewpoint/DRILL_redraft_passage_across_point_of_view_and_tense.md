# Redraft a Passage across Point of View and Tense

## Practice Task
Rewrite one consequential passage through several narrative positions and temporal stances, then select by demonstrated effect rather than familiarity.

## Target Skill
Recognize how viewpoint and tense change access, distance, authority, voice, and the information a passage can credibly reveal.

## Setup
Choose a passage of 250 to 500 words containing an event, at least two characters, one withheld fact, and a change in knowledge or relation. Keep an untouched copy.

## Instructions
1. Mark who currently tells, what that narrator knows, when the telling occurs, and what readers cannot access.
2. Redraft the complete passage in first person past without importing knowledge the speaker lacks.
3. Redraft it in third-person limited present through a different character, changing noticed details and interpretations as required.
4. Make a third version using one conspicuous option: first-person plural, second person, omniscient, or future tense.
5. Underline every fact, thought, or description that had to appear, disappear, or change when access moved.
6. Annotate each version for reader distance, narrator authority, urgency, irony, and the cost of maintaining the device.
7. Choose the version whose gains serve the story's central pressure, then revise it for consistent knowledge, tense, and voice.

## Success Check
- Each version changes more than pronouns and verb endings.
- Private knowledge remains within its established access boundary.
- The conspicuous version has a credible narrative reason and a named payoff.
- The selected tense governs hindsight, anticipation, and scene time consistently.
- The final choice can be defended by an effect another version cannot deliver as well.

## Common Failures
- Keeping identical details even though a different observer would notice or name the scene differently.
- Granting a new first-person narrator the original narrator's private knowledge.
- Assuming present tense automatically creates energy.
- Selecting the most unusual version because it appears inventive.
- Comparing isolated opening sentences instead of sustaining each choice through a complete event.

## Notes
Sustained redrafting exposes costs that a pronoun substitution cannot. A viewpoint may produce an arresting first line but fail when the passage needs inaccessible knowledge; an unusual tense may energize one moment while making summary or hindsight cumbersome. The exercise makes those tradeoffs visible before they govern an entire draft.
