# Design Unreliable Narration with Readable Evidence

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a narrator's bias, ignorance, self-deception, or fabrication should make the account uncertain
**THEN** let the narration reveal its teller while placing enough observable friction around the account for readers to infer where and why trust should weaken

## Do
- Establish what the narrator wants readers or the self to believe before introducing contradictions.
- Use mismatches among claims, behavior, remembered details, other voices, and material consequences as evidence.
- Calibrate the gap so readers can suspect a distortion without needing an external correction for every fact.
- Allow uncertainty to remain where deciding exactly what happened is less important than understanding the teller's need to shape it.

## Don't
- Withhold all counterevidence and expect the label unreliable to make confusion meaningful.
- Make every statement false; a narrator needs enough ordinary credibility for deviations to register.
- Explain the deception immediately after dramatizing evidence that already makes it inferable.
- Treat a continuity mistake, impossible access, or unstable tense as intentional unreliability after the fact.

## Checklist
- The narrator has a legible motive or limitation that can distort the account.
- At least two independent signals invite readers to reassess a consequential claim.
- The degree of uncertainty serves character, tension, or theme rather than hiding missing causality.
- Readers can distinguish the narrator's mistake from the story's own loss of control.
- The account remains compelling even if some facts cannot be finally settled.

## Notes
An unreliable voice creates a second reading task: follow the narrated event while also reading the narrator. The method can produce intimacy and complicity because readers are confined to a voice they must continually calibrate. Its success depends less on announcing deception than on making the pattern of selection, contradiction, and consequence available.

`writing_fiction_variant_use_character_resistance_as_counter_narrative` places counterevidence inside an active relationship. An opposing character can ridicule, refuse, reinterpret, or expose the narrator's account, causing readers to reconsider both the narrated claim and their allegiance. The resistance should remain motivated and contestable; a character who only announces the correct interpretation turns inference into explanation.
