# Design the First-Person Telling Situation

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a first-person fiction has a distinctive speaker but no clear conditions governing why, when, and to whom the account is told
**THEN** establish the narrator's role in the events, intended listener, temporal position, telling pressure, and control over disclosure before stabilizing the voice

## Do
- Decide whether the narrator drives the central action or witnesses another person's story, then give every indispensable event a plausible route into that role.
- For a peripheral witness, route offstage events through named testimony, records, situated inference, or openly signaled reconstruction, and preserve what remains unknown rather than letting hindsight masquerade as access.
- Name the listener the narrator imagines and let that relationship determine what needs explanation, what can remain implicit, and what the narrator wants the telling to accomplish.
- Separate event time from telling time; state how much has changed, what hindsight now permits, and how the later narrator regards the earlier self.
- Use remembered uncertainty selectively when the struggle to recover an event reveals the narrator's present need or strengthens rather than dissolves reader trust.
- Pace revelation according to what the narrator already knows and is willing to disclose, not according to suspense conventions imported without a speakerly motive.

## Don't
- Treat every fictional I as the protagonist when a witness has better access to the central person's impact or mystery.
- Call a peripheral participant an objective outsider or treat a later report as direct knowledge of what really happened.
- Call a journal private while filling it with background the writer supplies only for strangers.
- Address a named listener in the opening and then let that relationship disappear from diction, candor, and selection.
- Give a distant narrator exact conversations and sensations by default while ignoring the passage of time.
- Let retrospective knowledge leak into the acting self as though the outcome were already understood.

## Checklist
- The narrator's participant or witness role accounts for every significant event being told.
- Every consequential offstage claim is attributable to testimony, a record, inference, or acknowledged reconstruction, and unsupported detail remains absent.
- The addressee changes at least two of explanation, tone, withheld material, directness, or persuasive strategy.
- The elapsed time establishes a legible amount of hindsight and emotional distance.
- Memory gaps are purposeful and do not obscure facts the story promises to settle.
- Revelation order follows the narrator's knowledge and motive rather than arbitrary concealment.
- The speaking situation remains credible beyond the opening paragraph.

## Notes
First person is not only a grammatical position; it is an act of communication performed under imagined conditions. The same event becomes a different story when it is privately recorded, aimed at one consequential listener, or shaped for a general audience after years of reflection.

`writing_fiction_variant_use_private_self_communion` removes an outside audience and therefore permits shorthand, omission, and candor that public narration may not. Use it only when the prose consistently behaves as something the narrator writes or thinks for the self.

`writing_fiction_variant_use_directed_first_person_address` makes a listener part of the story's pressure. A letter, confession, appeal, or spoken account earns this form when the recipient changes what the narrator dares to say and how the account tries to act upon that person.

`writing_fiction_variant_use_detached_retrospective_account` uses elapsed time as an instrument of judgment and construction. It can openly foreshadow outcomes or reinterpret an earlier self, but the distance must remain distinct from the consciousness that originally moved through the event.

`writing_fiction_variant_admit_fallible_recollection` replaces implausible total recall with selective uncertainty. A narrator who distinguishes remembered fragments from conjecture may become more credible, provided the uncertainty reveals a mind at work rather than making every event unusably vague.
