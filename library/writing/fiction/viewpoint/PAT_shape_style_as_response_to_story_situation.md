# Shape Style as a Response to Story Situation

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a fiction passage has a generic verbal surface or maintains one tone despite changing narrative pressures
**THEN** arrange words, sentences, and paragraphs so diction, rhythm, density, and clarity respond to the narrator, subject, pace, and immediate situation

## Do
- Let sentence length and arrangement register whether the moment observes, rushes, hesitates, catalogs, argues, or withholds.
- Prefer exact nouns and active verbs when modifiers or explanatory phrases only approximate the action.
- Cut verbal clutter that delays an effect without adding voice, rhythm, implication, or necessary orientation.
- Change the texture when the story situation changes, while preserving enough recurring habits for the governing voice to remain recognizable.
- Protect deliberate ambiguity by making the surrounding syntax and details exact enough for uncertainty to feel intentional.

## Don't
- Apply a blanket ban to adjectives, adverbs, long sentences, fragments, or any other class of useful language.
- Mistake decorative rarity for a voice shaped by the event.
- Make every sentence equally terse in the name of economy.
- Preserve accidental confusion because uncertainty is sometimes artistically valuable.

## Checklist
- Word choice belongs to this narrator and moment rather than to a generic literary register.
- Sentence and paragraph movement support the scene's pace and emotional pressure.
- Every retained modifier or expansion adds a distinguishable function.
- A tonal shift follows a change in situation and does not sound like an unmotivated new narrator.
- Readers can locate what is uncertain even when they cannot settle it.

## Notes
Style is not an ornamental layer added after content. Placement at the word, sentence, and paragraph levels determines tone and controls how the story reaches the reader. Economy therefore means removing language without function, not enforcing uniform minimalism. Strong verbs and precise words often concentrate a passage, but a modifier, repetition, or elaborate sentence remains justified when it carries voice, rhythm, relation, or perception.
