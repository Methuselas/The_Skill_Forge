# Render Stream of Consciousness in Controlled Bursts

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a fictional character's acute mental state needs to feel associative, recursive, or disordered rather than calmly narrated
**THEN** use a bounded passage of linked sensations, memories, words, and impulses whose movement follows that consciousness while retaining enough recurrence and external orientation for readers to follow

## Do
- Begin from a concrete pressure such as intoxication, panic, trauma, obsession, rage, or a repetitive physical task that can keep the mental movement charged.
- Let one perception trigger the next through sound, image, bodily sensation, verbal echo, memory, or contradiction rather than through orderly exposition.
- Repeat an object, phrase, question, or action as an orienting thread while associations move away from and return to it.
- Adjust punctuation, fragments, sentence length, and correction to the mind's pace; retain conventional marks wherever they help readers hear the movement without taming it.
- Reconnect the passage to an action, decision, or changed perception before private association stalls the surrounding event.

## Don't
- Remove punctuation and spaces as though visual difficulty alone reproduces thought.
- Assemble random images with no pressure, habit, fear, desire, or sensory link belonging to the character.
- Sustain unfiltered association until narrative time and external stakes disappear without a deliberate reason.
- Insert facts, vocabulary, or interpretations unavailable to the active consciousness merely because the form appears unrestricted.
- Explain every association afterward; preserve legible recurrence so the passage can carry its own coherence.

## Checklist
- A specific immediate pressure drives the mental movement.
- Associations can be traced through sensation, language, memory, or motive even when they are not logical arguments.
- At least one recurring element keeps the reader oriented.
- Syntax and punctuation change with the character's state rather than performing generic experimentation.
- The passage remains inside the established knowledge boundary.
- Its ending returns energy or altered meaning to the surrounding action.

## Notes
Consciousness rarely arrives as a tidy report, but complete randomness is no more psychologically convincing than complete order. A useful burst preserves the character's private logic: one detail catches on another, a phrase returns with a changed charge, or present sensation opens a remembered scene.

The method is especially effective when ordinary narrative syntax would falsely calm an experience. Its cost is pace and orientation. Short deployment can intensify a rant, intoxicated interval, traumatic flash, obsessive ritual, or moment of panic; longer deployment needs an unusually strong recurring structure to keep the story from dissolving into undifferentiated mental flow.

The variant `writing_fiction_variant_sustain_extended_interior_monologue_through_governing_mental_movement` permits a longer passage when thought itself develops. State the governing question or pressure, track returns that alter its meaning, and require the ending to produce a changed interpretation, commitment, decision, or available action. External action can provide resistance or orientation, but it is not a quota; retain it only when the character's encounter with the world redirects the interior sequence.
