# Redraft One Opening from Full Genre Signal to Subtle Genre Pressure

## Practice Task
Draft an opening with an unmistakable genre contract, then revise the same material so fewer transformed cues preserve useful expectation without reducing the story to imitation.

## Target Skill
Control genre expectations through selected and transformed opening cues.

## Setup
Choose a story situation and a genre whose central reader promise you can state. List five recognizable cues available through situation, setting, character role, viewpoint, diction, imagery, information, or pace.

## Instructions
1. State the anticipated reader experience in one sentence without relying only on the genre label.
2. Draft an opening of 150 to 250 words using at least four listed cues strongly enough that the genre is unmistakable.
3. Mark each cue and name the expectation it creates. Cross out any cue that merely repeats a promise another cue already establishes.
4. Redraft the same event at a similar length using only two genre cues. Transform at least one through the story's particular character, location, relation, or social context.
5. Add one detail that belongs to the primary story but resists the genre's easiest stock direction.
6. Compare both versions for clarity of promise, individuality, suspense, tonal control, and cliché. Keep the version or blend whose contract is legible without predetermining every turn, and state what a reader would still anticipate from the reduced signalling. A promise you cannot restate has probably gone out with the cues.
7. Write the next three sentences and confirm that the selected signals create paths the story can actually continue.

## Success Check
- The anticipated reader experience is stated without leaning on the genre label, since the label is the thing under test.
- The first opening uses at least four cues strongly enough that the genre is unmistakable rather than gestured at.
- Each cue is marked with the expectation it creates, and cues repeating a promise another already makes are crossed out.
- The redraft covers the same event at similar length using two cues, so what is compared is signalling rather than material.
- At least one surviving cue is transformed through this story's particular character, location, relation, or social world.
- The reduced version keeps useful expectation without becoming imitation, and what a reader would still anticipate is stated.

## Common Failures
- Changing the event between versions so cue density cannot be compared.
- Using costume, weather, weapons, or slang as genre shorthand with no relation to character or consequence.
- Making the subtle version so neutral that it promises no identifiable experience.
- Treating reader recognition as proof that every convention must be fulfilled literally.
- Adding a contradictory detail solely to appear original.

## Notes
The first pass deliberately overstates the contract so its component signals become visible. The second pass trains selection: genre remains available through a few coordinated choices while particular human pressure keeps the opening from becoming a catalogue of conventions. Continuing beyond the opening tests whether the promise is structural rather than cosmetic.
