# Turn Concrete Stimuli into Story Pressure

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a fiction session has no active premise but an observed object, image, anecdote, person, or place carries an unusual specific detail
**THEN** question and recombine the stimulus until it belongs to a character whose desire, history, or immediate trouble can generate consequential action

## Do
- Begin with the detail that resists a generic explanation: damaged shoes kept past usefulness, paint on a formal coat, a torn cover, an interrupted device, or another visible irregularity.
- Ask who owns or notices it, why it has not been repaired or discarded, what happened before the mark appeared, and what becomes difficult now.
- Pair two people or fragments whose wants, histories, or assumptions are likely to collide.
- Let sensory qualities and physical limits affect action instead of treating the stimulus as a decorative symbol.
- Move from speculative questions into a scene as soon as one answer creates motive and resistance.

## Don't
- Stop at a clever object or image with no person under pressure.
- Force a predetermined theme onto every detail before asking what concrete situation it suggests.
- Preserve the real identities or circumstances attached to found material when fiction needs transformation.
- Keep browsing for a more surprising prompt after the current one has generated a viable conflict.

## Checklist
- The starting stimulus contains a precise feature that can affect interpretation or action.
- A character wants something that the resulting situation complicates.
- At least one causal question converts observation into history, choice, or consequence.
- Combined elements create friction rather than a random collage.
- The premise can enter a scene without explaining the entire chain that generated it.

## Notes
Concrete material gives invention resistance: a physical fact or peculiar pairing asks to be accounted for. The productive move is not to decode an object's universal meaning but to attach it to a particular person's need and circumstance. Once the stimulus generates pressure, it has completed its prompting job and the story may depart freely from its origin.
