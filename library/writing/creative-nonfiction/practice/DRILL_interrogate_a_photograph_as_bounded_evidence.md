# Interrogate a Photograph as Bounded Evidence

## Practice Task
Describe what a photograph visibly records, investigate its production and surrounding event, and draft from it without presenting plausible out-of-frame material as photographed fact.

## Target Skill
Turn a photograph into a bounded research and reconstruction prompt while preserving the status of every consequential claim beyond its visible frame.

## Setup
Choose a photograph of an actual person, place, or event that may be examined and portrayed without avoidable privacy or safety harm. Obtain the clearest available copy and record any attached caption, date, inscription, custodian, or account separately from what the image itself shows.

## Instructions
1. Describe only what is visible in the frame. Name people only when identity is independently established, and distinguish observable position, clothing, expression, setting, objects, light, damage, cropping, and image quality from interpretation.
2. List the questions the frame cannot answer: who made the image, why, when, what preceded and followed it, what lies beyond the crop, who is absent, whether the scene was posed, and how the image was selected or preserved.
3. Investigate the photograph's production and custody proportionately. Consult inscriptions, related images, records, locations, and people with relevant knowledge; record disagreement and unavailable evidence instead of resolving it by preference.
4. Classify every candidate detail as visible, documented, reported, inferred, imagined, disputed, or unknown. Treat a confident family caption or repeated story as a report until corroboration changes its status.
5. Draft a compact account of the photograph and its surrounding event. Make the basis of consequential out-of-frame material legible through attribution, qualification, explicit reconstruction, or acknowledged absence.
6. Mark every sentence a reader could reasonably assume the photograph proves. Correct sentences that transfer the image's visual authority to motive, chronology, relationship, or off-camera action it does not record.
7. Draft one alternative that centers the photograph's limit rather than filling it. Let a crop, missing person, uncertain date, damaged area, or conflicting account create the inquiry when that absence is consequential.
8. Choose or combine the versions only if the resulting account remains vivid without disguising probability, imagination, or irrecoverable uncertainty as evidence.

## Success Check
- Visible description contains no unsupported identity, motive, relationship, or event claim.
- Caption, custody, testimony, and later memory remain separate from the image itself.
- Research changes or qualifies at least one initial assumption when consequential evidence is available.
- Out-of-frame material retains its documented, reported, inferred, imagined, disputed, or unknown status.
- The prose does not borrow certainty from the photograph merely because the object is tangible.
- A reader can understand both what the image contributes and what it cannot establish.

## Common Failures
- Describing an interpretation of facial expression as an observable fact.
- Assuming a photograph is candid, correctly dated, representative, or complete without support.
- Treating the caption writer, custodian, or most confident witness as automatically authoritative.
- Inventing the photographer, occasion, dialogue, or next action so the image yields a finished scene.
- Ignoring cropping, posing, selection, preservation, and missing companion images.
- Smothering the draft in generic doubt instead of identifying the specific evidentiary boundary.

## Notes
A photograph is material evidence of a bounded visual record, not a transparent window onto an event. It can preserve arrangement, surfaces, and selected context while concealing production, sequence, motive, coercion, exclusion, and everything outside the frame. Its strongest life-writing use may be to corroborate a detail, expose a conflict, trigger research, or make an absence visible. Vivid reconstruction remains possible when the prose identifies which elements come from the image and which come from other evidence or imagination.
