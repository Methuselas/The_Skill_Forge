# Audition Fragmented, Connected, and Through-Line Orders

## Practice Task
Arrange the same bounded set of life-writing incidents as independent snapshots, a connected sequence, and a through-line structure, then choose by the inquiry each order makes possible.

## Target Skill
Select a life-writing structure by demonstrated movement and reader inference while keeping the underlying factual material constant.

## Setup
Choose three to five supportable incidents, observations, records, or remembered moments related to one life-writing inquiry. Reduce each to a locally intelligible unit of no more than 100 words, label uncertainty outside the prose, and preserve an untouched copy of every unit.

## Instructions
1. Record the factual status, time, place, principal action, and unresolved pressure of each unit. Do not invent connective events or motives to make the set cohere.
2. Build a snapshot order. Let each unit stand separately and use only adjacency, contrast, omission, and silence to create relations.
3. Build a connected order. Arrange the units by chronology or supportable consequence, adding only the minimum bridges readers need to relocate and understand the movement.
4. Identify at least two candidate through-lines, such as a place, object, relationship, repeated action, question, or condition. Reject a candidate that merely appears several times without changing significance.
5. Build a third order around the stronger through-line. Alter placement and transitions, not the recorded facts; make every return accumulate, reverse, or complicate meaning.
6. Read all three versions continuously. Mark where each creates curiosity, orientation, implied causality, development, false completeness, repetition, or productive absence.
7. Choose the order that best serves the active inquiry, or combine methods only when every shift has a named structural job and readers can still relocate.
8. Remove one incident from the selected version. Keep it removed if the inquiry tightens; restore or replace its function if the structure loses a necessary contrast, turn, or evidentiary limit.

## Success Check
- Every version uses the same factual incidents until the explicit removal test.
- The snapshot order creates purposeful inference rather than random discontinuity.
- The connected order supplies orientation without pretending to tell a complete life.
- The through-line changes meaning or pressure at every return.
- No transition manufactures causality, motive, or resolution unsupported by the material.
- The selected structure can be defended by an effect the alternatives do not deliver as well.

## Common Failures
- Changing facts, adding a convenient event, or smoothing uncertainty so one order appears naturally superior.
- Calling disconnected draft fragments an intentional segmented form before adjacency has been designed.
- Treating chronological succession as proof of causation or personal growth.
- Selecting the first repeated object as a through-line although its meaning remains static.
- Explaining every juxtaposition until the snapshot version no longer asks readers to make an inference.
- Choosing connected narration because it feels complete when the evidence remains selective and gapped.

## Notes
The exercise separates material from arrangement. Fragmentation can preserve gaps, simultaneity, and associative thought; connection can clarify change and consequence; a through-line can make separated moments answer one another. None is inherently more truthful. The useful form is the one whose omissions, bridges, and recurrences reveal a supportable relation without turning selection into completeness.
