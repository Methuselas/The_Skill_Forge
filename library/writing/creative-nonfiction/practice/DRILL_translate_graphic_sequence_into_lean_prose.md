# Translate a Graphic Sequence into Lean Prose

## Practice Task
Reduce a short factual scene or inquiry to a rough sequence of panels, then rewrite it as a complete lean and imagistic prose passage.

## Target Skill
Compressing a short creative nonfiction inquiry while preserving its necessary images, actions, and turn.

## Setup
Choose one page or brief section from a creative nonfiction draft that contains a legible event, observation, or reflective movement. Prepare paper or a simple panel grid; drawing skill is irrelevant.

## Instructions
1. State the passage's active question or change in one working sentence.
2. Divide the essential movement into boxes, giving each panel one visible action, object, spatial relation, or shift in attention.
3. Sketch only what a reader must encounter; use labels when needed, but do not rescue an inert panel with a paragraph of explanation.
4. Add five to ten short, uncomplicated sentences across the sequence, selecting details that carry both factual orientation and implication.
5. Remove any panel or sentence whose loss does not weaken the inquiry's movement.
6. Rewrite the sequence as continuous literary prose without referring to panels, preserving the visual selection, compression, and strongest sensory details.
7. Compare the original and rewritten passages, restoring only context whose absence creates a factual gap or breaks the intended turn.

## Success Check
- The active question or change is stated in one working sentence before any panel is drawn.
- Each panel carries one visible action, object, spatial relation, or shift in attention, and an inert panel is removed rather than rescued by explanation.
- The prose holds to five to ten short, uncomplicated sentences across the sequence.
- Selected details carry factual orientation and implication together; a detail doing only one of the two is a candidate for cutting.
- Every panel and sentence is tested by removal, not only the ones that look expendable.
- The finished passage works as prose for a reader who never sees the panels.

## Common Failures
- Spending practice time polishing drawings instead of testing selection and sequence.
- Turning every sentence into a caption that names only what the panel already shows.
- Cutting the reflective or factual turn that made the passage more than a visual anecdote.
- Restoring all original explanation because the compressed version initially feels unfamiliar.
- Keeping a striking image that no longer contributes to the active inquiry.

## Notes
The medium shift makes selection visible. A panel cannot comfortably hold every remembered fact or explanatory aside, so it forces the writer to choose decisive actions, objects, and transitions. Rewriting back into prose then tests whether that visual economy can survive without the drawings. The exercise succeeds when compression strengthens the inquiry, not when the result merely becomes shorter.
