# Compress a Short Essay Around Its Active Inquiry

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a brief creative nonfiction draft must feel complete but contains explanation, incident, or reflection that does not intensify its central inquiry
**THEN** preserve the inquiry's full movement while cutting or combining every unit that fails to add a necessary fact, image, complication, reflection, or turn
**ELSE** keep material whose removal would make the essay merely shorter rather than more concentrated

## Do
- State the live question in working language, then label what each passage contributes to its development.
- Combine passages that perform the same contextual or reflective task.
- Prefer a particular image, action, or fact that can carry context and implication simultaneously.
- Retain enough setup, complication, and consequence for the ending to feel earned rather than clipped.
- Read after every substantial cut to confirm that transitions and accumulating thought still work.

## Don't
- Equate brevity with an excerpt that stops before its inquiry changes or resolves into a deliberate open question.
- Pad the piece with general background merely to make the subject appear weightier.
- remove concrete particulars while preserving abstract explanation that depends on them.
- Force the work into an exact word count when no venue or task requires one.
- Repeat the same insight as anecdote, interpretation, and summary when one charged passage can do all three jobs.

## Checklist
- Every paragraph or segment contributes a distinguishable pressure to the inquiry.
- The piece establishes enough context for a reader who did not live the experience.
- No necessary turn depends on material removed during compression.
- Specific detail carries more than one useful function without becoming obscure.
- The essay reaches a complete shaped stopping point without explanatory padding.

## Notes
A short essay is a complete work under pressure, not a long essay with pages missing. Its resolution may remain conditional or unsettled, but the inquiry must arrive somewhere different from where it began. Compression earns attention by increasing the proportion of sentences that carry discovery.
