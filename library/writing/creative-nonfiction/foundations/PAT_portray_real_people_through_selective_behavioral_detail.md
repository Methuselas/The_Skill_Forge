# Portray Real People through Selective Behavioral Detail

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a creative nonfiction draft reduces an actual person to a general judgment, grievance, virtue, or collection of eccentricities
**THEN** select supportable actions, speech patterns, gestures, and contradictory qualities that let the person appear specific and three-dimensional without becoming a caricature
**ELSE** keep a narrow portrayal only when the person's limited presence performs one clear factual function and does not imply a complete character judgment

## Do
- Replace a label with an observed interaction that shows how the person moves, speaks, chooses, or responds under pressure.
- Choose intimate particulars that affect motive, relationship, or the reader's inference rather than details that merely make the subject look odd.
- Include evidence that complicates the narrator's strongest praise or resentment when the omitted side would materially falsify the person.
- Let a small physical or verbal habit recur only when its changing context reveals more than the habit itself.
- Scale the portrait to the essay: one charged gesture may distinguish a minor presence, while a central person needs evidence across more than one emotional register.

## Don't
- Treat the narrator's passion about a relative, former partner, adversary, or admired figure as sufficient proof of a one-sided characterization.
- Overemphasize grammar, accent, body, mannerism, or cultural difference until individuality becomes ridicule or stereotype.
- Invent a private motive to make observed behavior easier to explain.
- Accumulate colorful traits that never change the inquiry, relation, or action.
- Protect a favored person from every unflattering fact when the essay claims to examine them honestly.

## Checklist
- Readers encounter the person doing or saying something consequential rather than only receiving the narrator's verdict.
- Selected details distinguish this individual without turning difference into a joke or diagnosis.
- Positive and negative evidence appear in proportion to what the essay actually knows and needs.
- Inferred motives remain distinguishable from observable conduct.
- Removing a retained detail would weaken character, relation, or inquiry—not merely reduce color.

## Notes
Actual people cannot be redesigned to satisfy a cleaner character arc. The writer's craft lies in selecting behavior that makes a person legible while respecting the limits of observation and memory. Specificity increases vividness and ethical risk at the same time: the more memorable an idiosyncrasy becomes, the more carefully the writer must ask whether it reveals a human being or merely fixes them in the narrator's most convenient interpretation.
