# Control Online Resume Visibility and Currency

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a resume will be posted to a searchable job service or built inside an online form
**THEN** retain practical control over who can see it, how it can be updated, and when it can be removed

## Do
- Read the service's access and privacy terms before exposing contact details, employer names, or other identifying information.
- Confirm that the entry can be edited and removed and that account access can be recovered throughout the search.
- Use available visibility controls when a current employer, broad public audience, or unnecessary contact exposure creates risk.
- Update changed experience, qualifications, availability, and contact information while the posting remains active.
- Keep a flexible local master because fixed online fields may prevent useful section ordering or additional headings.

## Don't
- Publish sensitive career information before learning which employers, recruiters, or other users can retrieve it.
- Leave stale versions active after facts change or the job search ends.
- Treat a platform's hide or block controls as a substitute for understanding its access policy.
- Make a constrained online builder the only editable copy of the candidate's career material.

## Checklist
- The service permits the required editing, access recovery, visibility control, and removal.
- Exposed identity, contact, and employer details match the candidate's privacy decision.
- The posted version agrees with the current local master on all material facts.
- Fixed fields have not silently omitted or distorted important evidence.
- An inactive search has a clear removal or deactivation step.

## Notes
Posting creates a maintenance obligation as well as a discovery opportunity. Searchability can help employers find the candidate, but it also reduces control over audience and version. A local master preserves structural freedom while the online copy remains a current, intentionally exposed projection rather than an abandoned record.
