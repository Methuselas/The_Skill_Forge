# Use an Academic CV for Comprehensive Professional Evidence

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a position requests a curriculum vitae or evaluates a long record of academic, medical, scientific, teaching, or research accomplishment
**THEN** present a comprehensive, role-relevant professional record rather than compressing the candidate into a brief sales-oriented resume
**ELSE** use a conventional resume unless the field's application practice clearly requires the longer genre

## Do
- Confirm that the recipient means a comprehensive CV rather than using the term casually for an ordinary resume.
- Select applicable headings from qualifications, thesis work, registrations, research, fieldwork, publications, presentations, conferences, grants, fellowships, patents, teaching, consulting, professional service, and community involvement.
- Put the categories most important to the appointment first instead of treating one universal heading order as mandatory.
- Update the record regularly, then tailor each submitted version so comprehensive does not become indiscriminate.
- Use a simple, consistent layout that lets readers navigate a long document without losing the hierarchy of accomplishment.

## Don't
- Send an academic-style dossier when the vacancy expects a concise employment resume.
- Pad the document with irrelevant detail or repeat one accomplishment under several headings to create artificial length.
- Assume comprehensiveness excuses weak organization, buried priorities, or inconsistent formatting.
- Omit disciplinary evidence such as publications, registrations, research, presentations, or grants merely to force the record into a résumé-sized limit.

## Checklist
- The vacancy or professional field justifies the comprehensive genre.
- The heading set represents the forms of accomplishment actually evaluated in that field.
- The most decision-relevant evidence appears before lower-priority background material.
- Every included item contributes distinct professional information.
- Formatting remains consistent and navigable across the full record.
- The submitted version is current and adapted to the role.

## Notes
A resume markets a selective employment match; a CV documents the breadth and development of professional standing. Length follows the evidence rather than serving as a target. The longer form earns its space when reviewers must assess scholarship, specialist qualification, research, teaching, clinical standing, or sustained contribution that a short employment summary would erase.
