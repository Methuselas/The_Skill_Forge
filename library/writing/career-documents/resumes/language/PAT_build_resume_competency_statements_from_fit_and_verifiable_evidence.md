# Build Resume Competency Statements from Fit and Verifiable Evidence

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a résumé needs to foreground a requirement-matched capability that is not obvious from job titles alone
**THEN** write a compact titled paragraph that states the fit and supports it with specific, traceable evidence from study, work, extracurricular activity, credentials, or independent assessment
**ELSE** let the visible role history, qualification, or achievement establish the capability without adding a redundant paragraph

## Do
- Use the employer's recognizable capability term as the heading only when the candidate can truthfully support that exact requirement.
- State the practical fit in one or two sentences that define what the candidate can do rather than stacking positive qualities.
- Select concrete applications such as an assignment method, project responsibility, workplace task, community role, test result, or measured performance.
- Distinguish ordinary exposure from demonstrated proficiency by naming the task completed, the conditions, and any credible assessment or result.
- Keep enough context for the reviewer to trace the evidence to a qualification, experience entry, portfolio item, credential, or informed referee.
- Place the paragraph near the opening or experience section according to where it best clarifies evidence the document already contains.

## Don't
- Put a skill label above a generic assertion such as quick learner or excellent communicator and call it proof.
- Claim advanced proficiency from merely using a tool for routine coursework or personal activity.
- Make a referee the only source of a claim the résumé itself could support through a concrete example.
- Repeat a full employment or education entry inside the paragraph when a concise traceable example is enough.
- Use a competency heading copied from the advertisement when no genuine candidate evidence matches it.

## Checklist
- The heading corresponds to an important requirement in the target role.
- The fit statement describes an assessable capability rather than a flattering trait.
- At least one specific action, result, qualification, or independent assessment supports the claim.
- Level words such as advanced, fluent, or expert have evidence proportionate to their strength.
- A reviewer can locate or verify the underlying experience without guessing where it came from.
- Removing the paragraph would lose useful interpretation rather than only shorten the résumé.

## Notes
The useful sequence is heading, fit, then proof. The heading gives the reviewer a familiar retrieval label; the fit sentence interprets why the candidate meets it; the evidence prevents the paragraph from becoming self-description. This structure is especially useful when study, placements, casual work, and community activity jointly establish a capability that no single occupational title communicates.
