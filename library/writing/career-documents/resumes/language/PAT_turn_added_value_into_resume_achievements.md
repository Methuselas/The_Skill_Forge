# Turn Added Value into Resume Achievements

## Pattern Rule
**IF** the candidate changed a result beyond merely carrying out an assigned duty
**THEN** state the action or initiative together with the benefit it produced, using a defensible measure when one is available

## Do
- Look for changes in revenue, cost, time, errors, waste, volume, service, customer growth, quality, safety, or target attainment.
- Connect the result to what the candidate personally initiated, improved, solved, delivered, or influenced.
- Give a number a comparison point such as the prior level, target, period, team size, budget, deadline, or percentage change.
- For projects, identify the type and scale of work plus the milestone, delivery, or outcome reached.
- Present an award or formal recognition directly when its significance is clear even without a business metric.

## Don't
- Relabel a routine responsibility as an achievement simply because it was performed competently.
- Drop an impressive number into a bullet without explaining the candidate's action or what the number measures.
- Claim a team or organization result as an individual result when the candidate's contribution was narrower.
- Invent precision where records support only a cautious factual description.

## Checklist
- The statement distinguishes assigned work from the extra value created.
- Action and benefit have an intelligible causal connection.
- Every number has a unit, period, baseline, target, or other useful frame.
- The scale and ownership of the claim can survive interview questioning.
- Qualitative achievements identify a concrete improvement rather than a flattering adjective.

## Notes
Achievement language answers a different question from responsibility language: not what the role required, but what became better because of the candidate's contribution. Measures strengthen that answer only when they clarify magnitude. A precise but contextless figure can be less credible than a bounded qualitative result.
