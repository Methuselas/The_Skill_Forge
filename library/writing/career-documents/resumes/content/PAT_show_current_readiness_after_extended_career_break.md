# Show Current Readiness After an Extended Career Break

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a candidate is returning to a previous field after enough time away that older experience no longer proves current readiness
**THEN** refresh the candidate's field knowledge and surface recent, dated evidence of professional currency before relying on the earlier career record

## Do
- Renew a relevant professional membership or licence when current standing matters to the occupation.
- Complete targeted refresher training in tools, standards, or practices that changed during the absence.
- Reconnect with practitioners, former colleagues, or industry groups to learn how the work and hiring language have moved.
- Follow current professional publications and role descriptions closely enough to distinguish enduring capability from obsolete terminology.
- Feature recent courses, certifications, volunteer work, projects, or technology use near the older experience they update.

## Don't
- Assume a former title proves present competence with systems or practices that have since changed.
- Claim currency through newly learned vocabulary without evidence of study or use.
- Collect unrelated courses merely to make the interruption look busy.
- Bury the freshest evidence beneath a long account of older achievements.

## Checklist
- Any licence, membership, or certification presented as current has been verified.
- Recent learning addresses a named change or requirement in the target field.
- The resume makes the connection between earlier depth and present readiness visible.
- Current terminology is supported by current knowledge or practice.
- The candidate can discuss what changed during the absence and how readiness was restored.

## Notes
Earlier experience establishes depth, but a long absence can leave the reviewer unsure whether the candidate can re-enter today's version of the work. Recent evidence closes that gap more credibly than claims of enthusiasm or adaptability. The strongest return narrative combines prior accomplishment with concrete proof that tools, standards, relationships, and terminology have been refreshed.
