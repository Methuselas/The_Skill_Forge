# Preserve Employer Continuity Through Name Changes

## Pattern Rule
**IF** one continuous employment relationship spans a merger, acquisition, rebrand, or other employer-name change
**THEN** connect the former and current names explicitly while structuring the entry around actual role changes rather than corporate renaming events

## Do
- When the candidate held different roles, list the roles separately under the employer name in use at each time and add a brief former-name connection.
- When the role stayed the same, state the responsibilities and achievements once under the current name and summarize the name sequence in one note.
- Keep the employment dates tied to the candidate's tenure, not the incorporation date of a successor entity.
- Use recognizable current and former names so references, records, and the resume can be reconciled.
- Verify the order of name changes before compressing several transactions into one explanation.

## Don't
- Make a merger look like a job change when the candidate's employment continued uninterrupted.
- Duplicate the same responsibilities and achievements under every corporate identity.
- Erase the former name when it is the name likely to appear in employment records or referee memory.
- Turn the entry into a corporate history that does not help the reader follow the candidate's role.

## Checklist
- A reviewer can tell whether the candidate changed jobs, employers, or only employer names.
- Each real role change retains its own title and date span.
- Unchanged work appears once rather than being multiplied by ownership events.
- Current and historical names can be matched to likely verification sources.
- The corporate explanation is shorter than the career evidence it clarifies.

## Notes
Renaming events can fragment a long tenure into what looks like unrelated employment, or they can cause a referee's organization to appear inconsistent with the resume. The remedy is a compact identity bridge. The candidate's role sequence remains primary; the corporate sequence exists only to make that record traceable.
