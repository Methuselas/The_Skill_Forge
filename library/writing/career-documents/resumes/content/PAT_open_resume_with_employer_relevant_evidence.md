# Open a Resume with Employer-Relevant Evidence

## Pattern Rule
**IF** the first page needs to orient a reviewer before the detailed history
**THEN** synthesize the strongest requirement-matched experience, achievements, qualifications, and skills into a concise evidence-led opening

## Do
- Compare the vacancy's principal requirements with the candidate's inventory before choosing what earns opening emphasis.
- Lead with established professional identity or experience when the record is mature enough to support it.
- Select a small set of strengths that the later history, education, or achievement sections can substantiate.
- Use short sentences or compact bullets so the reviewer reaches supporting evidence quickly.
- Let distinctive results, scope, credentials, or specializations carry the claim instead of relying on praise words.

## Don't
- Begin with generic adjectives such as dynamic, motivated, or results-oriented without proof.
- Claim expertise or personal qualities that the rest of the document never demonstrates.
- Repeat the key-skills list and work history in miniature until the first page becomes a second resume.
- Use a career objective that focuses on learning, advancement, or benefits the candidate wants from the employer.

## Checklist
- Every opening claim corresponds to a real requirement or likely employer concern.
- The strongest statements have evidence later in the document.
- The section can be scanned without delaying entry into the career record.
- Generic praise has been replaced by a result, credential, scope fact, or named capability.
- The wording fits this target role rather than any vacancy in the occupation.

## Notes
An opening earns its place by reducing interpretation time, not by announcing confidence. It previews the evidence the employer should notice and creates expectations that the body must fulfill.

`writing_resume_variant_career_objective_for_direction_change` changes the opening when the desired direction is not self-evident from recent roles. Use a brief statement that connects credible existing capability to a realistic contribution in the new setting. Omit it when the record already makes the destination clear or when the statement would chiefly describe what the candidate hopes to receive.
