# State Neutral Departure Context When It Resolves Concern

## Pattern Rule
**IF** repeated short roles or involuntary endings could reasonably be mistaken for a performance or commitment problem
**THEN** add a brief factual reason for externally caused departures that resolves the ambiguity
**ELSE** omit departure commentary from the resume and prepare a calm factual explanation for later discussion

## Do
- Use verifiable descriptions such as company closure, operational relocation, restructure, contract completion, project completion, or the permanent incumbent's return.
- State that a wider group or function was affected when that fact accurately distinguishes a redundancy from an individual performance decision.
- Put each explanation in the same predictable place after the relevant entry.
- Keep the sentence short enough that responsibilities and achievements remain the center of the role.
- Pair a series of brief jobs with evidence of promotions, awards, strong results, or successful adaptation when those facts exist.

## Don't
- Catalogue a reason for every departure when the history does not create a real concern.
- Criticize a former employer, manager, colleague, or customer in the document.
- Explain dismissal, misconduct, conflict, or contested performance through a one-sided resume narrative.
- Call a departure redundant, contractual, or externally caused without evidence that supports the description.

## Checklist
- Each included reason answers a likely concern rather than volunteering a new controversy.
- Wording reports an event without assigning motives or blame.
- Group impact and organizational circumstances are accurate.
- Negative or disputed context has been reserved for a setting where questions can be answered.
- The role's achievements still receive more emphasis than its ending.

## Notes
Reasons for leaving are selective context, not a mandatory section. A neutral fact can prevent several closures or contracts from being misread as instability. Contentious explanations behave differently: without questions and response, they consume space, invite rebuttal from an absent party, and shift attention away from the candidate's work.
