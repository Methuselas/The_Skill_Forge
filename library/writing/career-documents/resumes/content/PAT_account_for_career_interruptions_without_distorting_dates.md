# Account for Career Interruptions Without Distorting Dates

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a visible interval in the employment sequence came from travel, family responsibilities, caring, study, illness, unemployment, or another nonstandard period
**THEN** preserve accurate dates and use a concise neutral label when explaining the interval will reduce ambiguity, adding only truthful activity that contributes relevant evidence
**ELSE** leave the interval unexplained rather than inventing filler

## Do
- Use the same month-and-year precision as the surrounding employment entries.
- Choose a plain description such as overseas travel, full-time study, family commitments, or home carer without narrating private circumstances.
- Place a completed course in the education section and add a short timeline label only when the chronology still needs clarification.
- Describe volunteer work, community offices, or genuine assistance to a business as work performed, with its actual responsibilities and setting.
- Quantify a result from unpaid activity when the candidate can support the outcome just as they would for paid employment.

## Don't
- Switch the whole history to years only for the purpose of making a shorter interruption disappear.
- Mark a finished role as current or invent temporary work that did not occur.
- Turn routine household activity into inflated occupational experience merely to fill space.
- Disclose medical, family, or other sensitive detail beyond what the candidate chooses and the document needs to explain.

## Checklist
- Start and finish dates remain accurate and use the document's established precision.
- The label explains the interval without inviting unnecessary personal questions.
- Any listed responsibility or achievement came from real activity during that period.
- Unpaid and paid experience are not presented as interchangeable when their settings materially differ.
- The timeline is clearer without becoming falsely seamless.

## Notes
An interruption is not a defect that must be painted over. Omitting it may create speculation, while overexplaining can surrender privacy and distract from capability. A short truthful entry gives the reader enough chronology to proceed; supporting evidence belongs there only when the candidate actually performed relevant work or learning during the interval.
