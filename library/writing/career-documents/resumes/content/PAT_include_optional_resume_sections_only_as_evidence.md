# Include Optional Resume Sections Only as Evidence

## Pattern Rule
**IF** memberships, interests, community roles, awards, publications, or speaking work could be added to a resume
**THEN** include an optional section only when its genuine details contribute relevant capability, credibility, commitment, or missing context

## Do
- Name an office-bearing or organizing role when it demonstrates leadership, administration, fundraising, or another useful capability.
- Include professional memberships that establish current participation or a credential relevant to the occupation.
- Record awards, publications, presentations, or media contributions with enough identifying detail for the reviewer to understand their significance.
- Use interests to show transferable skill or sustained commitment when paid experience provides little evidence, especially early in a career.
- Title each optional section for the evidence it actually contains rather than hiding unrelated items under a catch-all heading.

## Don't
- Add private, controversial, or identity-revealing detail merely because a template has an empty interests section.
- List passive hobbies that invite assumptions but show no relevant skill or commitment.
- Invent shared interests or memberships to manufacture rapport with a reviewer.
- Repeat achievements already explained in the employment history without adding a distinct professional context.

## Checklist
- Every optional item answers a plausible hiring question or strengthens a stated requirement.
- Roles and memberships are current or clearly dated.
- Publications, awards, and speaking entries identify what the candidate actually contributed.
- Personal detail has been reviewed for relevance, privacy, and avoidable distraction.
- Removing the section would lose useful evidence rather than only shorten the document.

## Notes
Optional does not mean decorative. These sections are most valuable when the main history leaves a real evidentiary gap—for example, leadership shown through a community office or communication skill shown through publication. When no such gap exists, restraint protects both space and privacy.
