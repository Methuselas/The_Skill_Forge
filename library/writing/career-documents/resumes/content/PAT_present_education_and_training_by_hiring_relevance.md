# Present Education and Training by Hiring Relevance

## Pattern Rule
**IF** education, qualifications, or training must appear in a resume
**THEN** order and describe them by qualification level, recency, and relevance so formal credentials remain distinct from shorter professional development

## Do
- List recent qualifications in reverse chronology, placing postgraduate work before undergraduate work and degrees before certificates or diplomas where those distinctions matter.
- Give the full qualification title, major or specialization, institution, and completion year.
- Mark unfinished study accurately and provide an expected completion point only when the plan is current and supportable.
- Include grades, academic awards, or selected coursework when they are recent, strong, and useful to the hiring decision.
- Group relevant courses, licences, seminars, and workplace training under professional development rather than presenting them as equivalent to formal qualifications.

## Don't
- Retain old school results after substantial higher education or career evidence has made them irrelevant.
- Bury a required degree or credential beneath a long sequence of minor seminars.
- Describe incomplete study in a way that lets the reader mistake it for a completed qualification.
- Add unrelated recreational courses merely to lengthen the section.

## Checklist
- Required or strongly relevant credentials are easy to find.
- Qualification names and institutions are written in full where abbreviations could be unclear.
- Completion status and dates are accurate.
- Academic detail earns its space through recency, strength, or role relevance.
- Formal education and professional development use separate, intelligible groupings.

## Notes
The section has two jobs: establish credentials and show continued preparation for the work. Listing every learning experience weakens both. The reviewer should be able to distinguish a completed award from study in progress, and a formal qualification from a short course, without interpreting promotional wording.
