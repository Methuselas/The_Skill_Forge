# Scale Work-History Detail by Role Relevance

## Pattern Rule
**IF** employment entries differ in relevance to the target role
**THEN** give the strongest matching roles enough context, responsibilities, and achievements to prove the fit while compressing secondary roles without erasing the timeline

## Do
- Give a relevant entry its role title, employer, month-and-year span, useful organizational context, selected responsibilities, and achievements.
- Reduce a secondary entry to the title, employer, location, and dates when more detail would not improve the hiring decision.
- Explain an unfamiliar employer by its function, scale, market, or recognizable counterpart so the reader can interpret the candidate's scope.
- Expand duties selectively when a generic title hides work that matches the vacancy.
- Keep enough sequence information for the reviewer to see where the candidate worked throughout the represented period.

## Don't
- Spend equal space on every job regardless of what each contributes to the application.
- Delete inconvenient but material employment periods until the chronology becomes misleading.
- Assume a reader understands an overseas organization, small employer, or internal title without a short factual cue.
- Use grand comparisons or dismissive descriptions to make an unfamiliar workplace sound more impressive.

## Checklist
- The most relevant roles contain the evidence needed to judge the match.
- Minor roles remain visible without crowding out stronger material.
- Unfamiliar organizations can be understood from brief, neutral context.
- Detail level changes for a reason tied to the target role, not personal preference.
- Compression has not created an unexplained or inaccurate employment sequence.

## Notes
A resume is a selected account, not an equal-weight archive. The trap is treating relevance as permission either to narrate everything or to make awkward periods disappear. Variable detail preserves both priorities: depth where evidence matters and a truthful outline where it does not.
