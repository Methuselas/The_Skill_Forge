# Design a Resume for On-Screen Scanning

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a resume's content is ready to be arranged for recruiter review
**THEN** use a restrained, consistent hierarchy with readable type, useful white space, and scannable grouping that remains clear on screen

## Do
- Limit the type system to one or two legible families with a consistent role for headings and body text.
- Use bold, spacing, and alignment to distinguish headings instead of relying on decorative faces or prolonged italics.
- Break responsibilities and achievements into short bullets when continuous paragraphs would hide the evidence.
- Leave enough white space between roles and sections for the reader to recover the document's structure at a glance.
- Inspect the final page view at ordinary screen size and confirm that contrast, spacing, and hierarchy survive without magnification.

## Don't
- Shrink the body type or margins until all content fits at the cost of comfortable reading.
- Mix several colors, typefaces, heading treatments, or alignment systems to manufacture individuality.
- Use low-contrast color or elaborate decoration that competes with names, dates, roles, and achievements.
- Let every line become a bullet when grouping and hierarchy would communicate the relationship more clearly.

## Checklist
- Headings at the same level use the same type, capitalization, spacing, and alignment.
- Body text is readable at a normal on-screen viewing scale.
- Responsibilities, achievements, dates, and employers can be distinguished during a quick scan.
- White space separates information rather than creating accidental gaps or stranded headings.
- The design remains professional when color is removed or the document is viewed on a different display.

## Notes
Resume presentation is navigation under time pressure. A reviewer should not have to decode the page before evaluating the candidate. Restrained formatting is not a demand for sameness: individuality can come from proportion and emphasis, but each visual choice must preserve the hierarchy when the document is opened on an unfamiliar screen.
