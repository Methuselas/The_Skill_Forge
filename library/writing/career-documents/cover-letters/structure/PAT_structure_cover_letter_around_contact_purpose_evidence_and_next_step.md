# Structure a Cover Letter Around Contact Purpose, Evidence, and Next Step

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a cover letter must introduce a candidacy without repeating the resume
**THEN** give the opening one contact purpose, the body a selective employer-matched case, and the conclusion one credible route forward

## Do
- Use the opening paragraph to identify why the candidate is writing and the opportunity or organizational situation that makes the approach relevant.
- Build the body from a small set of qualifications, experience, and achievements that answer the employer's strongest criteria instead of reciting the work history.
- Connect at least one researched feature of the organization or work to the candidate's reason for applying and potential contribution.
- Let each paragraph perform one job, using enough detail to make the evidence credible while keeping the argument easy to follow.
- Close by confirming the appropriate availability, mentioning enclosed material when necessary, and naming the next action the candidate can honestly support.

## Don't
- Begin with a generic declaration of interest that leaves the reader to discover the role or reason for contact later.
- Convert the resume into prose or list every qualification regardless of relevance.
- Praise the organization with claims that could apply unchanged to any employer.
- End with vague enthusiasm but no indication of availability, response route, or desired next step.
- Promise a call, message, meeting, or deadline the candidate cannot reliably honor.

## Checklist
- The opening answers why this contact is happening now.
- Every body claim helps answer a stated requirement, workplace need, or credible reason for organizational fit.
- Evidence is selective enough that the letter adds interpretation rather than duplicating the resume.
- The conclusion identifies a realistic next action and keeps the candidate contactable.
- Removing the employer and opportunity details would break the argument rather than reveal a reusable generic letter.

## Notes
The three-part sequence is persuasive control flow rather than a ceremonial letter formula. The opening gives the reader a reason to continue, the body interprets selected career evidence against the employer's problem, and the conclusion converts interest into a feasible action. The resume remains the fuller evidence record; the letter selects and explains what the reader should notice first.
