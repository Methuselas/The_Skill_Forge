# Keep Cover-Letter Confidence Specific and Employer-Focused

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a cover-letter draft sounds boastful, apologetic, desperate, inflated, or centered on what the applicant hopes to receive
**THEN** recast its claims in direct business language that connects truthful evidence to the employer's work and needs
**ELSE** preserve the current register while removing any unsupported intensifiers or unnecessary self-reference

## Do
- Replace a negative concession with an affirmative account of the relevant capability the candidate does possess, without concealing a requirement that remains unmet.
- Follow an assertion of fit with a qualification, result, responsibility, or example that gives the confidence an evidentiary basis.
- Vary sentence openings and subjects so the organization, role, work, and contribution share attention with the applicant.
- Use plain occupational language an external reader can understand, retaining specialized terms only when they clarify genuine expertise or match the role accurately.
- Keep personal circumstances only when they explain a relevant constraint, decision, or readiness fact better than the professional evidence alone.

## Don't
- Pressure the reader with entitlement, intimidation, repeated demands, or certainty about being selected.
- Describe willingness to accept anything, dwell on a difficult search, or use misfortune as the central case for employment.
- Inflate ordinary claims with grand vocabulary, insider acronyms, slang, shortcuts, or false formality.
- Repeat the same first-person construction until the letter becomes a list of wants and self-descriptions.
- Embellish qualifications, achievements, relationships, or organizational knowledge to make the match appear stronger.

## Checklist
- Each strong claim can be traced to evidence the candidate could defend in an interview.
- The letter says what the candidate can contribute more often than what the candidate wants to obtain.
- Missing requirements are handled honestly without making absence the rhetorical center.
- Vocabulary is professional, specific, and intelligible outside the candidate's current organization.
- The tone invites evaluation and discussion without aggression, pleading, or manufactured intimacy.

## Notes
Confidence in an application is not a volume setting. It comes from choosing relevant evidence, stating it without apology, and making its usefulness to the employer explicit. Aggression and desperation both displace that case: one tries to force the conclusion, while the other asks personal need to substitute for fit. Plain, defensible claims leave the reader room to verify the candidacy.
