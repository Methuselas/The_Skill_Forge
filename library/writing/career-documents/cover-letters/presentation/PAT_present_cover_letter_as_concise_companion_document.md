# Present a Cover Letter as a Concise Companion Document

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a completed cover-letter draft must work beside a resume in a document, message body, or application portal
**THEN** compress and format it for one uninterrupted reading while keeping its visual identity compatible with the resume and its delivered medium

## Do
- Aim for a single page when the instructions and substantive case allow, cutting repetition before reducing type, spacing, or margins.
- Reuse the resume's restrained type choices and professional identity cues while giving the letter the simpler hierarchy its continuous argument needs.
- Separate paragraphs with enough white space that the opening, evidence, and close remain visible without decorative borders or graphics.
- Keep inline email text clean and conventionally readable, and preview attached or portal-generated documents in the form the recipient will see.
- Inspect the rendered page for stranded lines, dense blocks, accidental blank space, and formatting changes introduced by conversion or upload.

## Don't
- Force an overlong argument onto one page by making the type, line spacing, or margins uncomfortable to read.
- Add a photograph, ornamental frame, graphic device, or elaborate layout that competes with the evidence.
- Use long passages of capitals, italics, or emphasis that become harder to scan on screen.
- Assume a design that looks orderly in the editor will survive email, export, download, or application-portal conversion.
- Let the letter's styling conflict with the resume so strongly that the two documents appear unrelated.

## Checklist
- The full argument can be read quickly without skipping dense or repetitive passages.
- Type, spacing, margins, and paragraph breaks remain legible in the delivered rendering.
- The letter and resume clearly belong to the same application without being forced into identical layouts.
- Visual emphasis identifies useful structure rather than decoration.
- The final exported, transmitted, or uploaded version has been reopened and inspected.

## Notes
Concision is first a content decision and only then a page-count result. A compact letter respects the reader's limited attention, but unreadably compressed typography defeats that purpose. Visual consistency with the resume creates package coherence; the letter still needs a quieter design because its main unit is the paragraph rather than a sequence of scannable career entries.
