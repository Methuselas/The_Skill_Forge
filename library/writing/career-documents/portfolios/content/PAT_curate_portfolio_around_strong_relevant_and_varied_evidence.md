# Curate a Portfolio Around Strong, Relevant, and Varied Evidence

## Pattern Rule
**IF** work samples must demonstrate suitability for a particular role or professional review
**THEN** assemble a selective, current sequence that leads with the best relevant work, demonstrates useful range and process, and makes the candidate's contribution to every item explicit
**ELSE** maintain a broader private master collection until the audience and purpose are known

## Do
- Verify the employer's or profession's current expectations before deciding what kinds of sample belong in the submission.
- Choose a small set of finished pieces that answer the target's priorities, then add variety only when it proves another relevant capability rather than filling space.
- Include development material such as drafts, research, sketches, plans, or intermediate decisions when it reveals a process the final result cannot show by itself.
- Label each piece with enough context to explain its purpose, constraints, technical details, and the work personally performed, especially on collaborative projects.
- Put the strongest evidence first and arrange the remainder so a reviewer can follow a deliberate progression rather than hunt through unrelated material.
- Reproduce physical work through accurate, well-lit, high-quality photographs or scans and remove distracting backgrounds that misrepresent the original.

## Don't
- Include weak or obsolete pieces merely to make the collection look larger.
- Present several near-identical examples as range when they prove the same capability.
- Show only polished outcomes when the review needs to assess how decisions developed.
- Claim sole authorship of shared work or leave the candidate's contribution for the reviewer to guess.
- Let elaborate decoration compete with the samples or make their sequence difficult to navigate.

## Checklist
- Every item answers a role, employer, course, client, or professional-review need.
- The opening sample is among the strongest and most relevant pieces available.
- The set demonstrates more than one useful capability without padding or repetition.
- Process evidence appears wherever it materially improves evaluation of the work.
- Each collaborative item distinguishes personal contribution from the team's result.
- Images, scans, labels, dates, and technical details are accurate and current.

## Notes
A portfolio is an argument made through selected work, not an archive of everything completed. Range matters only when the differences help the reviewer judge readiness. Development evidence can make judgment, experimentation, and problem solving visible, while concise labels prevent a strong artifact from being discounted because its purpose or authorship is unclear.
