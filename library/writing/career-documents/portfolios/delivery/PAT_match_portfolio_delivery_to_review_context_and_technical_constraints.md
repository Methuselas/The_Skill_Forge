# Match Portfolio Delivery to Review Context and Technical Constraints

## Pattern Rule
**IF** selected work must be submitted, presented in an interview, or left for later review
**THEN** deliver it through the requested medium in a form the reviewer can open, identify, navigate, and assess under the actual viewing conditions
**ELSE** confirm the review context before investing in a physical, file-based, or web presentation

## Do
- Follow explicit instructions about physical presentation, file type, dimensions, naming, links, or permitted quantity before optimizing appearance.
- Choose a bound or loose physical collection, portable digital presentation, submitted file set, or website according to how and when the reviewer will inspect the work.
- Test every file, link, embedded element, and navigation path from outside the authoring environment and through the same route the reviewer is expected to use.
- Keep the candidate's identity and the sequence clear if files are downloaded, printed, detached, or reviewed without the covering message.
- Prepare a practical fallback when an interview presentation depends on connectivity, unfamiliar display equipment, or a service the reviewer may not be able to access.

## Don't
- Assume a digital presentation is automatically preferable to physical work or that every employer can use the same medium.
- Ignore requested specifications because another format shows the work more dramatically.
- Depend on a private login, expiring permission, local font, or unstable link that has not been tested from the reviewer's side.
- Send an oversized or difficult-to-navigate collection when the review context allows only brief attention.
- Treat the authoring view as evidence that the delivered version preserves image quality, order, and labels.

## Checklist
- The delivery method matches the stated submission or interview instructions.
- A reviewer can open the presentation without the candidate's software, account, or intervention.
- Identity, item order, captions, and the candidate's contribution survive download or printing.
- Image quality and legibility remain acceptable in the actual viewing medium.
- A tested fallback exists for presentation conditions with meaningful technical risk.

## Notes
The same evidence may need different containers for advance screening, live discussion, or leave-behind review. The medium is successful when it removes access friction and preserves the intended reading order. Technical novelty has no value if it blocks the reviewer or makes the candidate necessary just to open the work.
