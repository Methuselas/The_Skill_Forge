# Build a Career-Evidence Inventory Before Drafting

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a job application must be drafted or refreshed from incomplete memory or an old generic resume
**THEN** reconstruct the candidate's work history, capabilities, achievements, and corroborating details before deciding what the application will include
**ELSE** select from the existing inventory and update any stale dates, contacts, or evidence

## Do
- Record each employer's full name, start and finish month and year, reporting line, and the responsibilities actually performed.
- Recover job descriptions, offer letters, training records, praise, awards, and old correspondence that can restore forgotten detail.
- Separate paid responsibilities from achievements by noting where an idea, extra effort, or changed approach produced a result.
- List qualifications, certifications, transferable skills, and how each skill was developed rather than relying on an unsupported label.
- Locate former direct managers early enough to know whether appropriate referees can still be contacted.

## Don't
- Begin with a blank formatted document and treat whatever comes to mind first as the complete career record.
- Confuse routine duties with evidence that the candidate improved a result or added value.
- Pad gaps in recall with exaggeration; one exposed claim can cast doubt on the rest of the history.
- Wait until an employer requests references before checking whether former supervisors can be found.

## Checklist
- Employment dates, employer names, roles, and reporting relationships are internally consistent.
- Responsibilities and achievements are recorded separately for each relevant role.
- Qualifications and training include the institution or provider and completion date where available.
- Transferable skills have an origin or example showing how they were acquired or used.
- Awards, commendations, praise, and measurable results have been captured for later selection.
- Potential referees and current contact routes have been checked.

## Notes
Drafting directly from memory favors recent duties and generic traits while hiding the raw material that makes an application credible. The inventory is deliberately broader than any one resume. It preserves enough dated, specific material to support different applications without forcing every fact into every document.
