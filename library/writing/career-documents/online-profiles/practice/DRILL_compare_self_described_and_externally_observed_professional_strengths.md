# Compare Self-Described and Externally Observed Professional Strengths

## Practice Task
Build independent self and observer descriptions for one target role, then reconcile them into supported reputation claims and development gaps.

## Target Skill
Calibrating a professional reputation claim against role needs, evidence, and informed outside observation.

## Setup
Choose one target role or role family. Prepare three headings for personal attributes, professional attributes, and training or credentials. Select one colleague, peer, client, or former manager who has directly observed relevant work and is willing to give candid feedback.

## Instructions
1. Under each heading, list three to five qualities that matter to the target role and that the candidate believes are currently supportable.
2. Beside every quality, write one observed behavior, work result, qualification, or specific incident that could substantiate it.
3. Give the same headings and target role to the observer without sharing the candidate's words. Ask for favorable qualities, less flattering patterns, and one example behind each important description.
4. Compare the lists and mark agreement, wording differences that point to the same behavior, unsupported self-claims, outside strengths the candidate overlooked, and recurring gaps.
5. Rewrite no more than five professional reputation statements using the strongest intersections of role relevance, evidence, and observation.
6. Convert one important unsupported quality into a concrete development action instead of publishing it as a present strength.

## Success Check
- Each self-described quality carries an observed behaviour, result, qualification, or incident capable of substantiating it, rather than a restatement of the quality.
- The observer works from the same headings and role without seeing the candidate's words, so agreement counts as evidence rather than as an echo.
- The comparison keeps four categories distinct: agreement, different wording for the same behaviour, unsupported self-claim, and quality named only by the observer.
- At least one unsupported self-claim is found, or its absence is examined rather than accepted as flattering.
- Less flattering patterns are recorded as given rather than softened into development areas while being written down.
- Reputation claims are separated from development gaps, and each claim can be delivered with its example attached.

## Common Failures
- Selecting generic traits before naming the target work.
- Asking only for compliments or using an observer who has not seen the relevant behavior.
- Treating synonymous wording as disagreement while missing a shared underlying pattern.
- Keeping a desired quality after both evidence and observation fail to support it.
- Publishing the observer's praise verbatim without translating it into accurate professional evidence.

## Notes
Independent description makes the comparison useful. Showing the candidate's list first invites agreement and hides the very calibration the exercise is meant to produce. Repetition across self-assessment, observed conduct, and outside language identifies a strong public claim; divergence identifies either missing evidence, unclear signaling, or work still to be done.
