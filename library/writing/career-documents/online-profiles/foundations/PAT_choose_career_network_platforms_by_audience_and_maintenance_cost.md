# Choose Career-Network Platforms by Audience and Maintenance Cost

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a candidate is deciding where to establish a public professional presence
**THEN** select only platforms used by the intended employers, recruiters, peers, or clients and supportable with the candidate's available time, privacy boundary, and content
**ELSE** delay the profile until one channel has a defined audience and sustainable purpose

## Do
- Ask people in the target field which services they actually use to discover candidates, exchange professional information, or advertise work.
- Compare each platform's audience, searchability, connection model, publishing options, work-sample support, visibility controls, and removal path.
- Decide whether the channel will serve discovery, industry learning, networking, opportunity monitoring, work display, or a bounded combination of those jobs.
- Estimate the effort required to complete the profile, learn the controls, review invitations, publish useful material, and keep facts current.
- Use platform help and settings documentation to verify current capabilities rather than relying on remembered feature descriptions.

## Don't
- Join a service because it is fashionable among peers when the target professional audience is absent.
- Open several incomplete profiles whose combined maintenance cost exceeds the candidate's attention.
- Assume a primarily personal network can safely perform the same job as a deliberately professional channel.
- Pay for advanced features before the basic audience, profile, and participation route has demonstrated value.
- Treat a platform's current popularity or interface as a permanent career rule.

## Checklist
- The intended professional audience is active and reachable on the selected channel.
- The profile has one or more explicit career functions.
- Required visibility and privacy boundaries are supported by current controls.
- The candidate can supply enough accurate content and ongoing attention to avoid a neglected presence.
- A decision not to join is available when audience value does not justify exposure or upkeep.

## Notes
Platform choice is an audience decision with a continuing cost. A new service can be technically impressive yet useless for a field whose gatekeepers do not look there. Conversely, a modest channel can be valuable when it concentrates the relevant community and gives the candidate a sustainable way to contribute. Feature lists age quickly, so the enduring method is to test audience, function, control, and upkeep.
