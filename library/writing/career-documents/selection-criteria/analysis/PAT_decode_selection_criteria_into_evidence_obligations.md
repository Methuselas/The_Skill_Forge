# Decode Selection Criteria into Evidence Obligations

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an application criterion uses strength words, compound requirements, or capability descriptors to define what a panel will assess
**THEN** separate the required level of proof from every skill, knowledge area, attribute, and qualification that must be covered
**ELSE** map the role's ordinary requirements to evidence through the general application-targeting owner

## Do
- Preserve the criterion's exact wording while marking its response-strength terms separately from its subject terms.
- Treat `awareness` as familiarity; `knowledge` as command of fundamentals and how it was acquired or applied; and `understanding` as knowledge plus significance and practical consequences.
- Treat `ability` or `capacity` as permitting evidence of potential or transferable capability when direct performance is unavailable.
- Require specific past performance for `experience`, `demonstrated`, or `proven`, and proportionately stronger achievements for terms such as `high-level`, `well-developed`, or `strong`.
- Record a mandatory credential, registration, licence, or other absolute condition as a blocking requirement rather than a persuasive claim.
- Split joined criteria into separate obligations, including each object after `and`, `or`, `both`, a comma, or a descriptor list; then decide which obligations can honestly share one example.
- For capability headings, use the accompanying descriptors to define the expected behavior without turning every descriptor into a separate response unless the instructions require that structure.

## Don't
- Read all positive-sounding strength words as interchangeable requests for a confident assertion.
- Use mere exposure to support a request for demonstrated or proven performance.
- Explain what a topic is when the criterion asks how its significance changes workplace action.
- Answer the most visible noun in a compound criterion and leave its autonomy, teamwork, stakeholder, outcome, or qualification clause implicit.
- Treat a desirable criterion as irrelevant when credible evidence is available, or spend space polishing it while an essential criterion remains unsupported.

## Checklist
- Every criterion is classified as essential or desirable only where the current instructions make that distinction.
- Each response-strength term has a stated evidence burden.
- Every subject keyword and compound subrequirement appears in the evidence map.
- Mandatory conditions are verified independently of narrative examples.
- Capability descriptors have shaped the chosen evidence without being mistaken for optional decoration.
- No planned claim is stronger than the available qualification, example, action, or result.

## Notes
Selection criteria carry two messages at once: what the panel wants and how convincingly the candidate must establish it. Missing the first creates incomplete coverage; missing the second creates a response whose evidence is too weak for its own language. Decomposition should happen before prose, because a fluent paragraph can conceal an unanswered clause more easily than an evidence map can.
