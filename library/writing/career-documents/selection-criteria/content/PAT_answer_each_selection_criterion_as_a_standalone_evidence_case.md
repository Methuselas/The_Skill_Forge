# Answer Each Selection Criterion as a Standalone Evidence Case

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a panel will assess a written response against one named selection criterion
**THEN** make a direct supportable claim and prove every required element through enough context, candidate action, and outcome to evaluate the response independently
**ELSE** place the evidence in the application component that owns the employer's requested question or requirement

## Do
- Repeat or faithfully label the criterion in the required order so the panel can match response to requirement without reconstruction.
- Open with a concise claim calibrated to the requested evidence strength and the candidate's actual level.
- Choose a recent specific example whose duties or conditions resemble the target work, then explain why action was needed, what the candidate personally did, and what changed.
- Use Situation or Task, Action, and Result as an internal drafting scaffold; merge those parts into natural prose unless the application explicitly asks for labelled fields.
- When knowledge is requested, name the fundamentals and how they were learned or applied; when understanding is requested, show the practical significance and consequences for decisions or conduct.
- Supply enough role, organization, and process context for an outside panel member to understand internal work, acronyms, scope, and ownership.
- Edit to the current word or field limit without removing the action or result that makes the claim evidence rather than self-description.

## Don't
- Declare possession of a capability and move to the next criterion without an example proportionate to the claim.
- Let background consume the response while the candidate's decisions, actions, and outcomes remain vague.
- Leave visible `Situation`, `Task`, `Action`, or `Result` labels in the final prose merely because they helped organize the draft.
- Assume an internal panel knows the candidate, remembers the work, or will supply omitted context.
- Use one broad anecdote to imply coverage of compound requirements that the prose never actually connects to it.
- Substitute a definition of legislation, policy, or practice for evidence that the candidate understands how it affects the work.

## Checklist
- The heading and response can be matched to the correct criterion immediately.
- The opening claim does not exceed the proof that follows.
- Every decoded subrequirement is answered explicitly.
- The example distinguishes candidate action from team or organizational action.
- The result states an observable effect, decision, improvement, learning outcome, or other relevant consequence.
- A reviewer with no prior knowledge of the candidate can understand and assess the response.
- The final response fits the instructed location, format, and limit without depending on another document to complete it.

## Notes
A response is not a miniature autobiography. It is a bounded evidence case whose conclusion is the opening claim and whose proof is the candidate's relevant conduct. The internal STAR scaffold prevents unsupported assertion and activity-only narration, but its headings are drafting aids rather than a universal presentation format.
