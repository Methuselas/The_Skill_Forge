# Decompose Compound Selection Criteria Before Drafting

## Practice Task
Turn a small set of dense selection criteria into a complete evidence map before writing any response paragraphs.

## Target Skill
Separating selection-criterion strength signals and compound requirements, then matching each obligation to proportionate evidence before drafting.

## Setup
Choose three real or representative criteria: one knowledge or understanding criterion, one demonstrated or proven capability, and one compound criterion containing at least three subjects or descriptors. Create columns headed `Exact criterion`, `Strength signal`, `Required subjects`, `Evidence burden`, `Candidate evidence`, and `Gap`.

## Instructions
1. Copy each criterion exactly, then underline words that set the response strength without yet choosing an example.
2. Circle every skill, knowledge area, attribute, qualification, audience, setting, and joined clause the panel can assess separately.
3. Translate each strength signal into an evidence burden: familiarity, fundamentals and acquisition, practical significance, transferable potential, direct past performance, advanced achievement, or mandatory proof.
4. Assign a specific qualification or example to every circled obligation. Record the candidate's action and result, not just the name of the job or project.
5. Test whether one example genuinely covers several obligations. Split the evidence when the connection would otherwise require the panel to infer an unmentioned behavior.
6. Mark unsupported mandatory or essential requirements as gaps. For ability or capacity, try a truthful transferable example; for demonstrated or proven requirements, do not downgrade the burden to make a weak example fit.
7. Draft one direct opening claim for each criterion and read it against the mapped evidence. Reduce any level word the evidence cannot sustain.

## Success Check
- Strength signals and subject requirements occupy different columns.
- Every compound clause and capability descriptor is visible in the map.
- Knowledge, understanding, ability, and demonstrated experience lead to meaningfully different proof plans.
- Each retained example records candidate action and an outcome or consequence.
- No mandatory condition is hidden inside persuasive prose.
- Each opening claim is no stronger than its supporting evidence.

## Common Failures
- Underlining occupational keywords while overlooking words that set the proof threshold.
- Treating one job title as evidence for several capabilities.
- Mapping knowledge and understanding to the same definition-only response.
- Reusing one story across clauses without naming how it proves each one.
- Inventing certainty or advanced skill to avoid an honest evidence gap.

## Notes
The drill trains diagnosis before composition. It becomes progressively harder by increasing criterion length, adding descriptors, and mixing direct experience with transferable evidence. Speed matters only after the map consistently exposes obligations that a first reading would have missed.
