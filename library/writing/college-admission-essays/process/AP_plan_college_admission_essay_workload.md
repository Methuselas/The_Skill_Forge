# Plan the College Admission Essay Workload

## Objective
Turn a current college list and its actual writing requirements into a workable plan that covers every response, gives stable and high-leverage work enough time, and preserves capacity for changing prompts, added schools, and institution-specific supplements.

## Steps / Flow
1. **Establish the live entry state.** Begin with the working college list and the current instructions for each application route. Capture every required and optional prompt, word limit, and deadline. If a requirement is uncertain, mark it unresolved and verify it before treating the workload as complete.
2. **Map the whole workload.** Separate the central personal statement or other shareable core response from general short answers and institution-specific supplements. Give every requested response its own line even when several may eventually draw on the same material; never plan from the fiction that there is only one college essay.
3. **Sequence by stability and consequence.** Start stable shared work early and protect the responses tied to the earliest deadlines. Reserve explicit capacity for supplements that may change and for schools added later instead of filling the entire schedule with the first known set.
4. **Match work to attention.** Assign uninterrupted blocks to drafting, structural revision, and difficult prompt decisions. Use short intervals to talk through memories with knowledgeable people or capture details, phrases, and possible material. Keep collecting fragments until a usable cluster emerges, but do not let sentence cleanup displace structural work on an unstable draft.
5. **Branch on compatibility.** When two prompts impose substantially overlapping obligations, use the compatible-material Pattern before scheduling separate drafts. When their requested meanings, institutional purposes, or constraints diverge, budget distinct responses. Treat every institution-specific fit response as distinct work.
6. **Recover when requirements move.** If the college list, prompt, limit, route, or deadline changes, update the inventory first, identify which drafts are invalidated or need adaptation, and resequence the remaining work. Do not hide newly created work inside the old estimate.
7. **Close only against the current inventory.** Finish planning when every required response maps to a current prompt, limit, deadline, and application route; each has an owner and a drafting and revision window; volatile work retains buffer; and no unresolved requirement is being mistaken for optional work.

## Notes
This protocol governs the writing workload, not admission strategy or final submission. Dates, policies, prompts, and limits are volatile inputs and must come from current application requirements. The sequence matters because a missing supplement discovered after the shared statement has consumed all available time cannot be repaired by better prose alone, while premature line editing can make an early draft look finished before its content and structure are trustworthy.
