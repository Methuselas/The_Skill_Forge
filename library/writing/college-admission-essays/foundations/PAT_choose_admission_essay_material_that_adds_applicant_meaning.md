# Choose Admission Essay Material That Adds Applicant Meaning

## Pattern Rule
**IF** choosing what a college admission personal statement or supplement should reveal when the application already contains grades, activities, scores, and other facts
**THEN** select truthful material whose interpretation adds an applicant quality, value, response, context, contribution, or fit that the rest of the application cannot show on its own

## Do
- Read the surrounding application as an admission reader would, marking what it already establishes and what remains unseen, unexplained, or impersonal.
- Test each possible subject by naming the conclusion its handling could support: how the applicant meets difficulty, what they value, how their background shaped them, why an academic record needs context, what they may contribute, or why a particular institution fits.
- Keep the applicant's choices and understanding central even when the event, achievement, relative, or institution provides the visible subject.
- Evaluate a familiar or difficult topic by the specificity, self-focus, and reflection the writer can bring to it rather than by its reputation as impressive, ordinary, or taboo.
- Study sample essays for structural and stylistic possibilities, then rebuild the content from this applicant's own life and language.

## Don't
- Recap activities, honors, grades, or ambitions without adding meaning that the corresponding application field cannot supply.
- Chase a supposedly magic topic, gimmick, or admissions-office preference as if subject choice could guarantee an outcome.
- Reject a subject solely because someone has labeled its category overused or forbidden; reject it when the available treatment remains generic, unfocused, or unable to reveal the applicant.
- Copy another applicant's content because that person was admitted; the result cannot prove which part of the application mattered.
- Manufacture a flawless or strategic persona by saying what the writer imagines a committee wants to hear.

## Checklist
- Removing the essay would leave a meaningful dimension of the applicant unknown or unexplained.
- The intended impression arises from specific truthful material rather than praise the applicant simply asserts.
- The subject answers the actual prompt and, where relevant, establishes credible institutional fit.
- The topic remains worth writing after rumors about successful, failed, impressive, or taboo subjects are removed.
- Attention returns from the visible event or other person to the applicant's values, response, interpretation, or growth.

## Notes
An admission essay complements the application record rather than competing with it. The useful addition may be personal character, the influence of a background, context for an academic result, evidence of contribution, or a reason the applicant and institution fit. The document still has to be vivid and clear, but polish cannot rescue material that merely repeats known data.

No topic guarantees admission, and no broad category fails automatically. A common experience can become specific through the writer's perception and reflection, while an unusual experience can remain empty when it is treated as sufficient proof of distinction. Examples can demonstrate craft, but another applicant's outcome does not turn that applicant's subject into a formula.
