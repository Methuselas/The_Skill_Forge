# Protect Regular Writing Time at Peak Focus

## Pattern Rule
**IF** writing happens only when spare time and inspiration coincide
**THEN** reserve a recurring daily writing period at the time when concentration and creative energy are strongest
**ELSE** choose the most repeatable available period and defend it consistently.

## Do
- Separate a lack of available time from difficulty focusing during time that already exists; the remedies are different.
- Compare morning, evening, and other realistic windows by the work they actually produce, not by an idealized routine.
- Put the chosen period on the schedule before jobs, family duties, classes, and incidental demands consume it.
- Use the appointment to face the page even when the ideas that felt vivid beforehand temporarily disappear.

## Don't
- Do not wait for an unclaimed stretch of time to appear on its own.
- Do not call the problem “no time” when the available period is being lost to weak focus.
- Do not abandon the period because one session begins without an immediately usable idea.

## Checklist
- Is there a named recurring time for writing?
- Was that time selected from observed focus rather than convenience alone?
- Is the main obstacle scarcity of time or loss of attention?
- Did the session produce contact with the page even if the initial idea changed?

## Notes
Mosley's advice joins frequency to timing: write every day, but choose morning or night according to when the best work occurs. Starkey then separates overloaded schedules from available time without focus. That distinction prevents a calendar fix from being applied to an attention problem, or willpower from being blamed when the schedule genuinely has no protected space.
