# Read a Draft Aloud to Recover a Fresh Reader Perspective

## Pattern Rule
**IF** silent rereading has made a draft too familiar for its flaws to register
**THEN** speak the exact text at reader pace, preferably to a listener, and mark every place where voice, breath, expectation, or attention breaks
**ELSE** preserve silent reading for passes whose evidence depends on the page rather than the ear.

## Do
- Read what is written rather than silently correcting or paraphrasing it during delivery.
- Use an actual listener when available so the presence of another person's attention changes how the language is heard.
- Continue alone when no audience is available; a wall is enough to force the words into sound.
- Mark stumbles, unintended repetition, dead stretches, false emphasis, and places where the spoken sequence changes the expected meaning.
- Return to each mark and name whether the problem lies in language, pacing, structure, or missing context before revising it.

## Don't
- Do not perform around a weak sentence so convincingly that the delivery hides what the page cannot support.
- Do not stop at noticing that a passage sounds wrong; connect the audible break to a revision decision.
- Do not confuse this with explaining the draft's problem aloud: one speaks the text itself, while the other externalizes a diagnosis.
- Do not require a qualified critic when the immediate need is simply to make familiar language strange again.

## Checklist
- Was the draft spoken exactly as written?
- Did the pass expose a flaw that silent familiarity had concealed?
- Can each marked break be classified at a useful scale?
- If a listener was present, did the imagined reader response become more concrete?
- Did the audible evidence lead to at least one tested change on the page?

## Notes
Graham's method works by estrangement. Hearing familiar sentences turns them into an event unfolding in time, and a listener intensifies the effect by making the writer encounter the text through another person's attention. The route remains available without a workshop, teacher, tutor, or even a responsive audience; speaking to an empty room still interrupts the automatic corrections of silent rereading.
