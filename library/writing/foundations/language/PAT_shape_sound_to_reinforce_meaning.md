# Shape Sound to Reinforce Meaning

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a passage's phonetic texture is neutral, contradictory, or missing an audible relation that its subject or ideas could support
**THEN** select and arrange word sounds so recurrence, resemblance, pace, or pressure strengthens the intended meaning and movement
**ELSE** preserve unobtrusive sound when added patterning would become display

## Do
- Begin with the semantic job: name the words, actions, or ideas that should cohere, clash, accelerate, drag, strike, or soften before selecting sounds.
- Test correspondences by pronunciation rather than spelling; the ear determines whether a consonant, vowel, or imitative texture is actually present.
- Carry a chosen sound beyond adjacent words when a quieter recurrence across sentences or lines can bind distant material.
- Prefer the accurate word when a more sonically convenient one would weaken sense, voice, or tone.
- Read the passage aloud in context and adjust the density until the effect is audible without taking control away from syntax or thought.

## Don't
- Scatter a conspicuous letter through a passage merely to demonstrate a named device.
- Assume musical prose or poetry requires continuous decoration; neutral stretches can make selected sonic pressure more legible.
- Let an attractive echo bind words whose semantic association works against the intended effect.
- Imitate a sound so literally that the diction becomes cartoonish or calls attention away from the subject.

## Checklist
- The important recurrence or resemblance can be heard rather than inferred only from spelling.
- Each marked sound has a named relation to subject, action, tone, emphasis, or conceptual connection.
- Removing or relocating it would change the passage's movement or meaning.
- The diction remains accurate and the syntax remains intelligible.
- The designed texture is proportionate to the surrounding work rather than an isolated verbal stunt.

## Notes
`writing_variant_link_ideas_with_repeated_consonants_or_vowels` preserves the sound-linking route formerly owned by `writing_poetry_use_repeated_sounds_to_link_ideas`. Initial consonants can gather words conspicuously, consonance can carry a quieter relation through stressed positions, and assonance can sustain a tonal current across different endings. Use the variant when selected ideas benefit from audible cohesion, not as a quota of alliteration.

`writing_variant_use_onomatopoeia_to_echo_subject` turns resemblance toward the represented event. It may echo a literal noise, but it can also let the mouth enact force or movement through stops, friction, duration, or pace. Keep it only when the sound remains fitting to the work's tone and the chosen word is still semantically exact.
