# Choose a Truth Contract Before Shaping Real Material

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a creative work draws on actual people, events, memories, or historical facts
**THEN** decide whether it claims accountability to evidence or permits invention, and make every consequential transformation consistent with that declared relationship to truth
**ELSE** state the work's governing genre before factual and imagined material become difficult to distinguish

## Do
- Inventory which events, quotations, identities, and outcomes the work presents as having occurred before changing them for narrative effect.
- In evidence-bound work, shape through selection, emphasis, sequence, and perspective without fabricating incidents that the label asks readers to trust as fact.
- In fiction, research any factual frame that matters, then distinguish created minds, dialogue, scenes, or characters from documentary claims through the work's form and presentation.
- Revisit the contract when revision changes a remembered account into a composite, invented scene, or substantially altered outcome.

## Don't
- Treat the unavoidable selectivity of recollection as permission to invent freely inside nonfiction.
- Use a factual label to gain authority while relying on events that did not occur.
- Assume that retaining historical names and broad outlines makes invented interior experience into documented fact.
- Postpone the genre decision until after readers or editors discover incompatible claims in the draft.

## Checklist
- The work's label tells readers whether consequential details claim to be true.
- Every invented event, character, quotation, or interior state is permitted by that label.
- Evidence-bound passages can be distinguished from reconstruction, uncertainty, and interpretation.
- Research supports the factual frame without being mistaken for the primary artistic effect.
- Revision has not silently changed the work's truth claim.

## Notes
All narration selects and arranges details, but selection and fabrication are not the same permission. Fiction can adhere closely to real history while inventing access to characters' minds; nonfiction can use literary shaping while remaining accountable to what evidence supports. The decisive boundary is the promise made to readers, not how vivid, researched, or plausible the prose appears.

`writing_variant_signal_counterfactual_or_hybrid_fiction_before_borrowing_factual_authority` governs work whose documented frame can make invention look evidentiary. Use a title, subtitle, section label, framing statement, venue category, or immediately legible impossible premise to establish the fiction contract before invented access does consequential work. Maintain a private fact-and-invention map through revision, and recheck transitions where factual dates, quotations, or names meet created scenes. Subtlety may shape the artistic effect after the contract is legible; ambiguity must not recruit readers' trust in nonfiction to authenticate events the writer invented.
