# Reassess Inspired Drafts After Distance

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a first draft feels finished because it arrived fluently or during a burst of inspiration
**THEN** withhold final judgment, create distance or seek a trusted response, and return prepared to make both structural and sentence-level changes
**ELSE** continue the recursive cycle of drafting, rereading, feedback, and revision

## Do
- Let the immediate excitement cool before deciding that the piece is complete; even an overnight change in perspective can expose hidden flaws.
- Show vulnerable drafts to a small group of trusted writers or editors whose responses are candid without being needlessly destructive.
- Preserve the strongest material while remaining willing to cut most of a draft, rebuild its shape, or make smaller local adjustments.

## Don't
- Equate speed or emotional intensity during composition with accuracy, coherence, or completion.
- Limit later work to proofreading when the piece's ambition may require major reconception as well as polishing.

## Checklist
- The draft has been reread from a perspective separated from the composing moment.
- At least one global question and one local question were considered before declaring the work finished.
- Changes were chosen because they improve the piece, not merely because they are easy to make.

## Notes
Apparently effortless writing still has a process behind it: notes, thought, conversation, mental rehearsal, or earlier attempts may have prepared the fluent draft. Strong writing usually develops recursively. The writer returns, compares the existing work against what it could become, and alternates substantial surgery with careful refinement.
