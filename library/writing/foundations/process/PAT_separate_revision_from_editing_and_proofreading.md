# Separate Revision from Editing and Proofreading

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a creative draft needs more work before completion but the scale of the problem is unclear
**THEN** classify changes to conception, form, or content as revision, sentence-level error correction as editing, and the final search for residual mistakes as proofreading

## Do
- Test whether the protagonist, plot, governing shape, or selection of material must change before polishing sentences.
- Correct unintended sentence-level faults after the larger work is stable enough for local choices to hold.
- Reserve a clean final pass for mistakes such as wrong-word substitutions, apostrophe errors, and other oversights that survived earlier work.

## Don't
- Call a typo cleanup a thorough revision while the draft's purpose and overall shape remain unexamined.
- Use local correction to avoid deleting paragraphs, rebuilding a character, or reconceiving the work when those larger changes are needed.

## Checklist
- Every planned change is identified as global reconsideration, sentence-level correction, or final mistake removal.
- Structural and content questions have been answered before the last cleanup pass.
- Intentional departures from convention remain distinguishable from accidental errors.

## Notes
These activities can overlap in time, but they do different jobs. Revision reconsiders what the piece is and how it works; editing eliminates unintended sentence-level problems; proofreading is the final inspection for mistakes left behind. Treating them as synonyms encourages easy corrections to displace the harder work of reshaping a draft.
