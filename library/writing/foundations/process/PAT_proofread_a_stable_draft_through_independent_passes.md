# Proofread a Stable Draft Through Independent Passes

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a draft's content and structure are stable enough for final error removal
**THEN** inspect it through separate contextual, mechanical, auditory, and rendered-form passes, reviewing every suggested or applied correction
**ELSE** return to revision or editing before treating the text as a final proof

## Do
- Set the intended language variety and venue conventions before using automated spelling or grammar checks.
- Review suggestions in context because a checker can accept the wrong valid word, reject an intentional construction, or impose an unintended convention.
- Use a dictionary or other authoritative language reference when spelling, usage, or inflection remains uncertain.
- Change the reading mode: voice the text, inspect a printed or fixed-layout rendering, or ask a careful reader to look only for residual errors.
- Recheck the affected sentence and surrounding layout after every correction that changes wording, spacing, lineation, or pagination.

## Don't
- Treat a clean automated report as evidence that meaning, names, homophones, punctuation, and intentional departures are correct.
- Mix substantial rewriting into the final inspection without reopening the earlier stage and repeating the affected checks.
- Accept every automated correction merely because the proposed form is grammatical in isolation.
- Assume the editable file and the delivered rendering contain the same line breaks, characters, spacing, or version.

## Checklist
- Content and structure were stable before the final passes began.
- Automated suggestions were adjudicated rather than accepted in bulk.
- At least one pass changed how the text was encountered rather than repeating the same silent screen reading.
- Intentional departures remain distinguishable from accidental mistakes.
- The final rendered or transmitted form was checked after the last correction.

## Notes
Different passes expose different failures. Automation is useful for generating suspects but cannot reliably infer context or intention; oral reading interrupts visual familiarity; mechanical inspection catches recurring surface classes; and a rendered-form check reveals errors introduced by layout or transmission. A large late change invalidates the proof around it, so the honest recovery is to reopen editing and inspect again.
