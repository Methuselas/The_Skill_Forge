# Research and Shortlist Publication Outlets

## Objective
Turn one finished or nearly finished creative work into a current, evidence-backed shortlist of suitable publication, competition, or public-feedback outlets, with exact approach requirements and credible backups ready for the later submission stage.

## Steps / Flow
1. **Set the entry conditions.** Choose a particular work strong and stable enough to assess as a whole. Decide whether the immediate goal is public feedback, competition entry, magazine or journal publication, book publication, or another defined form of circulation. If the work still changes category or governing purpose whenever it is reread, return to development before searching broadly.
2. **Build the work's submission identity.** Name its form, scale or length, genre or governing mode, subject or theme, stylistic profile, likely readers, and any medium requirements. Activate `writing_calibrate_context_to_audience_and_venue` to distinguish intended readers, plausible wider circulation, genre promises, and context the destination must support. **Definition gate:** search terms and eligibility checks must be derivable from the work rather than from a vague wish to be published.
3. **Build a current candidate pool.** Use several routes: current professional or genre directories, official calls and listings, publishers or periodicals attached to comparable work, relevant booksellers or libraries, and trusted field organizations. Search at the level that receives the work--publication, imprint, department, series, or competition--and include different scales or media when they could serve the stated goal.
4. **Screen hard constraints first.** At each candidate's current official source, verify that it is active, open through the proposed route, willing to consider this form and length, and compatible with any stated eligibility, prior-publication, exclusivity, geographic, thematic, or technical conditions. Remove a candidate that fails a hard rule; do not spend close-reading time trying to argue around an automatic rejection.
5. **Test editorial and reader fit.** Activate `writing_match_finished_work_to_outlet_actual_list`. Read recent output and compare form, length, style, subject treatment, reader promise, and degree of innovation. State both why the work belongs and what distinct contribution it makes. **Fit gate:** retain no outlet supported only by reputation, a broad category, or an old listing.
6. **Compare the eligible routes.** Activate `writing_compare_publication_outlets_by_work_specific_tradeoffs`. Rank candidates against the publication goal using verified reach, editorial attention, speed, selectivity, medium, durability, access, presentation, and relationship factors. If the apparent first choice wins only by prestige, reopen the comparison.
7. **Create an ordered shortlist.** Keep the strongest match first and several credible alternatives after it. Record the evidence for fit, the decisive tradeoffs, and the reason each candidate remains preferable to discarded options. Preserve different fallback routes when they serve the work, rather than filling the list with near-identical names.
8. **Capture the approach contract.** For every shortlisted outlet, record the current contact route, requested manuscript portion or complete work, supporting materials, formatting and file requirements, deadlines, fees where applicable, response expectations, and rules governing eligibility or simultaneous use. Leave proposal writing, packaging, and transmission to the later submission action.
9. **Complete.** Stop when every shortlisted outlet is currently reachable and eligible, recent output supports the claimed match, the ranking follows work-specific priorities, the first-contact requirements are complete enough to act on, and rejection by the first choice would route directly to a researched alternative rather than to a fresh unfocused search.

## Notes
The order prevents expensive research from attaching to the wrong work or the wrong receiving desk. Broad discovery comes before narrow comparison, but hard eligibility comes before deep reading; actual output then tests the fit that labels only suggest. The process does not promise acceptance. It replaces avoidable mismatch with a defensible submission route and preserves alternatives so rejection becomes a branch in the plan rather than a verdict on the work.
