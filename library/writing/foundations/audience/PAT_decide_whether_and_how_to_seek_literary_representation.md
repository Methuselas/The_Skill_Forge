# Decide Whether and How to Seek Literary Representation

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a polished book-length or otherwise representable project is ready for professional submission and an agent may provide useful market access, negotiation, or career guidance
**THEN** verify whether representation serves this form and project, then compare legitimate candidates by current fit, relevant sales or placements, terms, working relationship, and conflicts before agreeing to representation
**ELSE** use a verified direct-submission route or defer the search until the work and market justify it

## Do
- Check current publisher and agent submission routes to determine whether this kind of work is normally represented, optionally represented, or commonly submitted directly.
- Define what representation must add for this project, such as access to otherwise closed destinations, informed placement, contract and rights negotiation, strategic advice, or continuing career management.
- Research agents through current first-party profiles, recent relevant deals or placements, named clients and publishers, professional organizations, and recommendations from writers with direct experience.
- Compare each candidate's stated interests with the work's form, audience, subject, scale, and likely market instead of treating general reputation as sufficient fit.
- Examine the proposed scope of representation, commission and other costs, duration, termination, rights covered, handling of money, communication expectations, and authority to act before signing.
- Ask focused questions about submission strategy, editorial involvement, reporting, response times, foreign or subsidiary rights, conflicts, and what happens when the project does not sell.
- Seek qualified independent advice when contract language, rights, money handling, or termination consequences remain unclear.

## Don't
- Assume every publisher requires an agent, every project benefits from one, or short work follows the same route as a book-length manuscript.
- Choose solely by prestige, enthusiasm, a conference encounter, or an unverified recommendation.
- Pay or surrender rights without understanding what service, authority, duration, and exit terms the agreement creates.
- Treat an agent's interest as proof that the manuscript is ready, the agent can place this particular work, or publication is guaranteed.
- Depend on old directories, historic client lists, remembered commission rates, or generalized industry advice when current information is available.
- Query broadly before confirming that each candidate is open, legitimate, and a plausible match for the project.

## Checklist
- The project is complete to the degree its current market expects and polished enough for professional evaluation.
- A current route check explains why representation is useful or necessary for this work.
- Each candidate has verified, relevant experience and a plausible destination strategy.
- Services, costs, rights, authority, communication, conflicts, duration, and termination are understood.
- Unclear contractual or financial consequences have received qualified independent review.
- The chosen route remains defensible if the most prestigious candidate is removed from consideration.

## Notes
Representation is a business and working relationship, not a ceremonial mark of seriousness. Its value depends on the project, the destinations available without it, the candidate's current knowledge and relationships, and the terms under which the agent acts. A strong match can reduce unfocused searching, reach otherwise inaccessible editors, and improve negotiation; a poor or unnecessary match can add delay, cost, divided incentives, or loss of control. Because markets, personnel, practices, and contract terms change, the durable skill is to verify the present route and agreement rather than preserve inherited percentages or universal rules.
