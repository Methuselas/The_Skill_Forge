# Track Each Writing Submission as a Distinct Transaction

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a creative work or proposal has been sent, entered, uploaded, or otherwise placed under consideration
**THEN** create one dated record that identifies the exact work version, receiver, route, materials, constraints, confirmation, status, and outcome of that transaction
**ELSE** keep the candidate in research or preparation rather than marking it as submitted

## Do
- Record the work title and exact version or archival copy that the receiver received.
- Identify the publication, imprint, competition, agency, editor, or receiving desk precisely enough to distinguish similar routes within one organization.
- Capture the transmission date, method, package components, amount of material, and any confirmation or receipt identifier.
- Note the receiver's stated response window, follow-up rule, simultaneous-submission condition, exclusivity, deadline, or other constraint that affects the next action.
- Give the transaction a controlled status such as prepared, sent, acknowledged, more material requested, withdrawn, declined, accepted, or closed.
- Date every material status change and preserve the outcome or next action without overwriting the original submission facts.
- Link a request for more material, revised resubmission, or transfer to a new record when the receiver sees a meaningfully different package.

## Don't
- Track only the work title and outlet name while leaving the sent version and package contents ambiguous.
- Treat preparation, an unsent draft email, or an incomplete upload as a completed submission.
- Infer rejection from silence before the stated response or follow-up rule permits that conclusion.
- Overwrite a declined or withdrawn record when sending the same work elsewhere.
- Keep essential restrictions only in memory, an inbox, or a portal that may become unavailable.
- Mix research notes, wish-list candidates, and actual transmissions under one undifferentiated status.

## Checklist
- One record corresponds to one actual receiving transaction.
- The exact work version and every submitted component are recoverable.
- Receiver, route, date, and available confirmation are present.
- Response timing and action constraints are visible before another submission is made.
- Status changes retain dates and do not erase the original facts.
- The outcome or next permitted action is unambiguous.

## Notes
Once several works or destinations are active, memory cannot reliably preserve which version, sample, or promise went where. Transaction-level records protect both craft and professional decisions: the writer can answer a request with the right material, avoid incompatible commitments, interpret silence according to the correct rule, and route a declined work to the next researched destination without reconstructing the past.
