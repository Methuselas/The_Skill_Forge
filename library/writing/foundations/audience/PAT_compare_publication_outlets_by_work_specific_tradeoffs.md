# Compare Publication Outlets by Work-Specific Tradeoffs

## Pattern Rule
**IF** several publication channels or outlets are credible matches for the same work
**THEN** rank them by the work's actual goals and by verified differences in reach, editorial attention, speed, selectivity, medium, durability, access, and author relationship
**ELSE** return to fit research before using prestige or convenience as a substitute for eligibility

## Do
- State what this publication attempt is meant to achieve, such as informed feedback, a particular readership, discoverability, durable availability, professional editing, community, compensation, or a credible next step.
- Compare individual outlets rather than assuming every large, small, local, print, digital, or competition venue behaves like its category.
- Test claimed reach against evidence of actual readership, distribution, discoverability, participation, or sales rather than treating potential access as an audience already won.
- Weigh broad distribution and production capacity against the possibility of slower access, less individual attention, narrower acquisition priorities, or heavier internal competition.
- Weigh a smaller or more specialized outlet's closer fit and attention against its staffing limits, pace, distribution, editorial resources, and stability.
- Account for medium-specific effects, including searchability, multimedia capability, interaction, revision or removal, permanence, and the way the work will actually be encountered.

## Don't
- Equate a famous name, large prize, national scale, or printed format with the best destination for this work.
- Assume digital access guarantees readers, low barriers, weak editing, or lower selectivity.
- Assume a small or local outlet will necessarily provide careful editing, quick decisions, close relationships, or durable distribution.
- Choose only by acceptance odds when the likely readership or published form would defeat the reason for submitting.
- Treat one rejection as evidence that a whole medium, scale, or class of outlet is closed to the work.

## Checklist
- The desired result of publication is explicit.
- Each comparison uses current evidence about a named outlet rather than a category stereotype.
- Reach means plausible contact with the intended readers, not theoretical availability.
- Editorial process, timing, selectivity, presentation, access, and durability have been considered where they affect the work.
- The preferred outlet wins on work-relevant priorities rather than prestige alone.
- The remaining shortlist preserves materially different fallback routes.

## Notes
No scale or medium is universally superior. A large organization may offer distribution and production capacity while giving one project less attention; a small or specialized outlet may understand an unusual work while having limited staff or reach. Digital publication may combine speed, global access, search, sound, motion, and interaction, yet still require active discovery and may be as selective as print. The useful comparison asks what the work needs from publication and what each actual outlet can reliably provide.
