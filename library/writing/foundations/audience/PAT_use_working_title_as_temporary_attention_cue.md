# Use a Working Title as a Temporary Attention Cue

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an untitled creative project is hard to identify or discuss, or its current title overcommits the draft to one interpretation
**THEN** choose a clearly temporary title by comparing what different candidates foreground, then revisit it after the work reveals its actual center
**ELSE** keep the existing title while it continues to frame the intended work without constraining discovery

## Do
- Generate candidates from different elements: a central character, relationship, place, theme, significant object, governing action, consequential phrase, or transformed allusion.
- For each serious candidate, state what it causes a reader or writer to notice first and what part of the work it pushes into the background.
- Prefer a working title that makes the project easy to retrieve and holds open enough space for the current drafting question.
- Mark the title as temporary in project notes when collaborators, readers, or filenames might otherwise mistake it for a final commitment.
- Reopen the choice after a major discovery, completed draft, genre change, or shift in the story's central relation.
- Test late candidates against the finished work's tone, scale, distinctiveness, and reader-facing promise before finalizing one.

## Don't
- Delay drafting because no candidate yet feels memorable, marketable, or definitive.
- Keep the first label merely because it has become familiar while the work has moved elsewhere.
- Choose a title that reveals an unintended secret, misstates the genre promise, or narrows attention to a minor element.
- Treat a publisher's, producer's, or collaborator's later title change as evidence that the drafting title failed at its temporary job.
- Borrow a recognizable title or phrase so closely that the new work depends on another work's identity.

## Checklist
- Several candidates foreground meaningfully different elements of the work.
- The selected label helps identify and discuss the project now.
- Its temporary status is clear wherever confusion would matter.
- The title does not block discoveries the draft is still entitled to make.
- A late-stage review checks the title against the work that actually exists.
- The final choice creates an accurate and intentional emphasis rather than merely sounding attractive.

## Notes
A title directs attention before the first sentence is read, but the writer may not know the work's true emphasis until drafting is advanced. Treating the early title as a tool rather than a verdict resolves that tension. It can stabilize project identity, make versions findable, and keep one live question visible while remaining disposable when the story grows beyond it. Final title selection is a later framing decision made against the completed work, not an entrance exam the idea must pass before writing begins.
