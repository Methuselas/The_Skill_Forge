# Write a Factual Outlet-Specific Query

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an editor, agent, publication, or submission system requires a query, cover letter, introductory email, or equivalent first-contact statement
**THEN** state the offer, work, verified destination fit, relevant author qualification, requested materials, and response path concisely in the receiver's required form
**ELSE** do not manufacture a pitch document that adds friction or violates the submission route

## Do
- Address the named relevant person when the receiver identifies one; otherwise use the correct role or department without guessing identity or form of address.
- Open by stating why contact is being made and what work is offered.
- Describe the work factually: give its title, form, completed status when relevant, approximate length, central subject or story promise, and any category the receiver uses.
- Connect the work to the exact outlet, imprint, list, or agent through one or two specific fit claims supported by current research.
- Include only publishing history, awards, training, lived or professional expertise, audience access, or other background that materially helps evaluate this work.
- State which requested sample, synopsis, manuscript, or proposal accompanies the contact and what further material is available.
- Close with accurate contact details and any response information the receiving contract requests.

## Don't
- Praise the work with predictions of greatness, commercial certainty, cultural importance, or inevitable reader response.
- Retell the whole plot when a synopsis is a separate component, or substitute atmosphere for a clear account of what the work is.
- Give an autobiography, apologize for limited credentials, or pad the letter with irrelevant achievements.
- Send generic praise that could name any outlet, or claim fit without showing what in the destination's current list supports it.
- Become casual or expansive because the same questions appear in an email or online form.
- Offer attachments, links, or materials the receiver forbids or has not requested.

## Checklist
- The contact is addressed to the correct person, role, or desk.
- The opening identifies the offer without delay.
- The description supplies form, scale, subject or premise, and completed status where relevant.
- The fit claim is specific, current, and consistent with the work.
- Every author detail helps evaluate this project.
- Included and available materials match the receiver's instructions.
- The whole query is concise, factual, accurate, and complete in the required medium.

## Notes
The query is both an access document and a sample of professional judgment. Its job is not to prove that the work is a masterpiece; it lets a receiver decide quickly whether the project belongs in the next stage. Restraint makes that decision easier. A concrete description, a researched fit claim, and only relevant credentials create more confidence than unsupported enthusiasm or a long personal history.
