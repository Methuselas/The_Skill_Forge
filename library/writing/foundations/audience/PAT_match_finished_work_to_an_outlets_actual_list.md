# Match Finished Work to an Outlet's Actual List

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a finished creative work is being matched to a publisher, magazine, competition, imprint, or other public outlet
**THEN** compare the work's form, genre, length, style, subject, readership, and distinctive contribution with the outlet's current rules and actual recent output
**ELSE** keep developing or testing the work instead of performing an unfocused market search

## Do
- Describe the particular work before searching: name its form, scale, genre or governing mode, subject or theme, stylistic profile, and plausible readers.
- Verify from a current first-party source that the exact publication, imprint, department, series, or competition accepts this kind of work and is open to the proposed route of approach.
- Read enough recent output to compare length, style, form, subject treatment, audience promise, and degree of experimentation rather than relying on a directory category alone.
- Test compatibility and distinction together: the work must belong in the outlet's list while giving an editor a reason to choose it over material the list already supplies.
- Record the outlet's initial-contact method, required materials, formatting constraints, eligibility limits, and any rule whose violation would prevent consideration.
- Keep several evidence-backed alternatives so one rejection does not force a new search from the beginning.

## Don't
- Send work to a large organization by its broad name when only one imprint, department, or title handles the relevant category.
- Assume strong writing can overcome a mismatch in form, length, readership, eligibility, or submission route.
- Depend on an old directory entry, remembered policy, search-result summary, or famous backlist when current official information is available.
- Treat resemblance to existing titles as sufficient when the work adds no legible difference, or emphasize novelty that makes the project incompatible with the list.
- Reshape a finished piece around a prestigious destination before establishing that the destination is genuinely suitable.

## Checklist
- The work can be described concretely enough to direct a search.
- The exact outlet and receiving route are current and open to the proposed submission.
- Recent published work supplies evidence for the claimed fit.
- Both list compatibility and a meaningful point of difference can be stated.
- Every disqualifying rule and required first-contact element has been captured.
- At least one credible backup remains if the preferred outlet declines the work.

## Notes
Publication decisions are made within lists, audiences, production constraints, and competing projects; quality is necessary but does not erase those contexts. The writer's practical task is therefore narrower than finding a prestigious publisher and broader than matching a genre label. A defensible fit connects the particular manuscript to the particular receiving list, shows how readers of that list can enter it, and identifies the difference that keeps compatibility from becoming redundancy.
