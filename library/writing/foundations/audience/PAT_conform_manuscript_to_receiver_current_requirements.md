# Conform a Manuscript to the Receiver's Current Requirements

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a stable creative manuscript is being prepared for a particular editor, agent, publication, competition, or submission system
**THEN** make its final presentation satisfy that receiver's current explicit requirements, using restrained readability and consistent identification wherever the instructions are silent
**ELSE** keep the work in an editable drafting form rather than imposing a speculative submission layout

## Do
- Capture the receiver's current rules for layout, file type, filename, length, identifying information, pagination, title material, and delivery before formatting the manuscript.
- Let explicit instructions override remembered industry custom, examples from another outlet, or a personal house style.
- Use a clean, legible, unobtrusive presentation where no rule is stated, with enough identification and ordering to keep the work intelligible if its parts are separated.
- Confirm that the author name or permitted pseudonym, title, contact route, word count, and version are accurate and consistent wherever the receiver requests them.
- Proofread the stable text before presentation, then inspect the actual file, preview, print rendering, or uploaded copy after the last layout change.
- Keep a locked copy of exactly what was sent so the submitted version can later be identified.

## Don't
- Assume a preferred font, paper size, margin, title-page convention, or postal method is universal.
- Use decorative typography, novelty materials, or elaborate design to compensate for weak fit or unfinished writing.
- Leave comments, tracked changes, revision marks, hidden notes, broken characters, or accidental metadata visible in the delivered form.
- Rely on a spellchecker or the editable document alone as proof that the rendered manuscript is accurate.
- Add rights statements, personal data, or supporting pages that the receiver did not request merely because another market expects them.

## Checklist
- The requirements came from the exact current receiver and route.
- Every explicit layout, file, naming, identification, length, and ordering rule is satisfied.
- Silent choices are readable, restrained, and internally consistent.
- The offered text is the intended stable version and has completed final proofreading.
- The actual delivered rendering opens correctly and contains no production residue.
- An identical archival copy can identify what the receiver saw.

## Notes
Presentation is part of access: avoidable friction can prevent a reader from reaching otherwise strong work. Yet professional presentation is not a frozen set of dimensions or materials. Receiving systems, media, and house preferences change. The durable decision is to distinguish explicit local requirements from fallback conventions, minimize visual interference when choice remains, and verify the delivered object after formatting has had its own chance to introduce errors.
