# Choose Privacy or Critique Before Sharing Personal Work

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a draft's personal meaning makes craft criticism feel like an intrusion
**THEN** decide whether the piece will remain private or enter a public revision process before distributing it for response
**ELSE** share it with a clear statement of the feedback the next draft needs.

## Do
- Separate the value of the lived experience from the effectiveness of its treatment on the page.
- Ask whether the present need is private expression, unqualified support, or help making a communicable work stronger.
- Keep the piece out of workshop when meter, character, structure, or other craft comments would violate the purpose for writing it.
- When choosing critique, tell readers the intended audience and the decisions on which response would be useful.
- Substitute another piece when participation is required but this material is not ready to become public.

## Don't
- Do not assume that writing about loss, intimacy, or another painful event creates an obligation to expose it.
- Do not request craft response while treating every observation about the artifact as a judgment of the experience behind it.
- Do not offer a piece to a workshop merely to obtain praise and then defend it against all revision evidence.
- Do not claim a public readership while reserving every choice from a reader's scrutiny.

## Checklist
- Is this version meant for the writer, a named intimate, a workshop, or a wider audience?
- Would specific craft criticism be useful now, or would it violate the reason the piece exists?
- If response is invited, can the desired effect and feedback question be stated?
- Is there another piece available if this one should remain private?
- Has the decision been made before, rather than after, readers receive the draft?

## Notes
Starkey grants the writer's right to keep intensely personal material outside critique, especially when a recent experience makes technical comments beside the point. The boundary changes when the work is presented to classmates and an instructor for improvement: circulation makes the artifact public enough to receive craft response. The useful decision therefore precedes sharing and distinguishes protection, praise, and revision rather than treating them as the same request.
