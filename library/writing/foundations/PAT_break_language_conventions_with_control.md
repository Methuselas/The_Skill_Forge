# Break Language Conventions With Control

## Pattern Rule
**IF** creative effect calls for fragments, nonstandard grammar, speech rhythm, or another departure from edited prose
**THEN** make the departure intentional and carefully wrought while editing every surrounding mistake that does not serve the effect

## Do
- Distinguish a chosen sentence fragment from a sentence that simply lost control of its syntax.
- When characters speak nonstandard grammar, shape the pattern to reveal voice rather than using inconsistency as shorthand.
- Proofread spelling, punctuation, and grammar around every deliberate exception.
- Study how professional poets, fiction writers, nonfiction writers, and playwrights remain highly conscious of sentences while experimenting.
- Use brevity, grace, rhythm, or another observable payoff to justify the deviation.

## Don't
- Do not submit an unrevised first draft on the assumption that creativity will make unintended errors irrelevant.
- Do not treat freedom from one convention as freedom from care with language as a medium.
- Do not label an accidental lapse experimental after the fact.

## Checklist
- Can each nonstandard choice be connected to a specific effect?
- Are unintended errors removed from the same passage?
- Does the language show control at sentence and rhythm level?
- Would restoring the convention weaken the intended voice or effect?

## Notes
The source allows fragments and mimicked speech errors while rejecting the belief that creative writing has no standards. Its comparison to abstract painting and free jazz locates experimentation after command of the medium, not before it. The practical boundary is visible control: professional experimental writing remains carefully made even when it refuses a conventional surface.
