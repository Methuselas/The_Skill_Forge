# Develop Writing Through Recursive Returns

## Objective
Move a seed through drafting, distance, response, structural revision, and polishing without treating the first complete draft as the final act of writing.

## Steps / Flow
**Entry Conditions**
- A seed exists as an idea, image, phrase, note, or spoken possibility.
- The work can be revisited rather than judged from one inspired session.

**Persistent Invariants**
- Completion of a pass creates material for the next decision; it does not prove the work is finished.
- Returning may preserve nearly everything, alter a few sentences, or retain one passage and discard the rest.
- Ambition increases the probable need for both major surgery and later polishing.

**Flow**
1. **Enter by the available route.** Talk through the seed, make notes, or begin drafting directly.
2. **Reach a complete first pass.** Continue through easy momentum or repeated stalls until the whole draft exists.
3. **Create distance or response.** Show the work to a small trusted reader group, or put it aside long enough to encounter it with fresher judgment.
4. **Return to the governing decisions.** Reopen the piece and decide what remains alive, what misdirects it, and whether the structure needs reconstruction.
5. **Revise at the necessary scale.** Tweak sentences when the design holds; when it does not, save the strongest passage and rebuild or delete the rest.
6. **Generate again.** Make new notes, talk through the changed problem, and produce another pass.
7. **Polish after structure stabilizes.** Tinker with language and finish only after the major choices can support that attention.

**Failure / Rollback Rules**
- If overnight brilliance looks weaker in daylight, resume at structural inspection rather than defending the first draft's origin story.
- If feedback feels too exposed to use, narrow the reader group to people trusted not to respond with indiscriminate negativity.
- If line editing repeats while the piece remains wrong, return to the governing idea, order, or retained passage.

**Completion Criteria**
- The piece has survived at least one deliberate return.
- Feedback or distance produced a named revision decision.
- Structural work and polishing occurred at the scales the piece required.
- The current version is stronger because of re-entry, not merely newer.

## Notes
Starkey's process is recursive rather than a fixed march: notes, talk, drafting, stalls, readers, incubation, cuts, and fresh notes can recur in different orders. Even apparently effortless deadline writing contains invisible preparation such as reporting notes, private thought, conversation, or mental rehearsal. The protocol makes those hidden returns explicit without insisting that every writer use the same entry route.
