# Revise Toward Intended Effect Before Sentence Polish

## Objective
Bring an existing draft closer to the work it is trying to become by diagnosing whole-piece differences before spending attention on sentence correction and final cleanup.

## Steps / Flow
**Entry Conditions**
- A draft exists, even if it is scattered, incomplete in shape, or disappointing on rereading.
- The writer can state a goal, desired effect, or working ideal for the work.

**Persistent Invariants**
- Revision may begin before a complete first draft and recur throughout composition.
- The relevant unit of change may be a protagonist, plot, sequence, paragraph, image, phrase, or word.
- Editing and proofreading can accompany revision, but they do not substitute for reconsidering the work's governing choices.

**Flow**
1. **Name the desired state.** State what the piece is trying to do, become, or make the reader experience.
2. **Read for the whole shape.** Set aside sentence errors long enough to observe the draft's general patterns of development, emphasis, and form.
3. **Diagnose the difference.** Compare the existing text with the desired state and name where the draft diverges.
4. **Choose the scale of change.** Decide what should be included, excluded, expanded, cut, reordered, or reconceptualized.
5. **Make global changes first.** Rework the governing structure, character, plot, or sequence before protecting local passages.
6. **Compare again.** Reread the changed whole against the goal and repeat the diagnostic cycle while consequential gaps remain.
7. **Edit the stable draft.** Eliminate sentence-level errors after the larger decisions can support the attention.
8. **Proofread last.** Make a final pass for residual slips such as confused words, punctuation errors, and accidental omissions.

**Failure / Rollback Rules**
- If line edits accumulate while the piece still feels wrong, return to the desired state and whole-shape diagnosis.
- If the first draft's wording blocks a necessary redesign, preserve only the live material and rebuild around it.
- If no ideal form can yet be named, describe the intended reader effect as a working target and revise toward that testable result.

**Completion Criteria**
- A named goal or intended effect guided the pass.
- At least one inclusion, exclusion, or structural decision came from whole-draft comparison.
- Sentence editing followed rather than concealed the global diagnosis.
- The revised whole is measurably closer to the desired state, and the final proofread did not introduce new design decisions.

## Notes
Starkey separates revision from two smaller operations: editing removes sentence-level errors, and proofreading catches final slips. The larger process compares the actual draft with a goal or ideal, diagnoses the difference, and alters whatever scale the gap requires. Sommers's account supplies the whole-piece lens: an early draft may only mark territory, while a later pass discovers patterns and decides what belongs. The cycle is recursive because each change creates a new whole to compare.
