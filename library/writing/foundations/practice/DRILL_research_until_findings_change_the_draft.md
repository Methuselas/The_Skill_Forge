# Research until Findings Change the Draft

## Practice Task
Investigate one bounded uncertainty in a premise or draft, then revise so verified findings change its action, material conditions, language, or interpretation.

## Target Skill
Use research as a selective generative and corrective tool rather than as decoration, accumulated background, or private reassurance.

## Setup
Choose a short premise or passage containing one important unknown about a place, period, practice, object, institution, event, or social condition. State the work's truth contract and prepare a copy that can be compared with the revision.

## Instructions
1. Convert the unknown into two or three answerable questions whose answers could change what happens, what is possible, or what the writing responsibly claims.
2. Match each question to suitable evidence such as authoritative reference works, primary documents, reliable reporting, specialist scholarship, interviews, direct observation, maps, or field inquiry.
3. Gather several candidate findings and record enough source information in working notes to verify them. Prefer corroboration when a consequential claim depends on one doubtful or interested account.
4. For each finding, name one possible function: prevent a false note, create a constraint or opportunity, sharpen language, complicate an assumption, redirect causality, or establish necessary context.
5. Revise the passage using only findings that perform a function. Let factual conditions alter action or understanding instead of adding a detached paragraph of information.
6. Recheck every included fact after compression, paraphrase, or fictional integration. Keep invention within the declared truth contract.
7. Compare versions. Remove any researched detail whose deletion changes neither credibility, causality, atmosphere, character, inquiry, nor reader understanding.
8. Stop when the named questions are answered well enough for the draft's purpose; write a new question only when the revision exposes a consequential gap.

## Success Check
- Research began from bounded questions rather than indiscriminate collection.
- Sources were chosen for suitability and reliability, not mere availability.
- At least one verified finding changes a draft decision or prevents a material error.
- Included information performs a named narrative, poetic, reflective, or contextual function.
- The revision contains no research dump and remains intelligible after unused notes disappear.
- The stopping point follows the work's needs rather than a quota of facts or sources.

## Common Failures
- Searching broadly until an interesting fact appears without knowing what decision it could affect.
- Treating search rank, repetition, or confident wording as evidence of reliability.
- Keeping every gathered detail to prove that research occurred.
- Using factual information only as atmosphere when it should constrain action or correct causality.
- Continuing research to avoid drafting, or stopping as soon as the writer feels informed.
- Letting vivid invention silently replace evidence in a truth-bound work.

## Notes
Research expands direct experience by supplying material conditions and possibilities the writer could not responsibly invent from intuition alone. Its value appears in the changed work: a route becomes impossible, an ordinary tool suggests an action, a social practice alters a relationship, or a fact complicates the original premise. Most notes may remain off the page because their job was to improve the writer's available decisions, not to become exposition.
