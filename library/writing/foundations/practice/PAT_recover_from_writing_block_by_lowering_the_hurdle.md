# Recover from Writing Block by Lowering the Hurdle

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a writing task stalls because its scope or demands exceed the writer's current experience
**THEN** reduce the assignment to a short form with explicit boundaries, then keep producing material inside those limits
**ELSE** diagnose the obstacle by explaining aloud what you are trying to make and why it feels difficult

## Do
- Choose a form small enough to finish, such as one brief poem, scene, or compact prose piece with a clear endpoint.
- Reserve a recurring writing time that coincides with the part of the day when focused work comes most readily.
- Keep reading and talking about the work while drafting; either activity can expose the problem that silent effort has concealed.

## Don't
- Treat a temporary loss of ideas as proof that you lack the ability to write.
- Wait for resistance to disappear before making more language; the next manageable attempt is part of overcoming it.

## Checklist
- The reduced task has an observable finish line and fewer demands than the stalled version.
- The session produced new words rather than only plans for future writing.
- If the smaller scope did not help, the obstacle can now be named more precisely after being explained aloud.

## Notes
Block often appears when the current hurdle is too large for the writer's present experience and skill. Clearly bounded short forms turn an indefinite ambition into a reachable completion, while continued writing supplies the experience needed for harder work later. Talking remains useful even when the listener has no special expertise because articulating the difficulty can reveal it.
