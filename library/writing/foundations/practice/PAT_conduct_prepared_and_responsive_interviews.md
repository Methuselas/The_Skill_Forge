# Conduct Prepared and Responsive Interviews

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a written portrait or inquiry depends on another person's knowledge, experience, or voice
**THEN** research the subject, prepare broad question routes, record the exchange reliably, and let attentive follow-ups pursue what the answers actually reveal
**ELSE** use documentary research or direct observation when an interview would add no distinct access

## Do
- Learn available background first, then turn the remaining gaps into questions the subject is uniquely positioned to answer.
- Use who, what, where, when, why, and how to widen the prepared list beyond yes-or-no confirmation.
- Arrange a few accessible opening questions before material that requires longer reflection, vulnerability, or detailed chronology.
- Listen for an unexpected claim, hesitation, contradiction, image, or change in tone and ask a focused follow-up while the thread is live.
- Capture the exchange with a reliable recording method when possible and keep contemporaneous notes that mark names, spellings, gestures, setting, and moments worth locating again.
- Attend to how an answer is delivered as well as what it states when rhythm, emphasis, silence, or manner materially affects the portrait.

## Don't
- Recite the prepared list so rigidly that a revealing answer cannot change the next question.
- Ask only for information already available from elementary background research.
- Depend on memory for exact wording, sequence, or nuance when the interview will support consequential claims.
- Treat a polished anecdote as complete merely because it is vivid; test dates, causes, and missing participants where they matter.
- Convert delivery, accent, or mannerism into caricature when it adds no insight into the subject.

## Checklist
- Background work made the interview questions more specific than a generic questionnaire.
- Prepared questions cover factual grounding, experience, interpretation, and consequence where relevant.
- At least one follow-up arises from the subject's answer rather than the original list.
- Quotable wording and factual details can be recovered from a recording or dependable notes.
- The resulting material distinguishes what the subject said from the writer's inference about it.
- Details of delivery retained for the draft serve characterization or meaning rather than ridicule.

## Notes
Preparation and responsiveness solve opposite interview failures. Research prevents the conversation from spending its limited time on facts available elsewhere; listening prevents that preparation from turning the subject into a source who can only fill predetermined blanks. The useful interview follows a planned field of inquiry while remaining alert to the answer that reveals a better question.
