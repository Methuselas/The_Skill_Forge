# Dramatize a Bounded Memory without Faking Certainty

## Practice Task
Draft one compact remembered scene, audit every vivid detail for its basis, and revise the scene according to an explicitly chosen fiction or nonfiction truth contract.

## Target Skill
Shape remembered experience through sensory presence and temporal movement without disguising reconstruction as certainty or letting fidelity prevent permitted fictional transformation.

## Setup
Choose one remembered place, person, or event containing a perceptual or emotional change. Before drafting, state whether the exercise will become evidence-bound nonfiction or fiction that permits invention.

## Instructions
1. Limit the scene to one governing moment with a starting condition, a turn, and an altered understanding. Note later context separately so hindsight does not enter the experiencing self unnoticed.
2. Draft the moment in present tense for a short fixed length. Use ordinary actions, exact sensory particulars, and what the earlier self noticed, feared, wanted, misunderstood, or could not yet know.
3. Admit uncertainty where it belongs instead of pausing to solve every gap. Do not add an image merely because a vivid scene seems to require one.
4. After drafting, mark each consequential detail as securely remembered, externally supported, inferred, uncertain, or invented.
5. For nonfiction, activate `writing_choose_truth_contract_before_shaping_real_material` and `writing_creative_nonfiction_interrogate_memory_before_claiming_precision`; qualify, paraphrase, verify, or remove precision that exceeds its basis.
6. For fiction, use the memory as a starting pressure rather than a transcript. Transform identities, motives, setting, sequence, and outcome as needed, while keeping researched or presented factual claims accurate.
7. Reread for temporal shape. Ensure the scene's turn arises through selected action and perception rather than through a retrospective conclusion the earlier self could not have formed.
8. Rewrite once in past tense or from a later reflective vantage and compare the available hindsight, distance, and uncertainty. Retain the stance that best serves the chosen contract and effect, and name the effect it produces that the other one did not. Both stances are defensible, so the choice is only legible through what it bought.

## Success Check
- The draft presents one bounded movement rather than a general period of life.
- Sensory details are selected for the scene's viewpoint and change.
- The experiencing self knows only what was available within the event.
- Consequential precision has an identified basis rather than assumed authority.
- Nonfiction revisions expose uncertainty responsibly; fiction revisions transform rather than merely disguise lived material.
- The final tense and vantage produce a named effect the alternative does not.

## Common Failures
- Declaring a truth contract only after unsupported invention has entered the draft.
- Treating vividness or present tense as proof of accurate recall.
- Giving the earlier self the narrator's later interpretation.
- Filling every sensory category with invented detail to make the scene feel complete.
- Keeping fiction bound to actual sequence when transformation would create the necessary pressure.
- Qualifying nonfiction so vaguely that readers cannot tell what the writer believes occurred.

## Notes
Memory arrives as a selective present construction, but fiction and nonfiction grant different permissions for shaping it. A bounded scene makes those decisions visible because precise dialogue, sequence, sensation, and interior access must each be supported, qualified, or invented under a declared contract. Present tense is a diagnostic pressure rather than a guarantee of immediacy; comparing it with a retrospective stance exposes where later knowledge has entered and whether that distance helps the work.

`writing_variant_trigger_bounded_memory_from_present_action_and_return` makes the present scene the memory's frame. Establish an ordinary action before the trigger, repeat one sensory or physical element across the temporal transition, and stop the remembered passage after the specific perception or event that changes the live moment. Return through action rather than a mechanical time label when orientation remains clear. The memory earns interruption by altering what the character notices, understands, avoids, says, or does next; association alone is not enough.
