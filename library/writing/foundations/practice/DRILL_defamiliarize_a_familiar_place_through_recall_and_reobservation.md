# Defamiliarize a Familiar Place through Recall and Reobservation

## Practice Task
Describe a familiar place, route, person, or routine first from memory and then from fresh observation, using the discrepancies to draft a more discriminating passage.

## Target Skill
Detect what habitual perception filters out, gather newly available particulars, and select the details that change how a familiar subject is understood.

## Setup
Choose a safe, readily accessible subject encountered often enough to feel visually or behaviorally settled. Prepare separate spaces for recalled details, observed details, and the final paragraph.

## Instructions
1. Without looking at or performing the subject, list the details you expect to find, including position, color, material, sound, smell, texture, sequence, and any habitual interpretation.
2. Observe or perform the subject slowly. Change one ordinary vantage by crossing the route, using a different seat, reversing direction, or pausing at a stage normally passed automatically.
3. Record what memory omitted, simplified, misplaced, or preserved after it changed. Include relevant senses without filling a quota.
4. Mark three discrepancies that alter scale, atmosphere, character, function, history, or the meaning of the routine rather than merely adding inventory.
5. Draft a short passage from the newly observed subject. Give the selected details a viewpoint, time, place, or pressure that explains why they matter now.
6. Remove any detail retained only to prove that it was noticed. Keep the few particulars whose absence would restore the earlier habitual picture.
7. Repeat later with a familiar subject from a different category and compare which kinds of assumption most often displaced observation.

## Success Check
- The remembered list is written before any observation and is not corrected afterwards.
- The vantage is genuinely changed — crossing, reversing, pausing — rather than the usual pass performed more slowly.
- What memory omitted, simplified, misplaced, or preserved and then altered is recorded in those categories, since each licenses a different move.
- Senses are included as the subject supplies them, without filling a quota.
- Three discrepancies are marked for what they alter — scale, atmosphere, character, function, history, meaning — not merely for having been wrong.
- The passage discriminates more finely than the remembered version, and the improvement traces to a recorded discrepancy.

## Common Failures
- Looking at the subject before completing the recall pass.
- Treating every discrepancy as equally meaningful.
- Adding one item for each sense even when it contributes nothing.
- Choosing a novel or exotic place instead of testing perception against familiarity.
- Ending with notes and never converting changed attention into prose.

## Notes
Familiarity replaces repeated observation with a serviceable internal model. Recall followed by reobservation makes that substitution visible: objects change, remembered materials prove inaccurate, routine suppresses peripheral details, and a different position alters relations that seemed fixed. The exercise trains both abundance and refusal. Fresh noticing supplies candidates, while the final passage keeps only what turns altered perception into meaning.
