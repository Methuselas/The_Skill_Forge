# Freewrite to Discover What the Draft Is About

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a memory, image, question, or subject feels alive but the writer cannot yet name what the piece should say or where it should go
**THEN** write continuously for a bounded interval without a prescribed plan or correction, follow emerging associations, then reread to mark the details, insights, questions, and turns that reveal a possible center
**ELSE** use goal-directed brainstorming when the writing problem is already clear and needs options rather than discovery

## Do
- Begin from one live piece of material rather than from a demand to explain an entire topic.
- Remove avoidable distractions and choose a writing interval long enough for the first obvious thoughts to give way to less rehearsed connections.
- Keep moving through awkward phrasing, missing punctuation, and uncertain direction; private exploratory prose does not need submission-level control.
- Follow an unexpected shift in subject when it carries energy, then notice what relation drew the writing there.
- After the interval, underline concrete details, surprising statements, unresolved questions, and passages that invite expansion; repeat from a different seed when nothing usable surfaces.

## Don't
- Force the writing to obey an outline or prompt before it has discovered a meaningful direction.
- Stop to repair grammar, polish sentences, or judge whether each line belongs in a final draft.
- Treat wandering as automatically productive when the new material has no discoverable relation to the starting pressure.
- Keep generating raw prose without rereading and selecting what changed the available understanding.

## Checklist
- The interval produced continuous language rather than an outline or list of planned points.
- At least one detail, relation, question, or insight was not fully known before writing began.
- A digression either reveals a useful connection or can be set aside without distorting the remaining material.
- Marked passages provide a possible subject, pressure, scene, or next inquiry.
- The raw writing remains exploratory material rather than being mistaken for a finished draft.

## Notes
Freewriting and brainstorming suspend judgment for different reasons. Brainstorming searches for alternatives inside a named problem; freewriting helps discover the problem itself. The review after the interval is essential: association creates raw material, while marking the passages that surprise, specify, or complicate converts that material into a usable next move.
