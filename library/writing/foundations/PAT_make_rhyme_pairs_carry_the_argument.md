# Make Rhyme Pairs Carry the Argument

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a rhymed poem's end words satisfy the sound pattern but do not help its conflict, comparison, or turn
**THEN** choose rhyme partners whose meanings also oppose, reframe, or answer one another so the scheme advances the poem's argument
**ELSE** keep a quieter semantic pairing when a conspicuous contrast would distort the thought.

## Do
- Draft each rhyme pair as a relationship between ideas before solving the surrounding lines.
- Place an initial judgment in one rhyme position and use its partner to revise, complicate, or answer that judgment later.
- Vary the semantic work across the scheme: one pair can expose a social contrast, another can revalue the past, and a final pair can convert accusation into reply.
- Carry syntax across selected line endings so the sound recurs without making every rhyme a complete slogan.
- Reserve the most decisive semantic answer for the closing pair when the form gives that position extra force.

## Don't
- Do not treat end words as interchangeable merely because they sound alike.
- Do not bend the poem toward a convenient rhyme that weakens the speaker's actual thought.
- Do not make every pair perform the same contrast; repeated sound can support several stages of an argument.
- Do not let an emphatic closing rhyme announce a resolution the preceding lines have not prepared.

## Checklist
- Can each prominent rhyme pair be described as a meaningful relation, not only a sonic match?
- Does at least one later partner change how its earlier partner is understood?
- Do the pairs trace movement through the poem rather than decorate an otherwise separate statement?
- Does syntax remain credible where it crosses a rhyming line ending?
- If the closing pair answers the poem, has the earlier scheme earned that answer?

## Notes
White's fourteen-line model supplies seven rhyme pairs and repeatedly makes their meanings participate in its movement. Early pairs bind social exclusion to intellectual difference; later pairs connect retrospective pity with belated revaluation and move from a parent's implied question to the speaker's answer. Alternating rhymes carry those changes through the longer body, while the final pair concentrates the comic rebuttal. The useful method is not the scheme alone but the double duty assigned to its end words: they recall one another by sound and revise one another by sense.
