# Explain a Stalled Draft Aloud to Find the Problem

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a draft feels blocked but the underlying failure cannot yet be named
**THEN** dissect the work aloud to any attentive listener until the intended move and the actual obstacle become explicit

## Do
- Describe what the piece is trying to accomplish before listing what feels wrong.
- Walk through the point where momentum, clarity, or confidence disappears.
- Use the pressure of complete spoken sentences to expose assumptions that remained vague in silent thought.
- Capture the first statement that turns “it does not work” into a specific conflict, missing link, or unwanted effect.
- Return immediately to the draft with that diagnosis while the language is fresh.

## Don't
- Do not require the listener to be a writing expert; the act of explanation performs much of the diagnostic work.
- Do not turn the conversation into a request for the listener to invent the solution.
- Do not keep talking after the problem becomes specific enough to test on the page.

## Checklist
- Was the intended effect stated aloud?
- Can the exact point of failure now be located?
- Did the diagnosis become more specific during explanation?
- Is there a concrete revision or experiment to try next?

## Notes
The source makes listener expertise optional to an almost comic degree: an instructor or peer is ideal, but a family member or even a silent pet can suffice. What matters is dissecting the work aloud. The pattern is therefore distinct from receiving critique; it externalizes the writer's own reasoning until an unnamed block becomes a revisable problem.
