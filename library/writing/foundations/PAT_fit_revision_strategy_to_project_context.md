# Fit Revision Strategy to Project Context

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a familiar revision routine is being applied automatically to a new draft
**THEN** select the pass, feedback source, distance, and scale of change from the project's actual purpose and constraints
**ELSE** keep the routine only while it remains compatible with those conditions.

## Do
- Name why the text is being written before choosing what a successful revision must optimize.
- Account for format, medium, and genre because each makes different features available for inspection and change.
- Distinguish unfamiliarity with the task, subject, or audience from a defect in the draft; each gap calls for different evidence.
- Include the projected formality, task length, and intended final length in the revision budget.
- Fit incubation, rereading, outside response, and number of passes to the time and responsibilities actually available.

## Don't
- Do not promote one expert's personal routine into a universal formula.
- Do not copy the revision sequence of a short familiar piece onto a long or unfamiliar project without rechecking its demands.
- Do not call context an excuse to avoid revision; it changes what can be done, not whether the work benefits from another vision.
- Do not diagnose the artifact before checking whether the real gap is knowledge of subject, audience, task, or form.

## Checklist
- Is the purpose of this text explicit?
- Have format, medium, genre, audience, and formality been considered?
- Which of task, subject, and audience are familiar or unfamiliar?
- Do the planned distance and feedback routes fit the available time?
- Is the chosen method responding to this project rather than habit alone?

## Notes
Faigley and Witte found extreme diversity in how expert writers revise and tied that variation to situational variables rather than one superior formula. Their list spans purpose, form, medium, genre, several kinds of familiarity, audience, formality, and length. Starkey adds the practical constraint of competing responsibilities. The result is not permission to skip another pass; it is a routing check before selecting how that pass will work.
