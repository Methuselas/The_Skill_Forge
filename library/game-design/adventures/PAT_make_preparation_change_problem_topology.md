# Make Preparation Change Problem Topology

## Pattern Rule
**IF** investigation, legwork, planning, reconnaissance, or acquisition is meant to be a major part of play
**THEN** let successful preparation remove, reroute, expose, combine, delay, or transform later obstacles so players face a meaningfully different problem rather than merely receiving bonuses against the same mandatory sequence
**ELSE** keep preparation lightweight when the downstream situation cannot actually change.

## Do
- Give security, opposition, routes, schedules, credentials, dependencies, and procedures discoverable structure that preparation can exploit.
- Let acquired information bypass a roll when the characters have genuinely removed the uncertainty or obstacle that roll represented.
- Let preparation convert a live contest into a prepared state when appropriate: valid credentials, a discovered backdoor, a planted device, a compromised insider, a mapped route, or a pre-positioned asset can replace later uncertainty rather than merely improve the same roll.
- Let preparation reveal alternate interfaces: a social credential instead of a lock bypass, a maintenance entrance instead of the guarded lobby, a physical jackpoint instead of remote intrusion, or another causal route.
- Attach costs to preparation through time, money, favors, exposure, opportunity, or incomplete information so the planning phase still contains decisions.
- Preserve consequences when preparation fails or is partial; bad information should change the route or risk rather than automatically terminate the scenario.

## Don't
- Resolve a long planning phase only as +1 or +2 to the same inevitable infiltration roll when the fiction says the team changed the situation materially.
- Require every designed obstacle to occur even after players obtained information or access that logically bypasses it.
- Make preparation mandatory busywork when no discovered fact can alter later choices.
- Hide all useful structural information until after the players have already committed to the route it could have changed.

## Checklist
- At least one preparation result can remove or transform a later obstacle rather than merely modify its probability.
- At least two materially different routes can emerge from different preparation outcomes when the scenario supports them.
- Preparation costs or risks create choices rather than a free optimal pre-roll checklist.
- A failed or partial preparation result still leaves the scenario operable.
- The post-preparation problem can be described differently from the unprepared problem without referring only to numeric bonuses.

## Notes
Preparation earns table time when it changes **what must be solved**. The strongest payoff is often procedural deletion or replacement: a later access test no longer occurs because the team changed the access state beforehand. A passcode, shift schedule, forged credential, compromised employee, map, remote-control route, or discovered dependency can remove an entire layer of risk. This makes planning mechanically consequential without requiring a separate planning minigame: the reward is a changed scenario topology.
