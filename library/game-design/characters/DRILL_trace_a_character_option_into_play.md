# Trace a Character Option Into Play

## Practice Task
Take one class, skill, feat, trait, template, advantage, power, or equivalent character choice and follow it from character creation through representative play and advancement.

## Target Skill
Verify that a character option creates a meaningful, differentiated contribution in play without becoming a dead choice, mandatory permission key, or exploit component.

## Setup
Choose an option important enough that a player might select it as part of the character's identity or specialization. Prepare at least two representative situations where the advertised competence could plausibly matter.

## Instructions
1. State the competence, fantasy, or problem-solving role the option advertises and the recurring situations in which that promise should matter.
2. Specify what the specialist can perceive, attempt, accomplish, or contribute better, more reliably, more cheaply, or more broadly than adjacent characters.
3. Test at least one nonspecialist route and verify that it preserves adventure viability without granting equivalent specialist performance for free.
4. Run one representative scene and record whether the option materially changes information, decisions, risk, access, or outcome.
5. Advance the character once and test whether the option remains meaningful relative to neighboring options.
6. Combine it with at least one other legal option and test for a dominant strategy, erased niche, or unintended change to the risk profile.
7. Decide whether to keep, revise, restrict, or remove the option based on its observed contribution, permission-gating, bookkeeping cost, and combination behavior.

## Success Check
- A representative scene was actually run or concretely simulated, and the record identifies what information, decision, risk, access, or outcome changed because the tested option was present.
- The specialist advantage over adjacent characters is stated concretely, and at least one nonspecialist route was tested rather than merely proposed.
- The test names a plausible near-miss in which the nonspecialist receives effectively equivalent specialist performance for free, and records why that result would erase the niche or what constraint prevents it.
- At least one legal option combination and one advancement state were actually tested, with the observed interaction recorded instead of assuming the pieces remain balanced in isolation.
- Any keep, revise, restrict, or remove decision includes the reason drawn from the observed tests; a preference such as “this feels fine” does not pass.

## Common Failures
- Evaluating an option from its character-creation text without placing it in a representative adventure.
- Treating universal access as inherently fair even when it destroys the reason a specialist exists.
- Protecting a niche by making one class a compulsory key for routine progress.
- Testing feats, abilities, or other selectable pieces one at a time while ignoring combinatorial exploits.
- Keeping a flavorful option whose opportunities almost never occur in the game being designed.

## Notes
Character options are part of the game's promise about who a player can become. This drill checks both sides of that promise: the content must give the option somewhere consequential to matter, and the option must remain differentiated when other characters, advancement, and legal combinations enter the same design space.
