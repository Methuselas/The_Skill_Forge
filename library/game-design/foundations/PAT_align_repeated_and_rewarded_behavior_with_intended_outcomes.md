# Align Repeated and Rewarded Behavior with Intended Outcomes

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a game or subsystem is meant to cultivate, reinforce, assess, or reward particular player behavior
**THEN** make the actions players repeat and the rewards they pursue reinforce the behavior that produces the intended experience or external outcome
**ELSE** when advancement or rewards are meant to be neutral, verify that their triggers do not quietly redirect play toward a different priority.

## Do
- State the desired behavior in observable terms, such as adapting to changing conditions, negotiating, coordinating, following a procedure, exploring, or taking calculated risks.
- Inspect the actions players actually repeat rather than assuming the game's subject matter or stated theme proves what it practices.
- Match uncertainty to the behavior: use it when adaptation and judgment matter; reduce it when exact procedural repetition is the capability being trained.
- Inspect what earns experience, development points, advancement checks, wealth, status, or other rewards, because repeatable rewards create strong incentives.
- When enjoyment and an external outcome both matter, evaluate them separately and check whether the reward loop reinforces rather than competes with the target behavior.
- Distinguish the reward economy from the development economy: what earns growth and what that growth can buy are separate design decisions.

## Don't
- Confuse “a game about a topic” with “a game that practices a capability related to that topic.”
- Reward shortcuts, farming behaviors, or dominant routines that contradict the activity the game is supposed to value.
- Assume a reward is behaviorally neutral merely because it is traditional for the genre.

## Checklist
- The intended behavior can be observed during play.
- The game's recurring actions actually exercise or express that behavior.
- Randomness, cooperation, competition, and feedback are compatible with the intended outcome.
- Reward triggers have been compared against the actions the game wants players to repeat.
- Obvious farming or optimization strategies do not produce a stronger incentive than the intended play.
- When an external outcome exists, experiential success and instrumental success can be evaluated independently.

## Notes
Players learn a game's priorities from what they repeatedly do and what the system rewards. A reward structure can therefore reshape play even when the rulebook describes broader goals. Advancement currency also has two jobs that should not be conflated: the game decides how growth is earned, then separately decides how players may direct that growth.
