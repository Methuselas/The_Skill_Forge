# Use the Defining Affordances of an Adopted Game System

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a design adopts an established game system, framework, genre tradition, or recognizable design family
**THEN** identify the features that create its distinctive play experience and give those features meaningful work in the new design
**ELSE** reconsider whether the adopted foundation is actually the right one.

## Do
- Play or closely study representative implementations to learn expectations that may live in play culture rather than in explicit rules text.
- Identify mechanics, pacing habits, resource pressures, character capabilities, or presentation conventions that users rely on when recognizing the system or tradition.
- Build situations that let the adopted system’s signature tools matter rather than merely preserving compatibility on paper.
- Preserve the expected experience while still changing content, setting, or emphasis where the design calls for it.

## Don't
- Claim compatibility while designing around or avoiding the foundation’s defining mechanics.
- Reduce a design tradition to surface aesthetics when its characteristic play comes from procedures and player expectations.
- Assume that reading a license or stat block is enough to understand how the system is experienced at the table or screen.

## Checklist
- The adopted system or tradition has a short list of defining play affordances.
- At least one recurring situation in the design activates each affordance that is supposed to remain important.
- Representative games have been observed in actual play or equivalent execution when feasible.
- Removing the adopted foundation would materially change the experience rather than only the terminology.

## Notes
Compatibility is not the same as fit. A design can obey every formal rule and still miss what users value about the framework. Studying representative play helps reveal conventions that are carried socially or procedurally, and using those affordances keeps an adaptation from becoming a generic design wearing borrowed labels.
