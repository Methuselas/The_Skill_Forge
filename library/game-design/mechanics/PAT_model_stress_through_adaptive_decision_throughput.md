# Model Stress Through Adaptive Decision Throughput

## Pattern Rule
**IF** stress is meant to impair a character's ability to reassess a fast-changing situation without simply making the character less skilled
**THEN** constrain adaptive decision throughput while allowing simple precommitted or repetitive behavior to continue when the fiction supports it
**ELSE** use a simpler competence penalty or acute panic rule when continuous cognitive friction is not part of the intended experience.

## Do
- Distinguish continuing a simple order from choosing a new response to changing information.
- Let the player decide where limited decision opportunities are spent when preserving agency is important.
- Allow leadership, orders, doctrine, automation, or another explicit mechanism to offload decision burden when appropriate, and price that help through attention or action cost.
- Keep acute shock, surprise, or panic separate from continuous stress when the game benefits from distinguishing them.
- Test whether experienced and inexperienced characters behave differently under pressure rather than merely receiving different attack modifiers.

## Don't
- Represent all battlefield stress as a flat penalty to trained competence.
- Replace a behavior-producing constraint with faster turn order unless the lost behavior is intentionally being removed.
- Seize character personality or force arbitrary flight when temporary hesitation or frozen decision throughput would express the intended state.
- Let a stress mechanic remove agency without giving the player meaningful allocation, precommitment, recovery, or mitigation decisions.

## Checklist
- The mechanic distinguishes adaptive reassessment from simple continuation when that distinction matters.
- A player can name at least one decision created by the stress constraint.
- More experienced characters recover, adapt, or receive decisions differently in a way visible at the table.
- Leadership or mitigation changes decision access rather than merely adding an unrelated numeric bonus when cognitive offloading is part of the model.
- Simplifying the mechanic does not silently remove the behavior it was designed to create.

## Notes
Stress can be modeled as loss of decision bandwidth rather than loss of skill. A character may remain capable of crawling toward a destination, firing repeatedly, or following a simple driving instruction while becoming slower at choosing a new action when conditions change. This keeps the pressure on player decision-making without requiring the system to dictate personality or make every trained action numerically worse.
