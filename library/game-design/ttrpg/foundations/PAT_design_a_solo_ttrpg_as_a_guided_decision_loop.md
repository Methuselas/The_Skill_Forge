# Design a Solo TTRPG as a Guided Decision Loop

## Pattern Rule
**IF** designing a TTRPG for one player without a live facilitator
**THEN** repeat a loop of prompted choice, rules-mediated outcome, and a clearly selected next situation.
**ELSE** use a group-facing structure when the intended experience depends on unconstrained negotiation among several players.

## Do
- Present a situation that gives the player a meaningful but bounded decision.
- Resolve uncertainty through the character state and the game's mechanics before directing the next prompt or branch.
- Let the result change the next situation so the player is guiding a character rather than merely reading a fixed story.
- Use the early loop to introduce the rules a player must understand before later choices become more demanding.

## Don't
- Promise the unrestricted choice space of a facilitated group game when the solo structure cannot respond to it.
- Make the player choose a branch before the relevant character state or resolution rule has any effect.

## Checklist
- Each turn identifies the current situation, a player decision, an outcome procedure, and the next state.
- The character sheet or other state changes what can happen next.
- The first sequence teaches the minimum rules needed to continue without outside explanation.

## Notes
Solo play needs structure to replace the facilitator's ability to react in the moment. Branches and random outcomes constrain the available paths, but they also preserve uncertainty and let a player discover a game through consequential action.
