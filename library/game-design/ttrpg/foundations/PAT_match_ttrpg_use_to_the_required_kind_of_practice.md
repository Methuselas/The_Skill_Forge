# Match TTRPG Use to the Required Kind of Practice

## Pattern Rule
**IF** considering a TTRPG for an educational, training, or applied purpose
**THEN** use it to practise adaptive judgment, communication, or collaborative response rather than exact procedural performance.
**ELSE** choose a more controlled instructional method when success depends on reproducing a fixed sequence precisely.

## Do
- Name the capability participants should practise before selecting the game's genre, roles, and resolution rules.
- Build situations that require participants to interpret changing conditions, negotiate, or make consequence-bearing choices.
- Keep the game's uncertainty aligned with the value of practising adaptation rather than presenting it as a substitute for expert instruction.

## Don't
- Use random outcomes to assess whether someone can execute a safety-critical procedure correctly.
- Assume a game suited to one audience or purpose will transfer unchanged to another.

## Checklist
- The intended learning or training outcome benefits from improvisation or social decision-making.
- The game does not claim to certify competence in a fixed procedure.
- Genre, audience, and scenario all support the stated purpose.

## Notes
TTRPGs make a structured imaginary space where participants can respond to uncertainty together. That makes them useful for rehearsing adaptive choices, but the same openness makes them a poor fit for teaching a procedure whose value lies in exact, repeatable execution.
