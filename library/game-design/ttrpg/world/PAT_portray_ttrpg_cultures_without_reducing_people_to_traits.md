# Portray TTRPG Cultures Without Reducing People to Traits
## Pattern Rule
**IF** designing cultures, ancestries, or societies for a TTRPG
**THEN** give people specific social contexts and individual choices instead of assigning fixed abilities, behavior, or worth to an inherited group.
## Do
- Build cultural differences through institutions, histories, practices, and material conditions.
- Seek perspectives beyond your own when adapting real cultural inspiration.
## Don't
- Relocate recognizable cultural imagery into fantasy while retaining its stereotypes and power assumptions.
## Checklist
- Characters can differ meaningfully within the same culture or ancestry.
- Cultural detail supports play without requiring a group to stand for one personality type.
## Notes
Worldbuilding becomes richer when cultures are lived systems rather than a short list of innate traits. This also leaves players room to make distinct characters.
