# Express Faction Power as Deployable Response Capacity

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an organization, faction, corporation, government, gang, cult, or other persistent antagonist is supposed to matter beyond one stat block
**THEN** define what resources, agents, access, information, institutions, and escalation options it can actually deploy in response to player actions
**ELSE** keep the faction lightweight when its power will never be queried in play.

## Do
- Translate abstract influence into deployable forms: personnel, specialists, money, transport, surveillance, legal authority, safe sites, media reach, supply, political pressure, or other concrete capabilities.
- Connect response strength to the faction's current motive, knowledge, jurisdiction, and willingness to spend resources rather than treating all power as omnipresent.
- Let persistent enemies escalate causally from known state so players can anticipate, evade, bargain with, sabotage, or exhaust the response capacity.
- Track only the resource categories likely to affect future play; a faction does not need a full national budget to send a team, freeze an account, or close a route.
- Make damaged or lost faction assets matter when the campaign is supposed to support strategic pressure against the organization.

## Don't
- Describe a faction as “powerful” without giving the referee any practical answer to what it can do next.
- Use faction power as arbitrary permission for the referee to negate any player precaution.
- Give every organization infinite off-screen resources unless the setting explicitly requires that asymmetry and players can still make meaningful decisions around it.

## Checklist
- The faction has at least three concrete response channels relevant to the campaign.
- Responses depend on motive, information, access, and cost rather than pure referee fiat.
- Players can take actions that alter at least one response channel.
- A character-generated enemy can be connected to actual deployable capacity when activated.
- The representation is no more detailed than the campaign is likely to query.

## Notes
Persistent antagonists become operational when their power has form. “A megacorporation hates you” becomes playable once the referee knows that it can send covert operatives, leverage a hospital contract, trace communications, deploy security, or pressure an employer—and also knows what information and motivation would cause it to do so. Causal response preserves decision value better than arbitrary genre punishment.
