# Trace a Setting Assumption Into Play

## Practice Task
Choose one significant fact about a setting and trace only its genuine dependencies until it produces concrete decisions or adventure possibilities.

## Target Skill
Turn a significant setting fact into coherent player-facing consequences without over-propagating it into unrelated parts of the world.

## Setup
Use a setting premise that could plausibly affect access, travel, resources, institutions, information, culture, or character capabilities.

## Instructions
1. State the setting fact, then define where, when, for whom, at what cost, and under what limits it operates.
2. List the world elements that genuinely depend on those constraints and draw a short dependency chain for each.
3. Identify at least three concrete effects on player choices, character opportunities, information, access, or adventure structure.
4. Test one apparent contradiction to determine whether the conflicting element actually depends on the premise; preserve it when no dependency requires a change.
5. Convert one dependency chain into something characters can seek, combine, protect, trade, misunderstand, negotiate over, or exploit.
6. Remove at least one consequence that exists only because the premise was assumed to affect everything.

## Success Check
- The setting fact has explicit operating constraints, and at least one dependency chain has actually been traced from those constraints to a concrete player-facing decision, access condition, information problem, or adventure opportunity.
- At least three consequences have identifiable causal dependencies; a consequence justified only because it “fits the theme” is named as a near-miss and excluded.
- One apparent contradiction has been run through the dependency test, and the result records why it survives or why it must change rather than merely recording the choice.
- One discarded consequence is named with the missing dependency that disqualified it, demonstrating that the premise was not propagated universally by assumption.
- The final playable chain can be used without adding an unrelated bespoke subsystem; lore that never changes what characters can learn, attempt, choose, or access does not pass.

## Common Failures
- Declaring that one invention or magical fact changes the entire civilization without tracing why.
- Protecting a preferred setting detail with an arbitrary exception instead of testing whether a real contradiction exists.
- Producing only lore consequences that never alter player information, access, choices, or opportunities.
- Treating restrictions as defects to remove instead of possible sources of decisions and adventure.

## Notes
The exercise is especially useful for high-impact premises such as teleportation, resurrection, ubiquitous magic, artificial intelligence, scarce resources, or unusual political structures. The goal is not maximal extrapolation; it is enough causal propagation that the setting feels coherent and produces playable consequences.
