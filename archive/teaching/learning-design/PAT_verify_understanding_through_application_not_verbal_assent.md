# Verify Understanding Through Application, Not Verbal Assent

## Pattern Rule
**IF** the target is procedural or craft understanding that should guide action
**THEN** verify it with a bounded application that makes the relevant decision observable, rather than inferring mastery from recognition, agreement, terminology, or the learner saying they understand
**ELSE** use a simpler recall or explanation check when the learning target itself is declarative knowledge.

## Do
- Ask for a small task whose result exposes the specific decision the learner is supposed to understand.
- Diagnose the resulting artifact or performance against that decision, not merely against surface polish.
- Use the application to distinguish “I recognize the explanation” from “I can use the principle when making.”
- Let later attempts revise and deepen the learner's understanding when new consequences become visible through practice.
- Keep the task bounded enough that failure can be attributed to the target decision rather than to dozens of unrelated demands.

## Don't
- Do not treat confident vocabulary or agreement with the instructor as proof of procedural mastery.
- Do not interpret one weak attempt as definitive evidence of misunderstanding when the task also depends on unrelated skills.
- Do not turn every factual concept into a performance assessment when a direct knowledge check is sufficient.
- Do not give application without feedback; the diagnostic value comes from connecting the result back to the governing decision.

## Checklist
- Does the task make the target decision visible in the learner's work?
- Can the evaluator distinguish misunderstanding from unrelated execution difficulty?
- Is feedback tied to what the application revealed?
- Can the learner apply the principle again after the diagnosis?
- Is verbal explanation being used as support rather than as the sole evidence of mastery?

## Notes
In his commentary on Howard Pyle, Loomis warns that what seems obvious to an experienced instructor may not be equally clear to a learner, and that a learner can believe a principle is understood while the work shows otherwise. His durable teaching contribution is the assessment distinction: procedural understanding becomes trustworthy when it survives application, and its meaning can deepen through repeated use and self-discovery. This complements `AP_teach_craft_from_orientation_to_generation`, which already moves instruction into generative practice; this Pattern owns the narrower question of how to verify that the learner actually understood the operative principle.
