# Match Models to Learner Output Scale

## Pattern Rule
**IF** you are selecting exemplar artifacts for learners who must produce a bounded practice result
**THEN** choose strong, complete examples near the length and structural complexity learners can realistically read closely and emulate within the assignment
**ELSE** label larger masterpieces as aspirational references rather than operational models for that task.

## Do
- Compare each candidate model’s length, number of moving parts, and refinement burden with the actual deliverable before assigning it.
- Preserve quality while reducing scale. Choose a concise work by a skilled practitioner instead of replacing an overlarge masterpiece with a weak example.
- Budget enough time for learners to inspect how the model works, not merely finish reading it.
- Prefer examples that are complete at their smaller scale, so learners practise making a whole artifact rather than an amputated fragment of a larger one.
- State which features transfer to the assignment and which belong to a scope the learner is not yet being asked to reproduce.

## Don't
- Do not choose the most famous or impressive example without checking whether its scale makes it imitable under the current time and length limits.
- Do not imply that a learner should reproduce the complexity of a long work in a short assignment.
- Do not confuse brevity with low standards; the smaller example still needs enough craft integrity to reward close study.

## Checklist
- Can learners read the example closely within the available session?
- Is its length in the same practical range as the assigned output?
- Can the learner identify and attempt the relevant craft moves without also solving a much larger structural problem?
- Is the model a complete work rather than a convenience excerpt?
- Are aspirational examples clearly distinguished from models for immediate practice?

## Notes
Starkey contrasts a frequently anthologized 6,000-word story with a much shorter story by the same author. His teaching decision is not to lower literary quality; it is to remove a scale mismatch between what students study and the brief work they can reasonably produce. The model earns its place when close reading can lead to a feasible attempt rather than admiration without transfer.
