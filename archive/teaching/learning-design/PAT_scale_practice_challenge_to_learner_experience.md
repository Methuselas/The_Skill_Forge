# Scale Practice Challenge to Learner Experience

## Pattern Rule
**IF** the size, openness, or complexity of a practice task exceeds learners' current experience and prevents them from completing a meaningful attempt
**THEN** reduce the challenge to a bounded complete form with clear parameters, then expand it as successful experience accumulates
**ELSE** preserve the larger challenge when learners can enter, sustain, and finish it productively.

## Do
- Diagnose whether the obstacle is missing experience with the task's scale rather than a temporary lack of effort.
- Preserve the target capability inside a smaller complete attempt instead of replacing it with disconnected preparatory fragments.
- State the form, limits, and completion condition clearly enough that learners can tell what finishing means.
- Let completion create evidence and confidence for selecting the next increase in scale or complexity.
- Increase one demanding dimension at a time when possible so the source of a new stall remains visible.

## Don't
- Do not treat an experience mismatch as proof that the learner lacks talent.
- Do not reduce challenge until the practice no longer exercises the intended capability.
- Do not confuse this decision with shortening an otherwise manageable artifact merely to preserve feedback and revision time.
- Do not keep the task permanently small after learners demonstrate readiness for a larger form.

## Checklist
- Is the learner stalled by the task's present scale, openness, or complexity?
- Does the reduced task still produce a complete meaningful artifact?
- Are its parameters and completion condition explicit?
- Will completing it supply evidence for the next increase in challenge?
- Is this an experience-scaling problem rather than a feedback-capacity problem?

## Notes
Starkey advises an inexperienced writer daunted by a novel to begin with a short poem, story, essay, or play whose parameters are easier to grasp. `VAR_starkey_begin_writers_with_short_defined_forms` retains that writing-specific route. The foundation differs from `PAT_shrink_practice_scope_to_preserve_revision`: this pattern responds to a mismatch between challenge and experience, while the earlier pattern protects the time and attention needed for feedback and revision.
