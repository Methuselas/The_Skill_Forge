# Shrink Practice Scope to Preserve Revision

## Pattern Rule
**IF** learners rush, resist restarting, or receive shallow feedback because a practice artifact is too long
**THEN** reduce the deliverable to a complete short form that leaves time for a second attempt, close response, and substantive revision
**ELSE** expand scope only while the full feedback-and-revision loop still fits.

## Do
- Set the maximum size from the time available for drafting, possible restart, feedback, and structural revision—not from the prestige of a longer final form.
- Keep the shortened task complete: the smaller artifact must still exercise the target structure or decision cycle rather than stopping at a disconnected fragment.
- Reserve enough shared time to inspect several professional examples closely and discuss more than one learner artifact.
- Reserve enough response capacity outside the session for specific comments rather than superficial coverage.
- Increase difficulty through craft demands and revision depth before increasing page count.

## Don't
- Do not assign so much output that learners protect a weak draft simply because replacing it would waste too many pages.
- Do not count proofreading as the revision cycle; the preserved time must support changes to structure, character, emphasis, order, or other governing choices.
- Do not shrink the task into a fragment that cannot demonstrate the target form.
- Do not assume more pages produce more learning when they reduce the attention available per page.

## Checklist
- Can a learner restart after an unsuccessful first attempt without losing the whole schedule?
- Is there time for close feedback on multiple artifacts?
- Does the task still produce a complete form?
- Can revision reach governing decisions rather than only sentence cleanup?
- Would adding length reduce the number or depth of feedback-and-revision cycles?

## Notes
The source contrasts the emotional and practical cost of restarting three pages into a four-page story with restarting ten pages into a twelve-page story. It also connects shorter work to more professional models discussed per class, more learner drafts receiving feedback, and more careful instructor response. Short scope is therefore a capacity decision, not an easier grading standard.
